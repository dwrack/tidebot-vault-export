# Role Guide: Kayak Guide

## What This Job Actually Is

You're not just paddling people through a swamp. You're the driver, the naturalist, the safety officer, the entertainer, and the only person between 12 strangers and a bad day on the water. Some days that means narrating cypress tree ecology while an alligator cruises past. Other days it means talking a nervous guest off the ledge before they even get in the kayak.

The guides who last here genuinely like being around people, don't mind physical work, and can stay calm when things get weird. Because things will get weird. A guest will panic-paddle into a stump. The trailer will give you trouble at the launch. Someone will show up drunk for a 9 AM tour. You handle it.

## Minimum Requirements

- Valid Louisiana driver's license with a clean driving record
- At least 18 years old
- Current CPR certification (or willingness to obtain before your first solo tour)
- Ability to lift 70 lbs (you're loading kayaks on and off a trailer, repeatedly)
- Comfortable driving a full-size van with a kayak trailer attached
- Reliable personal transportation to 1407 Piety St, New Orleans

## What Makes a Great Guide

These aren't requirements, but the guides who have these qualities tend to thrive:

- Genuine interest in Louisiana ecology, swamp systems, local history. Guests can tell the difference between someone reading from a script and someone who actually finds this stuff fascinating.
- Comfort leading groups of 6-14 people, including families with kids, bachelorette parties, and nervous first-timers, sometimes all on the same tour.
- Calm under pressure. Not "I say I'm calm" calm. Actually calm when someone flips a kayak or lightning appears on the horizon.
- Strong communication. You need to project your voice across open water, give clear instructions, and read body language to spot a struggling guest before they ask for help.
- Physical fitness beyond the minimum. Tours are 2+ hours of paddling, loading, and being on your feet in Louisiana heat.

## Disqualifiers

These are non-negotiable and will result in immediate termination:

- Evidence of substance abuse on the job
- Violent or sexual misconduct of any kind
- Failed background check
- Repeated failure to follow safety protocols after documented warnings

## Training Progression

### Shadow Shifts (2-3 tours)
You ride along with an experienced guide. Watch everything: how they greet guests, how they run check-in, how they back the trailer, how they read the group's energy and adjust. You'll also practice the physical stuff during these shifts, loading and unloading kayaks, backing the trailer at the launch.

### Co-Guide Shifts (1-2 tours)
You start leading parts of the tour. Maybe you run the safety briefing one day, handle check-in the next. The experienced guide is still there, filling gaps and giving feedback after.

### First Solo
Management is on call the entire time. You have a phone number that gets answered. After the tour, you debrief with management same day. This is where you get honest feedback.

### Ongoing
You don't stop learning after your first solo. Routes change seasonally. Equipment gets updated. You'll hear feedback from guests and management. Stay open to it.

## Code of Conduct

### Appearance and Professionalism
- Wear the NKST uniform or approved branded gear on every tour
- Professional tone with guests at all times
- Be on time. If you're not 5 minutes early, you're late.

### Absolute Prohibitions
- No smoking, vaping, drugs, or alcohol before or during tours
- No phone use while driving, period
- No profanity in front of guests
- No feeding wildlife or allowing guests to feed wildlife
- No allowing any guest on the water without a properly fitted PFD
- No leaving guests unattended at any point during the tour cycle
- No allowing guests on rope swings or other hazards
- No using personal vehicles for tour operations

### Tour Humor
This is worth spelling out because it's a common place where new guides get in trouble. Adventure humor works great. Self-deprecating jokes, funny stories about wildlife encounters, light ribbing when someone paddles in circles. Guests love it.

What doesn't work: drunk stories, profanity-laced anecdotes, sexual jokes or innuendo, making fun of guests (even gently), or political commentary. You've got families with 8-year-olds and bachelorette parties on the same tour. Keep it clean and inclusive.

## Violation Protocol

We don't want to fire anyone. But safety and guest experience aren't negotiable.

**First violation:** Equipment duty (deep clean, inventory, maintenance) plus a full re-read of the relevant SOP section. Documented in your file.

**Second violation:** Formal review meeting with management. Probationary period with specific benchmarks.

**Third violation:** Termination of contractor relationship.

Serious safety violations (allowing guests without PFDs, driving under the influence, etc.) can skip straight to termination. Use your judgment and we'll use ours.

## Pay and Scheduling

### Pay
- Paid bi-monthly via PayPal
- Pay periods: 1st through 15th, and 16th through end of month
- Payment issued within a few days of each period closing

### Scheduling
- Tours are scheduled through FareHarbor and assigned by management
- If you need to cancel a shift, provide 48 hours notice minimum
- Less than 48 hours notice without a genuine emergency will be documented

### Tips
Tips are 100% yours. We don't take a cut. Guests tip well when the experience is good, which circles back to everything else in this document.

## The Bottom Line

This job is physically demanding, socially intense, and sometimes unpredictable. It's also one of the best gigs in New Orleans if you're the right person for it. You spend your days on the water, you meet interesting people, and you get paid to share a place you care about. Take it seriously and it'll take care of you.
