# SOP — Incident Investigation & Corrective Action
*Owner: Contractor + Owner | Triggered: any Incident Report submission*

When a guide files an Incident Report, the work isn't done. We investigate, document root cause, take corrective action, verify the fix, and retain the records. This SOP exists because plaintiff's lawyers ask "what did you do about it?" and we need a documented answer every time.

---

## When this SOP triggers

- Any guest injury (no matter how small)
- Any guest property damage
- Any vehicle accident or near-miss
- Any guide injury on duty
- Any equipment failure that affected a tour
- Any harassment or conduct complaint (parallel to the Anti-Harassment Policy investigation)
- Any near-miss that could have been bad (capsize, animal encounter, weather call, lost guest)
- Any complaint that mentions safety, conduct, or "could have been hurt"

If you're not sure whether something rises to the level of an Incident Report, file it anyway. Cheap to over-document, expensive to under-document.

---

## Step 1 — Containment (within 24 hours of incident)

Before investigating, contain.

- Verify all injured parties have received care
- Verify the equipment / vehicle / location involved is out of service until cleared
- If a guest needs follow-up (medical, refund, communication), contractor owns that
- If a guide is shaken up, contractor checks in personally (text, then call)
- Preserve all evidence: photos, the equipment itself, witness contact info, the original Incident Report

---

## Step 2 — Investigation (within 48 hours)

Contractor (or owner if contractor is involved) conducts the investigation.

**Interview:**
- The guide who filed the report
- Any other guides on tour
- Any witnesses (other guests, bystanders, other operators)
- The injured / affected guest (carefully, with an eye toward not making it worse legally; coordinate with insurance carrier if claim is likely)

**Collect:**
- Original Incident Report
- All photos, video, audio (the guide's tour log audio, per Slack channel)
- FareHarbor manifest for the day
- Weather data for the day (Wunderground archive, NOAA)
- Equipment maintenance history (pull from Vehicle Maintenance Log or equipment log)
- Guide's training file (acknowledgement, quiz pass date, CPR cert)

**Document interviews in writing.** Date, who was present, what was said. Sign and date the notes.

---

## Step 3 — Root Cause Analysis (within 5 business days)

Don't stop at "the guide didn't tighten the strap." Keep asking why.

The Five Whys:
1. Why did the kayak come loose? Strap wasn't tight.
2. Why wasn't the strap tight? Guide didn't double-check after loading.
3. Why didn't they double-check? Pre-drive walkaround says to check, but they were rushed because the prior tour ran late.
4. Why did the prior tour run late? Booking spacing was 15 minutes too tight on a hot day.
5. Why was spacing tight? Booking system default; nobody had reviewed it for the new launch ramp distance.

The actual fix is at level 5, not level 1. Root cause is rarely "the guide messed up."

Write the root cause statement in plain English. One paragraph. What actually happened, all the way down.

---

## Step 4 — Corrective Action Plan (within 7 business days)

Every root cause gets a corrective action. Write it as a specific, dated commitment.

| Root cause | Corrective action | Owner | Deadline |
|------------|-------------------|-------|----------|
| Booking spacing too tight | Update FareHarbor to add 30 min between back-to-back tours on hot days | Contractor | YYYY-MM-DD |
| Pre-drive walkaround skipped under time pressure | Add "if you're rushed, the walkaround is more important, not less" to Handbook + next monthly retraining | Owner | YYYY-MM-DD |

If the action is "retraining," the retraining session is scheduled and dated, not vague.

---

## Step 5 — Verification (within 30 days)

After the corrective action is implemented, verify it actually changed something.

- Did the booking spacing update? (Pull screenshot of FareHarbor settings)
- Did the Handbook get updated? (Show the diff)
- Did the retraining happen? (Date and signed roster)
- Has the issue recurred? (Check next 30 days of reports)

Document verification in the same incident file.

---

## Step 6 — Retention

All incident files retained **7 years** in `Operations/Incident Files/[YYYY-MM-DD] [short title]/`. Contents:

```
2026-04-27 kayak strap loose Highway 11/
  01 original incident report.pdf
  02 photos/
  03 interview notes - guide.md
  04 interview notes - witness.md
  05 weather data.pdf
  06 maintenance log excerpt.pdf
  07 root cause analysis.md
  08 corrective action plan.md
  09 verification.md
  10 closure note.md
```

7 years matches Louisiana's general statute of limitations for personal injury (1 year) plus the longer statute for contract / fraud claims, with margin. Some claims (minors) toll until age 18 + 1 year, so retain longer for incidents involving children.

---

## Step 7 — Closure

Contractor and owner both sign the closure note. The note states:
- Date of incident
- Summary of root cause
- Summary of corrective action
- Verification status
- Whether any insurance claim was opened
- Whether any litigation was threatened
- Whether the file is closed or remains open

Closed files stay in retention; they don't get touched again unless a related claim surfaces.

---

## What this protects against

- Plaintiff's lawyer: "What did your company do about the prior incident on March 14 with the loose strap?"
- Us: pulls the closed file, hands over root cause + corrective action + verification.
- Result: we look like a company that takes incidents seriously and acts on them. The "negligence" theory weakens.

Without this file, the same question gets us "we talked about it" and a much worse-looking record at trial.
