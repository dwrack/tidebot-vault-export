# Operations Quiz

**Passing score: 80% (8 out of 10 correct)**

Operational problems are rarely dramatic. They're the kind of thing that derails your day if you don't handle them fast and smart.

---

### Question 1

You arrive at 1407 Piety St and discover the van has a flat tire. Your tour starts in 90 minutes. What do you do?

A) Change the tire yourself and hope you make it in time.
B) Contact management immediately. They need to know ASAP so they can arrange a backup vehicle or reschedule the tour. Don't try to fix this solo on a tight timeline.
C) Cancel the tour and text all guests.
D) Call an Uber for the guests and drive them to the launch in their own cars.

**Best answer: B**

Management has options you don't: a backup vehicle, a contact who can help fast, or the authority to reschedule. Every minute you spend trying to handle this alone is a minute management doesn't have to solve it. You can't cancel unilaterally (that's management's call), and Uber to the swamp isn't a thing.

### Question 2

You're backing the trailer at the launch and the hitch feels loose. It's not obviously disconnected, but something doesn't feel right. What do you do?

A) It's probably fine. Keep going carefully.
B) Stop. Get out. Inspect the hitch visually and physically. If you can't confirm it's fully secure, do not move the trailer. Call management.
C) Unhitch and re-hitch from scratch.
D) Have a guest hold the trailer while you back up.

**Best answer: B**

A hitch that "doesn't feel right" is not a hitch you drive on. Stop, inspect, and confirm. If you're unsure, call management. Answer C isn't wrong in principle, but if you're not confident in your ability to diagnose the problem, re-hitching might not fix it. Never involve guests in equipment operations (Answer D). And "probably fine" (Answer A) is how trailers end up in ditches.

### Question 3

You're at 740 N. Rampart and 2 of your 10 guests are 15 minutes late. Everyone else is in the van, checked in, and ready. What do you do?

A) Leave. They had their chance.
B) Text the late guests one more time. Then contact management before leaving without them. Management may have context you don't.
C) Wait another 15 minutes to be safe.
D) Send the 8 guests ahead in the van and wait for the other 2 in your personal car.

**Best answer: B**

The SOP says wait max 15 minutes, contact management before leaving without anyone. You've hit that limit. Text them, call management, and follow management's direction. They might know the guests called in late, or they might tell you to go. Don't use your personal vehicle for tour operations. Waiting indefinitely isn't fair to the other 8 guests.

### Question 4

You're loading kayaks at the launch and realize you're 2 PFDs short for your guest count. How did this happen, and what do you do now?

A) Give PFDs to all but 2 guests and keep those 2 close to shore.
B) Do not launch. This should have been caught during morning inspection. Contact management. You need more PFDs before anyone gets on the water.
C) Share PFDs between guests in tandems.
D) Buy some at a nearby store.

**Best answer: B**

No one goes on the water without a PFD. Period. This is a morning inspection failure, the count step exists to prevent exactly this. Contact management to figure out how to get 2 more PFDs to the launch. This might mean a delay, but a delay is infinitely better than putting guests on the water unprotected. No sharing, no exceptions, no "close to shore" workarounds.

### Question 5

You're 45 minutes into a 2-hour tour when the sky darkens quickly and wind picks up noticeably. You don't hear thunder yet. What do you do?

A) Keep paddling and monitor. No thunder, no problem.
B) Start moving the group toward shore or the launch. Communicate what you're doing: "Hey everyone, let's start heading back this way. Weather's moving in and I want to make sure we're ahead of it." Monitor constantly. If you hear thunder, get off the water immediately.
C) Call the tour and head straight back.
D) Find a covered area on the water and wait it out.

**Best answer: B**

Dark sky and rising wind in Louisiana often means a storm is coming, even if you don't hear thunder yet. Start positioning the group closer to safety while maintaining a fun, calm tone. You're not cancelling yet, you're being smart about positioning. If thunder starts, you're already close to shore. Answer A waits too long. Answer C might be premature. There's no good covered area in the middle of a swamp.

### Question 6

You open the guide bag at the launch and the first aid kit is missing. Tour starts in 10 minutes. What do you do?

A) Run the tour without it. You'll be fine.
B) Check the van, check under the seats, check if it got moved. If you truly can't find it, contact management before launching. Do not run a tour without a first aid kit.
C) Stop at a pharmacy on the way to the launch.
D) Ask guests if anyone has first aid supplies.

**Best answer: B**

A missing first aid kit is a missing safety requirement. Check the obvious spots first because it might have shifted during loading. If it's genuinely gone, contact management. They may be able to bring one or tell you where a spare is. Running a tour in the swamp with no first aid supplies is not acceptable.

### Question 7

You're driving guests back from the launch and you're running 20 minutes behind schedule. Some guests mention they have dinner reservations. What do you do?

A) Speed up to make up time.
B) Acknowledge it: "I know we're running a bit behind. I'm going to get you back as quickly and safely as I can." Drive the speed limit. If specific guests need to be somewhere urgently, note it but don't compromise safety.
C) Skip the gas fill to save time.
D) Drop guests at Rampart first, then do post-tour tasks.

**Best answer: B**

Acknowledge the situation, take ownership without over-apologizing, and drive safely. Speeding with a loaded trailer is never the answer. Answer D is actually reasonable in a time crunch too (drop guests, then fuel up and close out), but B is the best overall answer because it addresses the guest relationship. Never skip gassing up because the next guide depends on it.

### Question 8

A guest left their sunglasses in the van at the launch. They realize it on the water, 30 minutes into the tour. They're upset. What do you do?

A) "Sorry, nothing I can do right now. We'll check the van after."
B) Acknowledge it with empathy: "That's frustrating. The van is locked and your stuff is safe. I'll make sure we find them as soon as we're back. In the meantime, I've got a spare pair in my guide bag if you want them." Then hand them the spare sunglasses.
C) Paddle back to shore so they can grab them.
D) Tell them you warned everyone about valuables.

**Best answer: B**

This is why the guide bag has extra sunglasses. Empathy plus a practical solution turns a minor frustration into a "wow, they thought of everything" moment. Saying "I told you so" (Answer D) is never the right move. Paddling back derails the whole tour. Answer A is technically fine but misses the chance to create a great experience out of a small problem.

### Question 9

Three things go wrong at once: a guest in the back of the group has stopped paddling and looks tired, the lead boat has drifted 200 yards ahead, and you notice a fishing boat approaching from the side channel. How do you prioritize?

A) Deal with the fishing boat first since it's a collision risk.
B) Signal the lead boat to stop (horizontal paddle). Address the fishing boat (wave or call out to establish awareness). Then paddle to the tired guest and check on them.
C) Paddle to the tired guest first since they seem like they need help.
D) Whistle to get everyone's attention and regroup.

**Best answer: B**

Triage based on immediacy. The lead boat drifting away means your group is splitting, so stop that first with a paddle signal (takes one second). The fishing boat is a moving hazard, so establish awareness next (a wave or call-out, takes 5 seconds). Then deal with the tired guest, who is stationary and wearing a PFD, meaning they're safe for the moment. Answer D isn't bad but three whistle blasts is the distress signal, which could cause panic.

### Question 10

It's 6 PM. You've finished everything on the closeout checklist except you realize you forgot to spray the PFDs with MiraZyme. They're already hung up. Do you...

A) They're hung up, that's good enough. MiraZyme can wait until next time.
B) Go back to Piety and spray them. It takes 5 minutes and prevents a problem that's much harder to fix later.
C) Text the morning guide and ask them to do it.
D) Make a note to double-spray them next time.

**Best answer: B**

Go back and spray them. It takes 5 minutes. PFDs that go untreated start developing the kind of smell that survives future MiraZyme treatment, meaning you're creating a problem that ends up as a guest experience issue and eventually a replacement cost. Passing the task to the morning guide dumps your job on someone else. "Double spray next time" isn't how MiraZyme works.

### Question 11

You're at the Shell station after a tour and the fuel card gets declined. What do you do?

A) Try a different Shell station.
B) Pay with your own card and submit for reimbursement later. Let management know the card was declined.
C) Leave the van without fueling and tell management.
D) Try a different gas station brand.

**Best answer: B**

The van needs to be fueled for the next guide. If the card is declined, pay out of pocket and notify management for reimbursement. That's the reality of the situation: the task needs to get done. Leaving the van unfueled (Answer C) creates a problem for the next person. Trying another brand (Answer D) won't work because the card is coded to Shell. Another Shell station (Answer A) might work if it's a terminal issue, but paying yourself is the fastest resolution.

### Question 12

You're loading the trailer after a tour and a strap looks frayed. It's still holding but clearly wearing out. What do you do?

A) Use it for now. It's still holding.
B) Note it in your end-of-day report to management. Use the strap for today but flag it as needing replacement before the next tour.
C) Remove the strap and don't use it. Drive with one fewer strap.
D) Replace it yourself from a nearby hardware store.

**Best answer: B**

A frayed strap that's currently holding is a strap that might not hold next time. Use it for the immediate trip back (you need to secure the kayaks), but report it to management so it gets replaced before the next tour. Don't drive with unsecured kayaks (Answer C). Don't ignore degrading equipment (Answer A). Buying a replacement yourself (Answer D) is going above and beyond, but management needs to know about equipment wear regardless.
