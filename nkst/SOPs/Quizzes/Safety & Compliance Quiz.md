# Safety & Compliance Quiz

**Passing score: 80% (8 out of 10 correct)**

These scenarios test whether you understand not just the rules, but the reasoning behind them.

---

### Question 1

You check the Honey Island water gauge the night before and it reads 4.2 feet. What do you do?

A) Run Honey Island anyway. 4.2 is close enough to 5.
B) Move to Manchac. Text guests about the route change and notify management.
C) Cancel the tour and offer refunds.
D) Wait until morning and check again before deciding.

**Best answer: B**

Under 5 feet is the hard cutoff for Honey Island. Kayaks will drag on the bottom, making the tour miserable and possibly damaging boats. Manchac is a great alternative. Cancelling is unnecessary since you have a backup route. Waiting until morning is risky because you need to notify guests of the change, and a morning text isn't enough lead time.

### Question 2

A guest says, "I'm a lifeguard. I don't need a PFD." How do you respond?

A) "I appreciate that, but it's policy. Everyone wears one on the water."
B) "That's awesome, but this isn't about swimming ability. It's about what happens when you go under unexpectedly, disoriented, maybe you've hit something. The PFD does the thinking for you. It stays on."
C) "OK, as long as you keep it in your kayak in case you need it."
D) "Let me check with my manager if we can make an exception."

**Best answer: B**

Answer B addresses the actual objection. A lifeguard thinks they don't need one because they can swim. You reframe it: PFDs aren't about swimming, they're about involuntary immersion and disorientation. Answer A is technically correct but doesn't explain why, which means the guest wears it resentfully. Answer C violates the PFD rule entirely. Answer D has no good outcome because the answer is always no.

### Question 3

You're mid-tour in Manchac and you hear distant thunder. You can't see lightning. What do you do?

A) Keep going but watch the sky closely.
B) Start heading toward shore at a normal pace. If you see lightning, speed up.
C) Immediately head to shore. Get all guests out of kayaks and into the van.
D) Call management for guidance.

**Best answer: C**

If you can hear thunder, lightning is within 10 miles. The rule is simple: get off the water. Don't wait to see a bolt. Don't "keep watching." Sound travels roughly 10 miles, so audible thunder means you're already in the danger zone. Get to shore, get in the van, wait 30 minutes after the last thunder.

### Question 4

A guest capsizes and is floating next to their kayak, clearly panicking, grabbing at the hull and splashing. What's your sequence?

A) Yell instructions from your kayak: "Climb back on! You're fine!"
B) Call out "Stay with your boat, I'm coming." Signal other guests to stop. Paddle directly to them and assist with re-entry.
C) Send the nearest guest over to help them.
D) Throw the throw bag immediately.

**Best answer: B**

Call out to reassure them and let them know help is on the way. Signal the group to hold position so nobody else gets involved or capsizes. Approach as the trained guide. Yelling instructions at a panicking person doesn't work because they can't process directions. Sending another untrained guest creates a second capsizing risk. The throw bag is for when you can't paddle to them, not the first move.

### Question 5

Before the morning tour, you're inspecting PFDs and one smells noticeably bad. The rest are fine. What do you do?

A) Spray it with MiraZyme and use it anyway.
B) Set it aside, don't use it for today's tour, and flag it to management.
C) Give it to whoever seems least likely to complain.
D) Toss it in the trash and note that you need a replacement.

**Best answer: B**

A PFD that already smells bad even after regular MiraZyme treatment likely needs deep cleaning or replacement, not a quick spray. Don't put it on a guest. Don't throw away company equipment without authorization. Set it aside, use a different one, and let management decide what to do with it.

### Question 6

A guest on the water starts showing signs of a serious allergic reaction: swelling face, difficulty breathing. You have a first aid kit but no EpiPen. What do you do?

A) Administer Benadryl from the first aid kit and continue the tour.
B) Get the guest to shore immediately. Call 911 with your GPS coordinates. Keep the guest calm and monitored until EMS arrives. Have another guest call management.
C) Paddle the guest back to the van as fast as possible and drive to the nearest hospital.
D) Call management and ask what to do.

**Best answer: B**

Anaphylaxis is a 911 emergency, period. Getting to shore and calling EMS with your coordinates is faster than trying to load everyone up and drive to a hospital you may not be able to find quickly. Benadryl alone won't stop anaphylaxis. Calling management for guidance wastes critical minutes. EMS knows where to go if you give them accurate coordinates.

### Question 7

What's wrong with using a generic QR code for waivers instead of the booking-specific waiver link?

A) The QR code doesn't work on all phones.
B) A generic waiver isn't tied to a specific booking, which creates a documentation gap that weakens its enforceability if there's an incident.
C) Guests find QR codes annoying.
D) FareHarbor doesn't support QR codes.

**Best answer: B**

The waiver's legal value depends on it being clearly tied to a specific guest, on a specific tour, on a specific date. A generic waiver that can't be definitively linked to that booking gives an attorney a gap to exploit. The extra 60 seconds to pull up the booking-specific link is the difference between a defensible waiver and a questionable one.

### Question 8

The van gets a flat tire on I-10 while you're driving 8 guests to the launch site. What's your priority order?

A) Fix the tire, then continue to the launch.
B) Pull over safely. Ensure all guests are safe and accounted for. Call management. Do not leave guests unattended to deal with the tire.
C) Call AAA and wait.
D) Have guests get out and help you change it so you can get back on schedule.

**Best answer: B**

Guest safety is always the first priority. Pull over safely, make sure nobody is at risk from traffic. Then call management because they can coordinate a solution (spare van, roadside assistance, tour reschedule) while you stay with the guests. Never leave 8 guests sitting in a van on the highway shoulder while you're under the vehicle.

### Question 9

What is the universal distress signal on the water?

A) Waving both arms overhead
B) Blowing your whistle once, loudly
C) Three whistle blasts
D) Holding your paddle vertical and waving it

**Best answer: C**

Three whistle blasts is the standard distress signal. This is why every PFD has a whistle attached. Holding a paddle vertical and waving is also a distress signal in our paddle signal system, but the whistle carries further and doesn't require visual line of sight. In practice, both work, but three whistle blasts is the universal standard.

### Question 10

You're at the launch and realize you only have 12 PFDs but 13 guests plus yourself. What do you do?

A) Give PFDs to all guests and go without one yourself.
B) Put 2 smaller guests in a tandem with one PFD between them.
C) Do not launch. Call management. You need 14 PFDs (13 guests + 1 guide) minimum.
D) Ask if any guests are strong swimmers and would be comfortable without one.

**Best answer: C**

Every person on the water wears a PFD. No exceptions for the guide, no sharing, no exemptions for swimmers. If you're short, you don't go out. Call management and figure out a solution: borrow PFDs, adjust the group size, delay until you can get more. This should have been caught during morning inspection, which is why the count step exists.

### Question 11

A guest falls in the water and says their neck hurts after hitting something underwater. They're conscious and floating with their PFD. What do you do?

A) Help them climb back in the kayak quickly before they get cold.
B) Stabilize them in the water. Do not move them. Call 911. Keep their head above water if possible and wait for EMS.
C) Tow them to shore by their PFD and then call 911.
D) Have another guest hold them while you paddle to get help.

**Best answer: B**

Neck pain after an underwater impact is a potential spinal injury. Moving someone with a spinal injury risks paralysis. Stabilize them in the water, keep their head above water, call 911 with your coordinates, and wait. The PFD is doing the flotation work for you. Do not drag, lift, or reposition them.
