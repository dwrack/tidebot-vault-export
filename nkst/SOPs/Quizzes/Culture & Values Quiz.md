# Culture & Values Quiz

**Passing score: 80% (8 out of 10 correct)**

Test your judgment on how we do things at NKST. These aren't trick questions. They're scenarios you'll actually face.

---

### Question 1

You show up for your shift and the guide who ran the morning tour left PFDs piled in the van instead of hanging them to dry. What do you do?

A) Leave them. Not your mess.
B) Hang them and spray with MiraZyme yourself. Mention it to management later.
C) Text the other guide and tell them to come back and fix it.
D) Post about it in the group chat so everyone knows.

**Best answer: B**

You don't leave PFDs in a pile because that's how they start smelling, and smelly PFDs affect your guests on the next tour. Handle it, then let management know so they can address the pattern. Calling someone out publicly in a group chat creates drama. Expecting someone to drive back across town is unrealistic.

---

### Question 2

A guest asks if they can feed bread to the turtles. They've brought a whole bag. What do you say?

A) "Sorry, it's against park rules."
B) "I get it, they're awesome, but we never feed wildlife out here. A fed animal is a dangerous animal. They start associating boats with food, and that makes it unsafe for every group after us. Let's just watch them do their thing."
C) "Sure, just a little bit."
D) "Absolutely not. That's a violation of federal wildlife law."

**Best answer: B**

Explain the why, not just the rule. Guests respect you more when they understand the reasoning. Answer A is technically fine but lacks context. Answer D is unnecessarily aggressive and probably not even accurate. Answer C is wrong, full stop.

### Question 3

You're running 10 minutes late leaving Piety St because the morning routine took longer than expected. What should you have done differently?

A) Skipped the PFD smell check to save time.
B) Arrived earlier. The SOP says 1 hour 25 minutes before tour time for a reason.
C) Texted guests that you'd be late.
D) Driven faster to make up time.

**Best answer: B**

The morning buffer exists because things take longer than you expect, especially when you're new. Cutting safety checks to save time is never acceptable. Speeding with a trailer full of kayaks is dangerous. Texting guests is better than nothing but doesn't fix the root cause.

### Question 4

You overhear another guide telling a group a story that includes some pretty colorful language and a joke about a drunk tourist from a previous tour. How do you handle it?

A) Mind your own business. Their tour, their style.
B) Laugh along, it's funny and guests seem into it.
C) Talk to the guide privately after the tour. Mention the humor guidelines.
D) Report it to management immediately.

**Best answer: C**

Handle it peer-to-peer first. The humor guidelines are clear: adventure humor is great, drunk stories and profanity aren't. A private conversation after the tour gives them a chance to adjust without embarrassment. If it keeps happening, then it becomes a management issue.

### Question 5

A guest tells you at check-in that they have a bad knee and aren't sure they can get in and out of the kayak. What's your approach?

A) Tell them they probably shouldn't do the tour.
B) Explain how the sit-on-top kayak works, that entry and exit are the hardest parts, and offer to help them in and out personally. Let them decide.
C) Put them in a tandem so their partner can compensate.
D) Call management and ask what to do.

**Best answer: B**

Challenge by choice. Give them the real information and let them make an informed decision. Telling someone they can't do it is presumptuous. Just stuffing them in a tandem doesn't address the actual concern (getting in and out). Calling management for something you can handle on-site wastes time and makes you look uncertain.

### Question 6

Why do we fill up at Shell stations specifically, with code 6006?

A) We get a corporate discount there.
B) The company fuel card only works at Shell. The code is tied to our account.
C) Shell has the best fuel for the van's engine.
D) It's the closest station to Piety St.

**Best answer: B**

The card is coded to Shell stations. Other stations will decline the transaction. This isn't a preference, it's a logistical constraint. The code 6006 identifies the purchase to our account.

### Question 7

You finish a tour and you're exhausted. It's hot, you've been on the water for 3 hours, and you just want to go home. Which of these can you skip?

A) Spraying PFDs with MiraZyme (you can do it tomorrow).
B) The social media post (nobody checks).
C) Filling the gas tank (it's still half full).
D) None of these. All are part of the closeout.

**Best answer: D**

Every single closeout step exists because someone skipping it once created a problem for the next person. Half a tank means the morning guide has to stop for gas and runs late. Skipping MiraZyme means PFDs start to smell within days. The social media post is part of our marketing engine. Do the full closeout every time.

### Question 8

A guest at check-in says something casually racist about the neighborhood around Rampart St. How do you handle it?

A) Ignore it and move on with check-in.
B) Laugh nervously and change the subject.
C) Redirect casually: "This is actually a really cool part of the city. Lots of history here." Then move on.
D) Tell them that kind of talk isn't welcome on the tour.

**Best answer: C**

You're not their teacher and you're not going to change their worldview in a parking lot. But you don't co-sign it either. A quick, natural redirect shows the other guests (who definitely heard it too) that you're not on board, without turning check-in into a confrontation. If it escalates or continues on the tour, then you address it more directly.

### Question 9

You see another guide on a different company's tour letting their guests paddle without PFDs. What do you do?

A) Nothing. Not your operation.
B) Call wildlife enforcement on them.
C) Make a note of it and mention it to management. It's useful to know what other operators are doing.
D) Paddle over and tell them to put PFDs on.

**Best answer: C**

You're not the water police, and confronting another operator on the water creates problems. But it's worth flagging to management because it affects the broader reputation of kayak tours in the area, and management may have a relationship with that operator or know the right channel to raise it.

### Question 10

It's your second week. You're co-guiding with an experienced guide and you notice they skip the paddle signal practice during the safety briefing. What do you do?

A) Nothing. They've been doing this longer than you.
B) Jump in and run the paddle signal practice yourself.
C) After the tour, ask them about it. "Hey, I noticed we skipped paddle signals. Is that intentional or did it just slip?"
D) Report it to management.

**Best answer: C**

Experience doesn't mean infallible. Maybe they forgot, maybe they have a reason, maybe they've gotten lazy about it. A curious, non-accusatory question after the tour is the right move. If you're co-guiding and feel comfortable, answer B isn't terrible either. But going straight to management before talking to the person skips a step.

### Question 11

A group of 8 friends are being loud and rowdy at check-in. They're clearly pregaming for a bachelor party. How do you set the tone?

A) Match their energy. They're here to party.
B) Be stern immediately. "This is a safety-focused tour, not a party boat."
C) Be friendly and warm, but deliver the safety briefing with clear authority. Let them know you're the guide and what you say on the water goes.
D) Call management and ask if you should cancel.

**Best answer: C**

Rowdy groups can have an amazing time if you establish yourself as the authority figure early, with a smile. Matching their chaotic energy means you lose control of the tour. Being overly stern makes you the buzzkill and they'll push back harder. The safety briefing is your moment to show that you're fun AND serious about safety.
