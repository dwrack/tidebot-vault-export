# Launch Site Procedure

Get guests on water safely, efficiently, and feeling confident. Target: 15 minutes from trailer to first paddle stroke.

---

1. Back trailer to launch. Go slowly. There are two sharp turns on the route in.

> [!tip]
> Take your time backing in. Nobody judges a careful trailer back. They absolutely judge a jackknifed trailer.

2. Direct guest vehicles to park away from the motorized boat launch area.
3. Send guests to the Pit Stop bar for restrooms and water refill. This buys you time to set up.
4. Unload kayaks and paddles onto the grass. Max 15 minutes for full unload.
5. Keep the launch area clean. Don't block other users.
6. Check trailer straps are secure. Lock trailer wheels before walking away from the vehicle.
7. Check the hitch.

## PFD Fitting

8. Fit every guest with a PFD. Type III, USCG approved.
9. Show each guest: loosen all straps, put it on, then cinch snug. Test by pulling up on the shoulders. If it rides over their head, tighten more.
10. For larger guests needing the triangle-style PFD, say: "This one will be more comfortable for you."

> [!warning]
> Don't say "this is the big one" or anything that calls out body size. "More comfortable" handles it.

11. Check every PFD has an attached whistle.
12. Quick smell check on each PFD as you hand it out. If one is bad, swap it.

## Safety Briefing

13. Deliver the full safety briefing (see Safety Briefing Script in Deep Read).
14. Paddle demonstration: forward stroke, turning, stopping, reverse.
15. Practice paddle signals with the group.

## Boat Assignments

16. Assign boats:
    - Tandems (Rambler/Tribe 13.5): heavier person in back seat. Max combined weight ~400 lbs.
    - Singles: for odd-numbered groups or strong/experienced paddlers.
17. Appoint a lead boat (experienced or confident paddler) and a sweep boat (you or second guide).
18. Help guests into kayaks and push off.
