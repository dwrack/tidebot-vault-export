# Guest Safety Video — 7 Concept Briefs

Goal: a short video every guest watches before launch. Funny enough that people actually watch it, complete enough that it counts as real safety coverage, and it quietly sets expectations (heat, bugs, mud, paddling effort, wildlife not guaranteed on demand).

**The safety beats every concept must cover:**
1. PFD on and buckled, the whole time
2. Stay seated, no standing in the kayak
3. Hands and feet stay in the boat (gator country)
4. Never feed or touch wildlife
5. Follow your guide, stay with the group
6. If you flip: stay calm, stay with the boat, guide comes to you
7. Sunscreen, water, bug spray before launch (also: we sell all three in the van)
8. Phones: waterproof case or it's a swamp donation ($10 in the van, hint hint)
9. Bathroom before we launch, there isn't one in the swamp

**Where it plays:** FareHarbor confirmation email link, QR code laminated in the vans (same lamination run as the price sheets), and it can loop on a phone/tablet during the shuttle ride. The van ride is the golden slot, guests are captive and bored.

---

## 1. "7 Ways to Not Become a Gator Snack"

Countdown listicle, one guide hosting, deadpan delivery. Each "way" is a real rule dressed as survival advice. "Way #4: keep your hands in the boat. The gators think fingers are Vienna sausages. They are not wrong about the shape."
- Length: 90 seconds
- Production: one guide, one afternoon, phone on a paddle mount
- Why it works: the title alone makes people press play. Countdown format keeps retention.

## 2. The Guide Confessional

Each guide delivers one rule straight to camera in their own words, cut together fast. Personality carries it: one guide dead serious, one whispering like it's gossip, one mid-paddle. Doubles as the guide facetime content already planned for the FB ads, same footage, two uses.
- Length: 2 minutes
- Production: collect clips over a week of tours, edit once
- Why it works: guests meet the crew before they arrive. The reviews prove guides ARE the product.

## 3. Airline Safety Demo, But It's a Kayak

One guide in the full flight-attendant routine: the two-finger point, the seatbelt mime, "in the event of a water landing, you are already there." Every airline beat mapped to a swamp rule.
- Length: 60-90 seconds
- Production: one guide who commits to the bit, scripted, one take-ish
- Why it works: universally recognized format, the parody does the comedy work for you.

## 4. Everything Wrong Guy

POV: a first-timer (a guide acting, or David) does every single thing wrong in sequence, standing up, dangling a hand, feeding a nugget to the water, no sunscreen. The real guide corrects each one with growing exhaustion. Ends with him sunburned, phoneless, and wiser.
- Length: 2 minutes
- Production: two people, half a day, needs light editing
- Why it works: showing the wrong thing is funnier and more memorable than stating the right thing.

## 5. Narrated by the Gator

Nature-documentary voiceover from the swamp's point of view, welcoming guests to the neighborhood and explaining the house rules. "We've lived here two million years. We have three requests." Footage is just normal tour B-roll.
- Length: 60 seconds
- Production: existing tour footage + one good voiceover. Cheapest option on this list.
- Why it works: zero new filming needed, and the conceit lets safety rules land as charm.

## 6. 1950s Instructional Film

Black and white filter, old-timey narrator voice, title cards, "So! You've decided to paddle a swamp." Guides act out the rules in exaggerated silent-film style.
- Length: 90 seconds
- Production: a fun crew afternoon, moderate edit for the retro treatment
- Why it works: highly shareable, works double-duty as social content and ads.

## 7. Kids Explain the Swamp

Kids (guides' kids, or a guest family that's game and signs a release) explain the safety rules in their own words, guides react. Kids saying "do NOT touch the alligator" is both the funniest and most effective version of that rule.
- Length: 60-90 seconds
- Production: depends entirely on finding the right kids; release forms required
- Why it works: disarming, wholesome, on-brand for a family tour product.

---

## Recommendation

Start with #5 (Narrated by the Gator) because it can exist THIS WEEK with zero new filming, then build #2 (Guide Confessional) over the following weeks since it rides on the selfie-video push already in the Slack post plan. The others are social/ad content candidates later.

Slack tie-in: this becomes a natural week 3 morning post. "Making a pre-launch safety video and I want it funny. Which of these would you actually want to be in?" Let the guides pick the concept, same playbook as the hats.
