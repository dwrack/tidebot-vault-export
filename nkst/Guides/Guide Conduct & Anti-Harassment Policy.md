# Guide Conduct & Anti-Harassment Policy
*Last updated: April 2026 | Applies to all NKST guides and managers*

We run a small business in a high-trust environment. Guides spend hours alone with guests, in remote areas, often handling people in physical and emotional situations (water, wildlife, bachelorette parties, kids, anxious paddlers). The standard for conduct is high, and we have to put it in writing so there's no ambiguity for guides, guests, or a court if something goes wrong.

This policy covers: guest-to-guide harassment, guide-to-guest misconduct, peer conduct between guides, the reporting chain, and the no-retaliation rule.

---

## 1. Standard of Conduct

Every guide on every tour:

- Treats every guest with courtesy. No exceptions for guests who are difficult, drunk, or rude.
- Does not make sexual comments, jokes, or innuendo, regardless of how a guest is acting.
- Does not initiate physical contact beyond what's required for safety (helping into a kayak, fitting a PFD, hand-up after capsize).
- Does not exchange personal contact information with guests on tour for non-business reasons. If a guest asks for your number to "stay in touch," redirect them to NKST's Instagram or email.
- Does not give rides outside the tour itinerary.
- Does not consume alcohol or drugs before or during a shift, or at any point in an NKST vehicle.
- Does not discuss other guests, other guides, the owner, or business operations in front of guests.
- Does not use racial, ethnic, religious, gender-based, or homophobic slurs or jokes, even if a guest says something first.

If a guest crosses a line in conversation, the guide redirects, doesn't escalate.

---

## 2. Guest-to-Guide Harassment

Guides are not required to tolerate harassment from guests. Examples that should be reported:

- Sexual comments, advances, propositions, repeated unwanted compliments about your body
- Unwanted physical contact (touching, brushing past on purpose, attempted hugs)
- Slurs or hostile comments based on race, gender, sexuality, religion, national origin
- Threats of any kind
- Stalking behavior (following you to your car, asking other guests for your info, repeated unwanted contact after the tour)

**What to do in the moment:**
1. Set a clear verbal boundary. "I'm here to guide your tour. Please keep things professional."
2. If it continues, end the inappropriate behavior with a clear statement: "If this continues, I'm going to end the tour."
3. If safety is at risk, end the tour. Get back to staging. Call the manager.
4. Document immediately afterward: time, what was said or done, who else heard it.

**What we do as a company:**
1. Manager calls or texts you within the hour to check in.
2. Incident Report filed within 24 hours.
3. Guest is banned from rebooking. We refund or do not, depending on conduct, but they're done with us.
4. If criminal conduct (assault, threats, stalking), we support filing a police report.
5. No guide is ever asked to "tough it out" or "give them a chance" with a guest who has crossed this line.

---

## 3. Guide-to-Guest Misconduct

Any of the following by a guide is grounds for immediate termination under the ICA:

- Sexual contact, sexual comments, or sexual propositions toward a guest, before / during / after the tour
- Pursuing a romantic or sexual relationship with a guest met on tour, including via DM, text, or in-person follow-up
- Physical contact beyond what's required for safety
- Retaliating against a guest who complained
- Photographing or recording a guest without consent
- Driving a guest anywhere off-itinerary
- Soliciting tips beyond standard practice, asking for cash off-platform, suggesting "private tours" for cash

If you have a question about whether something is OK, the answer is "ask the manager before you do it."

---

## 4. Peer Conduct (Guide to Guide, Guide to Manager, Manager to Guide)

The same rules apply between people working together:

- No harassment based on sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, race, ethnicity, religion, national origin, age, disability, pregnancy, or family status
- No retaliation for raising concerns
- No quid pro quo (favors, scheduling, pay) tied to personal favors of any kind
- Disagreements get worked out professionally; if they can't, escalate to the owner

A manager has more power in the relationship than a guide does. Managers carry an extra responsibility to keep interactions professional, and to never use scheduling, pay, or training opportunities as leverage.

## 6. No Retaliation

We will not retaliate against any guide who reports a concern in good faith, even if the concern turns out to be unfounded. Retaliation includes:

- Cutting your hours or tour assignments
- Negative coaching that wasn't earned
- Cold-shouldering, exclusion, gossip
- Termination

Retaliation against a reporter is itself a fireable offense for whoever does it.

If you feel you're being retaliated against after making a report, escalate directly to the owner.

---

## 7. Investigation Process

Every formal complaint is investigated. Here's what that looks like:

1. **Acknowledgement within 24 hours.** Whoever you reported to confirms receipt.
2. **Initial conversation within 72 hours.** Manager or owner sits down with the reporter, takes a full statement, asks what outcome the reporter is hoping for.
3. **Witness interviews.** If others were present, manager talks to them confidentially.
4. **Subject of the complaint is interviewed.** Their side is heard. They're not told who reported, when possible, but in some situations identity is unavoidable.
5. **Decision within 10 business days.** Findings, action taken, and what changes if any.
6. **Documentation.** All of it goes in a confidential file, retained 7 years.
7. **Reporter is told the outcome,** even if details about the subject's discipline are kept confidential.

We don't promise we'll always agree with the reporter's preferred outcome. We do promise we'll listen, investigate, and act.
