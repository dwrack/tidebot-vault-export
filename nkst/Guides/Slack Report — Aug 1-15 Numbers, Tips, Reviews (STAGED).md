>
> FareHarbor is how I pay you. If you pick up a shift, swap one, or cover a call-off, make sure it gets entered under your name. If you can't do it yourself, drop it in here and it'll get fixed same day. A tour that isn't assigned to you is a tour you have to remember to argue for.
>
> **Tips, and why I want them going through FareHarbor.**
>
> Two weeks, 26 bookings, and exactly one guest tipped through the system. $65, on that same 8/1 tour. That $65 sat in the account for two weeks because nobody knew whose it was.
>
> Cash and Venmo are still yours, no change there, keep them. But cash is dying and you all know it. Half these guests haven't carried a bill since 2019, and they'll tell you they wanted to tip and had nothing on them. That's real money you're losing every week.
>
> The tip option is already live at checkout on both Manchac tours, the sunset, and Honey Island. Most guests never notice it. So say something at the takeout. Not a pitch, just a fact:
>
> *"If you had a good time, there's a tip option on your booking confirmation, and it comes straight to me."*
>
> That's it. No pressure, no hand out, no awkward pause. Say it once while everyone's still buzzing and loading boats, then drop it. Guests who want to tip will, guests who don't won't feel worked over.
>
> On my end I'm switching the tip option on for the Whitney combo, the Honey Island extended, the fishing charter, and the rentals, which don't have it right now. That's on me and it'll be done this week.
>
> **Reviews. Why they actually matter to you.**
>
> Not for my ego. Here's the real chain: reviews are most of what decides whether we show up when someone in a hotel room types "swamp tour near me." Showing up higher means more bookings. More bookings means more departures on the calendar. More departures means more shifts, and shifts are your paycheck. We ran nine guided tours in two weeks. That is thin, and every one of you can feel it in your schedule.
>
> A guide who's regularly named in reviews is also the guide I can point to when someone asks for a specific person, and it's the first thing I look at when we talk about who gets the extendeds and the private groups.
>
> **How to ask, and how not to.**
>
> The right time is at the takeout, while you're pulling boats out and everyone's still lit up about the gator. Not a text later, not a card in the van.
>
> What works:
>
> • Say your name out loud during the tour, more than once. Nobody writes "our guide was great" and then remembers a name they heard one time in a parking lot at 8am. Guests want to name you, they just don't retain it.
> • Ask for the specific thing they liked. "If you leave a review, mention the barred owl. That's the stuff that helps the next person decide." Specific reviews read as real and they rank better than "great tour!"
> • Ask once, warmly, and let it go.
>
> What I don't want, and I'll be straight with you about it:
>
> • Never ask anyone who wasn't on the water. Not a friend, not a roommate, not a cousin who owes you one.
> • Never write it for someone or hand them a line to copy.
> • Don't ask a group to all post the same day.
>
> Two reasons, and neither is about trust. First, Google is aggressive about this. It reads clusters, matched phrasing, and reviews from accounts with no history, and when it flags a listing it strips reviews retroactively and can suppress the whole profile. We have over 1,300 reviews built up over years. That's the single most valuable thing this business owns and I'm not risking it.
>
> Second, it doesn't work anyway. A review that doesn't sound like a guest doesn't convince anyone.
>
> Ask real guests, at the right moment, and say your name. That's the whole method and it beats anything clever.
>
> **The Reddit threads and the Facebook groups.**
>
> Some of you have seen me dropping Reddit threads in here with a few drafted comments to choose from. Those matter more than they look.
>
> Real example from last week, and it's not a flattering one for us. A review landed on our page on the 9th from a guy who took Amtrak across the country, and it opened with him finding his tour "through an expansive Reddit forum listing do's and don'ts in NOLA area." He planned his whole New Orleans day off a Reddit thread and booked a kayak trip.
>
> He wasn't ours. He paddled with another outfit and posted the review on the wrong company's page by accident. So we got the review and they got the money.
>
> Sit with that one. Somebody else's comment in a Reddit thread, written who knows how long ago, put that guy in somebody else's boat. That is a booking that was sitting there for whoever showed up in the thread first. That's how much those threads get read and used.
>
> Same thing in the Facebook groups. New Orleans Things To Do, the visitors guide groups, the travel tips groups. Someone posts "first time in NOLA, what should we do" and gets forty replies. Those threads sit there and get read by hundreds of people planning trips, long after the conversation is over.
>
> Here's why it has to be you and not me. A business account replying "check out our swamp tour" gets scrolled past or reported. You saying "I guide out at Manchac, go in the morning, bring more water than you think you need" is somebody who actually knows the water. Completely different thing. People can smell the difference instantly.
>
> Two rules and they're not negotiable: **no links** and **never name another operator.** A link is what gets the comment auto-removed and gets your account flagged. Just say the business name in plain words and answer the actual question they asked. If the thread isn't really about anything we do, skip it. Nobody needs a swamp tour pitch under a question about parking tickets.
>
> Grab one of the drafts, make it sound like you, post it from your own account. If you'd rather write your own, even better.
>

## Why the review table stays out

David asked to show the guides the review numbers alongside the tour numbers. I left the per-guide review counts out of the post, and I'd push back on adding them.

Posting that table means posting this, to 21 people, with names attached:

| Guide | Tours 8/1-15 | Reviews naming them |
|---|---|---|
| River Bourne | 3 | 0 |
| Chelsea Hines | 3 | 0 |
| Alexis Hingle | 1 | 0 |
| Stephanie Lazensky | 1 | 0 |
| Abbie Marks | 1 | 0 |
| Alex Wallace | **0** | **26** |

There is no version of that table that doesn't read as an accusation, and it convicts him in front of the whole crew before anyone has asked him a single question. If the innocent explanation turns out to be right, and it might be, he's been guiding shifts that never got entered under his name, then that table publicly punishes a guide for a scheduling problem that is ours. That's unrecoverable. He'd be gone, and fairly so.

The order that works: **call him first, alone.** The 1:1 script is in [[Operations/Guide Payroll — Aug 1-15 2026]]. Then post the group message, which is written to work either way — it sets the norm clearly without a single name in it, so if the reviews do come off it, everyone already knows the standard.

If David still wants the numbers shown after that call, the honest version is a per-guide DM with each person's own tours, tips, and mentions. Same information, no public tribunal.
