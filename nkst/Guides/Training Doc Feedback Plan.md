# Training Doc Feedback Plan

**Goal:** Improve the Guide Training Curriculum and Tour Script & Talking Points using input from the people actually leading tours. Most of our guides have years on the water. The questions below are written to pull out their expertise, not test it.

**Cadence:** One Slack question per week for ~6 weeks, dropped in the main guide channel. Plus a separate set for 1:1 coaching conversations where deeper stories can land.

**Tone:** Peer to peer. We're refining the docs together. No quiz energy. No "what is a cypress knee" basic stuff. Guides should feel consulted, not audited.

**How to run it:**
- Drop one question per week, ideally Tuesday or Wednesday morning (post-tour adrenaline is over, weekend is far enough away)
- Don't @everyone. Just post it. Let it breathe.
- React to every reply. Even a thumbs up. Silence kills these threads.
- After each round, paste the responses (or summary) into the bottom of this doc with the date and who weighed in
- After all 6 questions, do a sweep of the Curriculum and Tour Script and merge what came up

---

## Slack Question Sequence

### Week 1: Field-tested fixes to the Tour Script

> Hey all — working on a refresh of the Tour Script & Talking Points doc. You've all been doing this longer than the doc has existed. What's something you actually say on tours that's not in there, or something in there you've quietly stopped using because it doesn't land?

*What we're after:* the gap between the official script and what experienced guides actually do on the water. That gap is where the doc gets better.

---

### Week 2: The moment guests get it

> Different question this week. We talk about content stops in the script, but I'm curious about the actual moment on tour where you feel guests shift from tourists to actually invested in the place. What were you doing? What did you say? Was it a planned beat or did it just happen?

*What we're after:* the real "click" moments so we can stop teaching newer guides to chase generic enthusiasm and start teaching them what actually moves people.

---

### Week 3: Wildlife and ecology accuracy check

> The ecology section in the Tour Script has some specifics: cypress knees as stabilizers (not breathing), tannins from decomposition, the 40% wetlands stat, gator size estimation, water hyacinth invasion story, etc. Anything in there you've found out is wrong, outdated, or oversimplified? Anything you wish was in there that isn't?

*What we're after:* science accuracy from people who actually read up on this stuff. Several of our guides have specialized knowledge (herpetology, edible plants, birding) that's worth pulling into the shared baseline.

---

### Week 4: Honey Island vs Manchac

> Honey Island is a different animal than Manchac. The Tour Script is mostly Manchac-shaped. For those of you who've run Honey Island: what changes? What do you tell guests differently? What do you watch out for that doesn't apply at Manchac? Trying to make sure when we send a guide there, they're set up to deliver something good and not get burned by the differences.

*What we're after:* a Honey Island companion section, written from real experience instead of guesswork.

---

### Week 5: The awkward situations doc

> Section 7 of the Tour Script ("Situations That Come Up") has 7 scenarios pulled from old calls and texts. What's missing? What's a guest situation you've handled that newer guides should know about before it happens to them? Doesn't have to be dramatic. Sometimes the small awkward stuff is the hardest to figure out solo.

*What we're after:* expand the situational library so newer guides aren't reinventing the wheel under pressure.

---

### Week 6: The living manual

> Last one for now. We've talked about building a living wildlife/history reference for guides. Before I go off and build something nobody uses: what format would actually work for you? Notion page? PDF? Obsidian doc? Slack canvas? Print binder in the van? Genuinely asking. The point is something you'd open in the field or before a tour, not a thing that lives unread on a shelf.

*What we're after:* don't build it wrong. Pick the format guides will actually use.

## 1:1 Coaching Questions

These go deeper than Slack questions can. Use them in coaching sessions, post-tour debriefs, or casual sit-downs. Pull two or three per conversation, don't run through the whole list.

1. Walk me through the version of the safety briefing you actually deliver. Where did it diverge from the script and why?
2. What's the question you get from guests that nobody warned you about, that you've now got a really good answer for?
3. If you had to cut one content stop and add a new one, which would you cut and what would you replace it with?
4. What do you wish someone had told you in your first month here that the curriculum doesn't cover?
5. Tell me about a tour that went sideways and how you brought it back. What was the read on the group? What did you do differently than what the doc says?
6. Where are we still teaching new guides something that experienced guides know is wrong or outdated?
7. What's a story you tell on tour that's purely yours? Where'd it come from?
8. Have you ever had a guest fundamentally change how you describe something on tour? What was it?
9. If a brand new guide shadowed you tomorrow, what would you most want them to copy from you, and what would you want them to ignore?
10. What part of the job did experience teach you that the curriculum can't?
