### Cold open (0:00–0:20) — hook first
*(Drone pushing along the bridge, swamp underneath.)*

HOST (VO): This is the longest bridge over water in the country. Almost 23 miles of highway, and it crosses one of the oldest cypress swamps in Louisiana. People drive over it at 60 and never look down. We paddle underneath it. And the locals will tell you this swamp is haunted.

*(Beat. Cut to host in the kayak, under the cypress.)*

HOST: Let me tell you about Julia Brown.

### The legend (0:20–2:00)
HOST: A hundred years ago there was a town out here. Frenier. A little railroad and logging village right on the edge of the swamp. And Julia Brown lived there. People called her a healer, a hoodoo woman, depending on who you ask and how scared they were of her.

The story goes she used to sit on her porch and sing. And one of the songs went something like, one day I'm gonna die and I'm gonna take the whole town with me.

On the morning of September 29th, 1915, Julia Brown died. And the day they buried her, the hurricane came.

*(Cut to archival-feel stills or slow swamp footage.)*

HOST: No warning like we'd get today. The storm came up the lake and put water right over Frenier. Hundreds of people died. The town was just gone. And the people who survived swore they heard her song in the wind.

### The honest turn (2:00–3:30) — this is the NKST move
HOST: Now. Is the swamp haunted?

*(Host paddles into an open stretch, old cypress around.)*

HOST: I've guided out here for years. I've never seen a ghost. But I'll tell you what I have seen. These cypress trees were standing when Julia Brown was alive. Some of them were standing centuries before that. They watched the town go under and they're still here.

And here's the part that actually keeps me up. Louisiana is losing this. We lose a football field of wetland about every 100 minutes. The swamp that swallowed Frenier in one night, we are losing the slow way, every single day.

So when people ask me if the Manchac swamp is haunted, I tell them the truth. It's not the ghost you should worry about. It's that this whole place could disappear and most people will drive right over the top of it and never know it was here.

### Close + CTA (3:30–4:30)
HOST: That's why we put people in kayaks. No motor, no airboat, low to the water, quiet enough to actually hear it. You come out here, you see the bridge from underneath, you sit in the same water that took that town, and you get it.

We run this trip every week, 30 minutes from the French Quarter. Link's in the description. Come see the swamp before it's a story too.
