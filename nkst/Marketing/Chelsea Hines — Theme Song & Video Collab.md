# Chelsea Hines — Theme Song & Video Collab

Meeting packet. Drafted 2026-07-22 ahead of an in-person meet the week of July 27.

---

## Why her, in one paragraph

Chelsea guided for us in March 2026. Her thing on the water was edible plants. She is also a working New Orleans musician: singer, guitarist, violinist, born in New York but most of her adult life here, trained up around jazz players and street performers. She has an album (*Symptoms Of*, 2022, with producer Concret), a live record (*Live at Guava*, 2019), and a new single out in 2026, so she is active, not dormant. She says the clave rhythm from old New Orleans songs keeps showing up in her writing without her planning it.

The line to build the whole meeting around is hers: **"Every song I write, I am imagining the moments in which it will play, the scene it will set and who would be listening."**

So don't hand her a brand brief. Hand her the scene.

Our logo was drawn by a past guide. If a past guide also writes the theme song, that stops being a coincidence and starts being the actual answer to "why us and not the other swamp tour." The brand gets made by the people who work the water.

## The theme song brief

**Tempo.** 60 to 68 bpm. That's paddle-stroke speed. If the track sits there, every piece of footage we shoot for the next five years cuts to it without anybody trying. This is the single most useful constraint in the whole document.

**Rhythm.** Her clave, the one she says shows up on its own. It's the sound of the city, and our whole positioning is that we're the local operator and the other guys bus people out from Bourbon Street. Let the rhythm do that argument for us.

**Instrumentation.** Her violin sitting where the cicadas and tree frogs would be. Low guitar. As little percussion as she can stand. The swamp is loud but it's all high frequencies, so leaving the low end empty makes the picture feel wide.

**The scene to give her, verbatim:**

> Manchac, a little after seven. Fog on the water, not lifted yet. We come off the main channel into the cypress and the guide cuts the motor. There are about four seconds of complete silence before the frogs start back up, and every single person in the boat stops talking on their own. Nobody tells them to. That's the moment. What does it sound like?

**Deliverables to ask for, all from one session:**
- Full track, 2 to 3 minutes, for YouTube and the site.
- 30-second cut for paid.
- 15-second sting for Reels and TikTok.
- Stems, separated. This is the part people forget and it's the part that matters. With stems we can re-cut for years without going back to her for a new license.

## Script A — The flagship film (60 seconds)

The one that lives on the homepage and the YouTube channel banner. No voiceover for the first 40 seconds. Let the track and the swamp carry it.

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|------|---------|-------|
| 0:00-0:06 | Black. Then a paddle blade breaking the surface, very tight, slow motion. Water beading off the edge. | Silence, then one violin note. |
| 0:06-0:14 | Fog on the water at Manchac. Wide. A single kayak entering frame from the left, small in a big picture. | Track comes in underneath, clave barely there. |
| 0:14-0:22 | Guide's hands, close. Pulling a plant, turning it over, showing it to someone off camera. Cut to a guest laughing at whatever the guide just said. | Track builds. No dialogue, we don't need to hear the words. |
| 0:22-0:32 | Cypress knees. A heron lifting off. Spanish moss with the sun behind it. Cut fast enough to feel alive, slow enough to feel like a place. | Full arrangement. |
| 0:32-0:40 | Guests, actual guests, faces. Somebody pointing. A kid. Two people rafted up talking. | Track holds. |
| 0:40-0:46 | The boat drifting. Everyone quiet. Wide and still. | **Everything drops out. Four seconds of near silence, frogs only.** |
| 0:46-0:54 | Golden hour on the way back in. Paddles in rhythm. | Track returns, just the violin. |
| 0:54-1:00 | Logo. The guide-drawn one. Hold on it. | Last note. |

End card, small: *Theme by Chelsea Hines. She used to guide here.*

That end card is the whole point. It's the credit that makes the rest of it true.

## Script B — The 30-second paid cut

Same footage, different job. This one has to sell.

- **0:00-0:03** Hook, hard. Paddle blade, water, the sting from her track. No slow build, paid doesn't get a slow build.
- **0:03-0:10** Fast cuts: gator eye at the waterline, heron, guest face reacting.
- **0:10-0:18** Guide on camera, one line of real audio: something about how far we are from the highway, or how old the cypress is. Pick from actual tour audio, don't script it.
- **0:18-0:25** Guests laughing, boat in the fog, sun through the moss.
- **0:25-0:30** Logo, price or "Book Today," done.

Two versions: one with captions burned in for sound-off feeds, one clean.

## Script C — "What's Edible Out Here" (vertical series)

The cheapest, most repeatable thing on this list, and the one I'd build first. Chelsea in a kayak, one plant per episode, 45 to 60 seconds, her own music underneath. Shoot six in one morning.

**Episode 1 template:**

- **Hook (0:00-0:03):** She's already holding the plant. "You can eat this. Most people paddle right past it." No intro, no "hey guys."
- **Body (0:03-0:35):** What it is. Where it grows and why it grows there, which is really a lesson about the swamp. What it tastes like. One thing you'd actually do with it.
- **The catch (0:35-0:45):** The lookalike you don't want, or the rule about where not to harvest. This is the part that makes the video trustworthy instead of cute.
- **Close (0:45-0:55):** She takes a bite or she doesn't, and either way she says the honest thing about it. Then back to paddling, no CTA. The series earns the booking, individual episodes don't need to beg.

**Episode list to bring, let her correct it:**
1. Whatever the obvious one is that everybody paddles past
2. Something growing on the cypress
3. Something that looks dangerous and isn't
4. Something that looks fine and isn't
5. A plant with a Louisiana food story attached to it
6. The one she'd never eat and why

I'm guessing on this list. She's the expert. The point of bringing it is to give her something to react to.

## Script D — "Live at the Launch" (session shoot)

She already has a *Live at Guava* record, so this format is in her bones. Not an ad. A session.

**Setup.** Golden hour, on the dock. Her, guitar or violin, one or two players if she wants them. Two cameras: one locked wide with the water and cypress behind her, one handheld getting hands and face. Audio recorded properly, not off the camera, or the whole thing is wasted.

**Structure.** She plays the theme, then one or two songs of her own that have nothing to do with us. That's what makes it a session instead of a commercial, and it's what makes it something she'll actually want to post.

**Distribution.** She posts it, we post it. Full version on our YouTube. Her audience is local, culture-forward, and exactly the crowd our Google Ads never reach.

**Reality check.** This is the piece most likely to get cut for time and budget. If we can only do one shoot day, the film and the shorts win. Bring it up anyway, because it's the thing that makes the partnership feel like a partnership to her instead of a job.

## The money conversation

Have it. Don't let it drift to the end and get skipped.

**What I'd propose:** flat fee for the music, credit on everything it appears in, and a perpetual license covering New Orleans Kayak Swamp Tours and Nola Party Barge. Stems included. Separate day rate if she's on camera for the foraging series, because that's performance, not composition, and lumping them together is how people end up feeling underpaid six months later.

**What I wouldn't do:** trade it for tours. She's a working artist and a free-paddle trade will read as cheap. Paying her is what makes this something we can go back to for the next video, and the one after that.

## Questions for her

- What does that four seconds of silence sound like to you?
- Do you want to write to picture, or should we cut to the track?
- Which of your existing songs is closest to what this should feel like?
- Are you comfortable on camera for the plant series, or would you rather just score it?
- Who else should be in this? Other guides, other musicians, anyone you'd want to pull in?
- What are you working on right now that we could support, separate from all of this?

That last one is the one that matters. The whole reason to do this is the overlap, not the transaction.
