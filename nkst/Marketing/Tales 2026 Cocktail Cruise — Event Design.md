# Off the Clock — A Tales of the Cocktail Cruise on the Bayou

**Host:** Nola Party Barge (space + crew only)
**Lead/sponsor:** an invited spirits brand or cocktail vendor (drinks, bartenders, branding, budget)
**When:** one evening during Tales of the Cocktail week, July 19-24, 2026
**Status:** concept. Feasibility (on-water serving license) sent to Jeff + JT 2026-06-22. Vendor search underway.

## The one-liner

While the whole drinks world is jammed into the French Quarter trading business cards, we take 18 of the right people out onto the water at golden hour for the one thing nobody else is offering that week: a place to actually exhale. A branded cocktail in hand, the city skyline behind them, and a guest list worth being on.

## Why this works

Tales is 5,000 people elbow to elbow in hotel ballrooms and Bourbon Street bars. By Wednesday everyone is overstimulated and over-poured. The thing that stands out during Tales is not another tasting. It is the opposite: small, calm, hard to get into, out on the water. Scarcity does the marketing. 18 seats, one night, invite only.

For a brand, that is the whole pitch. They are not buying a booth. They are buying an hour with 18 people who shape what gets poured in bars across the country, in a setting none of their competitors can copy that week.

## The format (rough flow)

- **Golden hour boarding** at the marina. One signature welcome cocktail in hand before anyone sits down.
- **The slow loop.** Captain takes it out easy. No program, no pitch from a stage. The brand's bartender is building two or three drinks built for the brand, and people drift over to watch and talk.
- **The middle.** This is the point. Conversation, the skyline, a deck instead of a barstool. Light bites so nobody is drinking on empty.
- **Last pour at dusk** heading back in. Send each guest off with something small and branded so it shows up on their feed and in their bag.

Keep it under two hours. Leave them wanting the next one.

## Who we invite (the "special people")

This is the real asset. The boat is just the room. The guest list is what makes a brand pay for it:

- Visiting bartenders and bar owners, especially Spirited Awards nominees and the crews everyone follows
- Brand ambassadors and the spirits-brand people in town for the week
- Drinks writers and editors (the Punch / trade-press crowd) and a couple of NOLA food/lifestyle creators for reach
- A few local NOLA bar owners and operators so it is not all out-of-towners
- The vendor's own must-invite list (they will have one)

Mix of national pull and local credibility. We curate it together with the brand.

## Split of work: who does what

**Nola Party Barge brings:** the boat, the captain and crew, the marina, the timing, the curated guest list and the invites, and the New Orleans setting that cannot be faked.

**The vendor/brand brings:** the spirits and the cocktail program, the bartender(s), **the serving license, permits, and liquor liability**, the branding and signage, the takeaway, and the budget. They get naming rights on the night ("[Brand] presents..."), their drinks in everyone's hands, and the content.

Cleanest version: it is *their* event, we are the venue and the list.

## The licensing model (the vendor carries it, not us)

We do not solve the liquor license for this. The vendor brings their own serving license, permits, and liability. We are purely the venue. That is the entire reason to bring in a licensed bar service, or a brand that travels with its own permitted pour, rather than trying to serve under our own (still-pending) on-water license.
