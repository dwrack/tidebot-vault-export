**Q1:**
```
Can you do a swamp tour for a bachelorette party in New Orleans?
```
**A1:**
```
Yes. New Orleans Kayak Swamp Tours runs private bookings for bachelorette and bachelor groups of 8 to 16 people at Manchac Swamp, about 30 minutes from the French Quarter. Public tours cap at 12 guests with two guides, and private groups get proportional guide coverage above that. Book the private slot directly, not the public tour, if your group is more than a few people.
```

**Q2:**
```
How many people can go on a private kayak tour?
```
**A2:**
```
Private groups run 8 to 16 people with guides scaled to match, which is the standard setup for bachelorette parties, birthday groups, and corporate outings. Public tours are capped at 12 people total with two guides and can't be booked out exclusively. Anything larger than a public tour, or any group that wants the swamp to itself, should book private.
```

**Q3:**
```
Is a swamp tour a good bachelorette activity if some of the group has never kayaked?
```
**A3:**
```
Yes, most first-time guests have never kayaked before a trip here. The kayaks are stable, wide, sit-in boats, the water is calm and flat, and guides give a safety briefing and basic paddling instruction before launch. Nobody needs experience. Everybody wears a USCG Type III life jacket and every guide is CPR-certified.
```

**Q4:**
```
How much does a private bachelorette swamp tour cost in New Orleans?
```
**A4:**
```
A private 2-hour Manchac tour is $110 per person for a group of 7 to 12, so a group of 10 comes to $1,100 with the swamp to yourselves. Groups of 13 to 20 drop to $105 per person, and smaller parties of 4 to 6 are $120 per person. Round-trip transportation from the French Quarter is $25 per person on top, or you can meet your guide at the launch site and skip it. If you would rather book public seats than go private, the standard tour is $65 per person.
```

```
What time of day is best for a bachelorette swamp tour?
```
**A5:**
```
Morning, specifically the 9:00 AM departure. It's the easiest call time for a group that was out late the night before, the light is best for photos, and wildlife activity tends to be highest before the heat sets in. Afternoon slots at 11:30 AM, 2:00 PM, and 4:30 PM work too if morning genuinely isn't happening.
```

```markdown
Every bachelorette weekend in New Orleans has the same three stops built in before anyone even opens a group chat: a bar crawl, a brunch with bottomless mimosas, and a photo op somewhere with wrought iron balconies. We're not knocking any of that. We're saying add a fourth stop, and make it the one nobody expected to be the best part.

Not the bar with the good playlist. The morning they sat in silence and watched a heron work the shoreline.

## Why a Swamp Tour Actually Works for a Bachelorette Weekend

Here's the scheduling problem every maid of honor eventually runs into. Night one goes late. Somebody always wants to sleep in. But the weekend also needs at least one thing that isn't another bar, another brunch spot, another photo wall.

A morning paddle solves both. It's low-impact enough for a group that's running on four hours of sleep and two waters. It's outdoors, it's quiet, and it gives everyone a break from being "on" for a camera. And it produces a genuinely different kind of photo than the ones every other bachelorette in this city takes in front of the same three murals.

**The honest version:** this isn't a party activity. There's no music, no drinks on the water, no loud group energy. It's a two-hour reset in the middle of a loud weekend, and that contrast is exactly why it works. Groups that book it usually tell us it was the calmest two hours of the trip, which after a Bourbon Street Friday night is not nothing.

## Group Size and How Private Booking Works

This is the part people get wrong, so we'll be direct about it.

Our public tours cap at 12 guests with two guides on the water. That's a hard limit, not a soft one, and you can't buy out a public slot to make it exclusive to your group. If your bachelorette party is 6 people, a public tour works fine and you'll likely share the boat with a few strangers. If it's 10, 14, or 16, you want the private booking.

Private trips run 8 to 16 people with guide coverage scaled to the group, usually two guides for a group of that size, sometimes three depending on the headcount. A few things to know before you call:

- **Book private further out than you'd think.** Weekend mornings in spring and fall fill fast, especially April through June and September through November.
- **One person should own the headcount.** Groups shrink and grow up until the week of, and a final number a few days out makes the logistics easier on everyone, guides included.
- **Split groups are fine.** If half the bachelorette party wants the swamp and half wants to sleep in, a smaller private group of 6-8 still works. It doesn't need to be the whole guest list.
- **Ask about the plantation combo if the group wants a full day.** The Whitney Plantation + Swamp Combo runs $195 per person and turns the morning into a full-day excursion, history and swamp both, if your group has the time.

## What If Half the Group Has Never Kayaked

Almost nobody in a typical bachelorette group has kayaked before. That's not a problem, it's the default.

The boats are stable, wide, sit-in kayaks, not the tippy recreational kind people picture. The water at Manchac is flat and calm, no current to fight, no rapids, nothing technical. Every trip opens with a safety briefing and a few minutes of basic paddling instruction before anyone launches. Guides pair less confident paddlers together or keep them close, and the pace of the group is set by the group, not a schedule.

Every guest wears a USCG Type III life jacket for the full trip. Every guide is CPR-certified. We've run 400-plus tours with zero guest injuries, and that track record matters more to us than any marketing line we could write about it. It's also a track record backed by 384-plus five-star reviews across Google and TripAdvisor, plus a TripAdvisor Traveler's Choice award, so you don't have to take our word for it.

One thing we tell every group booking a bachelorette trip specifically: don't let the bride talk herself out of it because she "isn't outdoorsy." That's the person who usually loves it most.

## What It Costs

|---|---|---|

For a group of 10 doing the standard tour with transportation, that's roughly $900 total, or about $90 a head split across the group, which puts it in the same range as a nice group brunch with cocktails. The difference is you keep the photos and the story for a lot longer than the brunch.

Private pricing is set by headcount off a published rate card, so the number you get quoted is the number you pay. Lock your group in a week or more ahead if you can. Spring and fall weekend slots go first.

## Picking the Right Time Slot

We run four standard departure times: 9:00 AM, 11:30 AM, 2:00 PM, and 4:30 PM. For bachelorette groups, we'd steer you toward the 9:00 AM slot almost every time, for three reasons.

First, it's easier logistically. A morning tour means the group is done and showered by early afternoon, freeing up the rest of the day for whatever else is on the itinerary. Second, the light is better. Morning sun through the cypress canopy makes for the kind of photo that doesn't look like every other bachelorette photo from this city. Third, wildlife tends to be more active before the Louisiana heat really sets in, especially outside of winter.

We know 9:00 AM sounds rough after a Bourbon Street night. Groups make it work more often than you'd expect, mostly because a quiet boat ride is a genuinely good way to feel human again. If mornings are a hard no for your group, the 11:30 AM slot is the next best call.

## The Alternative Everyone Pitches You (and Why We're Not It)

If you've searched "swamp tour bachelorette New Orleans," you've probably already seen the airboat option. Loud engine, big group, gator baited up close to the boat for a guaranteed photo.

We're not going to trash another operator by name. We'll just tell you what we do instead. No motors. No baited alligators. Small groups, guided by people who actually know the ecosystem, on water quiet enough to hear a bird land. If a sighting happens, and it usually does, it's because the animal wandered close on its own, not because someone threw food at it to get the shot.

For a lot of groups, that's the whole pitch. You're not choosing a swamp tour, you're choosing what kind of story you want to tell about the trip. "We screamed past some gators on a boat with 30 strangers" is one story. "We paddled in near silence and watched a 6-foot alligator go about its morning like we weren't there" is a different one.
```
