```
Where does the New Orleans swamp tour shuttle pick up from?
```
**A2:**

```
Our shuttle pickup point is 740 N Rampart Street in New Orleans. The van departs 1 hour and 15 minutes before your scheduled tour time, so guests should arrive at the pickup point at least 10 to 15 minutes before that departure.
```

```
Can I drive myself to the swamp tour instead of taking the shuttle?
```

```
Yes. Our launch site is in LaPlace, Louisiana, inside the Maurepas Swamp Wildlife Management Area, about 35 minutes from the French Quarter via I-55. Self-driving saves the transportation add-on, and there's free parking at the launch. Just don't plan on a rideshare pickup afterward, more on that below.

```

```
We don't recommend it, at least not for the return trip. The launch site is a rural boat launch well outside New Orleans proper, and rideshare availability out there is inconsistent, especially for a return pickup timed to when your tour actually ends rather than when an app estimates. Drive yourself with your own car, or book our shuttle, which is timed to your tour.
```

```markdown
We field the same question every single day at New Orleans Kayak Swamp Tours, sometimes an hour before the tour starts: "Wait, how do I actually get there?" It's not a dumb question. We built this trip around a launch site 35 minutes outside the city, on purpose, because that's where the real swamp is. It just means getting there takes a little more planning than walking to a French Quarter storefront.

## The Two Ways Guests Get to This Tour

There are exactly two options, and both work fine. What doesn't work is guessing.

- **Take our shuttle.** We pick you up in the city and drive you out. Costs extra, zero planning required on your end.
- **Drive yourself.** You meet us at the launch site directly. Saves money, requires a car and a little advance planning.

Everything below is how to actually execute whichever one you pick.

## Option One: The Shuttle

Our pickup point is **740 N Rampart Street** in New Orleans. The van leaves **1 hour and 15 minutes before your scheduled tour time**, which sounds like a lot until you remember the drive itself is most of that window. We build in a buffer on purpose. A tour that starts without half its guests because traffic ran long on I-55 is a bad tour for everybody, so we leave room for that not to happen.

Show up 10 to 15 minutes before the departure time, not the tour time. That's the number that actually matters here. Guests who show up at the tour start time and expect the van to still be there have had a rough morning more than once, and we'd rather you not be that guest.

The add-on runs roughly **$25 per person round trip**. **[internal link: how much a swamp tour actually costs → 2026-08-14 - how-much-does-a-swamp-tour-cost-new-orleans post]** breaks down the full pricing picture if you're comparing this against driving yourself.

## Option Two: Driving Yourself

Our launch sits off Old Highway 51 in LaPlace, inside the **[internal link: Maurepas Swamp Wildlife Management Area, where the launch actually sits → maurepas-swamp-wildlife-management-area-guide post]**, about 35 minutes from the French Quarter via I-55 in normal traffic. There's free parking at the launch. No permit, no meter, no app.

This is the cheaper option every time, since you skip the transportation add-on entirely. It's also the more flexible option if your group is staying somewhere outside downtown, a rental near the airport or out toward Metairie, where the shuttle route doesn't help you anyway.

One thing worth saying plainly: cell service gets patchy the further you get from the interstate. Download directions before you leave your hotel rather than relying on live traffic rerouting once you're off the highway. We've had guests lose signal 10 minutes out and end up circling the same two turns. Set your navigation, then trust it.

## Why We Steer Guests Away From Rideshare

We get asked constantly whether an Uber or Lyft can just drop guests at the launch and pick them up after. Technically, sure. Practically, we don't recommend it, and we'll tell you exactly why instead of just saying "don't."

Drivers thin out fast once you're past the LaPlace exits. Getting a car TO the launch is usually fine, most rideshare drivers will take a fare out there. Getting a car back FROM the launch, at the specific moment your tour actually ends rather than when an app estimated it two hours earlier, is the part that goes wrong. Tours run long sometimes. Weather pushes a start time back. An app doesn't know that. A guest sitting alone at a rural boat launch waiting on a driver who canceled is not how we want anyone's afternoon to go.

If you've got a car, drive it. If you don't, take our shuttle. It's the one option built specifically around your actual tour time instead of an algorithm's guess.

## TripAdvisor Bookings Work Differently

If you booked through TripAdvisor instead of directly with us, transportation is already included in the price you paid. You don't need to add anything or figure out pickup separately, the shuttle logistics above still apply, you just won't see a separate charge for it. If you're not sure which way you booked, check your confirmation email or give us a call before tour day so there's no confusion at pickup.

## What to Actually Do When You Arrive

Whichever way you get there, here's the sequence once you're on site:

1. **Check in with a guide.** Someone will be waiting with a clipboard or tablet near the parking area or shuttle drop.
2. **Get fitted for a life jacket.** Mandatory, USCG-approved, sized to you, not optional even for strong swimmers.
3. **Hear the safety briefing.** Short, real, covers what to actually do if a kayak tips (rare, but we cover it every time).
4. **Load into your kayak.** Guides help with this, no experience assumed.
5. **Leave valuables in your car if you drove yourself.** We don't have secure storage at the launch, and a locked trunk beats a dry bag for anything you can't get wet or don't want to carry.
6. **Use the restroom before you launch.** There isn't one out on the water, and the trip runs 2 to 4 hours depending on which tour you booked.

## Which Option Is Right for You

Book the shuttle if you don't have a car in New Orleans, or if the idea of driving 35 minutes into rural Louisiana on a vacation morning sounds like a chore instead of part of the trip. Drive yourself if you've got a rental car anyway, you're staying outside the French Quarter, or you'd rather save the $25 and have full control of your own schedule on the way back.

Either way, **[internal link: your first kayak swamp tour, what to expect → /blog/first-time-kayak-swamp-tour-new-orleans/]** is worth a read before you go if this is your first time in a kayak. Ready to lock in a time? Book the **[internal link: Manchac Mystic Kayak Tour → /tours/swamp-kayak-tours/manchac-mystic-kayak-tour/]** and we'll handle the rest from there.
```
