## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
Thirty-eight times, someone has emailed us the same question: do you sell gift certificates? We do. We've just never actually written anything explaining how they work, which is a strange thing to admit in a blog post, but here we are.

If you're shopping for someone who's already been to New Orleans twice, already has a French Quarter print on their wall, and genuinely does not need one more souvenir, this is the gift we'd point you toward.

## The question we get 38 times and counting

We pulled our own customer email data recently and gift certificates came up 38 separate times, logged as a high-priority recurring question. That's not a huge number in absolute terms, but it's a real, counted signal that people are actively looking for this option and, until now, finding almost nothing written about it.

Here's the honest version of what we'd tell you if you called and asked.

## What you're actually buying

A gift certificate good toward any NKST tour. Manchac, Honey Island, the self-guided Bayou Bienvenue rental, whatever the recipient wants to book. It's not locked to one specific trip or date.

Our own gift certificate page puts the pitch plainly: **[internal link: buy a gift certificate → /gift-certificates/]** for "the opportunity to experience the incredible lore of the other half of New Orleans" on a kayak swamp tour. That's not us being precious about it. Most New Orleans gifts are consumable (food, drinks) or forgettable (another t-shirt). A swamp tour is neither. People remember the tour they took a lot longer than they remember what they ate that trip.

## How it works, start to finish

Nothing complicated here, which is kind of the point.

1. You buy the gift certificate through the website.
2. It's emailed immediately. Not "within 24 hours," not "processed and mailed." Immediately.
3. It doesn't expire. No countdown, no "use it by Christmas or lose it" pressure.
4. The recipient books whichever tour and date actually works for their trip.

That last part matters more than it sounds like it should. A lot of experience gifts fail because the recipient has to coordinate a specific date months in advance or lose the gift entirely. This one doesn't work that way. Book a swamp tour for a nephew visiting from Ohio in October, gift it in July, no problem.

## Who this gift is actually right for

Not every gift recipient wants this, and we'd rather tell you that than oversell it.

- **The person who's "been everywhere."** Experience gifts have grown fast for a reason. Real data backs this up: over half of consumers now say they plan to buy an experience gift over a physical one, and that share keeps climbing year over year. People are tired of stuff. They're not tired of stories.
- **The repeat New Orleans visitor.** Someone who's already done Bourbon Street, beignets, and a plantation tour twice has genuinely, probably, not done this. It's a real gap in the typical itinerary, not a repeat of it.
- **The out-of-town friend or family member.** You don't have to guess at sizes, colors, or taste. You're not shipping anything. It's an email.
- **Not** a great fit for someone with real mobility limitations that make kayaking difficult, or someone who's told you outright they don't like the outdoors. We'd rather say that here than have a guest who wasn't excited to be on the water.

## What to write on the card

Skip the generic "enjoy!" Name the actual thing they're getting. It reads better and it sets real expectations. Something like: "A real Louisiana swamp, a guide who actually knows it, no motor, no crowd. Yours whenever you want it." That's a more honest, more specific gift message than most people write, and it tells the recipient exactly what to look forward to instead of leaving them to guess.

## Buy one

If you already know who this is for, **[internal link: buy a gift certificate → /gift-certificates/]** takes about two minutes and lands in their inbox right after you check out. If you want to know more about what the actual tour is like before you commit to gifting it, **[internal link: our full guide to picking a swamp tour → /blog/swamp-tours-new-orleans-guide/]** is the honest rundown of NKST's options versus everyone else's.
```
