**Q1:**
```
Is it safe to book a New Orleans trip during hurricane season?
```
**A1:**
```
Yes, for the large majority of the season. Hurricane season runs June through November, but most days in that window see nothing more than a normal Louisiana thunderstorm. Named storms that actually threaten the New Orleans area are the exception, not the rule, in any given week. Booking ahead is normal, most guests who book August through October never experience any storm impact on their trip at all.
```

**Q2:**
```
What happens to my swamp tour if a hurricane is approaching?
```
**A2:**
```
If a parish emergency management agency issues any level of hurricane evacuation order, that is an automatic, no-exceptions cancellation for any tour scheduled during that window, no judgment call involved. Guests are notified directly and offered a full refund. Short of an actual evacuation order, we still watch tropical systems closely and communicate proactively rather than waiting for a guest to call and ask.
```

**Q3:**
```
What is the cancellation and refund policy if a storm affects my tour?
```
**A3:**
```
If we cancel your tour due to weather, including a hurricane or tropical system, you get a full refund, no questions asked. Separately, if you personally need to cancel, our standard policy is a full refund if you cancel at least 48 hours before your tour, and no refund inside that 48-hour window, though we ask guests dealing with a storm situation to just call or text us and we will work with the real circumstances.
```

**Q4:**
```
When is the highest-risk part of hurricane season for a New Orleans trip?
```
**A4:**
```
Statistically, late August through the end of September is the peak of Atlantic hurricane season, with activity tapering off through October and November. June and early July carry season-lowest average activity. None of this means storms only happen in that window, but if you have flexibility on trip dates, the shoulder months carry the lowest average risk.
```

## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
We get this question almost every August, usually from someone who already has flights booked: "Should I even worry about hurricane season?"

Short answer: mostly no. Long answer, because "mostly" is doing real work in that sentence and you deserve the actual reasoning, not just reassurance.

## The question we get every August

Hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30 on the books, but that's a six-month label covering wildly different actual risk. A guest booking a Manchac tour for June 10 and a guest booking for September 10 are not facing the same odds, even though both fall inside "the season."

Last year we ran tours nearly every single day of hurricane season. One storm came close enough to force a real cancellation window. That's the honest ratio: most of the season is just Louisiana summer, hot, occasionally stormy in the ordinary afternoon-thunderstorm way, and nothing close to a named system.

## What "hurricane season" actually means for a normal week

Here's the thing people get wrong: they picture six months of anxious storm-watching. In practice, the overwhelming majority of days in hurricane season look exactly like the rest of Louisiana summer. Afternoon thunderstorms roll through, clear up in twenty minutes, and a tour either waits them out or reschedules to morning. That's weather, not a hurricane.

A named storm that actually threatens New Orleans is a distinct, tracked, days-in-advance event. Nobody wakes up to a surprise hurricane. If one is coming, you'll know from the news well before we'd need to make a call about your tour. Day-of forecasts are a different question entirely, [internal link: our full rain and weather policy → swamp-tour-rain-weather-cancellation-policy] covers what actually cancels a tour on an ordinary stormy afternoon.

## The one threshold that cancels a tour automatically

Our guides work from a written weather decision protocol, and it has exactly one hurricane-specific rule: **if a parish emergency management agency issues a hurricane evacuation order at any level, that's an automatic cancel.** Not a judgment call, not a wait-and-see. Any tour scheduled during that window doesn't run.

Everything short of an actual evacuation order gets watched, tracked, and communicated, but doesn't automatically pull the plug. A tropical storm watch three parishes over doesn't cancel your Tuesday tour. An actual evacuation order for the parish you're touring in does, every time, no exceptions.

## Our real refund policy, no hedging

If we cancel your tour for weather, hurricane included, **you get a full refund, no questions asked.** That's not a "credit toward a future tour" policy dressed up as a refund. It's your money back.

If you're the one who needs to cancel because a storm is complicating your travel plans, our standard policy is a full refund at 48 hours or more out, no refund inside that window. We'll be straight with you: if a real storm situation is unfolding and you're inside 48 hours, call or text us at 504-571-9975 and we'll talk through the actual circumstances rather than reading you the fine print. A guest evacuating ahead of a real storm isn't the same situation as a guest who just changed their mind, and we treat it that way.

## If you have flexible dates, here's what the calendar says

If your trip dates aren't locked yet, the numbers are worth knowing. Late August through the end of September is statistically the peak of Atlantic hurricane activity. Early June and July run well below that peak. October tapers down, November tapers further.

None of that guarantees anything, a quiet system can spin up in July and a loud season can go quiet in September. But if you're choosing between two open weeks, the calendar has a mild preference, and it's not August 28. If heat and crowds matter more to your planning than storm odds, [internal link: what fall in New Orleans actually looks like → things-to-do-new-orleans-fall-outdoors] is the better starting point.

## What we tell our own guides about storm weeks

When a system starts getting real attention in the forecast, our guides start checking radar and NOAA updates more than once a day, not because we expect the worst, but because a hurricane call is one of the few decisions on our list where "wait and see" is the wrong instinct. We'd rather cancel a tour that turns out fine than run one that turns out badly.

That same instinct is why we'd rather tell you the real odds now than let you spend three weeks quietly worrying about a trip you already paid for. Most years, most guests, hurricane season is just a warm week on the water with better light and smaller crowds than October brings.
```
