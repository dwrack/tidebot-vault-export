## FAQ SCHEMA

**Q1:**
```
Can you propose during a kayak tour in the New Orleans swamp?
```
**A1:**
```
Yes. Email us ahead of time (before booking or right after) and tell your guide what's happening so they can position the group and keep things calm at the right moment. Bring the ring in a small dry bag or waterproof pocket, not loose in a jacket. We've had it happen more than once and it works because the water is quiet enough to actually hear the answer.
```

**Q2:**
```
Is there a private, just-the-two-of-us swamp tour option?
```
**A2:**
```
Not really, and we'd rather tell you that than oversell it. Private tours start at a 10-person minimum with guide coverage scaled to match, so booking one for two people means paying for the whole slot regardless of headcount. Most couples book the standard public tour instead, which caps at 12 guests total. On the water, groups spread out enough that it doesn't feel crowded.
```

**Q3:**
```
What's the best time of year for an anniversary or honeymoon swamp tour in New Orleans?
```
**A3:**
```
Spring (March through May) and fall (late September through November) give you the mildest heat and the most alligator activity, since they're cold-blooded and move more in comfortable temperatures. Summer works too if you don't mind humidity and want denser, greener cypress canopy. The 4:30 PM slot at Manchac catches the best light nearly year-round.
```

```
How much does a swamp tour cost for a couple in New Orleans?
```

```
The standard 2-hour Manchac Swamp Wildlife Tour is $65 per person, so $130 for two, plus $25 per person for round-trip transport if you need it. The 4-hour Extended Manchac Tour runs $130 per person. Couples who want a full day, history and swamp both, can book the Whitney Plantation + Swamp Combo at $195 per person with transport included.
```

## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
A guy emailed us last spring asking if he could propose on the 9:00 AM tour. We said sure, worked it out with his guide, and twenty minutes in, right as a heron lifted off the cypress line about ten feet from the bow, he did it. She said yes. The rest of the group, six strangers in kayaks, just sat there quiet and let it happen. Try getting that moment on an airboat with a PA system running.

We didn't set out to become a honeymoon and anniversary destination. It happened because couples kept telling us the same thing after their tours: this was the first two hours of the whole trip where nobody was performing for a camera or waiting in line for anything.

## Why Couples Keep Ending Up Here Without Planning To

New Orleans does big, loud, and social better than almost anywhere. Bourbon Street, brass bands, a table for two that turns into a table for twelve because the couple next to you started talking. Great stuff, and also exhausting by day three of a honeymoon.

A swamp tour is the opposite register. No motor, no speakers, no wake. Two hours where the loudest thing is a paddle stroke or a bird call. **That contrast is the whole point.** Couples on their first big trip together, or their twentieth anniversary, or a honeymoon wedged between a wedding weekend and going back to work, tend to remember the quiet morning more clearly than the loud night. We hear it constantly.

It also gives you something to actually talk about besides the food. Cypress trees older than the city itself. Land loss numbers that are hard to believe until you're sitting in the middle of what used to be solid ground. A guide, often Dylan or Michelle depending on the day, who can tell you why the water here runs the color of tea and what that has to do with the trees themselves.

## The Truth About Booking "Just the Two of Us"

Here's where we could oversell you and we're not going to.

Private, full-buyout tours exist, but they start at a 10-person minimum with guide coverage scaled to match. Book one for two people and you're paying for the whole slot regardless of headcount. Some couples do it anyway for a big-splurge anniversary or a proposal they want completely private, and if budget isn't the concern, ask us directly and we'll quote it.

Most couples book the standard public tour instead, capped at 12 guests total with two guides on the water. A few things worth knowing before you do:

- **It doesn't feel like a crowd.** Kayaks spread out on open water. You're not paddling in formation. Most tours, couples end up drifting off on their own for long stretches without anyone hovering.
- **You'll likely share the group with a mix of people:** other couples, a family, sometimes a solo traveler. Nobody's there to interrupt your trip.
- **Weekday mornings run smaller.** If a quieter group matters to you, a Tuesday 9:00 AM slot in shoulder season is about as close to private as the public tour gets.
- **Ask about the extended tour if two hours feels rushed.** The 4-hour Extended Manchac Tour gives you double the time on the water for $130 per person.

## If You're Planning a Proposal, Here's What Actually Works

We've done enough of these now to have real advice instead of guesses.

Email us before or right after booking and just say what's happening. Your guide will know to angle the group, ease off the ecology narration for a minute, and let the moment breathe. We're not going to promise a scripted alligator sighting to cue the ring, because we don't guarantee wildlife, ever. "Common, not guaranteed" is how we talk about it, and that's true here too. What we can promise is quiet water and a guide who won't talk over you.

Practical stuff nobody thinks about until it's a problem: put the ring in a small dry bag or a zipped pocket, not loose in a jacket you'll be leaning forward in all morning. Tell your guide if you want photos, most will happily grab a few on your phone. And build in a buffer. Weather moves plans around out here more than people expect, so if the date matters, don't make it the only day you're in town.

One guest told us afterward that the best part wasn't the ring, it was that nobody clapped or made it weird. Just paddles going still for a second and then starting back up.

## The Honeymoon Kayak

Small thing, but it's become a real tradition. If a couple mentions on booking or during pickup that they're on their honeymoon, we tie a little garland of fake flowers around their kayak before they get in. Takes a guide about two minutes. We've had people tear up over it, which was not the plan when someone first suggested it, but here we are.

It's not on the website anywhere. It's not a paid add-on. Mention it when you book, or just wear the "we got married last weekend" look, and odds are good your guide already knows.

## Manchac or Bayou Bienvenue: Picking Your Swamp

Most couples default to Manchac Swamp, in the Maurepas Swamp Wildlife Management Area about 30-40 minutes from the French Quarter. It's the second-largest bald cypress swamp in the country, wide open, dense wildlife, and the setting for most of what's in this post.

But we also run trips at Bayou Bienvenue in the Lower 9th Ward, a swamp that was destroyed by hurricane-protection levees and is now the site of active restoration work. It's a different kind of beautiful, quieter in a heavier way. Couples looking for a trip with some weight to it, not just pretty photos, sometimes pick this one on purpose. [internal link: read the full Two Swamps story → /blog/two-swamps-manchac-bayou-bienvenue-new-orleans/] goes into why we run both and what they mean next to each other.

Short version: pick Manchac for the classic postcard version of a swamp honeymoon. Pick Bayou Bienvenue if you want your anniversary trip to mean something beyond a nice photo.

That's less than most couples spend on a single dinner reservation during a New Orleans weekend, and it's the thing they bring up a year later. Book further out than feels necessary if your date is fixed, especially a honeymoon week or an anniversary that lands on a weekend. Spring and fall Saturdays fill first.

## Why We're Not the Candlelit Dinner Cruise

Search "romantic New Orleans activity" and you'll find dinner cruises with string lights, a jazz trio, and a set menu. Nothing wrong with that, some couples want exactly that. We're a different kind of romantic.

No engine, no menu, no scripted moment. Just two people, a guide who knows the ecosystem cold, and however many alligators, herons, and owls decide to show up that morning. It's a smaller, quieter thing than a dinner cruise, and it holds up better than most trip itineraries do a year out. [internal link: an honest comparison of airboat and kayak swamp tours → /blog/airboat-vs-kayak-swamp-tour-new-orleans/] covers the louder alternative if you're still deciding.

If you're planning a honeymoon, an anniversary, or a proposal and want the swamp to be part of it, tell us what's happening when you book. We've built a small tradition around it. Book the [internal link: Manchac Mystic Kayak Tour → /tours/swamp-kayak-tours/manchac-mystic-kayak-tour/], pick a morning or the 4:30 golden-hour slot, and bring a dry bag for anything you can't afford to lose in the water.
```
