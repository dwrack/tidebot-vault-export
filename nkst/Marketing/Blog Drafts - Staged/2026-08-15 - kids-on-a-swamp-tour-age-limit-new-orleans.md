```markdown
A dad called New Orleans Kayak Swamp Tours last spring asking if his 6-year-old could come on the Manchac tour. Wrong answer to give him would've been "sure, why not." Right answer took two more minutes and ended with him booking the Bayou Bienvenue rental instead, and a promise to bring the whole family back for Manchac in two years. That's basically this whole post.

## The short answer: our age policy

Minimum age is **8 years old, no exceptions.** Under 16, kids ride tandem with an adult guardian in the same kayak. Under 18, an adult needs to be on the tour with them. That's it, that's the policy, and it's the same one whether you call, text, or ask a guide on the dock the morning of.

We know that's not the answer every parent wants. A confident 7-year-old who's been in kayaks since they could walk still doesn't clear the line. We've had that conversation more than once.

## Why 8 is the number (and why guides can't bend it)

Here's the honest version. Eight is roughly the age where a kid can sit upright in a moving boat for two hours, wear a life jacket correctly, and actually follow a guide's instruction in the moment it matters, not five seconds later. Younger than that and even a calm, confident kid can shift their weight wrong at exactly the wrong second.

Guides don't get to make exceptions in the field. That's not us being rigid for the sake of it. A guide who waives the rule for one kid because they seem mature sets a precedent the next guide has to live with, and it's the kind of call that has real consequences if something goes sideways. If you ask on the water and your kid is under 8, the right answer you'll get is "let me check with management," not a shrug and a yes. We'd rather lose a booking than improvise a safety call.

Every guest, kid or adult, wears a **USCG Type III life jacket** for the full trip. Every guide is CPR-certified. We've run 400-plus tours with zero guest injuries, and that number matters more to us than any line we could write to sell you on it.

## What tandem paddling with a kid is actually like

If your kid is 8 to 15, they're in a tandem kayak with you or another adult in the group. You're steering, they're along for the ride, and they can paddle as much or as little as they want without it changing the boat's direction much. Most kids paddle in bursts, then stop to point at a heron, then paddle again.

The water on the standard Manchac route is flat, slow-moving, and shallow enough in most spots that "capsizing" mostly means getting wet, not going under. We're not selling this as extreme sports, because it isn't one. It's a two-hour paddle through a cypress swamp with a guide who's done this hundreds of times.

A few things guides notice, over and over, about kids on this tour:
- They spot wildlife first. Sharper eyes, lower to the water, paying closer attention than the adults checking their phones at the dock.
- They ask better questions than the grownups. "Why does the water look like tea" comes up more than you'd think, and it's a great one (tannins from the cypress bark, for the record).
- They're usually the last ones who want to get off the water, not the first.
- A nervous kid calms down fast once the motor-free silence sets in. No engine, no wake, nothing loud, that's usually the thing that gets a wound-up kid to settle.

## Under 8? The no-age-limit backup plan

If your youngest is under 8, don't write off the whole trip. The **Bayou Bienvenue self-guided rental** at the Paris Rd marina has no age floor at all. It's a different product, no guide, no narrated wildlife stops, you're renting a kayak and paddling a calmer stretch of water at your own pace. But it gets a 4-year-old on the water in a real Louisiana wetland, which is more than most families visiting New Orleans manage to do.

Some families split the trip: the older kids and adults book Manchac, the littlest one and a parent do the self-guided paddle at Bayou Bienvenue the same weekend. Not required, just an option that's come up enough times we figured it was worth mentioning here.

## What to bring for a kid's first time on the water

Nothing complicated. Closed-toe water shoes beat flip-flops, sun hat, a change of clothes in the car, and sunscreen applied before you leave the house, not on the dock. Bug spray in warmer months. If your kid gets carsick or overheated easily, bring a snack and water bottle they can grab mid-tour, we don't stop for a snack break but a quick sip between paddle strokes is fine.

Skip the life jacket from home. We provide USCG-approved ones sized for kids, and guides fit them before launch, that's not a step we let anyone skip.

## The questions parents ask us the most

**"Will they see an alligator?"** Common, not guaranteed. We don't bait gators to force a sighting, which means some tours see six and some see none. Most groups see at least one. [internal link: alligator sightings are common, not guaranteed → /alligators-vs-crocodiles/]

**"What if my kid panics?"** It happens less than parents expect, mostly because there's no engine noise to spike anxiety and the guide is talking the whole time. If a kid genuinely can't settle, guides can turn a kayak back toward the launch point without ending the tour for everyone else.

**"Is this too long for a kid's attention span?"** The standard tour runs 2 hours. Most kids who make the age cutoff handle it fine, especially with wildlife and a guide narrating instead of silence. If you're not sure, the standard tour is a safer bet than the 4-hour Extended Manchac Tour for a first trip.

If your family clears the age line, book the [internal link: Manchac Mystic Kayak Tour → /tours/swamp-kayak-tours/manchac-mystic-kayak-tour/]. If your youngest doesn't clear it yet, the self-guided Bayou Bienvenue rental is there in the meantime, and Manchac will still be here when they turn 8.
```
