## FAQ SCHEMA

**Q1:**
```
What should I wear on a swamp kayak tour in summer?
```
**A1:**
```
Wear light, quick-drying clothes you do not mind getting splashed. Most guests do a swimsuit or moisture-wicking shirt with quick-dry shorts. A light long-sleeve sun shirt beats sunscreen alone in the Louisiana heat. Add a hat with a strap, polarized sunglasses, and water shoes or strap sandals. Skip flip-flops and anything cotton that stays wet and heavy.
```

**Q2:**
```
What should I bring on a Louisiana swamp tour?
```
**A2:**
```
Bring water, reef-safe sunscreen, bug spray, a hat, sunglasses, and a phone in a waterproof case or pouch if you want photos. We provide the kayak, paddle, and a USCG-approved life jacket. Leave valuables, extra bags, and anything you cannot afford to drop in the car. Keep it light. There is not much storage in a kayak.
```

**Q3:**
```
Are there a lot of bugs on a New Orleans swamp tour?
```
**A3:**
```
Out on open water with a breeze, bugs are usually mild because mosquitoes prefer still, shaded, stagnant spots. The launch and any shaded stretch can be buggier, especially at dusk in summer. Bug spray before you arrive handles it for most guests. Light long sleeves help too. It is rarely the problem people fear, but come prepared.
```

**Q4:**
```
Can I bring my phone on a swamp kayak tour?
```
**A4:**
```
Yes, and most guests do for photos. Put it in a waterproof case or a floating pouch with a lanyard. Kayaks are stable and tip-overs are rare on our calm Manchac routes, but a phone in a back pocket near the water is a gamble. A cheap waterproof pouch is the best few dollars you will spend before a swamp tour.
```

## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
The number one thing people get wrong about a summer swamp tour is not the kayaking. It is the shoes. Every June we watch someone show up in flip-flops, lose one in the mud at the launch, and spend the next two hours half-barefoot in a boat. Do not be that guest.

Here is the packing list I would text a friend flying into New Orleans in July. No fluff, just what works after a few hundred trips out of Manchac.

## The mistake people make in June

Louisiana summer is its own animal. Mid-90s, humidity you can wear like a coat, sun that finds you even under the cypress. People pack for "a kayak trip" and forget they are doing it in a sauna with alligators.

The good news: the water keeps you cooler than you expect, and the cypress throws real shade on a lot of our Manchac route. The bad news: the open stretches are brutal if you dressed for a museum. Plan for heat and sun first. Everything else is secondary.

## What to wear, head to toe

Think light, quick-drying, and splash-proof. You will get a little wet. That is part of it.

- **Top:** a light long-sleeve sun shirt or rash guard. Sounds backwards in the heat, but covering up beats reapplying sunscreen every 40 minutes, and it is cooler than a sunburn. A moisture-wicking tee works too.
- **Bottom:** quick-dry shorts or a swimsuit bottom. Anything that dries fast.
- **Shoes:** water shoes, or sandals with a heel strap. Closed-toe is great. The launch can be muddy and there is the occasional cypress knee underwater.
- **Head:** a hat with a chin strap or a clip. A loose hat in a summer breeze becomes a swamp donation.
- **Eyes:** polarized sunglasses. They cut the glare off black water and let you actually see fish and gators under the surface.

What to skip: flip-flops (they die in mud), cotton everything (stays wet and heavy), and any outfit you would be sad to splash. Leave the white linen for the courtyard bar after.

## What to bring (keep it short)

There is not much storage in a kayak, so resist the urge to pack a day bag. The short list:

1. Water. More than you think. A bottle you can clip or wedge.
2. Reef-safe sunscreen, applied before you arrive and stashed for a midpoint reapply.
3. Bug spray, also applied before launch.
4. Phone in a waterproof case or floating pouch with a lanyard. Best few dollars you will spend.
5. A small dry bag if you have one, for keys and a card.

That is it. We provide the kayak, the paddle, and a USCG-approved life jacket, fitted at the launch. You do not need to bring gear.

## What to leave in the car

Lock the trunk and walk away from: extra bags, real cameras you are not ready to risk, jewelry, big wallets, anything fragile, and your good sandals. Bring what you can afford to drop in black water, because occasionally somebody does. The kayaks are stable and flips are rare on our calm Manchac routes, but a phone tucked in a back pocket near the waterline is asking for it.

## The bug question, answered honestly

People email about this more than alligators. So here is the straight version.

Out on open water with any breeze, bugs are usually mild. Mosquitoes like still, shaded, stagnant spots, and a moving kayak in moving air is not their favorite place. The launch can be buggier. Shaded stretches can be buggier. Dusk in midsummer is the buggiest. Spray down before you arrive and you will barely think about it. Light long sleeves close the gap.

It is rarely the horror show people imagine. But I would rather you come prepared than spend the morning swatting instead of watching a heron.

## Summer-specific: beat the heat

Two real moves for a June through August trip:

**Book the early or late slot.** Sunrise and the late-afternoon tours are cooler, the light is better for photos, and the wildlife tends to be more active in the shoulders of the day. The middle of the day is fine for the swamp itself, harder on you and harder for pictures. More on timing in [internal link: best time of day for a swamp tour → /what-is-the-best-time-of-day-to-go-on-a-swamp-tour-in-new-orleans/].

**Hydrate before, not just during.** Show up already watered. The heat sneaks up when you are distracted by a cypress cathedral.

> A guest last August told me she almost canceled over the forecast, then said the shaded paddle was the coolest she had felt all trip. The swamp has its own air conditioning if you time it right.

## The five-second packing rule

If you cannot get wet, get lost, or get muddy, leave it in the car. That is the whole philosophy.

Dress light, cover up from the sun, strap down your hat and your phone, and you are set. If you want the rest of the first-timer rundown, [internal link: what to expect on your first kayak tour → /blog/first-time-kayak-swamp-tour-new-orleans/] covers the parts that are not about packing. When you are ready, grab a cooler-hour slot on the [internal link: Manchac Mystic Kayak Tour → /tours/swamp-kayak-tours/manchac-mystic-kayak-tour/]. Two to two and a half hours, gear included, 12 people max. Bring the water. We will bring the swamp.
```
