**Q1:**
```
Do I need a fishing license for a kayak fishing charter in Louisiana?
```
**A1:**
```
Most likely yes. Louisiana requires a recreational fishing license for anglers 16 and older, and freshwater and saltwater licenses are regulated separately depending on which water you're fishing. Rules and exemptions change, so confirm with us when you book and check current requirements with the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries before your trip.
```

```
What fish can you catch kayak fishing near New Orleans?
```
**A2:**

```
It depends on the water. Freshwater bayous like Bayou Bienvenue hold largemouth bass, bream, and catfish. Where the water turns brackish toward the marsh, redfish and speckled trout come into play. Your guide picks the spot and the technique based on season, tide, and what's actually been biting that week, not a fixed script.
```

## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
People spend a fortune on a bass boat to fish water that a $30 rod and a pedal kayak can reach just fine. That's not a knock on boats. It's just the truth about the best fishing spots in this part of Louisiana: they're shallow, they're skinny, and they're choked with grass a hull can't get near.

This is not our regular swamp tour, and we tell every guest that up front. If you want to sit back and watch a heron fish, book the Manchac trip. If you want to be the one doing the fishing, this is the one.

## This Isn't Our Swamp Tour, and We Want You to Know That

Most of what we run is a sightseeing eco-tour: quiet paddling, wildlife watching, a naturalist guide narrating the ecology. The kayak fishing charter is a different product built for a different guest, someone who wants a line in the water, not just a paddle in hand.

We built a whole YouTube series around this because almost nobody in the New Orleans swamp-tour space makes fishing content. **That's the tell.** Everyone in this market sells the same eco-tour. Almost nobody sells the fishing trip, even though the marsh here is some of the best fishing water in the country. We saw an open lane and we're the ones running fishing footage while our competitors post gator selfies.

## Why a Pedal Kayak Beats a Boat Out Here

Our guides fish from pedal-drive kayaks. Hands free, feet doing the work, rod ready the entire time. That setup gets you into two feet of water over a grass flat without a motor telling every fish in the area you've arrived.

A boat has to stay in water deep enough to float it. A kayak doesn't care. **When you hook a fish out here, here's the thing nobody tells you: it pulls you, not the other way around.** The boat doesn't move. You do. Ask any guest who's had a decent redfish tow their kayak twenty feet sideways, they'll tell you that's most of the fun.

## What You're Actually Fishing For

Depends entirely on where your guide puts you, and that decision is made the morning of, not scripted in advance.

- **Bayou Bienvenue**, freshwater, holds largemouth bass, bream, and catfish along the grass lines. [internal link: Bayou Bienvenue, the freshwater bayou where we run a lot of these trips → /blog/bayou-bienvenue-vanishing-swamp/] is also one of our restoration sites, so you're fishing water we're actively working to bring back.
- **Marsh water that runs brackish** opens up redfish and speckled trout, which take different technique and different tackle than the freshwater bayous.
- **Season and tide decide the day**, not a fixed itinerary. A guide who fishes this water regularly knows what's been holding where that week, and that knowledge is worth more than any gear you bring.

We're not going to promise you a limit. We will promise you a guide who actually fishes, not someone reading off a script about where the fish "should" be.

## What a Trip Looks Like, Start to Finish

Expect a short briefing on the kayak, the pedal drive, and basic safety before you launch, the same baseline as every trip we run: life jacket on, guide watching the group, no exceptions. Then you're pedaling into water a boat physically can't follow, hunting whatever's biting that day.

Guides keep it honest on the water, including the misses. A guest last season landed nothing for the first forty minutes, then caught three bass in the last twenty near a downed cypress log the guide had been eyeing the whole trip. That's fishing. Anyone who promises otherwise is selling you something.

**Bonus for foragers:** if your guide spots oyster mushrooms growing on dead cypress along the route, don't be surprised if the trip turns into a small foraging detour too. The swamp feeds you two ways out here if you're paying attention.

## Crabbing and Shrimping, If You Want to Add It

The full charter covers crabbing and shrimping too, depending on season and what's running. Crab traps go out along the marsh edges, shrimp nets work differently and depend heavily on the time of year. Ask when you book which of the three, fishing, crabbing, or shrimping, makes the most sense for the week you're visiting. We'd rather steer you toward what's actually productive right now than sell you a version of the trip that's mostly quiet.

## Licenses, Limits, and What to Bring

Louisiana requires a recreational fishing license for most anglers 16 and up, and the freshwater and saltwater rules aren't identical. Confirm with us when you book, and check current Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries requirements before your trip since these change. Bring the license if you already have one. If not, ask us and we'll point you toward getting one before you arrive.

Pack closed-toe water shoes, sun protection, a hat with a strap, and a dry bag if you want your phone out on the water. Skip the cotton. You will get wet, and if the fish cooperate, you'll be glad you dressed for it.

This is the trip for the guest who's tired of watching from the dock. [internal link: the kayak fishing, crabbing, and shrimping charter → /kayak-fishing-crabbing-shrimping-tour/] runs small groups with a guide who fishes this water for a living, not a script. Thirty minutes from the Quarter, a world away from it, rod in hand this time.
```
