## FAQ SCHEMA

**Q1:**
```
Do I need kayaking experience to go on a swamp tour?
```
**A1:**
```
No. Most guests on a guided swamp kayak tour have never been in a kayak before. Guides run a safety briefing and basic paddle instruction on land before launch, and the kayaks used are stable sit-in models designed for calm water, not whitewater or ocean kayaks.
```

**Q2:**
```
Can you tip over a kayak on a swamp tour?
```
**A2:**
```
It's rare on calm bayou water with a stable sit-in kayak, and it hasn't happened on one of New Orleans Kayak Swamp Tours' 400+ tours. The water in Manchac Swamp is flat and slow-moving, not open water with wind chop or current, which is what actually causes most kayak capsizes.
```

**Q3:**
```
Is it safe to kayak near alligators?
```
**A3:**
```
Yes, with a trained guide who knows the ecosystem. Alligators in Manchac generally avoid kayaks and people unless they've been fed by humans, which is exactly why guides never bait them. Sightings are common in warmer months but never guaranteed, and guides brief every group on what to do if one is spotted close by.
```

**Q4:**
```
What age can kids start kayak swamp tours?
```
**A4:**
```
Every guest needs to be able to sit upright, wear a life jacket properly, and follow basic instructions, which in practice means most operators, including us, set a minimum age rather than a strict cutoff, so call ahead with your child's age before booking. Kids old enough to paddle a bit or ride along with an adult in a tandem kayak do well on the standard 2-hour tour.
```

**Q5:**
```
What if I'm afraid of the water or don't know how to swim?
```
**A5:**
```
You'll wear a USCG Type III life jacket the entire tour, and most of Manchac Swamp is shallow enough to stand in if needed. Guides are CPR-certified, and non-swimmers join our tours regularly. Tell your guide beforehand so they can keep an eye on you and answer any questions before you launch.
```

## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
"I've never kayaked before, is that a problem?" We hear a version of this question on almost every call at New Orleans Kayak Swamp Tours. Short answer: no. Long answer: here's every worry we hear on repeat, answered straight, no sales pitch attached.

## The Five Questions We Hear Every Week

Our guides keep an informal tally. In order of how often they come up: will I tip over, are the alligators dangerous, can my kid come, I can't swim well is that a dealbreaker, and what if I'm just out of shape. If you're nervous about booking your first kayak tour, you're not the outlier. You're most of our guest list.

## What Actually Happens Before You Launch

Every tour starts on dry land with a safety briefing, roughly 10 to 15 minutes. Your guide covers how to hold the paddle, how to get in and out of the kayak without flipping it, what the life jacket does, and what to do if you see wildlife. Then everyone practices a few strokes in shallow water before the group heads out together.

Nobody gets handed a paddle and pushed off blind. **We've never had a guest who couldn't get the hang of it within the first ten minutes.**

The kayaks themselves are stable sit-in models, wide enough that you'd have to work pretty hard to tip one over on calm water. These aren't whitewater kayaks or the narrow sit-on-top ocean kind you might picture. They're built for exactly this kind of slow-moving swamp water.

## Can You Tip Over? The Honest Answer

Technically, yes. Anything that floats can flip. Realistically, on the flat, slow water of Manchac Swamp, in a stable sit-in kayak, with a guide watching the group? It's rare. We've run 400+ tours with zero guest injuries, and capsizes are not a thing our guides deal with regularly.

Most kayak accidents you hear about happen in open water, ocean swells, whitewater rapids, or wind chop on a big lake. Manchac is a cypress swamp with barely a current. Different animal entirely.

## What About the Alligators

This is the big one, so let's address it directly. Alligators in Manchac are common in warmer months, never guaranteed, and they want nothing to do with your kayak. An unfed, unbothered alligator's default move is to avoid you, not approach you. That's exactly why we never bait them for a photo op, a baited gator learns to associate people with food, and that's the actual danger, not a wild one minding its own business.

Your guide briefs every group before launch on what to do if you spot one close by (spoiler: stay calm, keep paddling, don't reach for it, ever). A guest last summer paddled within maybe 15 feet of a 6-footer sunning on a log and told our guide afterward it was "the calmest I've ever felt near something that could technically eat me." That's a pretty good summary of how these encounters usually go.

## Bringing Kids on a Swamp Tour

We get this question constantly, and there's no single universal cutoff age across the industry, it depends on the kid. What matters more than a birthday is whether your child can sit upright in a kayak, wear a life jacket correctly, and follow basic instructions from a guide. A calm 6-year-old riding tandem with a parent often does better than an antsy 10-year-old.

**Call us before you book** and tell us your kid's age and temperament. We'd rather have that conversation upfront than have a family show up to the launch site and find out the tour isn't a fit that day.

## If You Can't Swim or You're Just Nervous About Water

You're wearing a USCG Type III life jacket the entire time, no exceptions, guide included. Most of the swamp is shallow enough to stand in if something genuinely goes sideways, which, again, hasn't happened on our tours. Guides are CPR-certified and trained for exactly this kind of situation, not just paddling instruction.

If water makes you nervous generally, say so when you book or the moment you meet your guide. We'd much rather know upfront and keep an eye on you than have you white-knuckle it through the whole two hours pretending you're fine.

## What to Expect Once You're Out There

Once the nerves settle, which for most people takes about the first five minutes, the tour becomes less about kayaking technique and more about what's around you: cypress trees older than the country, birds you'll hear before you see, and the kind of quiet that doesn't exist most places in a city 30 minutes away. A guest told our guide last month, "I spent the whole drive out here dreading this, and now I don't want to go back to the car." That's the usual arc.
