## 1. Mechanics that matter in 2026 (section G first, because it gates everything)

### What is solid enough to plan around

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram's top three ranking signals are watch time, likes per reach, and sends (DM shares) per reach. Watch time is the most important for both followers and non-followers. Sends matter more than likes for reaching NON-followers; likes matter slightly more for followers. | Reported from Mosseri, Jan 2025. Widely repeated, still the best public basis. Exact weights online (35/25/20/15/5 etc.) are NOT sourced. Ignore them. | https://www.eclincher.com/articles/how-the-instagram-algorithm-works-in-2026 and https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/how-the-instagram-algorithm-works |
| Saves are not on Mosseri's named top three. They still signal "worth keeping" and carousels earn higher save rates. | Directional only | https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/instagram-carousel-vs-reels |
| Asking for open-ended comments and replying personally is fine. Bait aimed purely at inflating counts (a forced word, number or emoji) can limit reach. | Instagram clarification, June 2024 | https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-advice-on-single-word-ctas-and-longer-reels/718151/ |
| Reels over 90 seconds are not recommended to non-followers. | Same source | same |
| Original content is favored; reposts keep reaching followers but stop being pushed to strangers. Reported that the rule was extended to photos and carousels on April 30, 2026. The "10 reposts in 30 days = aggregator" threshold is NOT confirmed by Instagram. | Blogs only, unverified | https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=563048 and https://metricool.com/instagram-reels-guide/ |
| Trial Reels show a reel only to non-followers first, off the grid. Eligibility and the test window (24 to 72h) are reported inconsistently. Check the app before relying on it. | Mixed | https://www.inro.social/blog/what-is-a-reel-trial |
| Reels vs carousels: sources disagree. Reels = better for new-audience reach; carousels = better engagement rate and saves; carousels may win reach above about 50k followers (not us at 5,314). | Mixed, directional | https://www.fanpagekarma.com/insights/?p=17037 |

### What I could NOT verify (do not treat as fact)

- Pinned comment boosts reach, and "reply to every comment in the first hour" boosts reach. No source found. Both are cheap and harmless, and the second one is directly supported by Instagram's "personal replies are not bait" line. Do them anyway, just don't promise a lift.
- "Hook in 1.5 seconds", "7-second ideal length", text safe zones. One blog says keep text out of the top 14 percent and bottom 35 percent of a 9:16 frame; another says keep it central and clear of the right-edge button stack. They don't agree. Practical rule for us: text in the middle third, large, 6 words per screen or fewer, check one real post on a phone before copying a template.
- Comment-bait prompts are demoted on Instagram in 2026. Some blogs say yes; Meta's own stated line (2024) is "open questions fine, forced single-word gaming not." No 2026 policy doc found.

### Working rules for NKST (JUDGMENT, built from the above plus the Oct study)

1. **Frame 1 is the hook.** Text on screen from frame 0, no logo card, no fade-in. The Oct study could not see frame one on competitors; assume 1.5 to 3 seconds to earn the stay.
2. **Length: 7 to 15 seconds for gags and quizzes, up to 45s only for the identity/story reels** (our 3/21 at 46s worked). Never over 90s.
3. **Last frame = the question.** Make the question the final 1.5 seconds on screen, large text, so people who loop see it and people who finish answer it. Plain question, not "comment YES."
4. **Sends beat likes for strangers.** Design for "send this to the friend who..." (caption: `Send this to whoever screams at herons`). A quiz with a reveal also earns sends ("did you get it?").
5. **Reply to every comment in the first hour, with a question or a one-line joke, not "thanks!"** Reply with a second reel if a comment is good (see format E3).
6. **Pin one comment.** Pin the funniest wrong answer or the best argument, not our own caption text. It shows new viewers what kind of place the comments are.
7. **Cadence: hold at 4 a week** (matches the Oct study). Rezdy's guidance for tour operators is a maximum of 5 a week to start (https://www.rezdy.com/blog/how-to-instagram-a-guide-for-tour-operators/). Quality of hook beats a fifth post.
8. **Carousel for depth (then-and-now, land loss, ID cards), reel for reach.** One carousel a week is plenty. Influencity's reported 60/40 carousel vs photo-dump split is a photo-dump idea, not a rule for us (https://influencity.com/blog/en/instagram-strategy-the-photo-dump-vs-reels).
9. **Original sources only.** Do not re-upload other accounts' clips or recycled TikToks. Guest photos are fine as a feature with credit and permission, framed as our post (see E1).
10. **Close the loop: log each post** in the Performance.md columns from the Oct study. Promote on share+save rate, not likes.

## 7. F. Local-issue content that stayed on-brand

What I found (VERIFIED where linked):

**Worked (or held up):**
- **Trashfish Cleveland** (Cuyahoga River kayaker): trash jokes plus river litter images grew the project beyond what the founder expected; people got curious in person first. https://neorsd.buzzsprout.com/1924356/episodes/16513239-a-kayaker-s-cuyahoga-cleanup
- **Emily Garbage Shark** (Pigeon River, Ontario): litter photos mixed with wildlife photos; educational angle, shows how long garbage takes to break down.
- **Trash-Your-Kayak cleanup crew, West Virginia**: since 2016 more than 3,000 tires and 3.5 tons removed; the ask is low: fill one bag and post a picture. https://wvexplorer.com/?p=38098
- **National Park Service** tone: humor for wildlife safety, serious for sensitive sites. "Edutainment" with a hook and one fact. https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2025/09/30/national-park-service-social-media-fat-bear-week
- What these have in common (JUDGMENT): they show the action (one bag, one tire), they say what was done, and they don't blame a person or company.

**Went badly:**
- **Louisiana swamp tour guide feeding wild gators** (Lafitte, Jefferson Parish, 2014): a tourist's video spread widely (reported 100,000+ Facebook shares); authorities then ordered swamp boat tour operators in the parish to stop feeding alligators. https://abc13.com/news/viral-video-leads-to-investigation-of-swamp-tour-guide/110789/ . Lesson: a stunt that wins attention can draw regulators. Applies directly to any Brutus-type "gator comes to the boat" content, and any marshmallow or food lure.
- **Spanish tourist pouring beer for an elephant in Kenya** (2025/26): the video was deleted after outrage, officials launched an investigation, and the ministry then tightened rules for tour operators. https://citizen.digital/article/outrage-after-tourist-filmed-pouring-beer-down-elephants-trunk-in-laikipia-n368771 . Lesson: even an individual's wildlife stunt gets tied to the operator and the destination.
- Sabah, Malaysia 2026: viral incident where a guide was reportedly assaulted after asking a person to stop feeding monkeys; officials reminded operators and tourists not to feed wildlife. https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/08/11/dont-feed-wildlife-tour-operators-and-tourists-warned . Lesson: "don't feed wildlife" is the safe, correct message, but a confrontation clip can go the wrong way. Don't film conflicts.
- I did NOT find a case of an outfitter's littering or oil post that backfired. Absence of evidence only.

**Rules for NKST (JUDGMENT):**
1. **Show the action and the place.** One bag, one tire, one clear channel. A before/after of a cleaned bank. No villain.
2. **Facts with a year and a source in the caption.** `About 2,000 square miles of coast lost since the 1930s (USGS).` If rates differ between sources, quote one and name it.
3. **Ask a question, don't give a verdict.** `What did the swamp near you look like when you were a kid?` invites memory, not a fight.
4. **No named companies, no politics, no oil-company names.** Oil and gas is a local employer; naming it turns a nature post into a sides post.
5. **Wildlife feeding:** state the rule plainly (feeding wild gators is illegal in Louisiana and teaches them to approach boats). Check the exact rule and cite LDWF before posting. Never show a lure.
6. **Moderation:** assume comments will get spicy; set up a 24-hour check and a short reply template ("We read all of these. Thanks for the facts, here is our source.").
7. **Land-loss content goes in a carousel or a map reel with a source slide**, once a month, not every week.

## 8. Guardrails across all formats

- Business name only in captions and replies. No person named for the business.
- No fake "log or gator" reveals using staged or lured animals.
- No re-uploading others' clips. Credit guests; get written permission for guest photos.
- Open-ended prompts only ("pick one", "name it"). No "comment YES for a discount."
- No "book now" in line one for the entertainment slots; the Oct study's finding holds.
- Anything that includes a fact gets a one-line source in the caption (or the first comment).
