## 1. Oil and gas in the wetlands

### Facts
- Plaquemines Parish jury verdict, April 2025: about $745M ($744.6M) against Chevron: $575M land loss, $161M contamination, $8.6M abandoned equipment. Parish had sought up to $3B. https://fortune.com/2025/04/04/chevron-louisiana-coastal-wetlands-pollution-court-ruling-damages (Apr 4, 2025); https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/three-takeaways-745-million-louisiana-verdict-against-chevron-coastal-wetland
- U.S. Supreme Court, April 17, 2026, Chevron USA v. Plaquemines Parish (No. 24-813): Chevron may move the parish suits to federal court under the federal officer removal statute. Opinion by Justice Thomas. Law firms disagree on whether the verdict is wiped or just sent back; treat verdict status as contested. https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/4/supreme-court-decides-chevron-usa-inc-v-plaquemines-parish-louisiana (Apr 2026); https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/608us1r25_2q24.pdf
- Settlements in 2026: state reported near a deal with ConocoPhillips (Mar 2026); Bloomberg Government item June 1, 2026 reports an agreement with ExxonMobil. Contents not read. https://www.eenews.net/articles/louisiana-nears-deal-with-conocophillips-over-coastal-erosion-2/ ; https://news.bgov.com/environment-and-energy/louisiana-enters-agreement-with-exxonmobil-over-coastal-erosion-1 (UNVERIFIED details)
- Canal count: 35,163 canals on land in 14 coastal parishes as of 2017, with a spatial and temporal link between canal permits and land loss (Turner and McClenachan, PLOS ONE 2018). https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6294348/ (Dec 2018)
- Pipeline scale: National Academies (2005) counted 10 major navigation canals and 9,300 miles of pipelines in coastal Louisiana. Share of land loss blamed on canals ranges from about 16 to 85 percent depending on who is counting. Cite a range, never a single number. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1332684/
- Local tie-in: logging canals in Maurepas/Manchac also let salt water into the swamp (see topic 4). Same cause, different era. We do not need to name any company.

### Risk note
Oil and gas workers, families, and some local officials get mad; defensible if we cite only canal counts and court dates and never blame a named company.

### Lines
- Fight-starter: Every straight canal you see out here was cut by people. Nature does not draw with a ruler.
- Unpopular opinion: the wetlands lost the most to jobs we all cheered for.
- Question: Did your family work the oil patch, and what do you tell your kids about the canals?

## 2. Coastal land loss, diversions, ghost forests

### Facts
- "Football field every 100 minutes" is the 2010-2016 USGS rate (2017 maps). Older figure was one per 34 minutes in the worst period. Louisiana has lost about 25 percent of its land since 1932 per USGS as cited by Axios. No confirmed 2025 or 2026 USGS refresh found; Axios (Dec 3, 2025) says the stat assumes constant loss. https://edf.org/media/updated-usgs-maps-show-decreased-rate-louisiana-land-loss (2017); https://www.axios.com/local/new-orleans/2025/12/03/louisiana-map-shrinking-climate-change (Dec 3, 2025; page blocked, summary via search)
- Mid-Barataria Sediment Diversion: paused by the Governor in April 2025 (90 days), then terminated by CPRA and the Louisiana Trustee Implementation Group, announced July 17, 2025. About $2.26B had been awarded in the settlement funding. https://coastal.la.gov/news/state-louisiana-trustee-implementation-group-announce-termination-of-mid-barataria-sediment-diversion/ (Jul 17, 2025); https://gulfspillrestoration.noaa.gov/2025/07/louisiana-moves-terminate-mid-barataria-sediment-diversion-project
- Maurepas Swamp river reintroduction (PO-0029): in construction, listed at about $425M, completion cited as 2028 on older pages and 2030 on newer ones. Reach 6 Phase 2 window Dec 2026 to Oct 2028 per June 17, 2026 CPRA board presentation. RESTORE Council approved a $60M grant in June 2025. First Louisiana diversion built to restore swamp forest. https://ap27.coastal.la.gov/projects/river-reintroduction-into-maurepas-swamp ; https://coastal.la.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/WSLP-and-Maurepas-Presentation.pdf (Jun 2026)
- Ghost forests: dead cypress and tupelo stands from salt water, levees cutting off river water, and subsidence. One study found up to 100 percent tree mortality in 11 years at some Maurepas sites; USGS-linked work expects most of the dying swamp to become open water without help. https://trcp.org/2019/03/04/ghost-forests-haunting-reminder-louisianas-lost-wetlands (Mar 2019); https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/25537
- Local hook: this is the swamp the Maurepas project is meant to save. Guests paddle the thing under debate.

### Risk note
Politically charged both ways (the diversion cancellation pits fishers and oyster growers against restoration advocates); defensible if we stay on the facts and avoid naming politicians.

### Lines
- Fight-starter: Cancelling the big diversion was the right call, and the swamp you are paddling still needs a river.
- Unpopular opinion: a ghost forest is the best tour stop we have. Ugly truth sells.
- Question: Would you trade a good oyster season for land that might last 50 years?

## 4. Cypress logging history

### Facts
- Lumbering began in the 1830s and the last virgin cypress was cut from the Manchac swamp in 1957 (Fox 8 report). Cypress forest once covered about 129,000 acres between Maurepas and Pontchartrain (SELU). Most logging 1889 to 1930. https://www.fox8live.com/story/29710838/heart-of-louisiana-turtle-cove-environmental-research-station ; https://www.selu.edu/acad_research/depts/biol/programs/wetlab/index.html
- Most trees today are second growth, many under 80 years old; a few hollow giants were left because they were useless to loggers. https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna8740606 ; https://southlands.substack.com/p/field-guide-lake-maurepas
- Logging canals still scar the swamp and changed the water flow; cypress is not regenerating in much of the south Maurepas swamp, studied by SELU since 1989. https://www.lacoast.gov/reports/project/3891454~1.pdf
- Cypress mulch campaign, 2006 to 2008: the Save Our Cypress Coalition pushed three big-box retailers to stop selling Louisiana cypress mulch. Walmart agreed first; Lowe's and Home Depot made narrower, disputed commitments. Current policy status UNVERIFIED. https://healthygulf.org/press_releases/wal-mart-says-no-to-louisiana-cypress-mulch/ ; https://news.mongabay.com/2008/11/louisiana-cypress-mulch-industry-devastates-old-growth-forests/ (Nov 2008)
- Image-able fact: occasional skeleton stumps of old trees still visible along old logging canals. Confirm on the water with a guide before claiming a specific spot.

### Risk note
Some locals have family tied to the timber history and mill towns; defensible if framed as history, not blame. Avoid naming retailers in a caption.

### Lines
- Fight-starter: Nearly every tree you will paddle past is a replacement. The originals went to build the country.
- Unpopular opinion: cypress mulch in a garden bed is still a swamp in a bag.
- Question: Does your house have cypress siding or doors, and do you know where it came from?

## 5. Wildlife debates

### Facts
- Alligator feeding: Louisiana has no statewide law banning it; local rules exist (New Orleans City Park, Jefferson Parish). A 2022 bill (HB 541, fines $100 to $350) was proposed; outcome not confirmed. In 2014 a viral video of a swamp tour guide feeding gators (chicken and marshmallows) led Jefferson Parish to order tour companies to stop. https://abc13.com/post/viral-video-leads-to-investigation-of-swamp-tour-guide/110789/ (2014); https://www.wlf.louisiana.gov/news/louisiana-department-of-wildlife-and-fisheries-warns-public-not-to-feed-alligators (Jul 16, 2019); https://houmatimes.com/news/bill-outlawing-feeding-gators-deferred-for-second-time
- Nutria: bounty is $6 per tail. 2025-26 season ran Nov 20, 2025 to Mar 31, 2026 with 235,220 harvested and $1.41M paid (interim, Apr 2026 CPRA). Prior seasons: 357,015 (2024-25) and 401,312 (2023-24). Goal is up to 400,000 a season. https://coastal.la.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/April-2026-CPRA-CNCP-presentation.pdf (Apr 2026); https://wlf.louisiana.gov/page/nutria
- Invasive apple snail (Pomacea maculata): first found in Louisiana around 2006-2007; lays pink egg clusters on cypress knees and trunks; eats submerged plants. Only practical control is knocking clusters off. https://www.usgs.gov/centers/wetland-and-aquatic-research-center-warc/science-topics/apple-snail ; https://www.fox8live.com/story/31603653/apple-snails-invade-louisiana-waterways
- Bald eagles: about 5 nesting pairs in the 1970s, now over 350; delisted federally in 2007. https://www.wlf.louisiana.gov/news/louisiana-bald-eagle-population-shows-growth-according-to-latest-nest-survey-conducted-by-ldwf ; https://bizneworleans.com/louisianas-bald-eagle-population-growing/
- Roseate spoonbills: near wipeout by plume hunters in the 1800s; Audubon cites about 1,250 breeding pairs holding steady in Louisiana for decades; range expanding. https://audubon.org/news/bp-deepwater-horizon-oil-spill-wildlife-update-roseate-spoonbills (older)
- Folklore (list only, per brand care): Rougarou, Cajun werewolf figure lures people into the swamp; Honey Island Swamp Monster (a different swamp, St. Tammany, sightings since 1960s); Julia Brown, Frenier healer and the legend of a storm prophecy tied to the 1915 hurricane. Details are from tourism blogs and conflict. Alan Brown's book Louisiana Legends and Lore is the better source. https://arcadiapublishing.com/products/9781467147514 ; https://acadianahistorical.org/items/show/59

### Risk note
Gator feeding is the hot wire: operators and fans of "close-up" tours get angry; defensible only as our own policy ("we do not feed") with no accusation at any named operator. Folklore stays gentle; Frenier was a real disaster with real families.

### Lines
- Fight-starter: If a gator comes to your boat for a marshmallow, the tour taught it to. We do not.
- Unpopular opinion: nutria are not cute. A $6 tail bounty is fair.
- Question: Rougarou, swamp monster, or just a very big gator. Which one are you betting on?

## 6. Airboats vs kayaks

### Facts
- Noise: a Virginia legislative study states airboats run around 100 to 110 decibels underway. Treat as one number from a non-Louisiana source; UNVERIFIED for local boats. https://rga.lis.virginia.gov/Published/1990/SD24/PDF
- 2013 Louisiana Senate concurrent resolution asked LDWF to study how local governments could regulate airboat noise; no completed study or rule found. https://legis.la.gov/Legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1331440
- Some wildlife areas and private marsh leases restrict airboats outright or by season (per an operator blog, so a viewpoint, not data). https://www.cajunencounters.com/blog/airboats-vs-flatboats
- No peer-reviewed Louisiana comparison of kayak vs airboat wildlife disturbance was found. Do not claim science that does not exist.
- Honest pros of airboats: speed and reach into shallow flats, shaded boats for people who cannot paddle, accessible for many ages and fitness levels. Honest cons of kayaks: slower, need basic fitness, weather and wind limited, smaller groups mean higher price per person. Our edge is quiet: birds and gators stay put and you can hear the swamp.
- 2026 press release from a Louisiana airboat operator says noise management is becoming a consideration within the industry. Promotional. https://www.einpresswire.com/article/915884921/airboat-tours-combine-high-speed-travel-with-louisiana-wildlife-exploration

### Risk note
Airboat operators and their guests will pile on; defensible if we say what we offer ("quiet") and credit what they offer ("speed and access") without naming anyone.

### Lines
- Fight-starter: You cannot hear a swamp from an airboat. You can only hear the airboat.
- Unpopular opinion: airboats are great for some people. Just not for seeing a heron.
- Question: Airboat or paddle: which one did you pick, and would you pick it again?

## 7. Local identity hooks

### Facts
- Manchac is likely from the Choctaw word often rendered imashaka, "the rear entrance" (Swanton; Byington dictionary), so the name is a best reading, not settled. Spelling variants exist. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchac,_Louisiana ; https://64parishes.org/what-might-have-happened-at-manchac
- Lake Pontchartrain: shallow (about 12 ft average), brackish, about 630 square miles. Many call it an estuary; Congress in 2000 found the basin an estuary of national significance. https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1996/of96-527/photoetc/slides/facts.htm ; https://www.congress.gov/106/crpt/hrpt594/CRPT-106hrpt594.pdf
- Middendorf's (Akers/Manchac) opened in 1934 with thin-fried catfish credited to Josie Middendorf. Thin vs thick has been argued for decades (Tom Fitzmorris, 2016). Current owners bought in April 2007. Reference only, no tagging, no endorsement language. https://wwltv.com/article/life/food/access-code-70421-middendorfs-home-of-the-world-famous-thin-fried-catfish/289-572683777 ; https://www.myneworleans.com/fried-catfish-is-high-eating-too/
- Pass Manchac / swamp vs bayou vs marsh debate: no single source needed, but a bayou is slow moving water and a swamp is forested wetland; local usage is loose. Our guides can settle it on camera.
- Anti-cliche hook: not every Louisiana swamp tour looks like the postcard. We paddle where the road ends, 45 minutes from the Quarter.

### Risk note
Pure local sport: thin vs thick and swamp vs bayou will fill comments with friendly insults; safe as long as we do not trash a restaurant or a parish.

### Lines
- Fight-starter: It is a swamp, not a bayou, and the people correcting you are also wrong.
- Unpopular opinion: Lake Pontchartrain is not a lake. It is a salty estuary with a good publicist.
- Question: Thin or thick catfish at the old Manchac spot, and why are you right?

## 8. Weather and water

### Facts
- 2026 season: Tropical Storm Bertha made landfall July 22, 2026 in St. Bernard Parish at about 45 mph, with 1 to 3 ft of surge forecast on Lake Pontchartrain; Arthur formed in June; season forecast below normal (NOAA 55 percent chance below normal; El Nino). Later storms not checked. https://www.wgcu.org/2026-07-22/tropical-storm-bertha-makes-landfall-in-southeast-louisiana ; https://lailluminator.com/2026/05/20/state-climatologist-hurricane-season-2026-looks-average-but-dont-shrug-it-off/
- 2023 Mississippi River saltwater wedge: low river flow and drought pushed salt up the river toward New Orleans; Army Corps built an underwater sill; 2024 wedge was slowed. https://ktvz.com/news/national-world/cnn-national/2023/10/01/saltwater-is-winning-why-low-water-levels-have-grown-into-a-huge-problem-for-the-new-orleans-area/ (Oct 1, 2023); https://nolawater.org/2023-saltwater-intrusion-updates
- Salt reaching the swamp: Maurepas gets salt from canals and Lake Pontchartrain during storms and droughts. https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/25537
- Hurricane Ike (2008) pushed a 10 ft surge through Pass Manchac (Middendorf's rebuild coverage). https://www.myneworleans.com/fried-catfish-is-high-eating-too/
- Fog: no sourced fact found for Manchac fog season. UNVERIFIED. A fatal I-55 Manchac pileup in marsh fire smoke and fog exists in news archives (date not confirmed); not usable as content.
- Water level extremes in the swamp itself: no current-year number found. Use our own guide observations or a USGS gauge reading pulled that day.

### Risk note
Safety and cancellations: people get touchy when we sound cavalier about storms; defensible when we frame it as "we cancel, here is why."

### Lines
- Fight-starter: A quiet hurricane season is a forecast, not a promise. The swamp remembers every storm.
- Unpopular opinion: foggy dawn is the best tour of the year. Bring a jacket and quit whining.
- Question: Which storm do you still measure everything by, and what did the water do?

## Do not touch

- Naming any oil company, retailer, restaurant owner, airboat or swamp tour operator, or politician in a caption. Facts stay about the public record, not blame.
