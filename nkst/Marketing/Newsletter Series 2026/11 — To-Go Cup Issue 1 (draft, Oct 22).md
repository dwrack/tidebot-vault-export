## 1. The pour

The heat finally broke. This is the stretch of the year locals wait all summer for: 70s in the afternoon, a breeze off the river, and a festival somewhere every weekend until Thanksgiving. If you are coming to town, this is the fortnight. If you live here, you already know, and you should still read the Po-Boy Fest line.

## 2. In town this week

- **Sat Oct 24, Treme Fall Fest.** Free, 11 am to 6 pm, kicks off with a second line at 11 am. Brass bands and food in the neighborhood that invented most of what you came for. (tremefest.org)
- **Sat Oct 31 to Sun Nov 1, NOLA Zydeco Fest, Slidell.** Across the lake on the Northshore. Worth the drive if you have a car. [VERIFY times]
- **Sun Nov 1, Dia de los Muertos Festival, Lafayette Square.** Downtown, free. [VERIFY hours]
- **Sat Nov 7, Beignet Fest, City Park Festival Grounds.** Beignets in every form, proceeds go to programs for kids with developmental delays. [VERIFY hours and ticket price]
- **Sun Nov 8, Oak Street Po-Boy Festival, 10 am to 6 pm.** Uptown, 8100 to 8800 Oak Street. New date this year (it used to be the 15th), so do not trust an old flyer. (poboyfest.com)
- **Sat Oct 31, Halloween.** Frenchmen Street after dark is the show. The French Quarter is louder. Pick one.

Michelin announced its 2026 American South guide on Oct 21 in Nashville, and New Orleans cleaned up. Emeril's kept its two stars. Dakar NOLA got its first. And a pile of places locals have been sending visitors to for decades got the Bib Gourmand, which is Michelin's way of saying great food that won't wreck your budget. Also on the Bib list if you're making a list: Willie Mae's (fried chicken), Turkey and the Wolf (the sandwiches people fly in for), Cochon, and Mister Mao.

## 4. The thing only locals know

**Bayou St. John.** A four-mile bayou in the middle of the city that you can paddle, walk, or just sit beside with a to-go cup. Start at the Magnolia Bridge (the little white pedestrian bridge by the Pitot House), head toward City Park, and you will pass people fishing, a wedding or two, and the same egrets that fly over our swamp. If you want to paddle it, we rent kayaks nearby. If you want to read about it, we wrote a piece on Marie Laveau and the bayou's voodoo history. And the 4:30 pm start runs straight into golden hour now that the days are short. That is the slot to book if you want the light.
