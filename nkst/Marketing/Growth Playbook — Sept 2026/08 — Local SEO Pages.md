Most swamp tours near New Orleans put you on a motorboat. Ours put you in a kayak, and it changes the whole trip.

Since 2013, New Orleans Kayak Swamp Tours has taken small groups into the cypress swamps outside the city in silent kayaks, not airboats. You sit at water level. Your paddle is the only sound. A trained naturalist guide leads 12 guests or fewer through channels that bigger boats can't fit into. Our flagship tour runs in Manchac, inside the Maurepas Swamp Wildlife Management Area. It's the second-largest bald cypress swamp in the US, about 35 minutes from the French Quarter.

## Why a kayak?

### You hear the swamp

With no engine running, you hear the swamp itself: frogs, woodpeckers, the splash of a turtle sliding off a log. Guests tell us the quiet is the part they didn't expect.

### Wildlife stays put

A quiet boat doesn't push wildlife out ahead of it. Herons keep fishing. Turtles stay on their logs. At Manchac, alligators are common from spring through fall, often sunning on the banks. That's **common, not guaranteed**. We never bait wildlife, so what you see is how the swamp actually is that day.

### You go where it's narrow

A kayak slips under low cypress limbs and down back channels that a larger boat has to skip. That's where the good stuff tends to be.

### You don't need experience

The water has no current, so you're never fighting it. You'll paddle a stable tandem sit-on-top kayak, and your guide runs a full safety briefing before launch. Most of our guests have never kayaked before.

The French Quarter is the center of the city for most visits. By day two, a lot of people want to see what's around it. Our pickup point sits on North Rampart Street, on the lakeside edge of the Quarter, and we've pointed guests in every direction from it since 2013. Here's what we tell them.

We've grouped this by how much time you've got. Everything in the first section is walkable. The second section takes a half day.

## Walking distance: an hour or two

### The riverfront

Walk toward the Mississippi and follow the riverfront. The Moonwalk runs along the water by Jackson Square, and heading downriver takes you toward Crescent Park, a long, open green space in the Marigny and Bywater. Go in the early morning or late afternoon, when it's cooler and the light is better.

### Across the river on the ferry

The ferry at the foot of Canal Street crosses to Algiers Point, a quiet neighborhood with a levee path and a skyline view back toward the Quarter. It's an easy, low-key hour. Check the ferry schedule before you go.

### Faubourg Marigny and Frenchmen Street

Cross Esplanade Avenue and you're in the Marigny: Creole cottages, small galleries, and Frenchmen Street's music clubs. Wander during the day, then come back at night.

### Tremé and Louis Armstrong Park

Across Rampart Street is Tremé, one of the oldest Black neighborhoods in America. Louis Armstrong Park and Congo Square sit right on its edge, a short walk from our pickup point. Take your time here. Tremé has real history, and the people who live there are still part of it.

### Bayou St. John and City Park

A short ride up Esplanade Avenue gets you to Bayou St. John, a calm urban waterway lined with historic homes, and to City Park beyond it. It's a good stop if you want water and green space without leaving the city. The bayou has its own history too: read [Bayou St. John and the Marie Laveau story](/bayou-st-john-kayak-marie-laveau-voodoo-history/).

## Half-day escapes: three to five hours

### The Garden District by streetcar

Take the St. Charles streetcar uptown to the Garden District to see big oaks, old mansions, and a slower pace. It's a good morning trip, and you'll be back in the Quarter for lunch.

### A plantation with the full history

Whitney Plantation, upriver, tells the story of the people who were enslaved there. It's a serious visit, and worth it. If you'd like to add a swamp to the same day, our Whitney Plantation + Swamp Combo includes Whitney admission and transport ($195/person, about 8 hours). Read the [Whitney and swamp combo guide](/whitney-plantation-swamp-tour-combo-guide/).

### An urban wildlife refuge

Bayou Sauvage, inside New Orleans East, is one of the largest urban wildlife refuges in the country. See our [Bayou Sauvage guide](/bayou-sauvage-wildlife-refuge-new-orleans/) before you go.

### The nature pick: a kayak swamp tour

If you only do one thing outside the city, make it a swamp. The Quarter's history is all about the water around it, and nothing explains that faster than an hour among cypress trees.

Our [Manchac Swamp Wildlife Tour](/manchac-swamp-kayak-tour/) runs in the Maurepas Swamp Wildlife Management Area, the second-largest bald cypress swamp in the US, about 35 minutes from the Quarter. It happens in silent kayaks, not airboats. It's a small group, 12 guests max, led by a trained naturalist guide. You don't need any experience, and the water has no current. Alligators are common from spring through fall: common, not guaranteed. Herons and egrets are there most days.

## What Honey Island is

Honey Island Swamp sits along the Pearl River near Slidell, close to the Mississippi state line, roughly an hour east of the French Quarter. The Pearl River feeds it directly, so the swamp rises and falls with the river. Manchac is lake-fed and stays steadier. That river connection shapes everything about the trip, including why we run it only in summer.

## What it's great for

### The legends

People know Honey Island for its stories. The most famous is the Honey Island Swamp Monster, a cryptid legend that goes back to unusual tracks reported in the 1960s. Nobody has produced hard evidence of it, and our guides won't pretend otherwise, but they'll tell you the story. Older stories involve outlaws, and there's Prohibition-era bootlegging history around the Whiskey Tree. For the full versions, read [the Pierre Rameau legend](/pierre-rameau-honey-island-swamp-legend/), [the Whiskey Tree history](/whiskey-tree-honey-island-swamp-history/), and our [Louisiana swamp legends hub](/louisiana-swamp-legends-honey-island-manchac-hub/).

### The scenery

It's river-swamp forest: cypress and tupelo, bottomland hardwoods, and a green canopy in summer. It looks different from Manchac and feels rawer. Photographers and repeat swamp visitors tend to love it.

### The quiet

You'll paddle silent kayaks, not airboats, with 12 guests or fewer and a trained naturalist guide. It's a slow, calm couple of hours on the water, and that's the main reason to come.

## What to know before you book

- **Summer only.** Water levels decide when Honey Island is paddleable, and they make it less consistent than Manchac. Even in summer, a dry stretch can lower the river and change the route. Your guide will tell you honestly what conditions are like.
- **Alligators are rare.** We'll say it again because it matters. Honey Island is for scenery, stories, and quiet. It's not a gator tour.
- **It's a longer drive.** Plan on about an hour each way from the Quarter, compared with about 35 minutes to Manchac.
- **Beginners are welcome.** You'll paddle a stable tandem kayak. Your guide gives a full safety briefing, and every guest wears a USCG Type III life jacket.

## Who Honey Island is for

- **Repeat swamp visitors.** If you've already done Manchac, or another swamp tour, and want a different landscape, this is it.
- **Legend fans.** If the Honey Island Swamp Monster is why you found this page, you'll paddle the swamp the story comes from, with a guide who knows it.
- **Photographers.** The summer canopy and the river light make it a very different set of photos from Manchac.
- **People who want quiet.** A small group, no motors, and a slow pace.

It's probably **not** the right first swamp for a short visit, for guests who have their heart set on seeing an alligator, or for anyone visiting outside summer. For those trips, book [Manchac](/manchac-swamp-kayak-tour/).

## How the tour works

Your guide fits you for a life jacket, runs the safety briefing, and gets everyone settled in tandem kayaks. Then you head out onto the water for about two hours. The pace is slow. Your guide stops to point out the forest, the river's high-water marks, and whatever wildlife turns up, and tells the stories that belong to this swamp. You'll end back at the launch. This is our flagship tour and the one we run most. Manchac is a real cypress swamp, about 35 minutes from the French Quarter, and we paddle it in silent kayaks, not airboats. You'll be in a small group of 12 guests or fewer, led by a trained naturalist guide. We've been paddling here since 2013.

## Where you'll paddle

Manchac is what locals call the swamp between Lake Pontchartrain and Lake Maurepas. Its official name is the **Maurepas Swamp Wildlife Management Area**, the second-largest bald cypress swamp in the United States. Our launch is off Old US Hwy 51 in LaPlace. The swamp is fed by lakes, so its water stays steadier than river swamps like Honey Island. That's a big reason the trip is so consistent. For the full background, read our [Maurepas Swamp WMA guide](/maurepas-swamp-wildlife-management-area-guide/).

## What you'll see

### Alligators

From spring through fall, alligators are common at Manchac: common, not guaranteed. You'll often find them sunning along banks and floating low in quiet channels. We never bait wildlife, and from a silent kayak you see them behaving the way they normally do. Below about 65°F, reptiles go quiet. Read more about [the best time to see alligators](/best-time-to-see-alligators-louisiana-swamp/).

### Birds

Herons and egrets are there most days. In winter, migratory birds arrive, which makes it a good season for birders. See our [swamp birding field guide](/birding-louisiana-swamp-field-guide/).

### The forest

Bald cypress and tupelo grow straight out of the water, with knees poking up all around you. Your guide explains how the swamp works, why cypress matters, and what coastal land loss means for places like this. More in [cypress trees and Manchac ecology](/cypress-trees-manchac-swamp-ecology-restoration/) and the [wildlife field guide beyond alligators](/louisiana-swamp-wildlife-field-guide-beyond-alligators/).

### The stories

Manchac has a ghost story tied to real history: the 1915 hurricane, the lost villages on the swamp's edge, and the legend of Julia Brown. Ask your guide. The full story is in [the haunted Manchac swamp legend](/haunted-manchac-swamp-legend/).
