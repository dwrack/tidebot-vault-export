## How this was written

Two-pass process, same as every prior volume, run fresh against this batch. `/copy-mentors` and `/copywriting-2.0` were both loaded fresh for this pass before any line was drafted.

1. **`/copy-mentors`** — master routing by piece:
   - **Schwartz's identification principle** drove the majority of this batch again — 230, 231, 234, 237, 238, and 239 all assume the viewer already recognizes the moment (a captain's stale-but-beloved joke, the life-jacket size scramble, the whose-idea-was-it dispute, two boats waving at each other, a butchered place name, a paper towel roll quietly doing everything) and just name it rather than arguing it's funny.
   - **Sugarman's slippery-slide short openers** drove 226, 230, 233, and 239 — each opens with a short, plain, easy-to-swallow line before the bit builds, continuing the device Vols. 13-14 used repeatedly. This round the exact wording was deliberately varied ("7am:...", "Confession:", "Breaking:", "Item:") instead of reusing "Public record" / "Public notice" a third and fourth time, which is exactly what the copywriting-2.0 pass caught — see below.
   - **Halbert's conversational directness** drove 228's and 240's closing lines — "Nobody's checking a clock tonight. Nobody needs to be anywhere else" and "It's the last thing you do on the boat, and somehow it's the part everybody remembers doing" are flat, unhedged statements, no qualifier.
   - **Ogilvy's factual-specific-over-adjective principle** shaped 229 and 236 directly — instead of a vague "we run at night too" or "kids are welcome," both pieces borrow the exact real material already on file (the Booking Push doc's own after-dark recommendation; Brand Story's literal "ages 8 and up" policy line) rather than inventing softer, less specific claims.
   - **Blair Warren's 27 words** (applied during drafting, from copywriting-2.0): piece 225 leans on "allay their fears" — a faith-group trip doesn't need to look like the rest of this brand's marketing to still belong here. Piece 235 leans on "allay their fears" from the guest's own side — you don't have to feel ready for the water before you book it. Piece 228 leans on "confirm their suspicions" — that the quiet house isn't actually the reward empty-nesters were promised.

2. **`/copywriting-2.0`** — every spoken line run through the 3-test filter (can I visualize it, can I falsify it, could nobody else say it). What that pass caught and fixed in this batch specifically:
   - **The single most important catch in this batch: piece 227's alcohol-adjacent line.** The first draft of the Sweet Sixteen piece read "Nobody's carding anybody. Nobody needs to," which reads as an ID-checking/drinking reference — completely wrong for a piece whose entire subject is a minor's birthday party. Rewritten to "Nobody's rushing anybody home tonight. Nobody needs to," which keeps the same reassuring rhythm and cadence without the implication. Flagged again in Open Items.
   - **Fixed a logic error in piece 226's opening line.** The first draft said "the backpacks went back on the shelf" the same evening they were used for the first day of school, which doesn't actually happen and doesn't visualize cleanly. Rewritten to a 7am/6pm contrast (backpacks and porch photos that morning, none of it the parents' problem twelve hours later), which is concrete and checks out.
   - **Fixed an overclaim risk in piece 228's closing line.** "Nobody needs a ride home tonight" could read as implying an overnight-stay product this business doesn't offer. Rewritten to "Nobody's checking a clock tonight," which keeps the sentiment without implying a service that doesn't exist.
   - **Fixed template-repetition across three openers.** Pieces 230, 233, and 234 originally opened with "Public record," "Public notice," and "For the record" respectively — reusing, almost verbatim, short-opener phrasing Vol. 14 had already used twice ("Public record" for pieces 214 and 223) and once more ("Public notice" for piece 218). Re-drafted to "Confession," "Breaking," and "Ask four people..." so the *device* (a short, plain, easy opener) keeps working without the *exact wording* going stale across five pieces now built the same way.
   - **Fixed a confusing self-correction in piece 238.** The first draft had the line contradict itself mid-sentence ("Not KEE-mah like it looks. Well — actually, yes, KEE-mah") in a way that reads as a mistake rather than a joke once captions are on screen. Rewritten to state the correct pronunciation once, cleanly, before the joke about guests getting it wrong anyway.
   - **Fixed an internal number inconsistency in piece 239.** The working title claimed "forty jobs" while the script itself only ever states "eleven" recorded uses — the same category of inconsistency piece 215 (Vol. 14) was built specifically to avoid. Retitled to match the lower, actually-stated number instead of inflating the headline past what the piece itself claims.
