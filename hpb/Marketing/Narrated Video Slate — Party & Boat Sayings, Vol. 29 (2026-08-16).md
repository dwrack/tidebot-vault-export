## How this was written

`/copy-mentors` and `/copywriting-2.0` were both run fresh for this pass before a line of copy was drafted (not applied from memory).

**Copy-mentors framing:** Sugarman's Slippery Slide governs the shape of all 16 — line 1 is always the invented word said live and cold, under 8 words, no narrator setup; line 2 is the first proof the word is spreading (someone else repeats it); line 3 is the "how can that be" beat, the word crossing into crew/captain territory; line 4 is the payoff, flat and specific, never explained. Halbert's kitchen-table rule governed every line's register — nothing that reads like copy instead of something actually said on a dock survived a draft. Bencivenga's specificity rule governed every payoff: "and it stuck" was never allowed to survive a draft; every payoff names who said it, where, or exactly when.

**Copywriting-2.0, 3-test filter applied line by line, catches made during drafting:**
- **Narrator-summary catch, systemic.** Every first draft across the batch was checked for whether line 1 explained the joke ("a guest called the cooler 'the vault'") versus simply delivering the line live ("don't touch the vault till we're past the no-wake zone"). All 16 openers were rewritten to the live version — this was the single most common first-draft failure in the batch.
- **Vague-payoff catch on 452 and 461.** First draft of 452 closed "and everyone just started saying it" — flagged as unfalsifiable wallpaper, rewritten to name the captain announcing it himself on the next trip. 461's first draft closed "flip-flops are basically extinct on this boat" (an unfalsifiable exaggeration) — rewritten to a specific, checkable image: the deckhand announcing "boat shoes off past this line" over the mic.
- **Genericness catch, applied to the whole batch.** Every piece was checked for whether it could belong to any boat business — cut where it could. 449 anchors to the actual swim deck and no-wake zone. 452 and 453 anchor to the safety briefing and the no-wake zone specifically, both real operational moments on this boat, not invented set dressing. 461 anchors to the flip-flops-off-the-swim-deck reality of a party boat with a bar.
- **Falsifiability catch on 450.** First draft's payoff claimed the captain used the mispronunciation "every single trip since" — softened to "once, deadpan," since a permanent company-wide habit is a bigger claim than one script can support without actually happening.
- **Repetition catch across the batch.** First drafts of 456 and 463 both closed on "and that's just what we call it now" — flagged as a near-identical closer inside one volume. 463 was rewritten to the countdown-ritual detail instead, so the two payoffs land differently even though both are "word replaced the real name."
- **Safety posture check on 449.** The swim-deck jump stays a normal, supervised feet-first entry, no rail-climbing, matching every prior volume's posture on deck action.
