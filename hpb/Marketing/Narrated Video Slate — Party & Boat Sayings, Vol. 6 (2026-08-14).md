## Why these 16 topics

Vol. 5 closed by listing every mainstream boat-party bit as covered at least once: toasts, BYOB/cooler, dress code, dock farewells, aux cord, boat rivalry, bathroom FAQ, safety briefing, sunburn, group chats, phones-at-sunset. It also flagged three specific structural limits worth respecting here: the mock-formal-voice-interrupted device (used 7 times across pieces 01-80, retired as a repeatable device after piece 80's deliberate variation), boat-name rivalry lore (explicitly capped at two uses since Vol. 1), and the "vs. a competitor" positioning device (two uses, treated as tapped out).

Vol. 5 itself added weather policy, post-trip food, first-timer/regular contrast, two holidays (July 4th, Halloween), tipping, an opportunistic proposal piece, sea legs vs. seasickness, wildlife, winter regional pride, referral, crew-guest bond, boat turnover, first-date observation, headcount chaos, and a second corporate angle — and then named the least-mined remaining ground itself: birthday (touched once, piece 12), a second bachelor-party bit (touched once, piece 19), and a second nickname/mock-title piece (touched twice, pieces 27 and 37, from two different angles already).

This volume works that remaining ground plus territory nobody had opened yet:

- **A third and fourth holiday.** Only July 4th and Halloween existed. New Year's Eve and Valentine's Day are both genuinely untouched, both have obvious visual hooks (midnight countdown on the water, a public tour with an unsuspecting couple), and neither risks becoming a "third holiday piece that reads as a formula" because each has a distinct joke mechanism (a countdown ritual vs. a bystander-comedy premise).
- **Milestone occasions with zero prior pieces.** Graduation season and a returning-couple anniversary tradition — neither graduation nor "guests who come back on purpose, repeatedly" had been written yet.
- **A genuinely new inclusivity angle.** Every existing cooler/BYOB piece (six of them, per Vol. 5's own count) is about drinking. Nothing in 80 pieces addressed a guest who isn't drinking. That's a real gap and a real objection worth killing.
- **Pre-boarding and pre-departure beats.** The parking lot and the float-inflation scramble both happen before a single existing piece picks up the story — every prior piece starts at boarding or later.
- **The three "lightly touched, revisit from a different angle" categories Vol. 5 named directly:** a second birthday piece (the cooler-cost custom, not the crown gag), a second bachelor-party piece (a subverted-expectations quiet joke, not another toast), and a second nickname piece (captain-generated, in-the-moment, distinct mechanism from both prior nickname pieces).
- **New character beats nobody had written.** A reunion trip (old friends, long silence, distinct from every existing group-chat piece, which are all about pre-boat logistics or post-trip renaming), the drone guy (a specific character type never given his own piece despite two existing phone/photo pieces), a no-work-talk house rule (distinct from every prior corporate piece — neither an icebreaker arc nor a formal-memo bit), the crew's actual two-minute docking call (the companion beat to "Five More Minutes," which only covered the guest side of not wanting to leave), a small recurring boarding ritual (the high-five, never covered — prior boarding pieces are about seating and mock-announcements, not the literal welcome gesture), and a Monday-after hangover closer (the only piece in the series that jumps a full day past the trip itself).

None of the 16 below repeats a premise, punchline, or joke structure already used in pieces 01-80.

## How this was written

Same two-pass process as every prior volume, run fresh against this batch:

1. **`/copy-mentors`** — Halbert for the conversational, one-person-talking structure; Sugarman for the short-opener slippery-slide (short first line, then build); Bencivenga for specificity (La Croix and string cheese in a cooler beats "some people don't drink"; a half-inflated flamingo and an out-of-breath guest beats "people blow up floats"); and Blair Warren's four levers doing the persuasion work. Confirm a suspicion: piece 78's cousin-piece energy is echoed here in piece 87 — everyone who's watched a group inflate pool floats on a dock recognizes "two guests, zero floats, mild betrayal" instantly. Allay a fear: piece 85 exists specifically to tell a non-drinking guest "you'll fit in fine," the same reassurance function piece 67 served for first-timers. Throw a small rock at a shared target: piece 93 pokes at the reflexive "just a quick email" instinct everyone who's ever half-worked through a day off recognizes.
2. **`/copywriting-2.0`** — every spoken line run through the 3-test filter (can you visualize it, can you falsify it, could a competitor say it). What that pass caught and fixed in this batch specifically:
   - **Cut and rewritten:** piece 83's first draft ended on the obvious joke — tossing the graduation cap into the lake. Killed it. It's litter on the water, and airing a video that shows a guest doing that is a bad look regardless of how funny the beat reads. Rewritten so the cap comes out only for a photo and goes straight back in the bag — funnier because it's a twist on the expected gag, not just a version of it, and it doesn't imply HPB is fine with things going in the lake.
   - **Cut:** piece 81's first draft had confetti in the countdown visual. Piece 49 ("No Glitter, No Confetti, No Exceptions") already established that as a standing house rule three volumes ago. Kept the countdown energy, swapped confetti for noisemakers, so the b-roll doesn't contradict a rule the series itself wrote.
   - **Kept and built around:** "one cooler this trip had nothing but La Croix and string cheese" (piece 85) — passes all three tests. Visualizable, checkable against any actual cooler on any actual trip, and not something a bar or a rooftop lounge (the stated competition, per the Brand Story doc) would ever bother saying, because nobody there is thinking about what's in someone else's cooler.
   - **Flagged, not softened:** piece 84's "same two people, same week, every year" is a strong specific claim, exactly the kind of thing the 3-test filter rewards — except nobody has confirmed it's true. Rather than invent a fake specific (which would fail the falsifiability test the moment someone checked), the piece stays as written but is explicitly gated in Open Items below: verify with Connor before this is shot, or rewrite the line as forward-looking if no real repeat-anniversary guest exists on file.
   - **Kept, with a documented limit:** piece 93 gives Roger, the mock-PSA voice, a sixth appearance (prior: 11, 29, 40, 53, 72). That's more reuse of one register than any other character in the series. The premise (banning work talk, a rule) is genuinely new territory rather than another "explain a practical thing in a fake-official voice" bit — but the NOTES file for piece 93 flags this directly: a Vol. 7 Roger piece would need a different device, not another PSA, or the character starts reading as a tic rather than a bit.
