## How this was written

Same two-pass discipline as Vol. 1 and Vol. 2, run against this batch specifically:

1. **`/copy-mentors`** — Halbert and Sugarman for structure (one person talking, a short opener, momentum before the punchline), Bencivenga for specificity. Blair Warren's three levers still carry most of the weight: these lines land because they **confirm a suspicion** ("yeah, somebody always brings a bigger speaker than the boat has"), **allay a fear** (the life-jacket piece turns "will I look stupid" into a joke instead of dodging it), or **throw a friendly rock** at something every boat group has actually lived through (the group chat's fake "omw," the one friend glued to the cooler, the promise to do this every month that nobody keeps).
2. **`/copywriting-2.0`** — every spoken line run through the 3-test filter: can you visualize it, can you falsify it, could a competitor say it. What that pass caught in this batch:
   - Cut a first draft of piece 35 that read "life jackets are important for safety" — a fact, not a joke, and every boat company on earth can say it. Rewrote around the actual thing guests complain about ("doesn't match my outfit") and the actual thing the crew says back ("the one rule that isn't a joke") — visualizable, falsifiable by asking the crew, and it still lands the safety point without reading like a liability waiver.
   - Cut a version of piece 44 that had the deckhand saying guests "don't listen at all" during the safety briefing — not just unflattering, it undersells that the safety content actually gets delivered and heard. Rewrote so the joke is about *attention*, not compliance: everyone perks up hard at one specific line ("yes, there's a real bathroom"), which is funnier, true, and doesn't imply anyone's tuning out the parts that matter.
   - Cut "an amazing time was had by all" language from an early draft of piece 47 — same generic-superlative failure flagged in both prior volumes. Rebuilt around the checkable, specific behavior: nobody's ever actually booked the "let's do this every month" follow-up, and somebody's always quietly starting the next group chat anyway.
   - Kept and built piece 37 around a single named, ownable detail ("Ask 'Guac Girl.' She knows.") instead of a generic "everyone gets a nickname" line — same discipline as Vol. 1's cooler-of-deviled-eggs line and Vol. 2's "heels beat sneakers" line: the specific, slightly absurd, checkable detail is what makes it quotable.
   - No dollar figures anywhere in this batch either. The $59–69 vs. $55 discrepancy flagged in Vol. 1 (sourced from `Fresh Clip Batch — Aug 2026.md`) is still open — see Open Items below.

## On-screen text system (unchanged from Vol. 1 and Vol. 2)

Same three-beat structure, same reasoning: **Impact, yellow `#FFD400`, heavy black outline**, readable on busy sunlit boat footage. These are gag pieces meant to be watched sound-off in a feed, so the joke has to land from text alone.

1. **Hook** — funniest/most quotable line, biggest type, first 3–4 seconds, top third of frame
2. **Body** — one supporting line, doesn't repeat the VO word-for-word, carries the joke for sound-off viewers
3. **Tag/CTA** — last 1–2 seconds, small white text, `houstonpedalbarge.com`, present on the four pieces where the joke is also doing objection-handling or booking work (35, 43, 44, 48) and absent on the twelve pure-vibe pieces, same 4-of-16 ratio both prior volumes held so it doesn't read like every post is a pitch.

**Music:** light acoustic/ukulele pop bed, playful, no drop needed. **On-screen text:** Hook = "CONGRATULATIONS. YOU OUTRANK EVERYONE BUT ME." · Body = "the title's the whole gift" · no CTA.
