## How this was written

`/copy-mentors` and `/copywriting-2.0` were both run fresh for this pass before a line of copy was drafted.

**Copy-mentors framing:** Halbert governs the register — every line has to sound like something actually shouted over engine noise and wind on a moving boat, not written copy, so contractions, sentence fragments, and the "read it out loud" test drove every draft. Sugarman's Slippery Slide set the shape of all 16: line 1 is the real trigger, said plainly, no narrator explaining a chant is coming; line 2 is the exact moment the chant starts, with a name attached to who started it; line 3 is the chant spreading to people who don't fully know why; line 4 is the specific, visualizable resolution — never a vague "everyone laughed." Blair Warren's 27 words did work in three pieces specifically: 504 confirms a suspicion every group has about the one friend who turns any drink into an event, 510 allays the mild embarrassment of overreacting to nothing on a calm lake by making it a shared bit instead of a solo moment, and 501 lets the group throw an affectionate rock at the captain's total deadpan by chanting at a man who won't turn around.

**Copywriting-2.0, 3-test filter applied line by line, catches made during drafting:**
- **Vague-payoff catch, systemic first pass.** Early closers for 502 and 510 both leaned on "everyone thought it was hilarious" — flagged immediately as the wallpaper phrase this series keeps catching. 502 was rewritten to a concrete image (the captain got "a whole pay-per-view card" instead of a headcount); 510 was rewritten to a specific, repeatable beat (every bump after the first one gets "a standing ovation" on the calmest lake in Texas) rather than a generic laugh.
- **Falsifiability catch on 504 and 507.** 504's CTA draft originally read "tag the friend who does this constantly" — softened the implied claim by keeping it addressed to the viewer's own friend group rather than asserting it happens on every HPB trip. 507's first draft had the whole boat cutting off the toast; narrowed to "three more join" so the claim stays proportional to a fifteen-second clip instead of overclaiming full-boat unanimity.
- **Narrator-summary catch, checked across the batch.** Every opening line was tested for whether it explains that a chant is about to happen before it happens. All 16 pass — line 1 in every piece is the plain trigger (a real announcement, a real spill, a real bump), and the chant itself only appears starting in line 2, same discipline Vol. 31 flagged and fixed mid-draft.
- **Genericness catch, applied to the whole batch.** 497 and 499 anchor to HPB-specific texture (the BYOB cooler swap, a deckhand actually tying off a dock line) rather than generic "boat stuff." 505 anchors to the bachelorette shoe game specifically rather than a generic party game. 509 anchors to two HPB boats sharing the same lake at once, which only reads as real for an operator running more than one trip on the water simultaneously — flagged for shoot logistics below.
- **Mishearing/plausibility check, carried over from Vol. 31's convention but applied to chant triggers instead of relayed words this time.** Every "why would a crowd start chanting at THIS moment" beat was tested for whether the trigger is actually the kind of small thing that plausibly earns a bit on a loud, sun-drunk party boat — a countdown, a mishap, a bump, a blank stare, an overlong dramatic pause. Two early candidates were cut for failing this: a chant over someone tying their shoe (too small even for this device) and a chant over the boat's speed (too close to Vol. 31's "warp speed" piece 486 in subject matter).
- **Repetition catch across the batch.** First drafts of 500 and 510 both used the exact phrase "rollercoaster" to describe the reaction — kept in 510 only, since it's the stronger fit (a real bump), and rewritten out of 500 (a swim-stop cut, not a bump) to avoid two pieces leaning on the same reference in one volume.
