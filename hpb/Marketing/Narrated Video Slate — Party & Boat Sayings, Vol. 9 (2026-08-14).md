# Houston Pedal Barge — Narrated Video Slate: Party & Boat Sayings, Vol. 9

**Format modeled on:** Admire NOLA's narrated series (`Content/Narrated/01-poetic-NOTES.txt`, `Content/Script & Caption Bank.md`, `Content/Launch Slate - Daily Posts.md`) — timed script with a timestamp and bracketed visual on every line, one NOTES.txt per piece, voice/music/length header.

**Lane:** funny, loose, quotable — party sayings, boat sayings, toasts, deck-hand slang, bachelorette-party lines, captain lines, the running jokes of a group on a boat. Same lane as Vols. 1-8, deliberately the opposite of Admire NOLA's poetic swamp narration. Pedaling is never sold as a feature in this volume — it gets one direct, brand-mandated mention (piece 141, the objection-kill piece for nervous first-timers), delivered as reassurance, never as the pitch.

**Pass note — this is pass 26, and Vol. 9 in this specific narrated-slate lane.** 128 prior pieces exist on file (`Narrated Video Slate — Party & Boat Sayings`, Vols. 1-8, pieces 01-128). Every prior volume's NOTES.txt file and every prior volume's document was read in full before this batch was drafted — every title, every device, every voice-cast total, and every closing "Open items" note, specifically Vol. 8's own roadmap flag (Open Items #9): the one clearly under-mined category left across all 128 pieces was "anything involving actual weather beyond the captain's weather check — rain-day handling, wind calls, or the difference between a spring and late-summer trip." ## Why these 16 topics

- **Vol. 8's flagged weather roadmap, answered.** Piece 138, "August Boat, Spring Boat," is the seasonal-judgment piece Vol. 8 named as open ground — distinct from piece 21 (a policy reassurance about weather) and piece 65 (the written weather policy nobody reads). This one is about a crew member's read on how the same trip changes by season: what the crew watches for in March versus what they watch for in August. No policy, no reassurance — just the texture of the job changing with the calendar.
- **Three brand-new occasion types the series hadn't touched.** 128 prior pieces cover bachelorette, bachelor, birthday, corporate, anniversary, graduation, reunion, and six different holidays — but never a retirement party (135), never a proposal (131), and never what the industry is quietly calling a "freedom party" — a celebration of a divorce or a major life reset (144). All three are real, bookable occasion types this business could own before anyone else in Houston claims the positioning, and none of them existed anywhere in the prior 128 pieces.
- **One genuinely new structural device.** Piece 142, "What Guests Say vs. What They Mean," is the first two-column translation bit in the series — a paired say/mean joke structure distinct from every prior single-line deadpan list (04, 14, 73, 87, 100, 126) or single-voice character study. It also carries a new on-screen text treatment (a "THEY SAY: / THEY MEAN:" caption pair instead of the locked hook/body system) — flagged below as an experiment, not a system change.
- **A crew-culture piece that flips an existing device instead of repeating it.** Piece 90 ("The Captain Names Everyone By Sunset") is about the crew naming guests. Piece 143 flips that exact relationship: guests naming the crew, and the crew quietly living with it. Same emotional territory, opposite direction, so it reads as a sequel rather than a repeat.
- **Two pieces that needed a specific number to work, and got a real one.** Piece 136 ("The Only AC Out Here Is The Lake") uses the boat's actual cruising speed as the punchline mechanism rather than a vague "it's breezy" claim. Piece 134 ("The Banner Took Longer Than The Ride") does real math on screen — twenty minutes of hanging time against a two-hour trip is a sixth of the ride, not a rounded-off "forever." Both numbers are checkable, which is the whole point.
- **Three character studies built on a device none of the prior 128 used.** Piece 132 ("The One Photo Nobody's In") is about the person who takes every photo and appears in none of them — distinct from piece 26 (everyone's a photographer at golden hour, about phones coming out) and piece 92 (the drone guy, about a specific piece of gear). Piece 139 ("Submitted Three Weeks Early") is a second angle on playlist culture built on over-preparation and its collapse, distinct from the aux-cord fight (08), the same-three-songs joke (28), and the anthem moment (58). Piece 129 ("The Roast Before The Toast") introduces a genuine roast-then-toast tonal flip, distinct from every prior toast piece, which are all sincere from the first line.
- **A closer built on a real callback instead of a new joke.** Piece 137 ("Glitter Turns Up Three Trips Later") is the first piece in the series to directly reference an earlier piece's specific detail (the no-glitter house rule from piece 49) as its entire premise, rather than just sharing a lane with it.

None of the 16 below repeats a premise, punchline, or joke structure already used in pieces 01-128.

## How this was written

Same two-pass process as every prior volume, run fresh against this batch:

1. **`/copy-mentors`** — Sugarman's slippery-slide and short-opener rule shaped the openers on pieces 132, 136, and 144 (all start on a fragment: "Every group's got one," "People ask if the boat has air conditioning," "Attention aboard"). Schwartz's Sophistication-5 identification device drives piece 142 (the say/mean translation): the audience doesn't need convincing that guests say one thing and mean another, they need to recognize the exact lines they've said themselves. Bencivenga's proof-ladder logic shows up as real, checkable numbers doing quiet credibility work: "a sixth of it" in piece 134 is actual math (20 minutes against a 2-hour trip), "twelve knots" in piece 136 is a real speed a boat like this can hit, "ninety-four songs" in piece 139 is a deliberately non-round number that reads as observed rather than invented. Halbert's conversational-urgency register drives piece 141's dialogue structure (the quiet pull-aside at the dock, the direct answer, no hedging).
2. **`/copywriting-2.0`** — every spoken line run through the 3-test filter (can you visualize it, can you falsify it, could a competitor say it). What that pass caught and fixed in this batch specifically:
   - **Fixed a math error:** piece 134's fourth line originally read "the banner ate a fifth of it" — twenty minutes against a two-hour ride is a sixth, not a fifth. A joke built on a checkable number has to check out, so it's fixed to "a sixth of it."
   - **Fixed an internal-consistency problem:** piece 131's early draft called the other guests "strangers" without establishing why strangers would be aboard a private charter with the couple. Rewritten to specify the proposal happens on a *public* sunset tour, where other unrelated guests genuinely are strangers — the detail now has to be true for the joke to work, not just sound good.
   - **Cut an invented, unverifiable claim:** piece 138's closing line originally read "Twenty-three years and it still surprises me a little" — an invented tenure number with no basis in any brand document. Rewritten to "Been doing this awhile. Still catches me off guard some seasons," which keeps the character beat without asserting a fact nobody can back up.
   - **Cut a line that only made sense inside this document:** piece 142's fourth line originally closed with "see piece one twenty-nine" — an internal cross-reference to this volume's own piece numbering, which means nothing to an actual viewer who has never seen a NOTES.txt file. Rewritten to "Translation: it never once has," a self-contained punchline that doesn't depend on the audience having read this document.
   - **Kept and flagged as doing double duty:** piece 141 ("What I Tell The Nervous Ones") is built entirely around a direct, unhedged answer to the brand's #1 flagged objection territory (Brand Story & Values, Section 2 and 4: pedaling is optional, safety is the baseline not the selling point). It's written as a warm character moment first, but it's the clearest reassurance piece in the entire series to date, and it's the one piece this volume that mentions pedaling at all, exactly once, framed as "you don't have to."
   - **Kept, not softened:** piece 144 ("Freedom Party") names a real, sensitive occasion type. It's written to be warm and celebratory rather than mocking the underlying event, and it never claims the cruise has any actual legal role ("by the power vested in absolutely nobody" is explicit about that) — see Open Items for the consent bar this one needs before it can be shot.

## On-screen text system (unchanged from Vols. 1-8, with one flagged experiment)

Same locked treatment: **Impact, yellow `#FFD400`, heavy black outline**, chosen because it survives busy, sunny, high-motion boat footage where a thin white sans-serif disappears. Three-beat structure per piece — hook in the first 3-4 seconds, a body line mid-piece that doesn't repeat the VO word-for-word, and a CTA tag only on pieces built to convert.

**Experiment this volume:** piece 142 swaps the hook/body system for a two-line "THEY SAY: / THEY MEAN:" caption pair timed to each beat of the script, since the piece's whole structure is a paired translation rather than a single arc. This is the first on-screen text deviation in nine volumes — flagged for David's call on whether it works or whether the piece should be reformatted to fit the standard system instead.

**CTA ratio this volume: 4 of 16** (pieces 131, 135, 141, 144), matching the exact ratio locked across every prior volume. The other 12 close cold on purpose.
