## How this was written

Two-pass process, same as every prior volume, run fresh against this batch. `/copy-mentors` and `/copywriting-2.0` were both loaded fresh for this pass before any line was drafted.

1. **`/copy-mentors`** — master routing by piece:
   - **Schwartz's identification principle** drove the majority of this batch again — 243, 245, 246, 248, 249, 250, and 254 all assume the viewer already recognizes the moment (a bridge everyone yells under, a captain's directions nobody follows, a shoe in the water, a bend in the lake, a sunset genre-flip, spontaneous applause, a fumbled line toss) and just name it rather than arguing it's funny.
   - **Ogilvy's factual-specific-over-adjective principle** shaped 241, 242, and 244 directly — instead of a vague "come relax with us," 241 states the specific denial ("nobody's tracking steps"), 242 states the specific absence ("no menu, no maître d'"), and 244 states the specific cause ("Coast Guard rule, not ours") before the joke lands.
   - **Sugarman's slippery-slide short openers** drove 244 and 254 — each opens with a short, plain, easy-to-swallow factual line before the bit builds, continuing the device Vols. 13-15 used repeatedly. Wording was checked against every prior volume's opener list (see the catch below) rather than reused.
   - **Halbert's conversational directness** drove 241's and 256's closing lines — "No. Nobody's tracking steps. Nobody's counting anything." and "Same two hours. Completely different boat by the end." are flat, unhedged statements, no qualifier.
   - **Blair Warren's 27 words** (applied during drafting, from copywriting-2.0): piece 252 leans on "allay their fears" from the guest's own side — a mother doesn't have to choose between a nice morning and a two-hour wait for a table. Piece 253 leans on "confirm their suspicions" — that thirty years on the job actually does earn a real send-off, not just a card signed in the break room.

2. **`/copywriting-2.0`** — every spoken line run through the 3-test filter (can I visualize it, can I falsify it, could nobody else say it). What that pass caught and fixed in this batch specifically:
   - **The single most important catch in this batch: piece 241's original body line oversold pedaling as a benefit.** The first draft read "You'll get a light workout in without even trying," which markets pedaling as a fitness feature — a direct violation of the standing house rule (`Entity Canon.md`: "Never market as a workout or fitness experience," reinforced independently by this pass's own instructions). Rewritten to a flat denial: "No. Nobody's tracking steps. Nobody's counting anything." The piece now exists specifically to *undercut* the workout assumption rather than confirm it.
   - **Fixed an unconfirmed policy claim in piece 244.** The first draft opened "We don't allow glass on our boats," attributing a specific house policy to this operator without any document confirming it. Rewritten to "No glass allowed on the water. Coast Guard rule, not ours," which reframes the fact as a general maritime norm rather than a specific claim about this business's rules — still flagged in Open Items as needing confirmation before it's treated as documented.
   - **Fixed an ambiguous closing line in piece 247.** The first draft closed on "By the end, even grandma's throwing her hands up," which reads two ways — dancing or frustration — and fails the visualize test on a caption-only watch. Rewritten to "By the end, even grandma's favorite made the cut," which is unambiguous.
   - **Fixed a flat second beat in piece 253.** The first draft closed on "Nobody's asking what's next" alone, which is a feeling, not an image, and reads generic. Added a second, concrete beat — "No alarm set for tomorrow, either" — so the piece closes on an action rather than a mood.
   - **Fixed a cross-piece opener collision.** Piece 249 originally opened "Two hours in, the sun starts dropping," which duplicates the exact opening frame ("Two hours...") used to open piece 251 ("Two hours ago, half this boat were strangers"). Caught on a full read-through of this batch's sixteen openers against each other (not just against prior volumes). Piece 249 was rewritten to open "Right around sunset, the playlist always flips to country," which drops the shared frame entirely.
