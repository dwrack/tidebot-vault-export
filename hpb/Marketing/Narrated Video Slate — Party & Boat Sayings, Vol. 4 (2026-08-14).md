## How this was written

Same two-pass process as Vols. 1-3, run fresh on this batch rather than reused from the prior passes:

1. **`/copy-mentors`** — the whole batch went through as one request, routed primarily to Halbert (conversational, one person talking, not a broadcast) and Sugarman (short opener, slippery-slide momentum) for structure, with Bencivenga's specificity lens applied line by line. A few concrete fixes that pass made:
   - Piece 49's confetti line got a name attached — "we're still finding confetti in **Mama Tried's** floor vents" beats a generic "on the boat," because only this business has a boat called Mama Tried.
   - Piece 50's closer originally read "they're too good not to post," which is a sentence any tour company could say about any photo. Anchored it to Clear Lake specifically instead.
   - Cut a first draft of piece 62 that ended on "Come see the difference" — flagged as a straight ad-tag, not something a captain would actually say. Replaced with "we'll let the sunset make the rest of the argument," which ties the line to the visual instead of asking for the booking directly.
2. **`/copywriting-2.0`** — every line run through the 3-test filter (visualize it, falsify it, could a competitor say it). Two pieces needed real surgery:
   - Piece 58 ("The Anthem") opened with "you don't pick it, it picks you," which is a fine-sounding sentence any brand could bolt onto anything — beer, gyms, road trips. Rewritten to "it just happens, halfway through, every single trip," which is a claim you could actually check by being on the boat. The second line got the same treatment: "twenty-six people know every word" became "twenty-six people are somehow singing it off-key together" — off-key is the detail that makes it a real moment instead of a marketing sentence.
   - Piece 61 kept every line as drafted — "he has never once had one" passed clean on all three tests (you can picture it, it's a checkable claim among the friend group, and the mock-scientific "we track this now" framing isn't something a competitor bothers to say).
   - Everything else passed with minor tightening (cutting redundant setup lines, trading "no matter what" and other stock phrases for the specific detail already sitting in the line).

**One line to keep from this batch, if David wants a favorite:** piece 52's "Nobody's mad. It's a boat. Not a spreadsheet." — three flat sentences, no adjectives, and it's the kind of line that gets repeated back verbatim by a guest in a review, which is the actual test for whether a party-saying piece worked.

## On-screen text system (unchanged — same standard across all four volumes)

Same treatment locked in `Fresh Clip Batch — Aug 2026.md` and reused through every narrated volume: **Impact, yellow `#FFD400`, heavy black outline**. Three-beat structure per piece: Hook (biggest type, first 3-4 seconds, top third) → Body (supporting line mid-piece, doesn't repeat the VO word-for-word) → Tag/CTA (last 1-2 seconds, small white text, `houstonpedalbarge.com`, present only on pieces built to convert).

CTA present on 4 of 16 this volume (56, 62, 63, 64) — bachelorette-intent, objection-kill, and practical-FAQ pieces, plus the natural closer. The other 12 close cold on purpose, matching the ratio Vol. 1 established (4 of 16).
