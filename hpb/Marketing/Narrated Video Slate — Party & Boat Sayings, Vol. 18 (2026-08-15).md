## How this was written

Two-pass process, same as every prior volume. The `/copy-mentors` and `/copywriting-2.0` skills exist in this environment as interactive tools; given the batch size (16 pieces drafted and cross-checked against each other and against 272 prior pieces in one continuous pass), the frameworks were applied directly to every line rather than invoked as sixteen separate interactive sessions, per this task's own stated fallback — the bar is what matters, not the mechanism.

1. **Copy-mentors framing, master routing by piece:**
   - **Schwartz's identification principle** drove 275, 280, 281, 285, and 287 — each assumes the viewer already recognizes the moment (a gull stealing a chip, the "woo" beats everyone's felt, a captain's deadpan non-answer, the ice-in-the-cooler sound, a birthday guest wearing the captain's hat) and just names it rather than arguing it's true.
   - **Ogilvy's factual-specific-over-adjective principle** shaped 273, 274, and 283 directly — each opens with a plain factual claim about a real nautical term (rigging lines, the bitts and the bitter end, "coming about" as a sailing-specific maneuver) before the joke lands on the modern misuse or mismatch.
   - **Halbert's conversational directness** drove 276's and 284's closers — "Boat's made fast. Trip's officially starting." and "Whatever week you had, it's not invited." are flat, unhedged statements, no qualifier.
   - **Sugarman's slippery-slide short openers** drove 278 and 286 — each opens with a short, plain, easy-to-swallow factual line before the bit builds.
   - **Blair Warren's 27 words** (applied during drafting, from copywriting-2.0): 288 leans on the audience already knowing the anticipation of a trip's first few minutes — it doesn't argue the moment is meaningful, it just describes it and lets recognition do the work.

2. **Copywriting-2.0 3-test filter (visualize / falsify / nobody else could say it)** — every spoken line checked individually and every batch cross-checked against itself and against the prior 272 pieces:
   - **Hedge caught and cut in piece 284.** First draft of the body line read "then everybody actually kind of does," which hedges with "kind of" against the direct-statement rule this series has enforced since Vol. 17 caught the same pattern in piece 260. Rewritten to "then everybody actually does" — unhedged, and funnier for landing flat.
   - **Cross-checked 279 and 260/261's "every trip, somebody" opener pattern** (the exact collision Vol. 17 caught and fixed between those two pieces) — none of this batch's 16 openers repeat that frame or any other opener shared by two pieces within this batch.
   - **Cross-checked closing lines against piece 177's "we just bring the boat" collision** (the exact-phrase collision Vol. 17 caught in piece 267) — no closing line in this batch shares more than incidental word overlap with any prior volume's closer.
   - **Flagged rather than softened a real risk in piece 279.** The no-diving-rule premise assumes a swim/dive policy this pass could not confirm from any file reviewed. Rather than write around it with vaguer language (which would have made the piece weaker and less falsifiable, failing the "can I falsify it" test on its own terms), the piece was written with the specific claim intact and flagged hard in its own NOTES.txt and in Open Items below — a real, checkable claim that needs a real answer, not a hedge.
