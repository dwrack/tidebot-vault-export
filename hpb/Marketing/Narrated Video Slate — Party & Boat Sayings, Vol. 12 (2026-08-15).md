## How this was written

Two-pass process, same as every prior volume, run fresh against this batch:

1. **`/copy-mentors`** — master routing by piece:
   - **Bencivenga's proof hierarchy** is the backbone of 178, 181, 182, 183, and 184. Each swaps an adjective claim ("we're affordable," "we're popular," "we're easy to book") for a real, checkable specific — a bar-tab comparison, an average booking size, a booking-channel percentage, a lead-time median, a star rating and review count. Tier 2/tier 6 proof, not tier 1 assertion, the same discipline Vol. 11 established with the Subchapter T and 26-person pieces.
   - **Schwartz's identification principle** drove 177, 187, 188, and 192 — none of these argue that BYOB disbelief, "ten more minutes," a confetti mishap, or hiding a birthday number are funny. They assume the viewer already recognizes the moment and just needs to see it named and played straight.
   - **Sugarman's slippery-slide short openers** drove 179 ("No. This is a lake that leads to a bay.") and 185 ("There are two kinds of people who book us."). Both open with a plain, short, easy-to-swallow line before the bit builds.
   - **Halbert's conversational directness** drove 186 — "That's not a suggestion. That's the boat." is a flat, unhedged line in the same register as Halbert's habit of answering the reader's next question before they ask it.
   - **Blair Warren's 27 words** (applied during drafting, from copywriting-2.0): piece 181 leans on "allay their fears" — that a small group doesn't need to justify booking a whole boat. Piece 191 leans on "confirm their suspicions" (that weekday trips really are less crowded) and "let them off the hook" (nobody has to defend picking a Tuesday). Piece 190 leans on "encourage their dreams" (an easier version of a holiday tradition) and "justify their failures" (not hosting is framed as the smart move, not a letdown).

2. **`/copywriting-2.0`** — every spoken line run through the 3-test filter (can you visualize it, can you falsify it, could nobody else say it). What that pass caught and fixed in this batch specifically:
   - **Fixed an over-claim:** piece 179's first draft had the Old Salt say flatly "there are no sharks in this water at all." That's not defensible — Galveston Bay, which the same broader waterway connects to, can have bull sharks further out. Rewritten to distinguish Clear Lake itself (where the boats run) from the bay further out, and softened the title itself to "(Probably)" rather than asserting a hard zero.
   - **Fixed a specificity failure:** piece 182's first draft said "almost everybody books online now" with no number. Replaced with the real FareHarbor figure (82%, rounded to "nine out of ten" for spoken cadence — flagged in Open Items as a rounding choice worth double-checking against the exact 82% before this airs).
   - **Cut a dollar figure:** piece 178's first draft stated "$600 to $800" directly, pulled straight from Brand Story Section 3. Removed to keep this volume consistent with the no-dollar-figure rule every prior volume has followed — see Open Items for the recommendation to formally clear that number for future use.
   - **Fixed a consent/plausibility problem:** piece 189's first draft named a specific number of years ("twenty years on the water") attached to a specific unnamed deckhand, implying a real biography. Since no real crew tenure has been confirmed for any individual crew member (Brand Story Section 7 is still empty), this is flagged in Open Items as an illustrative number, not a confirmed fact, the same discipline applied to piece 169's dialect caveat in Vol. 11.
   - **Kept two bits honestly flagged as invented illustrations, not documented fact:** piece 188's confetti-cannon incident and piece 192's "sash almost gets thrown in the water" beat are both told as obvious runners-up to a real house-rule joke, not literal incident reports. Both flagged again in Open Items.
