<h2>Houston has a sunset. Most Houstonians have never seen it.</h2>
<p>Not the real one, anyway. The view from your car on 45 doesn't count, and neither does the sliver of orange between two Midtown towers. The real Houston sunset happens about 30 minutes southeast of downtown, where Clear Lake opens into Galveston Bay and there's nothing between you and the horizon but water. Big sky, no buildings, the whole show.</p>
<p>That's where we run our boats. A sunset cruise in Houston means driving to Seabrook, a few minutes from the Kemah Boardwalk, stepping onto a 30-foot party barge with your cooler and your playlist, and spending 1 hour 45 minutes on open water while the sky does its thing. The <a href="https://houstonpedalbarge.com/houston-party-barge-cruise/">Clear Lake party boat cruise</a> runs from $55 a person on public trips, or $800 for a private charter that holds up to 26 people. This post covers the timing, because catching the actual sunset takes about two minutes of planning, and most people skip it.</p>

<h2>Which time slot actually catches the sunset</h2>
<p>Here's the part people get wrong. They book a 5pm cruise in July, expecting a sunset, and dock an hour and a half before the sun even thinks about dropping. Texas summer sunsets are late.</p>
<p>The cruise runs 1 hour 45 minutes, so the rule of thumb is simple: <strong>pick the departure that starts 60 to 90 minutes before sundown.</strong> You'll get golden hour on the water, the sunset itself mid-trip, and the first stretch of dusk with the party lights on as you come back in. Rough sunset times for the Houston area by month:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>June and July:</strong> sun drops between 8:15 and 8:30pm. The latest evening slot is the only one that catches it. Bonus: the trip starts after the worst of the heat breaks.</li>
<li><strong>August:</strong> 8:15 sliding down to about 7:45 by Labor Day. Late evening departures still win.</li>
<li><strong>September:</strong> 7:30ish and falling fast. The slot that felt too early in July is now the sunset slot.</li>
<li><strong>October:</strong> right around 7:00, then under 6:45 by Halloween. Earlier evening trips catch it, and honestly October has the best sunset weather of the year out here. Less haze, cooler air, water like glass.</li>
<li><strong>November through February:</strong> sunset lands between 5:20 and 6:00pm, so late afternoon trips become full sunset cruises.</li>
</ul>
<p>Times shift week to week, so glance at your phone's weather app for the exact sundown on your date, then count backward. If you're between two slots, take the later one. A cruise that ends in the dark beats one that ends an hour before the color shows up.</p>

<h2>Photo tips from people who watch this every night</h2>
<p>The crew shoots group photos on basically every evening trip, so take their advice:</p>
<ul>
<li>Golden hour starts about 45 minutes before sundown. That's the window for the big group shot, faces lit, water glowing. Don't wait for the sunset itself to start shooting.</li>
<li>Shoot toward the west over the bay for the sky, then turn around. The light hitting people's faces from the other direction is the better portrait.</li>
<li>Stay out on deck after the sun drops. Blue hour, the 20 minutes after sunset, is when the party lights pop and the sky goes purple. Half the best shots in our reviews are from that stretch.</li>
<li>Hand your phone to the crew for the all-of-us shot. Organizers are missing from their own party photos at every other venue. Not here.</li>
<li>If it's hazy, don't sulk. Gulf haze makes the sun a giant red ball on the way down. Some of the wildest photos all year come from "bad" sky days.</li>
</ul>
