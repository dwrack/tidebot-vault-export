<h2>Three very different boats walk into one search bar</h2>
<p>Type "paddle boat Houston" into Google and you get three completely different afternoons. One is a swan-shaped pedal boat at Hermann Park. <h2>Option 1: The swan paddle boats at Hermann Park</h2>
<p>This is the classic. Hermann Park rents pedal boats on McGovern Lake, right in the Museum District, and they're exactly what you remember from childhood. You and one to three other people climb in, you pedal with your legs, and the boat goes where your legs take it. Slowly.</p>
<p>Honestly, they're great for what they are. A date that costs less than a movie. A toddler who wants to chase ducks. A Sunday where you're already at the zoo and have 30 minutes to kill. If that's your day, go to Hermann Park. Don't call us. We'd be a terrible fit and we'll tell you so on the phone.</p>
<p>What they're not: a party. No music, no coolers, no group of 20. The boats hold a few people and your quads do all the work.</p>

<h2>Option 2: Pedal pubs and cycle boats</h2>
<p>The second thing people mean is the pedal pub, sometimes called a party bike or cycle boat. You've seen the street version rolling through Midtown or downtown: a long bench of people pedaling a giant bar-on-wheels between stops. Some cities have the water version too, where the group's pedaling actually powers the boat.</p>
<p>Here's the honest read. Pedal pubs are fun if your group genuinely wants the workout as part of the bit. But know what you're signing up for, because the pedaling isn't optional on those. It's the engine. If half your crew shows up in sandals expecting to relax, they're about to spend 90 minutes being yelled at to pedal harder in Houston humidity. We've heard the stories from guests who tried one first.</p>
<p>This matters because our domain is houstonpedalbarge.com, and people see the word "pedal" and assume we're that. We're not. Which brings us to door number three.</p>
