<h2>Yes, You Really Bring Your Own Everything</h2>
<p>The number one question we get before booking, by a mile: "Wait, we can actually bring our own drinks?" Yes. Drinks and food. That's the whole setup. A BYOB boat party in Houston means your cooler comes aboard, your snacks come aboard, and there's no bar tab, no drink minimum, no catering package, and no markup waiting at the dock. You shop at H-E-B prices and party at sunset-over-Galveston-Bay views.</p>
<p>Houston Party Barge runs two 30-foot Coast Guard certified barges out of Seabrook, minutes from the Kemah Boardwalk. Roofs, bathrooms, Bluetooth speakers, party lights. And before anyone asks: the boats are fully motorized and a captain drives the whole time. The pedal stations on board are optional fun, not the engine. Nobody has to pedal anything. Now, the BYOB details, because doing this right makes the difference between a good cruise and a great one.</p>
<h2>What to Bring on a BYOB Boat Party</h2>
<p>After hundreds of cruises on our <a href="https://houstonpedalbarge.com/houston-party-barge-cruise/">Clear Lake party boat cruise</a>, here's what the groups who nail it show up with:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>A cooler with more ice than you think you need.</strong> This is Houston in summer. Double the ice. Then add a bag.</li>
<li><strong>Cans, not glass.</strong> Beer, seltzers, canned cocktails, canned wine. Glass doesn't come aboard, full stop, and honestly canned everything is better on a boat anyway.</li>
<li><strong>Water.</strong> The groups that bring a case of water are the groups still going strong at minute 100. Heat plus drinks plus no water is the rookie mistake.</li>
<li><strong>Snacks that survive a boat.</strong> Charcuterie boards are weirdly the signature move out here. We've watched bachelorette groups walk down the dock carrying boards that belong on Instagram. Chips, wraps, fruit trays, and sandwich platters all work great too.</li>
<li><strong>Koozies.</strong> A drink in the Texas sun has about eight good minutes without one.</li>
<li><strong>Your playlist, already built.</strong> Bluetooth speakers are on the boat. Don't be the group arguing over the aux at the dock.</li>
</ul>
<h2>What to Leave in the Car</h2>
<p>Short list, but it matters. <strong>No glass.</strong> That's the hard rule, and it's about bare feet on a boat deck, not vibes. Decant the wine into cans or a plastic carafe before you arrive. Past that, use judgment: skip anything that needs a kitchen, anything that melts into soup by minute ten (looking at you, chocolate-covered strawberries in July), and the second giant cooler that "somebody might want." One well-packed cooler per 8 to 10 people covers it.</p>

<p>This is where BYOB stops being a fun perk and starts being the reason groups pick us. A group of 20 at a Houston bar spends $600 to $800 easy. Cover, $14 cocktails, rounds nobody remembers ordering, 20% gratitude added automatically. That's before anyone opens a rideshare app.</p>
<p>Same group on our boat: a private charter is $800 flat for up to 26 guests, about $31 a head, locked in with a $150 deposit. The drinks? Maybe $150 of beer, seltzers, and snacks from the grocery store, split 20 ways. You're throwing the same party for a fraction of the spend, except this one comes with open water, a sunset, and a captain instead of a crowded patio on Washington Ave. Public tickets start at $55 a person if your crew is smaller. Either way, the bar tab line item just doesn't exist.</p>
<h2>Drinks That Hold Up in the Texas Heat</h2>
<p>Not everything drinks well at 95 degrees on the water. What works out here:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Light beer and Mexican lagers.</strong> The classics are classics for a reason. Add limes.</li>
<li><strong>Seltzers and ranch waters.</strong> Built for exactly this. Half the coolers on our dock are seltzer by volume.</li>
<li><strong>Canned cocktails.</strong> Margaritas, palomas, espresso martinis if your group is ambitious. No mixing, no spills, no glass problem.</li>
<li><strong>Frozen water bottles.</strong> Double duty as ice packs, then drinkable by the back half of the cruise. Old Gulf Coast trick.</li>
</ul>
<p>Whiskey-forward stuff and anything that wants to be room temperature tends to sit untouched. Save it for after, maybe at Cabo Bar and Grill next door to our dock.</p>

<p>BYOB doesn't mean unsupervised. Every cruise runs with a licensed captain on a Coast Guard certified boat, and the captain has full authority on safety. They drive, they watch the water and the weather, and yes, they'll slow someone down if it's needed. It almost never is. Cruises run 1 hour and 45 minutes, ages 8 and up, and the format kind of self-regulates: you're there for the sunset and the group, not a 6-hour bender. That balance is a big part of why we're sitting at 4.9 stars across 400+ reviews. People party hard and still get home with a story instead of a problem.</p>
