## FAQ SCHEMA

**Q1:**
```
Are there boat tours of Kemah Boardwalk?
```
**A1:**
```
Yes. You can see the Kemah Boardwalk by water on a captained party barge cruise out of Clear Lake in Seabrook, Texas. The trip runs 1 hour 45 minutes, holds up to 26 guests, and passes the boardwalk and open bay. A licensed captain drives, you bring your own drinks and food, and the boat has a roof, bathroom, and sound system.
```

**Q2:**
```
How much does a Kemah boat ride cost?
```
**A2:**
```
Public tickets run roughly $59 to $69 per person depending on the cruise. A private charter for a full group of up to 26 starts around $800 with weekday discounts and a small deposit to hold the date. Both options last 1 hour 45 minutes on Clear Lake near the Kemah Boardwalk.
```

**Q3:**
```
Where do Kemah boat tours leave from?
```
**A3:**
```
The boats launch from the Clear Lake side near Seabrook, just minutes from the Kemah Boardwalk. You park at the launch dock, the captain runs through safety, and you push off into open water. The whole cruise stays on Clear Lake and Galveston Bay with boardwalk and skyline views.
```

```
What is the best time of day for a Kemah boat tour?
```

**A4:**
```
Sunset is the standout. Golden hour over Galveston Bay lights up the water and the Kemah Boardwalk, and the group photos basically take themselves. Sunset slots fill fastest in summer, so book a weekend trip ahead rather than same-day. Midday cruises run cooler under the boat's roof if you want shade and open afternoon light.
```

## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
Everybody does Kemah the same way. Park, walk the boardwalk, ride the Ferris wheel, eat at the seafood place, drive home. Fine. But you just looked at the water the whole time without getting on it.

The boardwalk is built right on Clear Lake. The best view of Kemah isn't from the boardwalk. It's from a boat looking back at it. Most visitors never get that angle, and locals who've lived here for years usually haven't either.

Here's how to actually see Kemah by water, without renting a yacht or learning to captain anything.

## The view Kemah visitors never get

From the water, Kemah looks like a different place. The boardwalk lights stretch along the shore, boats drift across Clear Lake, and the open bay opens up behind you toward Galveston. At sunset it goes gold, and that's the part nobody gets walking the pier.

A captain on one of our boats keeps a running count of guests who say some version of "I've been to Kemah a hundred times and never seen it like this." It's basically every trip. That reaction is the reason to go.

## What a Kemah boat tour actually is

It's a **captained party barge** that launches from the Clear Lake side near Seabrook, minutes from the Kemah Boardwalk. Up to 26 people, a roof for shade, a bathroom on board, a Bluetooth sound system, and a USCG-certified captain doing the driving. You bring drinks and food. The trip runs **1 hour 45 minutes** of open water and boardwalk views.

Quick note since the boats have pedal stations: you don't have to pedal. The boat is motorized. The captain drives. The pedals are an optional thing to mess with for a few minutes if you feel like it. That's it.

## Boardwalk by foot vs. Clear Lake by water

Both are good. They're just good at different things. Here's the honest split:

- **Boardwalk by foot** wins for rides, restaurants, the aquarium, and walking around with kids during the day.
- **Clear Lake by water** wins for the actual view, your own group, your own music, and your own drinks.
- The boardwalk gets crowded on weekends. The water doesn't.
- On foot you're on Kemah's schedule. On a boat you're on yours.
- For a birthday, bachelorette, or group hang, the boat is the thing people remember. The boardwalk is the warm-up.

Smart move: do both in one day. Hit the boardwalk in the afternoon, then catch a sunset cruise and watch the lights come on from the water.

## Best time to go

Sunset, every time. Golden hour over Galveston Bay does all the work, and your phone fills up with photos you didn't have to try for. Those slots book out first in summer, so grab a weekend sunset ahead of time instead of trying same-day.

If you want shade and a cooler ride, a midday cruise under the roof is the call. Either way, summer afternoons in Houston are hot, so plan for water and sunscreen.

## What to bring

Keep it simple:

- A cooler with your drinks (BYOB) and any food you want, full spreads welcome.
- Sunscreen and sunglasses.
- A phone or speaker connection for the playlist.
- Flat shoes or sandals are easiest. Heels are fine too, it's a stable boat.

## Who it's for

Groups, mostly. Bachelorette parties, birthdays, family reunions, work crews that want out of the conference room, out-of-town friends you're trying to impress. Ages 8 and up. The boat bends to the group, so a loud celebration and a chill catch-up cruise both work.

## How to book a Kemah cruise

Two options. Public tickets per person if you're rolling with a few people, or a **private charter** for the whole boat if you've got a group. Private charters start around $800 for up to 26 guests, with a deposit to lock the date and weekday discounts available. For a group, a private boat with your own drinks usually beats a Kemah restaurant tab anyway.

So next time you're headed to Kemah, don't just walk past the water. Get on it.
```
