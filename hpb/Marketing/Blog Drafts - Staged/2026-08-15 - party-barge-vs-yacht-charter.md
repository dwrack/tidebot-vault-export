## CONTENT (Markdown body)

*Written by the Houston Party Barge dock crew, Seabrook, TX*

Somebody in your group chat is going to say "let's just get a yacht." It sounds right. Yachts sound like the Houston flex you've been waiting for. Then someone actually calls for a quote and the number lands like a bucket of ice water.

**A yacht charter and a private party barge are not the same purchase, even though they both get you on Galveston Bay.** One's built for formal. One's built for loud. Here's the real breakdown before you commit a deposit to either.

## The Yacht Sounds Better on Paper

Yacht charters have a real appeal. A crewed boat, a catered bar, white tablecloths if you want them. For a wedding rehearsal dinner or a client dinner where you need the room to feel a certain way, that's not nothing. It's the whole reason yacht charter companies exist on Clear Lake and Galveston Bay.

The catch shows up fast once you start pricing it against your actual guest list.

## What a Yacht Charter Actually Costs

A private yacht out of Kemah or Galveston Bay typically starts around $1,500 for a few hours. That number climbs once you add crew gratuity, fuel surcharges, and a catering minimum, and most of those extras aren't in the headline price you see online. By the time the quote email lands, a lot of groups are looking at $2,000 to $3,000 for a Saturday evening.

Compare that to a private charter on Delta Dream or Mama Tried: $800 to $950 flat, up to 26 guests, one $150 deposit, no gratuity math, no catering minimum. [internal link: how the pricing breaks down → post-04-party-boat-cost] walks through the full numbers.

## Where a Yacht Genuinely Wins

We're not going to pretend a barge beats a yacht at every job. If you're proposing, closing a deal, or hosting a formal rehearsal dinner where you need a quieter room and a catered menu, a yacht is the right tool. Nobody wants BYOB coolers at a proposal.

Yachts also handle bigger formal guest lists better once you're past 30 or 40 people and need actual dining service, not just a party deck.

## Where the Party Barge Wins

Everything that isn't the scenario above. Bachelorette parties, birthdays, team outings, a Saturday with your closest 18 people who just want music, drinks, and a sunset. On a formal yacht, that same group is paying for white-tablecloth service they're not using and probably not even wearing the right shoes for.

**BYOB is the real difference maker.** [internal link: bring your own cooler instead → post-06-byob-boat] means your group buys drinks at grocery store prices instead of catered bar prices, and there's no minimum spend hovering over the night. We had a 22-person bachelorette group last spring that had gotten a yacht quote first at $2,400 with the bar package included. They booked our barge for $950 and put the difference into a hotel suite for the weekend instead.

## The Math for a Group of 20

Run it straight. A yacht charter for 20 people, with a bar package, lands somewhere between $2,000 and $2,800 depending on the operator and the season. A private barge charter for the same 20 people is $800 to $950, BYOB, no extras. That's roughly a third of the cost for a group that doesn't need white tablecloths anyway.

If your group wants formal, pay for formal. If your group wants a party, don't pay formal prices for it.

## Pick the Boat, Not the Brochure

Before you book either one, ask two questions. How many guests, and does this event need to feel formal or does it need to feel like a party. If the answer is formal and 30+, call a yacht broker. If the answer is party and under 26, [internal link: book the private charter → /houston-party-barge-cruise/] and put the savings toward the open bar you're bringing yourself.
