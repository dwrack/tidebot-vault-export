## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
Picture the company holiday party. Hotel ballroom. Beige carpet. A DJ nobody asked for, a cash bar with a line, and half the team sneaking out by 8. You spent a real budget and people still talk about how they'd rather have stayed home.

There's a better version. It floats.

Houston has a waterfront, and most companies plan their entire event calendar like it doesn't. Conference room with stale donuts for the quarterly. Same ballroom every December for the holiday party. Then they wonder why nobody's fired up about "team culture." The fix isn't a trust fall. It's getting your team out of the building and onto Clear Lake.

## The ballroom problem

A ballroom is a neutral box. That's the whole issue. It doesn't do anything for you, so you have to manufacture the energy with decor, an open bar, and forced activities. By the time you've paid for the room, the catering markup, the bar minimum, and the AV, you've spent a fortune to put your team somewhere they'd never choose to be.

A boat does the opposite. The setting carries the night. Open water, the Houston skyline, the Kemah Boardwalk lights, golden hour over Galveston Bay. You don't have to manufacture anything. People relax because they're actually somewhere worth being.

## Why the water beats the venue

Here's what changes when you move a corporate event onto a private charter:

- **It's your boat.** Private charter means just your team, no strangers from another company's outing.
- **No bar tab spiral.** BYOB means you control the drink budget instead of watching a venue tab balloon.
- **People show up.** "Holiday party on a boat" gets a yes. "Holiday party in the Marriott ballroom" gets a maybe.
- **The photos are real.** Skyline behind the team beats a step-and-repeat banner every time.
- **No itinerary to babysell.** No assigned seating, no program. The captain drives, the team hangs out.
- **It scales.** Up to 26 per boat, and we can run two side by side for bigger companies.

A captain told me about a work crew last fall, a pretty buttoned-up group, who spent the first ten minutes standing around polite and stiff. By the time the skyline lit up, they were taking goofy group photos and arguing about the playlist. That's the shift you're paying for, and a ballroom can't manufacture it.

## What it actually costs

A private charter starts around **$800 for up to 26 guests**, with weekday discounts and a small deposit to hold the date. Because it's BYOB and BYO food, you skip the two things that quietly wreck event budgets: the per-drink bar markup and the catering surcharge.

Run the math against a downtown venue for the same headcount. A room rental, a bar minimum, and a catering contract usually clears your boat cost before anyone's had a second drink. For most teams under 26, the boat is the cheaper option and the better one.

## What's handled and what you bring

**We handle:**

- A USCG-certified captain and crew.
- The boat, the motor, the driving (the team powers nothing).
- Roof for shade or weather, a bathroom on board, a Bluetooth sound system, party lights for evening cruises.
- The 1 hour 45 minute route on Clear Lake past Kemah.

**You bring:**

- Drinks. Coolers welcome.
- Food or catering. Trays, spreads, whatever the team likes.
- A playlist.
- The crew.

That's the whole arrangement. No menu negotiations, no AV rider, no seating chart.

## Big team? Run two boats

One boat caps at 26 for safety and comfort, which we don't budge on. But there are two boats, **Delta Dream** and **Mama Tried**, and they can run the same slot together. That gets you up to 52 people on the water at once, staying near each other the whole cruise. For a department holiday party or a company-wide thing, that's the move.

## When to book (sooner than you think)

This is the part people get wrong. Fall and winter holiday dates, especially Friday and weekend evenings from October through December, book out early. If you're reading this in summer, you're right on time. Wait until November and the good slots are gone, and you're back to the ballroom by default.

Booking two boats for one date needs even more lead time, so if you've got a big headcount, start now.

## Lock the date

A small deposit holds your slot, and you settle the rest before the cruise. Pick your date, decide one boat or two, and reserve it before someone else takes the evening you wanted.

Your team outing doesn't have to suck. Houston has a waterfront. Use it.
```
