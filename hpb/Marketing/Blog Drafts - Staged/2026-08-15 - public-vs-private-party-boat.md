## FAQ SCHEMA

**Q1:**
```
What is the difference between a public and private party boat ticket?
```
**A1:**
```
A public ticket puts you on a scheduled cruise alongside other small groups who bought individual tickets, starting at $55 to $69 per person. A private charter reserves the entire boat for your group only, up to 26 guests, for a flat $800 to $950.
```

**Q2:**
```
How many people do you need to book a private charter?
```
**A2:**
```
There's no strict minimum, but private charters make the most financial sense once your group hits 10 to 12 people. Below that, public tickets are usually cheaper per person. Above it, the flat charter rate starts beating individual ticket prices.
```

**Q3:**
```
Will I be sharing the boat with strangers on a public cruise?
```
**A3:**
```
Yes. Public cruises are scheduled departures open to anyone who books a ticket, so you'll likely share the boat with other small groups, couples, or families. A private charter guarantees the whole boat is only your group.
```

**Q4:**
```
Is a private charter worth it for a small group?
```
**A4:**
```
For groups under 8 to 10 people, public tickets are usually the better value since you're not paying for capacity you won't use. Once a group grows past that, a private charter starts costing less per person while also giving the group full control of the music and schedule.
```

## CONTENT (Markdown body)

*Written by the Houston Party Barge dock crew, Seabrook, TX*

We field this one at the dock, in DMs, and in half our booking emails: "Wait, do we get the whole boat or not?" Fair question. It's actually the single most common thing people ask us before they book, more than pricing, more than the weather policy. So let's clear it up properly.

**Public tickets and private charters are two different products on the same boat, and picking wrong costs you either money or privacy.** Here's how to tell which one your group actually needs.

## The Question We Get More Than Any Other

Across thousands of booking emails, "can we get the boat for just our group" comes up more than anything else we track. More than pricing questions. More than "what if it rains." People assume every cruise is private by default, and it isn't. That mismatch is exactly why this post exists.

Short version: public means shared, private means yours. Everything else follows from that one distinction.

## What Public Tickets Actually Get You

Public cruises are scheduled departures. You buy individual seats, starting at $55 to $69 a person, and you're on the boat alongside other groups who did the same thing. Couples on a date night, a family of four, a group of six friends, all sharing the same 1 hour 45 minute cruise.

Public works great when your group is small, say 2 to 8 people, and you don't need the whole boat. You still get the sunset, the music, the open water, just at a lower price point since you're not paying for capacity your group isn't using.

## What a Private Charter Actually Gets You

A private charter is $800 to $950 flat, and it reserves Delta Dream or Mama Tried entirely for your group, up to 26 guests. No strangers, no sharing the aux cord, no negotiating who controls the playlist. [internal link: the full pricing breakdown → post-04-party-boat-cost] has the exact numbers, but the short version is a $150 deposit locks the date and the rest is due later.

This is the move for bachelorette parties, birthdays, and [internal link: booking a private team outing → post-10-team-building]. Anything where the whole point is that it's your group's day, not a boat you're borrowing space on.

## The Break-Even Number

Here's the math nobody spells out. Public tickets at $55 a person cost a group of 15 roughly $825, close to what a private charter costs outright, except you're still sharing the boat. Once a group crosses about 10 to 12 people, the private charter usually wins on both price and privacy at the same time.

Under 8 people, public tickets are almost always the smarter buy. You're not paying for 18 empty seats you don't need.

## The One Thing People Regret Not Asking

The regret we hear most isn't about price. It's groups who booked public tickets for a bachelorette party assuming it would feel private, then got surprised when a family with two kids boarded next to them. Nothing wrong with that family. Just not what the bride pictured.

If any part of your plan involves "we want this to feel like our own party," ask for private. If you're fine sharing the water with strangers and want the lower per-person price, public tickets do the job just fine.

## Book the Right One

Count your group, decide if privacy matters more than price, and book accordingly. Under 8 and don't care who's on the boat: grab public tickets. Over 10 and want the whole thing to yourselves, [internal link: book your cruise → /houston-party-barge-cruise/] as a private charter and skip the surprise. Either way, you're still getting the same sunset over Clear Lake.
