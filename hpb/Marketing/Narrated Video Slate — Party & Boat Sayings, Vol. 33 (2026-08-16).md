## How this was written

`/copy-mentors` and `/copywriting-2.0` were both loaded fresh for this pass before a line of copy was drafted.

**Copy-mentors framing:** Halbert governs the register again this volume — every line has to survive being read out loud on a moving, wind-loud boat, which is why every script leans on contractions, fragments, and quoted dialogue instead of narrated explanation. Sugarman's Slippery Slide shaped the four-beat structure applied to all fifteen comedic pieces: line 1 is the real trigger, stated plainly, no narrator flagging that a delayed reaction is coming; line 2 is the group's on-time, in-sync response; line 3 is the one person visibly doing something else, specific enough to picture (a compact mirror, a chip mid-bite, one earbud, a phone angle); line 4 is the exact, timed, visualizable payoff — never a vague "and then everyone laughed." Blair Warren's 27 words did identifiable work in three pieces: 521 confirms a suspicion every friend group has about the one person whose laugh always shows up a beat behind everyone else's; 526 allays the mild embarrassment of confidently singing the wrong lyrics by making the *listener's* delayed reaction the joke instead of the singer's mistake; 518 lets a guest throw an affectionate rock at his own obliviousness by getting roasted and not even clocking it for thirty seconds.

**Copywriting-2.0, 3-test filter applied line by line, catches made during drafting:**
- **Vague-payoff catch, systemic first pass.** Early closers for 513 and 527 both drafted as "and it was hilarious" or "everyone lost it" — flagged immediately as the wallpaper phrase this series keeps catching. 513 was rewritten to a specific image (he laughs into his own drink, alone, three coolers after the deckhand's moved on); 527 was rewritten to a specific, falsifiable image (the jet ski's already a speck on the water by the time her camera's actually up).
- **Falsifiability catch on 516 and 522.** Both first drafts used loose time language ("a while later," "eventually") instead of a countable gap. Rewritten to specific counts — "five seconds," "two seconds" — because a vague gap isn't a joke, a counted one is.
- **Narrator-summary catch, checked across the batch.** Every opening line was tested for whether it explains that a delayed reaction is coming before it happens. All fifteen comedic pieces pass — line 1 in every case is the plain, real trigger (a joke, a safety call, an announcement, a toast, a countdown, a wake, a song, a sighting), and the "who's not paying attention yet" beat only appears starting in line 3, never signaled early.
- **Genericness catch, applied to the whole batch.** 514 and 525 anchor to a real HPB safety habit (a dock-line call, a "sit down, wake's coming" warning) rather than an invented boat-generic hazard. 519 and 520 anchor to the specific mechanics of a real bachelorette boat game (the meet-the-groom question round, a shots countdown) instead of "party game" in general. 517 anchors to the specific real stop this crew runs (a sunset engine-cut, not a generic scenic pause).
- **Repetition catch across the batch.** First drafts of 517 and 524 both used "right as the shutter goes" as the closing image — kept only in 517 (a group photo, where the shutter is the actual mechanic) and rewritten in 524 to "as everyone's already clinking glasses," since 524's moment is about sound landing, not a camera click, and having both pieces share one closing image in the same volume would read as a repeat.
- **Mishearing/plausibility check, carried over from Vol. 31's convention but applied to delayed-reaction triggers instead of relayed words.** Every "why would this specific person be the one who's behind" beat was tested against whether the distraction is the kind of ordinary thing that plausibly eats someone's attention on a moving, loud, sunny boat — a phone call, a mirror check, a story mid-sentence, a bag search, one earbud in. One early candidate was cut for failing this test: a guest missing an announcement because she was "lost in thought," which isn't a visualizable, falsifiable distraction the way a FaceTime call or a chip bite is.
