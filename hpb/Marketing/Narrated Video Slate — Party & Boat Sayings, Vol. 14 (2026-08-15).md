## How this was written

Two-pass process, same as every prior volume, run fresh against this batch. `/copy-mentors` and `/copywriting-2.0` were both loaded fresh for this pass before any line was drafted.

1. **`/copy-mentors`** — master routing by piece:
   - **Schwartz's identification principle** drove the majority of this batch — 214, 215, 216, 217, 218, 219, 220, 221, and 224 all assume the viewer already recognizes the moment (a captain who doesn't look the part, a sober guy at the helm, the untouched veggie tray, the drink nobody can name, the humidity-fueled Caribbean joke, picking up "y'all," guests slowly turning into crew) and just name it rather than arguing it's funny.
   - **Sugarman's slippery-slide short openers** drove 210, 214, 218, and 223 — each opens with a short, plain, easy-to-swallow line ("Somebody wrapped the baby gift in a life jacket," "Public record," "Public notice," "Public record") before the bit builds, continuing the device Vol. 13 used for pieces 193, 198, 203, and 206.
   - **Halbert's conversational directness** drove 213 and 217's closing lines — "She'd forgotten how loud her own friends could be. The water helped remind her" and "We've stopped asking who packs it. We've accepted it's just part of the boat now" are flat, unhedged statements, no qualifier, same register Vol. 13 used for pieces 194 and 186.
   - **Ogilvy's factual-specific-over-adjective principle** shaped piece 223 directly — instead of a vague "Houston has plenty to do," the line borrows the exact framing and figure ("30 min from downtown") already validated in the sourced 532 Content Grid doc, rather than inventing a softer, less specific claim.
   - **Blair Warren's 27 words** (applied during drafting, from copywriting-2.0): piece 213 leans on "allay their fears" — the trip doesn't need to be a big planned event, just people showing up. Piece 209 leans on "confirm their suspicions" — that a mixed-generation shower can actually be relaxed rather than performative. Piece 211 leans on "encourage their dreams" — that celebrating Juneteenth close to where it started matters, without over-claiming ownership of that history. Piece 216 leans on "confirm their suspicions" from the crew's side — that the small, unprompted gestures of appreciation are the ones that stick.

2. **`/copywriting-2.0`** — every spoken line run through the 3-test filter (can I visualize it, can I falsify it, could nobody else say it). What that pass caught and fixed in this batch specifically:
   - **Fixed the number-borrowing risk in piece 215**, described in the pass note above — the single most important catch in this batch.
   - **Fixed an overclaim risk in piece 213**: the first draft implied the returning guest's absence was a military deployment. Genericized to "been gone eighteen months" with the reason left open, so the piece doesn't presume a real guest's story that may not be true — flagged again in that piece's own NOTES.txt.
   - **Fixed a factual-precision risk in piece 211**: the first draft said "thirty minutes south" for the distance to Galveston. Softened to "about an hour," which is closer to an honest driving estimate from the Clear Lake/Seabrook area, and flagged as an approximate figure rather than a measured claim — same discipline Vol. 13 applied to the NASA-proximity piece and Vol. 12 applied before it.
   - **Fixed a trademark-adjacent risk in piece 212**: the first draft named the actual rodeo event. Genericized to "rodeo season," no event name, no logo, no team — same discipline piece 201 applied to sports content in Vol. 13.
   - **Kept two bits honestly flagged as invented illustrations, not documented fact**: piece 213's "eighteen months" and piece 214's "a thousand times," both flagged in their own NOTES.txt files as illustrative figures, not logged data — same discipline the series has applied since pieces 158/160/163 in Vol. 11.
