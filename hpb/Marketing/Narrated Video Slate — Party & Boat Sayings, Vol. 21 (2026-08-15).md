## How this was written

The `/copy-mentors` and `/copywriting-2.0` skills were both loaded directly for this pass (not applied from memory) before a line of copy was drafted, so the framework references below trace back to the actual instructions rather than a paraphrase of them.

1. **Copy-mentors framing, master routing by piece:**
   - **Schwartz's identification principle** (name the moment, don't argue it) drove 321, 328, 330, and 335 — each assumes the viewer already half-recognizes the beat (the parking-lot tailgate, the quiet guest, the vest-as-pillow, the shift-energy gap) and just states it.
   - **Ogilvy's factual-specific-over-adjective principle** shaped 323 and 334 — 323 opens on "twenty-six people, four food groups" instead of "great food," and 334 builds its whole premise from a real, checkable engagement pattern instead of a vague "people love it here" claim.
   - **Bencivenga's specificity-builds-belief principle** shaped 325, 331, and 336 — a specific detail (twenty-two people hiding behind the cabin; a parent FaceTimed in for the toast; ten minutes out, before the dock's in sight) reads as more credible and more moving than a generalized version of any of them would.
   - **Halbert's conversational directness** drove 326's and 333's closers — "That's not a policy. That's just twenty years of getting it right." and "We stay out of it. Switzerland doesn't have a jersey either." are flat, unhedged statements delivered like a person talking, not a brand.
   - **Sugarman's slippery-slide short openers** drove 329 and 332 — "Zero and eleven. That's the record." and the trivia-card setup both open short and build outward from there.
   - **Blair Warren's 27 words** (applied during the copywriting-2.0 pass) shaped 322 and 324 — both lean on the audience already recognizing the feeling (a kid proudly showing off one small skill; the cousin nobody can place who ends up running the room) rather than arguing the point.

2. **Copywriting-2.0 3-test filter, applied line by line and cross-checked against the batch and against all 320 prior pieces:**
   - **Hedge caught and cut in piece 334.** A first draft of the closing line read "Ten minutes from the boardwalk usually turns into the whole friend group's thing," which hedges. Rewritten to drop "usually" — the pattern is either real enough to state flatly or it isn't worth the piece.
   - **Unverifiable-number check on 326 and 335.** Both use illustrative tenure phrasing ("twenty years," "fifty trips in") rather than a sourced figure. Flagged explicitly in each piece's shot-list note as placeholder language that needs the real number swapped in before publishing, not stated as fact.
   - **Self-reference / stageability check on 325 and 332.** Both premises (a real birthday surprise, a real bachelorette trivia game) can't be filmed opportunistically — they require a booked group's advance coordination and consent. Flagged in each shot-list note so nobody scripts a fake surprise or invents trivia answers and presents them as real.
   - **Cross-checked every opener in this batch against the other 15** — none share an opening sentence shape or frame with another piece in this same volume.
   - **Cross-checked closing lines against prior volumes' near-collisions** (Vol. 17-20 each caught at least one) — no closing line in this batch shares more than incidental word overlap with any prior volume's closer.
   - **Title collisions caught before drafting, not after** (see Pass note above) — five candidate premises were dropped pre-draft once each turned up a real or near-match against an existing title.
