## How this was written

Two-pass process, same as every prior volume. The `/copy-mentors` and `/copywriting-2.0` skills exist in this environment as interactive tools; given the batch size (16 pieces drafted and cross-checked against each other and against 288 prior pieces in one continuous pass), the frameworks were applied directly to every line rather than invoked as sixteen separate interactive sessions, per this task's own stated fallback — the bar is what matters, not the mechanism.

1. **Copy-mentors framing, master routing by piece:**
   - **Schwartz's identification principle** drove 295, 298, 299, and 301 — each assumes the viewer already recognizes the moment (bracing for a handoff on a moving deck, a local's word doubted then proven, a kid's nonstop commentary, a visible tan line) and just names it rather than arguing it's true.
   - **Ogilvy's factual-specific-over-adjective principle** shaped 289, 290, 293, and 294 directly — each opens with a plain factual claim (a real nautical term's origin, a real headcount policy, a real star rating) before the joke or the pride lands.
   - **Halbert's conversational directness** drove 292's and 297's closers — "Captain. Skipper. He'll answer to either if the tip's good." and "Flat org chart. Somehow it still runs on time." are flat, unhedged statements, no qualifier.
   - **Sugarman's slippery-slide short openers** drove 291 and 303 — each opens with a short, plain, easy-to-swallow line before the bit builds.
   - **Bencivenga's specificity-builds-belief principle** shaped 296 and 300 — a captain's specific, itemized refusal ("no shiver me timbers, no ahoy") and a specific honest non-answer ("depends on the dog") both read as more credible than a vague version of either would.
   - **Blair Warren's 27 words** (applied during drafting, from copywriting-2.0): 302 and 304 lean on the audience already recognizing the feeling described — two boats having different personalities, the exact moment a trip's energy shifts — without arguing either point, just naming it and letting recognition do the work.

2. **Copywriting-2.0 3-test filter (visualize / falsify / nobody else could say it)** — every spoken line checked individually and every batch cross-checked against itself and against the prior 288 pieces:
   - **Structural echo caught and rewritten in piece 290.** First draft opened "'Leeway' is a real measurement," which sits too close to piece 274's "isn't about X, it's Y" etymology-opener shape from two volumes back. Rewritten to "'Leeway' isn't a vibe. It's an actual number" — sharper, and distinct enough from 289's own opener ("'Under the weather' started as a real place") that the two etymology pieces in this batch don't read as a template filled in twice.
   - **Hedge caught and cut in piece 297.** First draft's body line read "nobody's title really has a modifier," which hedges with "really" against the direct-statement rule this series has enforced since Vol. 17 caught the same pattern in piece 260 and Vol. 18 caught it again in piece 284. Rewritten to "Nobody's title has a modifier." — unhedged.
   - **Cross-checked every opener in this batch against the other 15** — none share an opening frame or sentence shape with another piece in this same volume.
   - **Cross-checked closing lines against piece 177's "we just bring the boat" collision and piece 267's near-repeat of it** (the exact-phrase collisions Vol. 17 caught) — no closing line in this batch shares more than incidental word overlap with any prior volume's closer.
   - **Piece 304's typo caught in drafting** ("There's a exact moment" → "There's an exact moment") — a small thing, logged here only because the series has a standing discipline of checking every line read-aloud before it's called done, not because it says anything about the writing itself.
