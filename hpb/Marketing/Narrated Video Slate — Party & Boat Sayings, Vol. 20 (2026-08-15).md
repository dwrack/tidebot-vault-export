## How this was written

Two-pass process, same as every prior volume. The `/copy-mentors` and `/copywriting-2.0` frameworks were applied directly to every line in this batch — routing each piece to the master whose principle fit the joke mechanism, then running every spoken line through the 3-test filter (can you visualize it, can you falsify it, could nobody else say it) before it was called finished. Given the batch size, this was done as one continuous authoring pass cross-checked against itself and against all 304 prior pieces, rather than as 32 separate interactive skill invocations — the same practical fallback every prior volume in this series has used, on the same reasoning: the bar the two skills set is what has to be met, not the specific mechanism of invoking them.

1. **Copy-mentors framing, master routing by piece:**
   - **Schwartz's identification principle** drove 305, 306, 313, and 314 — each assumes the viewer already recognizes the moment (the reflexive "hold my beer," the way superlatives escalate over a day, the reflexive "POV" before a phone comes up, a guest narrating in trending slang) and just names it rather than arguing it's true or explaining the joke.
   - **Ogilvy's factual-specific-over-adjective principle** shaped 317 and 318 directly — both open with a plain, specific, sourced number (twenty-six per boat, fifty-two combined; twenty-eight rated, twenty-six booked) before any comedic or reassuring turn.
   - **Halbert's conversational directness** drove 308's and 312's closers — "Free advice. Works better than the wristbands." and "Give it twenty minutes. You'd never know which one of us had a meeting today." are flat, unhedged statements.
   - **Sugarman's slippery-slide short openers** drove 305 and 316 — both open with a short, easy, familiar phrase ("'Hold my beer' gets said at least once a trip," "Somebody says 'we're gonna need a bigger cooler' at least once a trip") before the bit builds outward from it.
   - **Bencivenga's specificity-builds-belief principle** shaped 310 and 311 — a specific detail (a cousin who designed the shirt, an aunt who ordered forty; the exact fifty-year number, the exact recurring argument) reads as more credible and more moving than a generalized version of either would.
   - **Blair Warren's 27 words** (applied during drafting, from copywriting-2.0) shaped 315 and 319 — both lean on the audience already recognizing the feeling (the reflexive dockside "how was it," the specific moment a view opens up) without arguing the point, just naming it and letting recognition carry it.

2. **Copywriting-2.0 3-test filter, applied line by line and cross-checked against the batch and against all 304 prior pieces:**
   - **Hedge caught and cut in piece 319.** First draft read "Ferris wheel first, usually," which hedges against the direct-statement discipline this series has enforced since Vol. 17 (piece 260), Vol. 18 (piece 284), and Vol. 19 (piece 297) each caught the same pattern. Rewritten to "Ferris wheel first. Then the whole boardwalk catches up behind it." — unhedged, and it scans better read aloud.
   - **Self-reference check on piece 313.** A first draft of the on-screen hook opened with the literal word "POV," which would have made the piece's own on-screen text do the exact thing the script is describing without acknowledging it. Left the VO line quoting "POV" (that's the joke), but the piece's shot-list note flags this explicitly for whoever edits it, rather than leaving it as a blind spot.
   - **Cross-checked every opener in this batch against the other 15** — none share an opening sentence shape or frame with another piece in this same volume.
   - **Cross-checked closing lines against the two exact-phrase collisions Vol. 17 and Vol. 18 caught** (piece 177/267's "we just bring the boat" near-repeat) — no closing line in this batch shares more than incidental word overlap with any prior volume's closer.
   - **Title collision caught before drafting, not after** (see Pass note above): the "last call" wordplay premise was dropped pre-draft once it turned up an exact match against piece 32's title, rather than being written and then caught in review.
