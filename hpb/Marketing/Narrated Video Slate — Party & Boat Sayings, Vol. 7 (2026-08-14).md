# Houston Pedal Barge — Narrated Video Slate: Party & Boat Sayings, Vol. 7

**Format modeled on:** Admire NOLA's narrated series (`Content/Narrated/01-poetic-NOTES.txt`, `Content/Script & Caption Bank.md`, `Content/Launch Slate - Daily Posts.md`) — timed script with a timestamp and bracketed visual on every line, one NOTES.txt per piece, voice/music/length header.

**Lane:** funny, loose, quotable — party sayings, boat sayings, toasts, deck-hand slang, bachelorette-party lines, captain lines, the running jokes of a group on a boat. Same lane as Vols. 1-6, deliberately the opposite of Admire NOLA's poetic swamp narration. Pedaling is never sold as a feature anywhere in this volume — none of these 16 topics touch pedaling at all.

**Pass note — this is pass 24, and Vol. 7 in this specific narrated-slate lane.** 96 prior pieces exist on file (`Narrated Video Slate — Party & Boat Sayings`, Vols. 1-6, pieces 01-96). Vol. 6 closed with an explicit map of where Vol. 7 should go (its Open Items #8): holiday coverage sat at four (July 4th, Halloween, New Year's Eve, Valentine's Day) with St. Patrick's Day, Mother's/Father's Day, and Cinco de Mayo still open; the birthday and bachelor-party "second angle" treatment had worked well and a third angle or the same treatment on a still-single-angle category (wildlife, regional pride) was named as the most promising direction; and Roger (the mock-PSA voice) was flagged as very likely tapped out at six total appearances. This volume works that map directly rather than reaching for genuinely new topic territory blind.

## Why these 16 topics

- **Four new holidays, closing out the calendar's obvious ones.** St. Patrick's Day, Mother's Day, Father's Day, and Cinco de Mayo were the four Vol. 6 named as open. Each has a distinct joke mechanism (a house-rule gag, a sincere seat-reassignment, a deadpan dad-joke tally, a cooler-upgrade bit) so four holiday pieces in one volume doesn't read as a formula the way a fifth or sixth would. Holiday territory is now at eight total across the series — see Open Items below on treating that as effectively saturated going forward.
- **Second angles on the two categories Vol. 6 named as still single-touch.** Wildlife had exactly one piece (73, a deadpan list). Regional pride had exactly one piece (74, winter). This volume gives each a second angle with a genuinely different mechanism: wildlife moves from a list bit to a recurring-character bit (a specific heron), and regional pride moves from a seasonal-weather brag to a daily-annoyance contrast (Houston traffic).
- **A third angle on birthday**, following the pattern that worked for Vol. 6's third-angle treatment: piece 12 was the crown ritual, piece 88 was the cooler-cost custom, this volume's piece 103 is playlist veto power — a third distinct mechanism, not a variation on either prior one.
- **New character archetypes nobody had opened.** A self-appointed toastmaster (distinct from every actual toast in the series — this is comedy about toast culture itself, not a toast), a "bow people vs. stern people" boat-tribe taxonomy (a device the series hasn't used — sorting guests into types rather than telling one story), a snack-culture one-upmanship bit (the charcuterie board, distinct from every prior cooler joke, which have all been about drinks or inclusivity), and a genuinely new emotional register: a friend group's post-breakup "reclaim the summer" trip, handled sincerely, the same family as pieces 38 and 71 but a story nobody had told yet.
- **Two new "rule" pieces that deliberately avoid Roger's device.** "No Sad Songs After Six" and "Nobody Asks For The WiFi Password" both touch house-rule territory, but neither uses Roger or the mock-PSA-announcement format. The first is framed as an old written rule discovered rather than announced (Announcer's formal-but-warm register, not Roger's PSA jingle); the second isn't a rule at all — it's a guest's own realization that nobody's missed the wifi. Roger rests this volume entirely, per Vol. 6's flag.
- **A closer that revisits photo culture from an angle nobody had used.** The series has covered phones-out golden hour (26), a photo becoming a profile picture (59), and the drone guy (92) — all about the taking. Piece 112 is about the sorting: the specific, relatable ritual of forty near-misses before one keeper.

None of the 16 below repeats a premise, punchline, or joke structure already used in pieces 01-96.

## How this was written

Same two-pass process as every prior volume, run fresh against this batch:

1. **`/copy-mentors`** — Halbert for the one-person-talking conversational structure on the sincere pieces (99, 111); Sugarman's short-opener slippery-slide on the deadpan list pieces (100); Bencivenga for specificity — "a tiny knife for the brie" beats "someone brought fancy snacks," "forty photos, one where everybody's eyes are open" beats "group photos are hard"; and Blair Warren's four levers doing real work here: piece 110 confirms a suspicion (you already knew you didn't miss your phone, you just hadn't said it out loud); piece 108 allays a fear for future guests (your cooler doesn't need to compete, but if you want to bring it, here's what "showing up" looks like); piece 105 throws a small, affectionate rock at a target everyone recognizes (the guy who won't sit down once he's got a drink and an audience).
2. **`/copywriting-2.0`** — every spoken line run through the 3-test filter (can you visualize it, can you falsify it, could a competitor say it). What that pass caught and fixed in this batch specifically:
   - **Cut and rewritten:** piece 97's first draft named a specific freeway exit and time of year, which read as overly precise for a claim nobody can actually verify from a video. Pulled back to "Friday at five" and "forty minutes," concrete enough to visualize, generic enough not to invite a fact-check nobody can win.
   - **Reframed to avoid device overlap:** piece 109's first draft opened with "attention, everyone" — too close to Roger's mock-PSA announcement structure that's resting this volume. Rewritten to discover the rule as a sign already posted, not a voice announcing it, which is a genuinely different comedic entry point.
   - **Kept and built around:** "a tiny knife for the brie" (piece 108) — passes all three tests. Visualizable, checkable against any actual charter's cooler, and not something a rooftop bar or a party bus (the stated competition) would ever think to mention, because nobody there is watching what's in someone else's cooler.
   - **Flagged, not softened:** piece 111 ("Her Reclaim-The-Summer Trip") is the most emotionally specific piece in the series to date. The 3-test filter rewards specificity, but this is also the piece most likely to misfire if the real guest hasn't clearly opted in. It stays as written because the underlying beat — friends rallying around someone after a hard stretch — is universal and not invasive on its own, but Open Items below flags the consent bar as higher here than anywhere else in the series.
   - **Cut:** an earlier draft of piece 104 ("Bow People And Stern People") had a third "type" (a person standing at the very back near the motor) that didn't add a new joke, just a longer list. Cut back to two types because the taxonomy device works better clean — the whole joke is the binary, not the completeness of the list.
