## How this was written

`/copy-mentors` and `/copywriting-2.0` were both run fresh for this pass before a line of copy was drafted.

**Copy-mentors framing:** Sugarman's Slippery Slide governs the shape of all 16 — line 1 is the award announced live and cold, under 10 words, no narrator setup explaining what an "award" is; line 2 is the first sign the group's taking it seriously (a nomination, a runner-up, applause); line 3 is the escalation, someone making it official (the captain repeating it on the mic, a second ceremony); line 4 is the payoff, flat and specific, naming who has the title now and what they're doing with it. Halbert's kitchen-table rule governed every line's register — nothing that reads like a press release for a fake award survived a draft; every line has to sound like something actually yelled across a boat deck. Bencivenga's specificity rule governed every payoff: no piece closes on "and it became a whole thing" — every closer names the person, the action, or the exact moment the title stuck.

**Copywriting-2.0, 3-test filter applied line by line, catches made during drafting:**
- **Narrator-summary catch, systemic.** First drafts of 466, 469, and 475 all opened by explaining the award before delivering it ("someone decided to give an award for..."). Rewritten so line 1 is always the award being announced live, mid-scene, no setup.
- **Vague-payoff catch on 468 and 477.** 468's first draft closed "and it stuck the rest of the trip" — cut for being the exact wallpaper phrase this series has flagged before — rewritten to the captain visibly wincing at his own announcement and a guest holding him to it at the dock. 477's first draft closed "everyone still talks about it" (unfalsifiable) — rewritten to a specific detail: the "trophy" (a life jacket) still sitting next to the sleeping guest when the photos go around.
- **Genericness catch, applied to the whole batch.** Every piece checked for whether it could belong to any boat business — cut where generic. 465 anchors to the actual swim-stop reboarding sequence. 471 and 476 anchor to the toast moment specifically, not a generic "party" beat. 478 anchors to "one more stop," a phrase specific to a multi-stop cruise, not a single-location party.
- **Falsifiability catch on 470 and 479.** 470's first draft had the captain awarding himself something every single trip — softened to one specific trip, deadpan, once, since a permanent habit is a bigger claim than one script should carry. 479's first draft claimed the guest "framed it when they got home" — cut as unverifiable; rewritten to the guest asking, on camera, if they can keep it, which is something the crew can actually confirm happened.
- **Repetition catch across the batch.** First drafts of 466 and 478 both closed on a version of "and that became the thing everyone said" — flagged as too close inside one volume. 478 was rewritten to the specific escalation of someone actually counting the "one more stop"s out loud on a later trip, so the two payoffs land differently.
- **Award-object realism check, new this volume.** Every "trophy" or prop named on screen is something that would plausibly already be on the boat — a pool noodle, a life jacket, a plastic cup, a captain's hat — never an invented prop that would require a purchase or a build. This keeps the device shootable with existing gear, not a production ask.
