## Why these 16 topics

The saturation read-through found these categories **fully mined across Vols. 1-4** and off-limits for Vol. 5: toasts/raise-a-cup format (11 uses), cooler/BYOB jokes (6), group-chat bits (5), phones/photos-at-sunset (4), dock-departure farewell closers (4), the mock-formal-voice-played-straight-then-undercut device (7 uses of the structure itself, regardless of topic), bachelorette lines (6), the bathroom objection-kill FAQ (2 near-duplicates already), aux cord/DJ/playlist jokes (3), boat-rivalry/name-lore (2, explicitly flagged in Vol. 1 as "don't do a third"), dress-code/outfit chaos (4), safety-briefing/life-jacket/man-overboard comedy (4), and the "vs. a competitor/alternative" positioning device (2 uses).

That ruled out most of the obvious boat-party territory. What was left, confirmed untouched by the extraction: weather/rain contingency (Vol. 3 itself flagged this as the safest unused ground), the after-charter food run, first-timer vs. regular guest contrast, holiday-anchored content (zero pieces across 64), tipping the crew (distinct from the two existing money bits, which are guest-to-guest BYOB savings and post-trip Venmo splitting), on-boat proposals, seasickness as its own topic (distinct from the existing "sea legs" wobble-on-land bit), wildlife/lake b-roll as its own joke rather than pure scenery, the Houston-doesn't-really-do-winter angle, referral/friend-brought-a-friend, a dedicated crew-introduction bit, boat turnover/cleaning between charters, first-date awkwardness, group-size overshoot logistics, and a second corporate angle that isn't a repeat of Vol. 1's "stiff office group loosens up" narrative.

All 16 pieces below come from that untouched list. None repeats a premise, punchline, or joke structure already used in pieces 01-64.

## How this was written

Same two-pass process as every prior volume, run fresh against this batch:

1. **`/copy-mentors`** — Halbert for the conversational, one-person-talking structure; Sugarman for the short-opener slippery-slide (short first line, then build); Bencivenga for specificity (a deflated flamingo float and one abandoned flip-flop beats "the deck gets cleaned fast"); and Blair Warren's four levers doing most of the actual persuasion work. Confirm a suspicion: "fifty percent chance of rain isn't a policy, it's a guess" (everyone's had that thought about every outdoor booking they've ever made). Allay a fear: the first-timer/regular contrast piece exists specifically to tell a nervous first-time booker "you'll be fine, everyone starts here." Throw a small rock at a shared target: the corporate piece pokes at the "team-building activity, per the calendar invite" euphemism everyone who's ever gotten that Outlook invite recognizes instantly.
2. **`/copywriting-2.0`** — every spoken line run through the 3-test filter (can you visualize it, can you falsify it, could a competitor say it). What that pass caught and fixed in this batch specifically:
   - **Cut and rewritten:** an early draft of piece 09's wildlife list used "a heron, judging everyone equally" as the closing beat. Killed it, not because it fails the 3-test filter (it doesn't), but because it's a near word-for-word echo of Admire NOLA's own signature line ("A heron stands so still, you'll drift right past, and never know you were judged"). HPB's whole reason for being in a different lane than Admire NOLA is to sound like a different business. Replaced with a seagull plotting to steal someone's chips, which is funnier for this brand anyway and owns its own joke instead of borrowing one.
   - **Cut:** a first draft of piece 01 stated an actual reschedule/refund policy ("we'll get you on the next available trip"). Pulled it. Nobody has confirmed the real cancellation terms with Connor, and a wrong policy claim in a video is worse than a vague one. The line that survived talks about *how the call gets made* (radar over app icon, lightning over drizzle) without promising *what happens to your money* if it's canceled — see Open Items below.
   - **Kept and built around:** "twenty minutes ago this deck had a deflated flamingo float, four coolers, and one abandoned flip-flop" — passes all three tests. You can see it, it's a checkable claim (ask any crew member what's actually been left on that deck), and no other Clear Lake boat operator has this exact true detail.
   - **Kept and built around:** "booking said twenty. Twenty-six people got out of that Uber." Ties a joke directly to a real, public fact (both boats hold 26 — straight from the Brand Story doc), which makes the punchline checkable instead of just funny.
   - **Softened:** piece 07's proposal piece originally read "we've had a dozen of these." Nobody has confirmed that number. Changed to "we've had a few of these happen on board" — still a specific-sounding claim, still unverified, flagged explicitly in Open Items below for Connor or the crew to confirm or correct before this one airs.
