# Houston Pedal Barge — Video Hook Bank

20 short-form hooks (Reels / TikTok / Shorts) for bachelorette parties, birthdays, corporate outings, and tourists. Every hook was run through `/video-mentors` for packaging strategy, then `/copy-mentors` and `/copywriting-2.0` (the 3-test filter: can I visualize it, can I falsify it, can nobody else say it) for line-level sharpening.

**Hard rule applied throughout: pedaling is never the hook.** The pedal stations are optional and barely used — the party, the BYOB freedom, the private group, and Clear Lake are the actual draw. Only one hook (#18) even mentions pedaling, and it does so by naming that reviews *don't* talk about it — proof the pedal mechanic isn't what people remember.

---

## Packaging strategy (from /video-mentors, applied to HPB)

**CCN test (Paddy Galloway) applied to a tour-business Reels account:**
- **Core** = past riders and local Houston/Kemah followers. They click for the boat and the crew they recognize.
- **Casual** = people who've seen one HPB video reposted or in their explore page. They click if the *scenario* (bachelorette, birthday, work outing) matches their life right now.
- **New** = cold scroll traffic anywhere. They only stop for a visual or a claim they can't scroll past in 3 seconds.
Every hook below is written to work on all three at once — it has to mean something to someone who's never heard of Clear Lake.

**First-3-seconds discipline (MrBeast memo + Jenny Hoyos):** no logo, no "hey guys," no slow establishing drone shot before the hook lands. Cold open on the line or the visual. Text overlay should be legible muted, since most scroll traffic watches without sound.

**Hook → Build → Payoff → Loop (Jenny Hoyos):** each concept below opens into a body that escalates (more of the party, more of the reveal, bigger number) and should close on a beat that echoes the opening line or shot, so autoplay/replay re-hooks.

**Niche discipline (Colin & Samir):** every hook ties to one of four real occasions — bachelorette, birthday, corporate, tourist/local — not generic "boat life" content. That keeps the account legible as "the Houston boat for your specific event," which is the actual positioning goal.

**Document, don't manufacture (Kara & Nate):** several formats below (crew POV, surprise reveal, real email screenshot) are built to be filmed from actual bookings, not staged. Use real groups, real reactions, real price screenshots wherever a hook implies proof.

**Never sell pedaling as the feature (brand constraint, non-negotiable):** the hook is always the social experience — BYOB savings, private group, no bouncer, no dress code, the view, the price. Pedaling shows up nowhere as a selling point.

## The 20 hooks

### Bachelorette

**1.**
> "$40 in drinks. Zero bar tab. This is what 12 bridesmaids did instead of dropping $700 at a Houston bar."

- **Concept:** Opens into a cost breakdown of a real bachelorette group's cooler vs. what the same night at a Houston bar district venue runs. Payoff shows the deck, the drinks, the music, the savings math on screen.
- **Format:** UGC group POV, text overlay with the two numbers, cut to dancing/deck footage.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** Bachelorette planners are already doing this exact math in a group chat somewhere. Real numbers ($40 vs $700) are checkable and specific — not "affordable," an actual comparison.

**2.**
> "Nobody told the bride she could wear a swimsuit and a veil at the same time."

- **Concept:** Leads into "here's why nobody polices what you wear on this boat" — heels, flip-flops, swimsuits, sashes, whatever the group shows up in.
- **Format:** UGC/talking-head, bride reaction shot, quick cut to the actual outfit.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** A single, visualizable, slightly absurd image (swimsuit + veil) that solves a real bachelorette-planning anxiety — "what do we even wear."

**3.**
> "She said no strangers, no bouncer, no last call — so we rented the whole boat."

- **Concept:** Private charter positioning via a negation list — everything a bar night has that this doesn't.
- **Format:** Text-on-screen list building line by line over group dancing/deck b-roll.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** Negation lists are a proven pattern interrupt, and each item names a real, specific bar-night annoyance instead of a vague "better experience" claim.

**4.**
> "This 'Cabo' photo dump wasn't Cabo. It was a Tuesday on Clear Lake."

- **Concept:** Fast-cut photo-dump edit (sunset, boat, drinks, group shots) that looks like a destination trip, then reveals the actual location and day of the week.
- **Format:** Text-on-screen reveal + fast photo-dump-style cut set to sunset/water b-roll.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** Genuine curiosity gap (where was this?) resolved with a twist that's directly relevant to a Houston audience deciding between a destination bach trip and a local one — no invented engagement numbers, the visual carries it.

**5.**
> "POV: three weeks of 'idk, whatever you guys want' in the group chat. Then someone found the one thing that shut everybody up: a private boat, BYOB, and total control of the aux cord."

- **Concept:** Mirrors the actual group-chat indecision every bachelorette planner has lived through, resolved by the three things that are genuinely different about HPB — private, BYOB, their own music.
- **Format:** POV with group-chat screenshot overlay, then boat reveal.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** The pain point (group chat that can't decide) is universal and instantly recognizable; the resolution names the actual structural differentiators instead of a generic "found the answer" beat, so it's a claim only this business can make.

**6.**
> "Her fiancé has no idea what's about to happen to his credit card... jk, it was $850 for the whole boat."

- **Concept:** Sets up an expectation (destination-wedding-level spending) then undercuts it with the real, flat charter price.
- **Format:** Talking-head with price-reveal text overlay.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** The joke creates a fear ("this is going to be expensive"), then a real, specific number resolves it — addresses cost anxiety directly instead of a vague "affordable" claim.

### Birthday

**7.**
> "My 9-year-old and my 70-year-old grandma were on the same boat. Both asked when we're doing it again."

- **Concept:** Opens into how the barge works as a genuine all-ages venue, solving the real headache of planning a birthday for a mixed-age guest list.
- **Format:** Talking-head organizer/parent + multi-generational b-roll.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** The age contrast (9 and 70) is concrete and visual, not a vague "fun for everyone" claim — and mixed-age planning is a real, common problem.

**8.**
> "I've thrown three birthday parties. This is the first time everyone actually showed up."

- **Concept:** Opens into what actually got people to RSVP yes — the water, the photos, no cover charge, the novelty of a boat.
- **Format:** Talking-head, testimonial style.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** Names a real insecurity (low turnout) that almost nobody admits out loud, with a specific number ("three parties") that makes the claim feel lived-in rather than invented.

**9.**
> "Told my husband we were going to dinner. Drove him straight to a party boat instead."

- **Concept:** Surprise-birthday narrative — the reveal moment when guests are already aboard.
- **Format:** UGC surprise reveal, phone-camera reaction shot.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** The surprise-reveal format is a proven scroll-stopper on its own; curiosity about the reaction shot pulls the viewer through to the payoff.

**10.**
> "Bowling alley quoted us $340 for two hours, shoes extra. Our private charter was $875 for the whole boat, up to 26 people."

- **Concept:** Direct price/value comparison against a typical Houston birthday-venue option.
- **Format:** Text-on-screen comparison over boat footage.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** Two checkable, specific numbers against a venue every viewer has actually priced out themselves — this is the exact math a birthday planner is doing right now.

### Corporate

**11.**
> "Our last team-building was trust falls. This one was a cooler and a boat."

- **Concept:** Positions the barge as the anti-cringe team outing by naming the specific bad experience it's replacing.
- **Format:** Talking-head (team lead/office manager) + b-roll of coworkers actually laughing.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** "Trust falls" is a shared, specific, slightly painful memory almost every office worker has — the contrast lands instantly because it's concrete, not "boring team building."

**12.**
> "I planned our office outing in one email. Here's exactly what I sent."

- **Concept:** Shows the actual low-effort booking process, screenshot included, aimed squarely at whoever got stuck planning the outing.
- **Format:** Text-on-screen real email screenshot + event footage.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** Speaks directly to the one person in every office assigned this task, and "here's exactly what I sent" promises a checkable artifact, not a vague "easy to book" claim. (Use a real email when this gets shot — don't fabricate the screenshot.)

**13.**
> "We canceled the $15-a-round Friday happy hour. This is what the office did instead."

- **Concept:** Reframes the barge as an upgrade to a specific, familiar office ritual instead of a vague "better than happy hour" claim.
- **Format:** Before/after cut — standard office happy hour vs. the boat.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** The $15-a-round detail is a real, specific cost anyone who's paid an office bar tab recognizes, which makes the swap feel like a real decision instead of an ad.

### Tourist

**14.**
> "Everybody visiting Houston does the Kemah Boardwalk. Almost nobody knows about this."

- **Concept:** Positions the barge against a landmark tourists already have on their list, not a vague "hidden gem" claim.
- **Format:** Text-on-screen + drone/water b-roll of the Kemah/Clear Lake sunset.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** Naming the actual competing activity (Kemah Boardwalk) makes the claim checkable and specific instead of the generic "hidden gem" line every city account uses.

**15.**
> "I googled 'something fun to do in Houston' and found a boat with BYOB and a dance floor."

- **Concept:** Mirrors the literal search behavior of someone planning a Houston trip, resolved with the barge.
- **Format:** POV / screen-recording-style opener, then reveal.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** Matches a search a viewer has plausibly typed themselves — native, low-friction curiosity resolution.

**16.**
> "Skip the boardwalk line. This is the better Kemah move."

- **Concept:** Positions the barge as the insider alternative to the crowded, touristy Kemah Boardwalk.
- **Format:** Comparison b-roll — crowded boardwalk vs. open water on the boat.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** Contrarian, insider framing ("skip the obvious thing") plus a real visual contrast between crowd and open water.

### General / local

**17.**
> "A group of 20 at a Houston bar: $700 and somebody's card gets declined. Same group on this boat: bring a cooler."

- **Concept:** Direct value comparison targeting anyone weighing options for a group night out, not tied to one occasion.
- **Format:** Text-on-screen math + party b-roll.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** Concrete numbers plus a universally recognized moment (the declined card at a big group bar tab) that's funny because it's specific and true.

**18.**
> "400 five-star reviews. Not one of them mentions pedaling."

- **Concept:** Neutralizes the "isn't this a workout" misconception by pointing at what the reviews actually talk about — the crew, the view, the party — without making pedaling the pitch.
- **Format:** Text-on-screen review screenshots, quick cuts, over boat/party footage.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** Real, checkable number (400 five-star reviews, matches the actual review count) plus a pattern interrupt on the exact objection that's actually costing bookings, without ever selling the pedal mechanic.

**19.**
> "This is what a random Tuesday turns into when someone in the group says 'what if we just rented the boat.'"

- **Concept:** Aspirational-but-accessible framing — this isn't a rare splurge, it's a spontaneous decision on an ordinary weekday.
- **Format:** Cinematic UGC, golden hour b-roll, minimal text.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** Grounds the fantasy (sunset boat party) in something achievable and specific (a random Tuesday, one person's suggestion) rather than a generic lifestyle-flex shot.

**20.**
> "Captain's POV: the exact moment a bachelorette party realizes they've got the whole boat to themselves."

- **Concept:** Crew-perspective footage of a real group's reaction on boarding — authentic UGC from an angle viewers rarely see.
- **Format:** POV from the captain/crew, genuine reaction footage.
- **Why it stops the scroll:** The captain's POV is a novel vantage point almost nobody uses in this category, and a real, unscripted reaction reads as more credible than a staged testimonial.
