## How this was written

Two-pass process, same as every prior volume, run fresh against this batch:

1. **`/copy-mentors`** — master routing by piece:
   - **Sugarman's slippery-slide short openers** drove pieces 162 ("Six months ago, this was a Cancún trip.") and 166 ("I don't have a dance floor."). Both open with a plain, short, easy-to-swallow sentence before the twist lands two beats later.
   - **Halbert's conversational directness** drove 167's structure — "There is not a buffet" is a flat, unhedged negation stated as its own sentence, the same register as Halbert's habit of answering the reader's skepticism before they voice it.
   - **Bencivenga's proof hierarchy** is the backbone of 172, 173, 174, and 175. Each swaps what would ordinarily be an adjective claim ("we're a safe, inclusive, no-nonsense operation") for a real, checkable specific (a certification name, a headcount, an age policy, a second business name) — tier 2 and tier 6 proof on Bencivenga's ladder, not tier 1 assertion.
   - **Schwartz's identification principle** drove 166, 168, and 171 — none of these try to convince the viewer that a small dance floor, a stranger-mixing seat rotation, or a reflexive drink-save is fun. They assume the viewer already recognizes the moment and just need to see it named.
   - **Blair Warren's 27 words** (from copywriting-2.0, applied during drafting): piece 162 leans on "justify their failures" — reframing a downgraded trip as the better choice, not a consolation prize. Piece 174 leans on "confirm their suspicions" (that families with kids and grandparents actually are welcome) and "encourage their dreams" (multi-generational time together). Piece 167 leans on "allay their fears" (this isn't formal, isn't going to feel like a cruise-ship obligation).

2. **`/copywriting-2.0`** — every spoken line run through the 3-test filter (can you visualize it, can you falsify it, could nobody else say it). What that pass caught and fixed in this batch specifically:
   - **Cut an unconfirmed, arguably unsafe claim:** piece 164's ("Boat Wrapped") first draft included a stat line about guests jumping off the boat's roof. Roof-jumping isn't documented anywhere in this vault as a confirmed, permitted guest activity, and this business's own contact records (`Houston Pedal Barge — Key Contacts.md`) show real insurance-claim and incident correspondence on file. Asserting an unconfirmed water-entry behavior as a fun, normal stat risked being both inaccurate and content this business wouldn't actually want circulating. Replaced with a safety-neutral deckhand-phrase line instead. Flagged in Open Items in case roof access is actually a real, approved thing worth confirming for a future piece.
   - **Fixed a falsifiability failure:** piece 174's first draft invented a specific anecdote ("our youngest rider was eight, our oldest was somebody's grandmother, twice") with no basis in any document on file. Rewritten to state only the real, confirmed Brand Story policy (ages 8 and up, no cutoff at the top) instead of a fabricated specific that could be checked and found untrue.
   - **Fixed a consistency/consent problem:** piece 176's first draft named a specific deckhand "Steve." Every other crew-facing piece in this series (01-160) uses archetypal role names — The Deckhand, The Captain, The Old Salt — never an invented individual's first name, because no real crew member has been confirmed or consented to being singled out by name. Rewritten to "the deckhand" to match the established convention.
   - **Softened a caricature risk:** piece 169's early draft leaned into phonetic accent spelling for comic effect. Rewritten to state "fixin' to shove off" plainly, once, and let the joke live in the guests' reaction and the definition-explaining beat rather than in an exaggerated performance of the accent. The shot list flags this only works with a crew member's genuine natural phrasing.
   - **Kept two bits honestly flagged as invented illustrations, not documented fact:** piece 161's "one real ring fished out of Clear Lake" and piece 170's "life jacket, activate" are both told as obvious tall tales / illustrative examples within the script's own delivery (deadpan exaggeration, "not on purpose, never on purpose"), not stated as literal claims. Both are flagged again in Open Items so nobody mistakes them for documented incidents when writing captions.
