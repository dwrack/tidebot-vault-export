## Buyer screening

Ask these three questions on the first call and you'll cut the tire-kickers in one pass:

1. Are you currently operating a passenger vessel, or would this be your first?
2. Where are you planning to run it, and have you talked to that Sector about the COI?
3. Cash, or are you financing? (Commercial passenger vessel financing is slow and often falls through. A financed buyer isn't a bad buyer, but you should know on day one.)

An operator who already has a COI'd boat somewhere else is your best buyer. They know what the certificate is worth, they won't haggle over the paddle wheel, and they close.

## Operational fallout to plan for

Once Delta Dream goes, you're a one-boat operation. That kills the 52-passenger simultaneous-run offering and it means one mechanical issue takes the whole business offline for the day. Before the boat leaves:

- Update every place that says "two boats" or "up to 52 guests": website, FareHarbor, OpenCX agent instructions, the Agent Instructions doc, GBP, entity.json, Brand Story & Values
- Decide what happens to peak Saturday demand with half the capacity. Raising private charter pricing is the obvious lever.
- Tell Connor before it's listed, not after. He'll be the one fielding questions from crew.
