### Question 1

You arrive for your morning inspection and discover that two PFDs are damaged and unusable. Your max capacity is normally 20 passengers. What do you do?

A) Run the tour. You still have enough PFDs for the booked guests.
B) Reduce capacity by 2 for the day and report the damaged PFDs.
C) Replace the PFDs before any tour departs. Do not reduce capacity, fix the problem.
D) Cancel all tours until new PFDs arrive.

**Best answer: C.** You need enough PFDs for max capacity plus crew. If you can't replace them before the first tour, then B is the fallback. But the right answer is fix it first.

---

### Question 2

During a USCG boarding inspection, the inspector asks where the COI is posted. Your COI is in a drawer in the console because it kept getting wet. What happens?

A) You explain the situation and they'll understand.
B) You're in violation. The COI must be posted visibly and accessible to passengers.
C) It's fine as long as you can produce it within a reasonable time.
D) The COI only needs to be posted during annual inspections.

**Best answer: B.** The COI must be posted where passengers can see it. Keeping it in a drawer is a violation, regardless of your reason. Get a waterproof sleeve or display case.

---

### Question 3

Your fire extinguisher gauge shows it's in the green zone, but the inspection tag expired 3 months ago. What's the status?

A) It's fine. Green means charged.
B) It needs to be serviced. An expired tag means it hasn't been professionally inspected.
C) Replace it immediately. Expired tags mean the extinguisher is dead.
D) Just sign a new tag yourself.

**Best answer: B.** The gauge might read green, but the tag tracks professional inspection. It could have internal issues the gauge doesn't show. Get it serviced before the next tour.

---

### Question 4

What VHF channel must be monitored at all times while operating?

A) Channel 9
B) Channel 22A
C) Channel 16
D) Channel 13

**Best answer: C.** Channel 16 is the international distress and calling frequency. It's monitored at all times. This is federal law, not a guideline.

---

### Question 5

A guest has a minor cut from bumping into a cleat. They say they're fine and don't want any first aid. What do you do?

A) Respect their wishes and move on. They said they're fine.
B) Offer first aid, clean and bandage it if they'll let you, and note it in your post-tour report.
C) File a full incident report with USCG within 24 hours.
D) Take a photo of the injury for documentation and ignore it otherwise.

**Best answer: B.** Offer aid, treat it if they allow you, and document it. A minor cut doesn't require a USCG report, but you still note it internally. If it turns into something more later, you have documentation.

### Question 6

You're reviewing your flares during morning inspection and notice one of three is expired. The other two are within date. What's the call?

A) Two good flares is enough. Go ahead with the tour.
B) Replace the expired flare before departing. Your flare count must match COI requirements.
C) Expired flares still work. Use it if needed.
D) Flares are just for nighttime. You don't need them for daytime tours.

**Best answer: B.** Your visual distress signals must meet the quantity and condition requirements on your COI. Replace the expired one. Don't depart with deficient safety equipment.

---

### Question 7

During a tour, you see a vessel in obvious distress about 200 yards away. No one is hailing on the radio. What's your obligation?

A) Not your problem. Focus on your passengers.
B) You have a legal obligation to render assistance if you can do so safely.
C) Call USCG and let them handle it. Don't get involved.
D) Only render assistance if the other vessel specifically asks for help.

**Best answer: B.** Maritime law requires you to render assistance to vessels in distress if you can do so without serious danger to your own vessel and passengers. Hail them on Ch 16, notify USCG, and render aid if safe to do so.

### Question 8

How often is a USCG inspection required for a Subchapter T vessel?

A) Every 6 months
B) Annually
C) Every 2 years
D) Only when the vessel changes ownership

**Best answer: B.** Annual USCG inspection. Don't let it lapse. Schedule proactively.

---

### Question 9

A USCG inspector finds a deficiency during your annual inspection. What's the correct response?

A) Argue the interpretation if you disagree.
B) Fix the deficiency as quickly as possible and request re-inspection.
C) Continue operating while you address it since the inspector didn't say to stop.
D) File an appeal with the USCG district office.

**Best answer: B.** Don't argue during the inspection. Fix it, document the fix, and request re-inspection. If the deficiency is serious, you may be ordered to cease operations until it's corrected.

---

### Question 10

A guest has a medical emergency on board. You've called 911 and are heading back to the dock. A different guest starts recording video and livestreaming the incident. What do you do?

A) Demand they stop recording. It's a privacy violation.
B) Focus on the emergency. You can't and shouldn't try to police phones during a medical situation.
C) Confiscate their phone.
D) Ask them politely to stop, but don't force it.

**Best answer: B.** Your job is the emergency. Trying to control bystanders with phones while managing a medical situation and navigating to the dock is a distraction you can't afford. Focus on what matters: the person who needs help.

### Question 11

What triggers a mandatory USCG MSIS report?

A) Any guest complaint
B) Death, injury beyond first aid, property damage over $2,000, complete loss of vessel, or hazardous discharge
C) Any time 911 is called
D) Only a death or complete vessel loss

**Best answer: B.** Those five triggers require a formal USCG Marine Safety Information System report within 24 hours. When in doubt, report. Over-reporting is always better.
