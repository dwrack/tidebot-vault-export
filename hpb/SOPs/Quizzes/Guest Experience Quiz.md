# Guest Experience Quiz

**Passing score:** 80% (8 of 10 correct)

---

### Question 1

A group arrives and one person hasn't signed their waiver. They say "I'll sign it on the boat." What do you do?

A) Let them board and have them sign while you prep for departure.
B) Hold everyone at the dock until that person signs. No one boards until all waivers are complete.
C) Let the rest of the group board while that person signs on the dock, then let them on.
D) Pull up a generic QR code so they can sign quickly.

**Best answer: C.** The group can board, but the unsigned person stays on the dock until their booking-specific waiver is signed. No generic QR codes. No boarding without a signature.

---

### Question 2

You're running a sunset tour and the group asks if they can play their own music on the Bluetooth speaker. What's the best response?

A) "Sorry, we have a preset playlist."
B) "Go for it, it's your party."
C) "Sure, connect and play what you want. Just keep the volume reasonable so I can communicate if I need to."
D) "I'll handle the music."

**Best answer: C.** It's their experience. Let them own it. But you need to be able to communicate over the music for safety reasons. Set that expectation up front.

---

### Question 3

A guest is clearly intoxicated before the tour even starts. They're wobbly but not aggressive. What do you do?

A) Let them on. They're an adult.
B) Refuse to let them board. Intoxicated passengers are a safety liability on the water.
C) Let them on but monitor them closely.
D) Ask their group to keep an eye on them.

**Best answer: C.** Unless they're a danger to themselves or others, refusing a paying guest who's part of a booked group creates more problems than it solves. But you monitor them. If they become a safety issue on the water, you address it directly. If they can't safely board the vessel (can't walk down the dock), then B is the right call.

---

### Question 4

Mid-tour, a guest asks you about the pedaling. "Do we have to pedal?" What's the best response?

A) "Yeah, that's kind of the whole point of a pedal barge!"
B) "Nope, the boat is fully motorized. Pedaling is totally optional. You can just sit back and enjoy."
C) "The pedaling powers the boat, so we need at least a few people going."
D) "We encourage everyone to pedal for the full experience."

**Best answer: B.** The boat is fully motorized. Pedaling is optional. Don't pitch the pedaling as a feature. Some guests don't like it and some can't do it. Let them relax.

---

### Question 5

A family with two kids (ages 8 and 10) boards. When do you address PFDs for the children?

A) During the general safety briefing, mention it along with everything else.
B) Before the general briefing. Get the kids fitted in PFDs first, then do the full briefing.
C) After the briefing, pull the parents aside.
D) Kids don't need PFDs unless conditions are rough.

**Best answer: B.** Kids under 13 are required to wear PFDs for the entire trip. Get them fitted before the briefing so they're already wearing them when you address the group. It also signals to the parents that you take this seriously.

---

### Question 6

A guest approaches you after the tour and says they had a bad experience because the boat was too rocky. The conditions were within safe operating limits. What do you do?

A) "Sorry about that. Weather is unpredictable."
B) "The conditions were within safe limits, so there wasn't really a problem."
C) Apologize genuinely, ask what would have made it better, and note it for future tours in similar conditions.
D) Offer them a refund on the spot.

**Best answer: C.** Their experience was real even if the conditions were technically safe. Acknowledge it, learn from it (maybe warn future guests when conditions are on the choppier side), and note it. Don't promise refunds on the spot. That's a management decision.

---

### Question 7

You're between tours and have 20 minutes. The next group is a corporate team of 15. How do you prep?

A) Same prep as every tour. Corporate groups don't need anything special.
B) Quick tidy of the boat, check the booking notes for any special requests, mentally prepare for a different energy than the last group.
C) Set up a formal welcome with name tags and a briefing packet.
D) Call the booking contact and ask what they expect.

**Best answer: B.** Corporate groups often have different expectations than a birthday party. Check the booking notes. Make sure the boat is clean. Think about how to set the tone for a professional group. You don't need name tags, just awareness.

---

### Question 8

During the safety briefing, a guest shouts "We've heard this before, let's go!" What do you do?

A) Skip the rest. They get the idea.
B) Laugh it off and keep going. Cover every point.
C) Stop and firmly tell them you're required to do this.
D) Speed through the remaining points to get it over with.

**Best answer: B.** Don't skip anything, but don't make it a confrontation either. Keep your tone light, keep moving through the points, and cover everything. "Almost done, 30 more seconds" usually works.

---

### Question 9

A guest asks to take a group photo with you as the captain. You're in the middle of prepping for departure. What do you do?

A) "Sorry, I'm busy right now."
B) Take 10 seconds for the photo. It means something to them and costs you almost nothing.
C) "We can do one after the tour."
D) Tell a crew member to take it.

**Best answer: B.** Ten seconds. That photo will end up on social media, in a group chat, at a minimum in someone's camera roll as a memory of the trip. It's free marketing and good hospitality. If you're genuinely in the middle of something safety-critical, C is fine.

---

### Question 10

A guest on a tour leaves a 1-star review saying the captain was "rude and dismissive." You know you had a normal interaction with them. How should you think about this?

A) They're just a difficult person. Ignore it.
B) Reflect honestly. What might have felt dismissive from their perspective, even if you didn't intend it?
C) Respond publicly defending yourself.
D) Ask management to remove the review.

**Best answer: B.** Most negative reviews have a kernel of truth, even if the severity is exaggerated. Maybe you were short during the briefing. Maybe you didn't make eye contact. Use it as a data point, not an indictment. Management handles the response.
