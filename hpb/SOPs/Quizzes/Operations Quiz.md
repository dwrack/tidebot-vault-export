### Question 1

You arrive for your morning inspection and the fuel level is at a quarter tank. Your first tour is in 90 minutes. What do you do?

A) Run the tour. A quarter tank is enough for one trip.
B) Top off the fuel before the first tour. Don't start the day low.
C) Call management and ask what to do.
D) Run the first tour and fuel up between tours.

**Best answer: B.** Top off before the first tour. Starting the day at a quarter tank means you're gambling on whether you have enough for the full route, especially if conditions change and you need to adjust. Don't start the day behind.

### Question 2

During your morning inspection, the VHF radio turns on but you're getting heavy static on Ch 16. What do you do?

A) It's fine. Static is normal.
B) Do not depart. Troubleshoot or replace the radio. You need clear Ch 16 reception.
C) Switch to another channel and monitor that instead.
D) Use your cell phone as a backup and run the tour.

**Best answer: B.** Clear VHF Ch 16 reception is a legal requirement. Heavy static means you might miss a distress call or be unable to communicate in an emergency. Fix it before departure.

### Question 3

You're running back-to-back tours with 30 minutes between them. The first tour ran 10 minutes over because of late arrival. What takes priority in the turnaround?

A) Getting the next group on the boat as fast as possible to make up time.
B) Cleaning the boat and running through the post-tour checklist properly, even if the next group waits a few minutes.
C) Skipping the morning inspection since you already did it once today.
D) Doing a quick wipe-down and loading guests immediately.

**Best answer: B.** The post-tour checklist exists for a reason. A 5-minute wait for the next group is better than boarding them onto a boat that hasn't been properly reset. You never skip the between-tour checklist.

### Question 4

FareHarbor shows 12 guests booked but only 10 show up. The booking contact says "two more are coming, they're 15 minutes away." Your departure time is now. What do you do?

A) Wait 15 minutes. The customer paid for 12.
B) Depart on time. Late arrivals miss the boat.
C) Wait 5-10 minutes max, communicate with the group, then make a call based on your next tour timing.
D) Leave immediately and don't wait at all.

**Best answer: C.** Be reasonable but not a pushover. A short wait is fine if it doesn't cascade into your next tour. Communicate with the group so they know the situation. If you have another tour right after and can't afford the delay, you depart on time.

### Question 5

After a tour, you notice the bilge pump ran more than usual during the trip. Everything seems fine now. What do you do?

A) Note it mentally and keep an eye on it tomorrow.
B) Report it to management immediately. Unusual bilge pump activity could indicate a developing problem.
C) Run the bilge until it's dry and don't worry about it.
D) Cancel the rest of the day's tours.

**Best answer: B.** Unusual bilge activity could be nothing, or it could be the early sign of a leak or fitting issue. Report it same day so it can be investigated before the next tour day. You don't need to cancel tours, but you need to flag it.

### Question 6

A guest calls the day before their tour asking if they can bring extra people beyond their booking. What do you tell them?

A) "Sure, just show up with them."
B) "You'll need to update your booking in FareHarbor so we have accurate headcount and waivers. We also can't exceed capacity."
C) "No, sorry. We can't change bookings."
D) "Just bring them and we'll figure it out at the dock."

**Best answer: B.** All guests need to be on the booking for waiver and headcount purposes, and you can never exceed vessel capacity. Direct them to update in FareHarbor so everything is tracked properly.

### Question 7

It's the end of the day. You secured the vessel, stored gear, and cleaned the boat. But you forgot to check the fuel level. What do you do?

A) Head home. You'll check it in the morning.
B) Go back and check it. Note the level so the next captain knows.
C) Text the next captain and tell them to check it.
D) It doesn't matter. Fuel is always checked in the morning anyway.

**Best answer: B.** Part of the end-of-day sign-off is fuel level check and noting if a fill is needed. If the morning captain shows up to a near-empty tank with a tour in 90 minutes, that's a problem you could have flagged.

### Question 8

You're reviewing FareHarbor the night before and see a booking with a note: "Guest is wheelchair-accessible and will need help boarding." What do you do?

A) Nothing special. Help them when they arrive.
B) Think through the boarding process. Is the dock ADA-accessible? Where will the wheelchair be stored? Do you need an extra set of hands? Plan it now, not when they're standing on the dock.
C) Call the guest and suggest they reschedule to a more accessible activity.
D) Contact management and ask them to handle it.

**Best answer: B.** Plan ahead. Think through every step: arrival, boarding, seating, disembarkation. If you need extra help, arrange it the night before. The guest should feel like you're prepared for them, not scrambling.

### Question 9

Your engine makes an unusual sound during the morning warm-up. It goes away after 30 seconds. What do you do?

A) It went away, so it's fine. Run the tour.
B) Note it, monitor it during the tour, and report it after.
C) Report it before departing. An unusual engine sound should be evaluated before you take passengers out.
D) Cancel the day's tours.

**Best answer: C.** An unusual sound that "goes away" is still an unusual sound. Report it before you take passengers out. It might be nothing, or it might be the early warning of a failure at the worst possible time. Captains who ignore weird engine behavior on passenger vessels are gambling with other people's safety.

### Question 10

You're halfway through the post-tour closeout and the next tour's guests start showing up early, 20 minutes before their time. They're hovering on the dock, eager to board. What do you do?

A) Let them board early while you finish closing out.
B) Acknowledge them warmly, let them know you'll be ready shortly, and finish your closeout properly before beginning check-in.
C) Rush through the remaining closeout steps to accommodate them.
D) Ignore them until their scheduled time.

**Best answer: B.** Greet them so they know you see them, but don't rush your closeout. Skipping steps because a group showed up early is how things get missed. "Hey folks, welcome! I'll be ready for you in about 10 minutes. Feel free to hang out on the dock."
