# Culture & Values Quiz

### Question 1

A guest asks you to recommend a good restaurant after the tour. What's the best response?

A) "I'm not really supposed to recommend things."
B) "Check Google, there are a ton of places around here."
C) Give them an honest recommendation of somewhere you actually like nearby.
D) Recommend whatever restaurant has a partnership deal with the company.

**Best answer: C.** Be real. People remember captains who gave them a genuine recommendation, not a scripted one. You're a local on the water, act like it.

---

### Question 2

You notice the boat has a small maintenance issue that doesn't affect today's tours but will need attention soon. What do you do?

A) Wait until it becomes a problem, then report it.
B) Report it to management the same day.
C) Fix it yourself without telling anyone.
D) Mention it to the next captain and let them handle it.

**Best answer: B.** Own your patch. Maintenance issues get flagged same day. Don't leave it for someone else to discover.

---

### Question 3

A group of 8 shows up and they're clearly in a low-key mood. Your last group was a wild bachelorette party. What do you do?

A) Try to hype them up since that's what worked last time.
B) Match their energy. Keep it chill, read the room.
C) Stick to a standard script regardless of the group.
D) Ask them if they want to reschedule since they don't seem excited.

**Best answer: B.** Be real. A chill family float and a bachelorette rager are not the same tour. You're reading the room, not running a script.

---

### Question 4

After your shift, you discovered a faster boarding process that saved 5 minutes. What's the right move?

A) Keep it to yourself. It's your competitive edge.
B) Share it in the team channel so other captains can use it.
C) Wait until someone asks you about it.
D) Write a formal proposal and submit it to management.

**Best answer: B.** Get it out of your head. A quick message in the channel takes 30 seconds and helps the whole team.

---

### Question 5

A guest is being pushy about bringing one extra person beyond the booked headcount. "Come on, what's one more?" What do you do?

A) Let them on. One extra person won't matter.
B) Hold the line. Capacity limits are capacity limits.
C) Call management and let them make the decision.
D) Charge them extra at the dock and let them on.

**Best answer: B.** Safety is the job. Capacity limits exist for a legal and safety reason. The answer is no, said politely but firmly.

---

### Question 6

You show up to your shift and the boat is dirty from the previous captain. What do you do?

A) Run the tour anyway. It's not your mess.
B) Clean it, run the tour, and then message the team about it.
C) Refuse to run the tour until someone else cleans it.
D) Take a photo, send it to management, and wait for instructions.

**Best answer: B.** Own your patch. The guests showing up in 30 minutes don't care whose mess it is. Clean it, handle it, and then address it with the team so it doesn't happen again.

---

### Question 7

You find out a better route that avoids some wake zones. What's the best way to share it?

A) Voice memo in the team channel.
B) Formal written proposal with maps.
C) Wait for the next team meeting.
D) Just tell the next captain you see in person.

**Best answer: A.** Any format works, but a voice memo or quick text in the channel is the fastest way to get useful info to the whole team. Don't over-formalize it. Don't save it for a meeting that might be weeks away.

---

### Question 8

Mid-tour, you realize you forgot to check the fire extinguisher during morning inspection. Nothing is wrong, but you skipped the check. What do you do?

A) Check it now and move on. No harm done.
B) Finish the tour and check it after. It's probably fine.
C) Check it now, and report to management that you missed the morning check.
D) Don't mention it. Nothing happened.

**Best answer: C.** Safety is the job. Check it now, but also flag that you skipped a step. Skipping inspection items is how problems get normalized.

---

### Question 9

A captain you're friendly with has been showing up 10-15 minutes late consistently. It hasn't caused any missed tours yet. What do you do?

A) It's not your business. Let management figure it out.
B) Mention it directly to them, captain to captain.
C) Report them to management immediately.
D) Start showing up late too since it seems fine.

**Best answer: B.** Be real. Say it directly first. If it keeps happening, then it's a management conversation. But don't ignore it, because showing up ready matters.

---

### Question 10

You're offered a shift on a day when you're exhausted and not feeling 100%. Not sick, just tired from a long week. What do you do?

A) Take the shift. Money is money.
B) Take the shift but plan to take it easy.
C) Decline the shift. You're responsible for people's safety and you need to be sharp.
D) Take the shift but ask another captain to be on standby.

**Best answer: C.** Respect the work. You're operating a vessel with passengers and alcohol. If you're not dialed in, you're a risk. Better to decline than to be the captain who wasn't paying attention when it mattered.
