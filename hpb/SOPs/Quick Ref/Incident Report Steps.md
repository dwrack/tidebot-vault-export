# Incident Report Steps

When something goes wrong: safety first, then document everything.

---

> [!warning] Any injury, near-miss, equipment failure, or property damage triggers this process. Do not skip steps.

### Immediate (At the Scene)
1. Ensure all guests and crew are safe
2. Call 911 if there is any injury or medical emergency
3. Hail USCG on VHF Ch 16 if the situation involves vessel distress, collision, grounding, or man overboard
4. Provide first aid within your training level
5. Secure the vessel: stop engines, anchor or tie off if needed
6. Do not admit liability or fault ("I'm sorry this happened" is fine, "It was our fault" is not)

### Document (At the Scene)
7. Photograph everything: damage, injuries (with permission), weather, vessel position
8. Get names and contact info for all witnesses
9. Note the time, GPS location, weather conditions, water conditions
10. Record passenger count and crew on board

### Report (Within 24 Hours)
11. Complete the internal incident report template
12. Notify David Rack immediately by phone, then send written summary
13. Notify insurance carrier
14. File USCG MSIS report if required: death, injury beyond first aid, property damage over $2,000, complete loss of vessel, or hazardous discharge

> [!tip] When in doubt about whether USCG reporting is required, report it. Over-reporting is always better than under-reporting.
