# Role Guide: Captain

## What This Job Actually Is

You're the captain of a motorized vessel carrying paying passengers, most of whom are drinking. On paper, that's a USCG-licensed operator running a Subchapter T small passenger vessel. In practice, it means you're simultaneously a boat captain, a party host, a safety officer, and occasionally a therapist for a bachelorette party having a meltdown.

The boat is fully motorized. Guests sometimes pedal, but propulsion is all engine. Don't pitch the pedaling. Customers don't care about it and some actively dislike it.

## Requirements

| Credential | Detail |
|---|---|
| USCG License | OUPV / Six-Pack minimum. 100-ton preferred. |
| CPR / First Aid | Must be current before first shift. |
| TWIC Card | Required for our operating area. |
| Background Check | Cleared before first shift. |
| Drug Test | Per USCG requirements, completed before first shift. |

You're an independent contractor (1099). That means you handle your own taxes, maintain your own certifications, and keep your license current. If your license lapses, you don't work. We're not going to chase you on renewal dates.

## The Guest Experience Philosophy

Read the room. A 2pm birthday party for a 30-year-old and a 6pm corporate team outing are completely different tours even though the boat and route are identical. Your job is to match the energy of the group, not impose your own.

Some groups want a captain who's cracking jokes and playing DJ. Some want you to point out landmarks and talk about the lake. Some want you to mostly leave them alone. All three are valid. The skill is reading which one you've got within the first 5 minutes.

What every group has in common: they want to feel like they're in good hands. Confidence without arrogance. You know the boat, you know the water, you're paying attention.

Things that kill the vibe fast:
- Being on your phone while guests are on board
- Seeming annoyed or checked out
- Being overly rigid about rules that don't matter (someone standing up briefly to take a photo is fine, someone standing on the railing is not)
- Making the safety briefing feel like a punishment

## Prohibited Behaviors

These are termination-level. Not a warning, not a conversation.

- Operating under the influence of alcohol or drugs
- Allowing passengers to board without signed waivers
- Exceeding vessel capacity for any reason
- Operating in conditions that violate weather hold thresholds without authorization
- Skipping the pre-departure safety briefing
- Skipping the morning vessel inspection
- Leaving the helm while underway
- Using a personal phone while operating the vessel

## Violation Protocol

Minor issues (late to a shift, forgot to restock, left the boat messy): verbal conversation first, documented if it repeats.

Safety violations: one and done. If you skip a waiver check, ignore a weather threshold, or operate impaired, that's your last shift. Someone died on one of our boats at another location. We don't have room for captains who treat safety as optional.

Guest experience issues (rude to a guest, phone use during tour, checking out mentally): documented conversation, one chance to correct. Second time, you're off the rotation.

## Pay and Scheduling

Pay rate and structure will be discussed during onboarding. Scheduling is done through the team channel. If you can't make a shift, you're responsible for finding coverage or giving enough notice for management to cover it.

No-shows without communication are treated as voluntary resignation. If something comes up, just say so. We're not unreasonable, but we need to know.
