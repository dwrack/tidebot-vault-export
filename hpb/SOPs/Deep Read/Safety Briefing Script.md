# Safety Briefing Script

## How to Use This

This isn't meant to be read word-for-word off a card. The goal is to internalize the content so you can deliver it naturally, like you're talking to the group, not reading to them. Cover every point, but make it yours.

The briefing happens before departure, after all guests have boarded. Keep it under 2 minutes. Friendly but clear. You want people to actually listen, which means you can't sound like a flight attendant on autopilot.

## The Briefing

Hey everyone, welcome aboard. Before we head out, I need to run through a few things. Takes about 90 seconds.

**Life jackets.** They're right here [point to location]. Kids under 13 are required to wear one for the entire trip. Adults, they're available if you want one. If you'd like help getting one fitted, just ask.

**Stay seated while we're moving.** Standing up briefly for a photo is fine. Climbing on the railing or leaning over the side is not. If I ask you to sit down, please do it the first time.

**Emergency equipment.** The throwable life ring is right there [point]. Fire extinguisher is here [point]. You don't need to touch any of it. That's my job. But now you know where it is.

**If you hear the horn sounding continuously,** that means stop what you're doing and follow my instructions immediately. That's our signal for an emergency. You'll probably never hear it, but if you do, it's not a suggestion.

**No swimming off the boat.** I know the water looks inviting. It's not. Stay on the vessel.

That's it. We're going to have a great time out there. Questions before we go?

## Key Points You Must Hit

No matter how you word it, these five points must be covered every single time:

1. PFD location and requirement for children under 13
2. Stay seated while underway
3. Emergency equipment locations (throwable PFD, fire extinguisher)
4. Continuous horn = emergency, follow crew instructions
5. No swimming off the vessel

## Delivery Tips

**Make eye contact.** Look at people, not at the water or the dashboard.

**Slow down.** New captains tend to rush through it because they feel awkward. Two minutes of clear safety info is better than 45 seconds of mumbling.

**Don't apologize for it.** "Sorry, I have to do this required safety thing" undermines the whole message. Just deliver it with confidence. Guests respect a captain who takes safety seriously.

**Handle the talkers.** If a group is chatting through your briefing, pause and wait. "Hey folks, I need about 90 seconds of your attention, then we're good to go." Most groups will quiet down. If they don't, make eye contact with the loudest person and keep going. You delivered it. That's what matters.

**Kids.** If there are kids on board, address the PFD requirement directly to the parents, not the kids. "Kids under 13 need to wear a life jacket for the whole trip. We've got them right here, let's get them fitted before we head out."

## What Not to Do

- Don't skip it because the group "seems experienced"
- Don't let a group talk you out of it ("We've been on boats before, we're good")
- Don't do it while the boat is already moving
- Don't make it a joke. Light tone is fine. Making safety the punchline isn't.
- Don't read it off your phone

Every guest, every tour, every time. No exceptions.
