# Weather Hold Protocol

## The Decision Framework

Weather decisions on the water are judgment calls, but they're structured judgment calls. Some conditions are hard stops. Some are gray areas where the captain makes the call. The point of this document is to make sure you know which is which.

The default bias should always be toward caution. Nobody ever got fired for cancelling a tour because the weather looked dicey. People have lost their careers (and worse) for pushing out when they shouldn't have.

## Hard Thresholds

These are not captain's discretion. These are automatic cancellations.

| Condition | Action | Refund |
|---|---|---|
| Lightning within 5 miles | Cancel immediately. If underway, return to dock. | Full refund |
| Visibility below 0.5 miles | Cancel. Do not depart. | Full refund |
| USCG advisory issued | Cancel. | Full refund |

Lightning within 5 miles means you can see it or a weather app puts it there. You don't wait for a direct strike on the water. You don't wait for the storm to "pass through." You cancel or return.

Visibility below half a mile means fog, heavy rain, or any condition where you can't clearly see other vessel traffic at a safe distance. If you're squinting, it's too low.

A USCG advisory for your operating area means you don't go out. Full stop.

## Gray Areas: Captain's Discretion

| Condition | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Wind 15-20 mph sustained | Likely fine. Monitor. Consider route adjustments to stay in protected water. |
| Wind 20+ mph sustained | Captain's discretion, but the default should be cancel. Guest comfort drops off fast above 20. |
| Rain without lightning | Tour goes out. Rain is fine. Warn guests in advance so they can dress for it. |
| Choppy conditions | Consider guest comfort. A bachelor party might love it. A family with small kids won't. |
| Forecast uncertainty | If the forecast shows a 50/50 shot of bad weather during your tour window, make a call 60-90 minutes before departure. Don't wait until guests are on the dock. |

The wind threshold is where most gray area decisions happen. At 15 mph, most guests won't notice. At 20, the boat handles differently and guests start to feel it. Above 20, you're fighting the wind the whole time and guests aren't having fun even if it's technically safe.

Trust your gut. If you're debating whether to go out, that debate is usually the answer.

## Communication

When you make a weather hold or cancellation decision:

1. Document the conditions (wind speed, visibility, radar screenshot if possible)
2. Notify management immediately
3. Notify all booked guests: offer reschedule first, refund if they prefer
4. Log the cancellation reason in FareHarbor
5. Full refund for any weather cancellation, no partial credits

Don't let guests pressure you into going out. Some will. "It's just a little wind" from someone who's never been on a boat in 20mph wind is not useful input. You're the captain. The decision is yours.

## Seasonal Patterns in Houston

**Spring (March-May):** Afternoon thunderstorms are common. Morning tours are usually safer bets. Check radar frequently from noon onward.

**Summer (June-August):** Heat is the main issue. Isolated afternoon storms pop up fast with little warning. Mornings are calmer on the water but still hot. Hydration matters for you too, not just guests.

**Fall (September-November):** Generally the best weather for tours. Occasional cold fronts bring gusty north winds. Check forecast for front timing.

**Winter (December-February):** Fewer tours, but when they happen, cold and wind are the main concerns. Vinyl enclosures help with comfort but don't change safety thresholds.

## If You're Already on the Water

If conditions deteriorate while you're underway:

1. Reduce speed and head for the nearest safe dock or harbor
2. Have all guests put on PFDs if conditions are rough
3. Hail USCG on Ch 16 if you're in distress
4. Continuous horn signals if visibility drops suddenly
5. Don't try to "finish the tour." Get back to the dock.

A shortened tour with a full refund is always better than a tour that ends with an incident report.
