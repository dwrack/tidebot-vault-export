# Safety & Compliance Overview

## Why This Document Exists

This vessel operates under USCG Subchapter T, which governs small passenger vessels. That's not a suggestion or a best practice. It's federal law. The Coast Guard can board you at any time, inspect everything, and shut you down on the spot if you're not in compliance.

A passenger died on one of our vessels at another location. That's not a hypothetical. Every requirement in this document, every piece of equipment on the checklist, every procedure we follow exists because the alternative is unacceptable.

## Subchapter T: What It Means for You

Subchapter T (46 CFR Part 175-185) applies to small passenger vessels carrying more than 6 passengers. It covers:

- Vessel construction and stability
- Fire protection
- Lifesaving equipment
- Navigation equipment
- Operations (manning, watchkeeping, drills)
- Inspection and certification

As a captain, you don't need to memorize the CFR. But you need to understand what's required on your vessel and why, because a USCG inspector will ask you questions and expect competent answers.

## Required Equipment

### Life-Saving

| Item | Requirement | Why |
|---|---|---|
| PFDs (Type I, II, or III) | One per max capacity + crew | Everyone gets one if they need it |
| Throwable PFD (Type IV) | Minimum 1, on deck | Can be thrown to someone in the water without leaving the helm |
| Ring life buoy | If vessel 26 ft+ | Same principle, better for distance throws |

### Fire Safety

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Fire extinguishers | Per COI specs, charged, tags current |
| Flammable storage | Compliant containers, away from ignition |

Fire on a boat is about as bad as it gets. Extinguishers aren't decoration. Know where they are. Know how to use them. Check the gauges and tags every morning.

### Navigation

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Nav lights | Red port, green starboard, white stern. All functional. |
| Horn / whistle | Sound-producing device, tested before departure |
| VHF radio | Operational, Ch 16 monitored at all times |
| Anchor | Appropriate for vessel size |

### Documentation

| Item | Location |
|---|---|
| Certificate of Inspection (COI) | Posted visibly on the vessel |
| Certificate of Documentation | On board |
| Captain's USCG license | On your person |
| Stability letter | On board (if applicable) |
| Manning certificate | On board |

## The Certificate of Inspection (COI)

The COI is your operating license. It specifies:
- Maximum passenger capacity
- Required crew (manning)
- Authorized route and operating conditions
- Required safety equipment

If the COI says 2 crew minimum, you run 2 crew. If the COI authorizes a specific route, you stay on that route. Deviating from COI requirements is a federal violation.

The COI must be posted where passengers can see it. Not stuffed in a drawer.

## Annual USCG Inspection

Every year, a USCG marine inspector will conduct a thorough examination of the vessel. They'll check:

1. Hull integrity and structural condition
2. All safety equipment (condition, quantity, expiration dates)
3. Fire protection systems
4. Electrical systems
5. Engine and propulsion
6. Navigation equipment
7. Documentation
8. Crew qualifications

**Before the inspection:** Run through the Compliance Inspection Checklist (Quick Ref folder). Fix anything that's deficient. Don't wait for the inspector to find it.

**During the inspection:** Be present, be helpful, answer questions honestly. If something fails, don't argue. Fix it and request re-inspection.

**After the inspection:** Address any deficiencies immediately. Keep the inspection report on file permanently.

## Texas State Requirements

Texas doesn't add much beyond what USCG already requires for an inspected vessel, but verify independently:
- State vessel registration (if applicable alongside federal documentation)
- Compliance with Texas Water Safety Act
- Captain holds USCG license (state boater education alone is not sufficient)

## The Bottom Line

Compliance isn't paperwork. It's the system that keeps people alive. Every item on every checklist exists because someone, somewhere, found out what happens when you skip it. In our case, we didn't have to imagine.

If you ever feel like a safety requirement is slowing you down, that's the requirement working as intended.
