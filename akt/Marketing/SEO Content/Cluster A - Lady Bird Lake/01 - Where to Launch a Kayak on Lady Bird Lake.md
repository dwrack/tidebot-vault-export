# Where to Launch a Kayak on Lady Bird Lake

If you've got your own kayak and you want to put it in the water in downtown Austin, you have more options than most people realize. The catch is that not all of them are equally good, and a couple of them are basically traps on a busy weekend.

Here's the honest list, ranked by how much I'd actually use them.

## 1. Festival Beach (1730 Bergman Ave) — best free public option

This is the launch most locals use. It's a real concrete ramp, the parking lot is free, and you can pull right up to load and unload.

**The catch:** the parking lot fills by 10am on weekends in summer. If you're not there by 9, you're parking on side streets or skipping it.

**Best for:** locals, weekday paddlers, anyone with their own boat who got an early start.

## 2. Holly Shores (off East 5th near Pleasant Valley)

Smaller, less crowded, but no developed ramp. You're carrying your boat across grass to a small beach. Fine for a kayak. Tougher with a paddle board if you don't have a cart.

**Best for:** people who don't mind a 50-foot carry and want to dodge the Festival Beach scene.

## 3. Auditorium Shores (800 W Riverside Drive)

West side of the lake, closer to downtown bars and the Congress Bridge. The launch is a small grassy slope into the water. Parking is paid.

**Best for:** visitors staying at downtown hotels who can walk over with a rental.

## 4. Roy G. Guerrero Park boat ramp (east end)

Far east end. Concrete ramp, free parking, almost no crowd. The downside is you're starting from the quietest end of the lake, so you'll paddle into the busier zone if you want skyline views.

**Best for:** longer downriver paddles past Longhorn Dam, or anyone who wants the calmest launch.

## 5. Private outfitter launches (including ours)

If you don't have your own boat, the simplest play is to walk into a rental shop with a launch on-site. We run [Austin Kayak Tours](/rental/) at 200 E Riverside Drive: parking is free for guests, restrooms on-site, gear included, and you skip the carry.

This is what we'd recommend to a visitor without a vehicle. Drive in, paddle out, drive home.

## What to know about all of them

- **No alcohol** anywhere on the water (it's all city park territory)
- **Life jackets required** for kids, recommended for adults
- **No swimming** at any launch, including the beachy ones
- **Sunset cutoff:** plan to be off the water about 30 minutes after sunset

## FAQ

**Is there a public boat ramp on Lady Bird Lake?**
Yes. Festival Beach and Roy G. Guerrero Park have proper ramps. Smaller launches are scattered along the shore.

**Can I launch a kayak from anywhere on the trail?**
No. The shore between official access points is technically off-limits and city ordinance prohibits launching from random spots.

**Do I need a permit to launch on Lady Bird Lake?**
Not for personal recreational kayaking. Commercial operators (tours, rentals) need permits.

**Is there parking at the kayak launches?**
Yes at Festival Beach (free, fills early), Roy G. Guerrero (free), Auditorium Shores (paid), and at private operators.

**What time do the launches close?**
Public launches are open during park hours, typically dawn to dusk. Roy G. Guerrero stays open later.
