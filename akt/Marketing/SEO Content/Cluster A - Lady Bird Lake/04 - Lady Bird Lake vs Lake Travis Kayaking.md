# Lady Bird Lake vs. Lake Travis: Which Should You Kayak?

Both lakes are technically the Colorado River dammed at different points. They look almost nothing alike. Pick the wrong one for your trip and you'll spend a lot of time wishing you'd gone to the other.

Here's the honest comparison.

## Quick answer

- **Visiting Austin and have a half-day downtown:** Lady Bird Lake.
- **Have a full day, a vehicle, and want a swimming hole experience:** Lake Travis.
- **Want the bat colony or the skyline:** Lady Bird, no question.
- **Want to swim and cliff jump:** Lake Travis.
- **Beginner with no experience:** Lady Bird Lake.

## Lady Bird Lake at a glance

- **Where:** Downtown Austin
- **Size:** 416 acres, roughly 6 miles long
- **Water:** Calm, no motorized boats allowed
- **Vibe:** Urban paddle with skyline backdrop
- **Best for:** Beginners, tourists, sunset paddlers, bat watching
- **Cost:** Free with your boat, [rentals from $20/hr](/rental/), [tours from $50](/austin-skyline-tour/)
- **Drive from downtown:** 0 minutes (it's right there)

## Lake Travis at a glance

- **Where:** Northwest of Austin, 30-50 min from downtown depending on the launch
- **Size:** 19,000+ acres at full pool, 64 miles long
- **Water:** Open water, motorized boats allowed (and there are a lot)
- **Vibe:** Hill Country recreation lake
- **Best for:** Day trippers, swimmers, locals with their own boats, parties
- **Cost:** Park entrance fees, fewer rental options on-water
- **Drive from downtown:** 30-45 min depending on traffic

## Beginner-friendliness

**Lady Bird Lake wins easily.** The water is flat. There are no boat wakes. If you flip (you won't), you're 50 feet from shore.

**Lake Travis is doable** but the wakes from passing motorboats can rock you, and the water gets choppy on windy afternoons. Beginners survive, but they don't always enjoy it.

## Crowds

**Lady Bird Lake:** Crowded near sunset on summer weekends, but the crowd is paddleboards and rowers, not motorboat parties.

**Lake Travis:** On a Saturday in summer, the lake is wall-to-wall pontoon boats and jet skis. Hippie Hollow and Devil's Cove are zoos. If you go on a Tuesday in October, you'll have it nearly to yourself.

## What you'll see

**Lady Bird Lake:**
- Downtown Austin skyline
- Frost Bank Tower, the Independent, the Austonian
- The Congress Avenue Bridge bat colony (March-October)
- Turtles, herons, the boardwalk
- Other paddlers and rowing teams

**Lake Travis:**
- Limestone cliffs and Hill Country shoreline
- Pace Bend Park sunsets
- Devil's Cove party scene (Memorial Day to Labor Day)
- Cliff divers at Pace Bend
- Bigger water, longer views

## Swimming

**Lady Bird Lake:** No. Swimming is banned. Period.

**Lake Travis:** Yes, and this is one of the best reasons to go. You can swim, dive off rocks, lounge in the cove with a floatie. If swimming is the point, this is your lake.

## Photography

**Lady Bird Lake** wins for skyline shots. There's no other place in Texas where you can paddle with a real downtown skyline reflected on flat water.

**Lake Travis** wins for landscape shots: limestone bluffs, sunset over open water, Hill Country panoramas.

## Difficulty getting there

**Lady Bird Lake:** Walk from any downtown hotel. Free parking at multiple launches. Rental shops with on-site parking. Easy.

**Lake Travis:** You need a car. Multiple parks (Mansfield Dam, Hippie Hollow, Pace Bend) and no single best launch. Parking fees vary. Plan an extra hour each way for traffic on weekends.

## Which one we recommend (and why)

If you've got 2-4 hours, you're a tourist, and you want to come home with a good story: **Lady Bird Lake**. The bat tour or the skyline tour at sunset is the version of Austin most visitors miss.

If you've got a full day, a car, and you want a swim-and-paddle combo: **Lake Travis**. Bring a cooler, a floatie, and plan for the drive.

If you live here and you've never done either: do both. They're different enough that you don't have to choose.

## Common questions

**Is Lady Bird Lake or Lake Travis better for kayaking?**
Different goals. Lady Bird Lake for downtown views and beginners. Lake Travis for swimming and full-day trips.

**Can you kayak Lake Travis as a beginner?**
Yes, but pick a calm day and stay near shore. Boat wakes can be rough.

**Is Lake Travis worth the drive from downtown?**
For a full day with swimming, yes. For a 2-hour paddle, no.

**Which lake has better sunsets?**
Both are good. Lake Travis has Hill Country open-water sunsets. Lady Bird Lake has skyline-against-the-sky sunsets. We'd pick Lady Bird, but it depends what you're after.

**Where can I kayak in Austin without driving?**
Lady Bird Lake. It's downtown, with [tours](/austin-skyline-tour/) and [rentals](/rental/) at multiple launches.
