# Kayaking on Barton Creek: When and Where It's Possible

Barton Creek is the local Austin secret nobody tells visitors about. It runs through the western edge of the city, has limestone bluffs, clear pools, and a Hill Country feel that Lady Bird Lake doesn't have. The catch: it's only paddleable after significant rain. Most of the year, the upper sections are dry rocks.

If you happen to visit in the right window, this is the most interesting urban paddle in the area. Here's how to do it.

## When Barton Creek runs

The creek depends on rainfall. Texas has wet years and dry years.

**Most paddleable:** spring (March-May) after a wet winter, especially if there's been recent significant rain.

**Sometimes paddleable:** late fall after hurricane remnants or autumn rains.

**Almost never paddleable:** July, August, September of a typical year. Drought conditions are common.

**The rule:** if it hasn't rained meaningfully in your area in 2+ weeks, don't drive out. Check the USGS Barton Creek gauge before you go. Above 200 cfs is usually paddleable on the lower sections.

## Where to launch

There are no developed boat ramps on Barton Creek. Access is informal.

**Twin Falls / Sculpture Falls area:** popular put-in for the upper section. Park along Loop 360 or in the greenbelt parking lots, walk in.

**Lower Barton Creek (closer to Barton Springs):** sometimes paddleable from points near the Barton Springs Pool entrance, but access is restricted by the pool itself.

**Realistically:** ask any local Austin paddling shop the day of. Conditions change weekly.

## What it's like

Limestone bluffs on either side. Clear, cool water. Pools deep enough to swim in. Short rapids in places. Almost no crowd because most tourists don't know about it.

You're not in wilderness, you're still in Austin. But the city disappears behind the bluffs and it feels much more remote than Lady Bird Lake.

## Skill level

This is harder than Lady Bird Lake.

- **Short rapids** in places (Class I, easy by whitewater standards but real)
- **Strainers** (downed trees) where you have to navigate around
- **Rocks** at low water levels
- **No motorized rescue** (lake patrol doesn't cover this)

We don't recommend this as a first kayak. If you've kayaked Lady Bird Lake a few times and you want something more involved, this is the next step.

## What to bring

- Real shoes (closed-toe water shoes or trail runners) — there are slick rocks
- Helmet (recommended in higher water)
- Dry bag for everything
- Water and snacks
- A buddy (don't paddle Barton Creek alone)
- Phone with the USGS gauge bookmarked

## Wildlife

- Turtles (everywhere)
- Herons
- Kingfishers
- Snakes (mostly water snakes, harmless)
- Sometimes deer along the bluffs
- Bald eagles in winter

## What we don't run

We don't run Barton Creek tours. The flow is too unpredictable to schedule reliably. If you want a guided experience here, you'd need to find a private outfitter who runs it on call when conditions are right (rare).

## Combining with Barton Springs

The mouth of Barton Creek empties into Lady Bird Lake near Barton Springs Pool. You can sometimes paddle from Lady Bird Lake into the lower creek mouth, depending on water level.

If you want a swim after, Barton Springs Pool itself is a 10-minute walk from the creek mouth.

## What can go wrong

- **Sudden water rise** (flash flood after upstream rain)
- **Low water requiring portage** (carrying boat over rocks)
- **Strainers** (downed trees that you can't paddle over)
- **Getting stuck** without a clean takeout
- **No cell service** in some sections (check before launch)

This is the most "real outdoor" paddle close to Austin. Treat it accordingly.

## Common questions

**Can you kayak Barton Creek in Austin?**
Yes, when it's running. Most reliable in spring after rain.

**When does Barton Creek run?**
Spring (March-May) is most reliable. Other times depend on recent rainfall.

**Is Barton Creek good for beginners?**
Not really. There's some current, occasional rapids, and no formal access. Stick to Lady Bird Lake if you're new.

**Where do you put in for Barton Creek?**
Twin Falls and Sculpture Falls areas are most common. Access is informal.

**Can you paddle from Barton Creek to Lady Bird Lake?**
Sometimes, depending on water level. The mouth of the creek connects to the lake.
