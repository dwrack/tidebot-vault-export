# Best Places to Kayak Near Austin (Outside of Lady Bird Lake)

Lady Bird Lake is the best in-city paddle in Austin. We're biased about that ([here's why](/best-places-to-kayak-in-austin/)). But if you've already done Lady Bird, or you want a different feel, the Hill Country has more options than visitors realize. Here are the ones worth the drive.

## 1. San Marcos River

**Drive from downtown:** 45 min south
**Best for:** day trip, summer heat relief, longer paddles
**Cost:** rental $20-50, tube/kayak combo packages available

The San Marcos is spring-fed, which means the water runs at 72°F and is crystal clear all year. You can see the bottom. It's the closest thing Texas has to a guaranteed-good summer paddle.

**Practical:**
- Multiple outfitters in San Marcos (Lions Club Tube Rental, others)
- Most rentals include shuttle (one-way paddle)
- Upper section is the touristy/college crowd; lower section is calmer
- Weekend summer is a circus; weekday off-season is heaven

## 2. Lake Travis

**Drive from downtown:** 30-45 min northwest
**Best for:** swimming + paddling combo, full-day trips
**Cost:** park entrance fees, fewer on-water rentals

Lake Travis is a real lake. Open water, motorboats allowed, jet skis everywhere on summer weekends. If you want a Hill Country lake feel with cliff diving and swimming holes, this is it.

**Where to launch:**
- **Pace Bend Park:** west side, free except for entrance. Cliff jumping at Cliffjumper Cove.
- **Mansfield Dam Park:** east side, paid park. Easier launch.
- **Hippie Hollow:** clothing-optional. Adults only.
- **Devil's Cove:** Memorial Day to Labor Day, party scene.

**Honest:** if you want quiet, go on a weekday. Saturdays are loud and chaotic.

## 3. Barton Creek

**Drive from downtown:** 15-25 min west
**Best for:** experienced paddlers when conditions allow
**Cost:** free

Barton Creek runs through the western edge of Austin. It's only kayakable when there's been recent rain (typically spring). Most of the year, the upper sections are dry rocks.

**When it's running:**
- Limestone bluffs and clear pools
- Almost no crowd (tourists don't know about it)
- Wildlife: turtles, herons, kingfishers
- Short rapids in places (intermediate skill required)

**Check before you drive:** USGS gauges or just call any local outfitter. If it hasn't rained in 2 weeks, skip it.

## 4. Inks Lake

**Drive from downtown:** 1.5 hours northwest
**Best for:** day trip, state park camping, prettiest water in the area
**Cost:** state park entrance + rental

Inks Lake is a constant-level lake (no fluctuation), so launches are reliable year-round. Granite outcrops, clear water, fewer wakes than Travis.

**What's good:**
- Devil's Waterhole is a popular paddle destination
- State park rents kayaks on-site
- Camping available
- Swimming allowed (unlike Lady Bird)
- 50% less crowded than Travis on a weekend

**Catch:** 1.5 hours each way is a real commitment.

## 5. Colorado River below Longhorn Dam

**Drive from downtown:** 10-15 min east
**Best for:** experienced paddlers wanting a real downriver run
**Cost:** free

The same Colorado River that feeds Lady Bird Lake continues east of downtown after Longhorn Dam. Few people paddle it because access is limited and the river feels more "natural" than the central downtown stretch.

**What's there:**
- Longer downriver runs (10+ miles to McKinney Roughs)
- More wildlife
- Almost no other paddlers
- Some current (not whitewater, but not flat)

**Skill level:** intermediate. Don't try this as a beginner.

## 6. Lake Austin

**Drive from downtown:** 15-20 min west
**Best for:** quiet paddling closer to town
**Cost:** rentals available at multiple docks

Lake Austin is the section of the Colorado between Mansfield Dam and Tom Miller Dam. It's narrow, shaded, and quieter than Travis. Good for an alternative to Lady Bird without the long drive.

**What's good:**
- Calmer than Travis, prettier than Lady Bird
- Close to downtown
- Good for half-day rentals
- Some wakeboard boat traffic but limited

## 7. Lady Bird Johnson State Park (Fredericksburg area)

**Drive from downtown:** 1.5 hours west
**Best for:** weekend trip, peace and quiet
**Cost:** state park entrance

Smaller, quieter, less famous than Travis. Worth combining with a Fredericksburg wine weekend if you're already heading west.

## So how do you choose?

| Goal | Pick |
|---|---|
| In-city paddle | Lady Bird Lake |
| Swim + kayak | Lake Travis |
| Spring-fed clear water | San Marcos |
| Most beautiful nature | Inks Lake or Barton Creek (when running) |
| Quietest | Lake Austin or Roy Guerrero downriver |
| Easiest with no car | Lady Bird Lake (rentals on-site) |
| Best day trip | San Marcos |
| Camping option | Inks Lake |

## Common questions

**Where can I kayak in the Hill Country near Austin?**
Lake Travis, Inks Lake, San Marcos River, Lady Bird Johnson State Park.

**What's the best day trip kayak from Austin?**
San Marcos River, 45 minutes south. Reliable spring-fed water year-round.

**Can you kayak Barton Creek?**
Yes, when it's running (typically spring). Check USGS gauges first.

**Where do locals kayak in Austin?**
Lady Bird Lake on weekday mornings, San Marcos on summer weekends, Inks Lake for camping weekends.

**How far is Lake Travis from downtown Austin?**
30-45 minutes depending on traffic and which launch.
