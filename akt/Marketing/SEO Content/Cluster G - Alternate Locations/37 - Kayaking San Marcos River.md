# Kayaking the San Marcos River: A Day Trip From Austin

If you've got a full day and a vehicle, the San Marcos River is the best Texas paddle within easy reach of Austin. Spring-fed, 72°F year-round, clear enough to see the bottom, lined with cypress trees. Forty-five minutes south of downtown.

Here's how to actually do it.

## Why the San Marcos is special

**Spring-fed.** The water comes from the San Marcos Springs (Aquarena Springs in Texas State University). It runs at a constant 72°F, year-round. Cold in summer (refreshing), warm in winter (compared to other rivers).

**Crystal clear.** You can see the bottom in most sections. Unusual for Texas rivers.

**Year-round flow.** Doesn't depend on rainfall. The springs pump 100+ million gallons per day, every day.

**Cypress-lined.** Big mature bald cypress trees along the banks. Feels distinctly different from Hill Country lakes.

## Two main sections

### Upper San Marcos (Sewell Park to City Park)

The "tubing" section. Texas State University students dominate this on summer weekends. Loud, crowded, alcohol-fueled in summer.

**Length:** 1-2 miles
**Vibe:** college party
**Skip if:** you want quiet

### Lower San Marcos (City Park downstream toward Martindale)

Quieter, less developed, longer paddle. Some of the best wildlife viewing in central Texas.

**Length:** 6-15 miles depending on takeout
**Vibe:** quiet, natural
**Pick this for:** real day trip

## Outfitters

Multiple in San Marcos:

- **Lions Club Tube Rental** (cheap, mostly tubes, some kayaks)
- **TG Canoes** (full-day rentals with shuttle)
- **Olympic Outdoor Center** (more kayak-focused)
- **Spencer Canoes** (Martindale, lower section specialist)

Most include shuttle service. You launch upstream, paddle to a takeout, they drive you back.

## Costs

- Tubes: $15-25
- Kayak rental: $30-50
- Full-day combo with shuttle: $50-80

Cheaper than most guided experiences in Austin. The shuttle is the value-add; one-way river paddles require it.

## When to go

**Best:** weekday in late April, May, September, October. Warm but not punishing, college off-season-ish.

**Worst:** Saturday in June, July, August. Tubing crowd is overwhelming.

**Surprising winter:** the water is 72°F year-round, so winter paddles are warmer than the air. Bizarre but pleasant.

## What to expect

- **Crystal clear water** with visible turtles, fish, plant life on the bottom
- **Cypress trees** lining both banks
- **Mild current** (unlike a lake, you're flowing with the water)
- **Some easy rapids** in spots (nothing scary)
- **Wildlife:** turtles, herons, sometimes water snakes (harmless), abundant fish

## What to bring

- Standard kayak gear (PFD, paddle from outfitter)
- Dry bag with phone
- Water bottle (refill at access points)
- Snack
- Waterproof camera or phone case (you'll want shots of the water clarity)
- Closed-toe water shoes

## What's different from a lake paddle

**Current matters.** You're going downstream. Don't try to fight back upstream.

**Strainers are real.** Downed trees can be obstacles. Stay alert.

**One-way paddle.** Plan your shuttle.

**Tubers.** In summer, you'll share water with tubers. They're slow and drunk. Pass on the outside.

## Combining with Austin

A typical day looks like:
- 9 AM: drive from Austin to San Marcos (45 min)
- 10 AM: rent gear, shuttle to launch
- 10:30 AM: launch
- 1-3 PM: takeout depending on section
- 3-4 PM: lunch in San Marcos
- 5 PM: drive back to Austin

You can absolutely do San Marcos in the morning and be back in Austin for an evening kayak tour on Lady Bird Lake (just kidding, that's overkill, but it's possible).

## Common questions

**How far is San Marcos River from Austin?**
About 45 minutes south on I-35.

**Is the San Marcos River good for beginners?**
Yes, the lower sections especially. Mild current, clear water, easy navigation.

**Can you kayak the San Marcos in summer?**
Yes. Avoid weekends in June-August (tubing crowd). Weekday mornings are great.

**Is the San Marcos cold?**
72°F year-round (spring-fed). Refreshing in summer, comfortable in winter.

**Do I need a shuttle for the San Marcos?**
Yes, for one-way paddles. Most outfitters include shuttle service in the rental.
