# Winter Kayaking in Austin: Yes, It's a Thing

Most visitors assume kayak season ends in October. Locals know it doesn't. Lady Bird Lake from November through February is one of the city's best-kept secrets. The skyline is dramatic, the lake is empty, the cold is mild compared to most of the country, and you can paddle in the middle of the day without melting.

Here's how to actually do it.

## Why winter on Lady Bird Lake works

**Mild winters by national standards.** Austin highs in winter average 60-65°F. Cold snaps drop into the 30s for a few days a year, then bounce back. It's not Minnesota.

**The water doesn't freeze.** Lady Bird Lake has stayed liquid every winter on record. Water temp drops to the high 50s.

**No crowds.** Parking is easy. Tours have spots. The lake feels empty.

**Skyline is at its best.** Lower sun angle, longer shadows, dry air. Photography is exceptional.

**Wildlife is calmer.** Birds are more visible. Turtles are scarce but herons are everywhere.

## What you actually need to wear

The biggest thing: layer up and avoid cotton.

**Base layer:**
- Wool or synthetic long-sleeve shirt
- Wool or synthetic leggings or quick-dry pants

**Mid-layer:**
- Fleece or insulated pullover

**Outer layer:**
- Light waterproof jacket or shell

**Extras:**
- Wool or synthetic socks
- Closed-toe water shoes or neoprene booties
- Beanie
- Light gloves (paddling gloves or thin running gloves)

**Skip:**
- Cotton shirts
- Down jackets that lose insulation when wet
- Bare ankles

## How cold is too cold?

Our threshold for running tours: above 40°F air temp, no significant wind, no rain or freeze warning.

Below that, we cancel for safety. The risk isn't the lake; it's hypothermia if someone unexpectedly gets wet on a 35°F day.

Most days from November through February are above this threshold.

## What about the cold snaps?

A few times each winter Austin gets a polar vortex blast. Temps drop into the 20s for 2-4 days. Tours cancel. The lake itself doesn't freeze but the experience would be miserable.

These are predictable 5-7 days out. Watch the forecast.

## What it feels like

You're in fleece and a light jacket on the water. The air is cool but the sun is on you. The skyline is sharp against blue sky. The lake is glassy. There are maybe 5 other paddlers in sight.

It's almost meditative. The summer chaos is gone.

## What to do if you get cold

- Pull up to the boardwalk or any public access point
- Walk around for 5 minutes
- Drink something warm if you brought a thermos
- The guide can call it short if needed

We don't push through guests being miserably cold. The trip can end early.

## What you'll see in winter

- The fully lit skyline (city lights stay on later in winter)
- Cormorants and herons (resident year-round)
- Migrating waterfowl on the lake itself
- Empty hike-bike trail (relatively)
- Bare cypress trees along the shore (which look great in low-angle light)

You won't see:
- Bats (gone to Mexico)
- Turtles (mostly hidden)
- The huge tourist crowd

## The sun in winter

Don't underestimate winter UV in Texas. Austin is at 30°N latitude. The sun in January is still strong enough to give you a real sunburn, especially with reflection off the water. Sunscreen and a hat still apply.

## Common questions

**Can you kayak Lady Bird Lake in December?**
Yes. Most days are above 50°F. Layer up and you're fine.

**Is it too cold to kayak Austin in January?**
Most days no. A handful of cold snap days, yes.

**What temperature is too cold for kayaking?**
We don't run tours below 40°F air temperature. Above that, with proper layers, it's comfortable.

**Are kayak tours running in winter?**
Yes, year-round. The schedule is lighter than summer.

**Is the bat colony there in winter?**
No. The bats migrate to Mexico from late October to early March.
