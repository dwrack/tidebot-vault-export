# The Mexican Free-Tailed Bats of Austin: Facts, Myths, and Why They Matter

The Austin bat colony is one of those things that's so iconic to the city that locals stop noticing it. Visitors are stunned. Most people who watch the emergence don't actually know much about the bats themselves.

Here's the actual story.

## What they are

**Species:** Mexican free-tailed bat (*Tadarida brasiliensis*). Also called the Brazilian free-tailed bat.

**Size:** Small. Body about 3.5 inches long, wingspan about 11 inches. Roughly the weight of a stack of three quarters.

**Lifespan:** Up to 11 years in the wild.

**Population at Congress Bridge:** Estimated 750,000 in winter to **1.5 million in summer**. It's the largest urban bat colony in North America.

## Why they live under the Congress Avenue Bridge

This is the part that surprises people. The bridge wasn't designed for bats. They moved in by accident.

In **1980**, the Congress Avenue Bridge was reconstructed. The new design had concrete expansion joints with quarter-inch gaps. Those gaps turned out to be perfect roosting habitat: warm, dark, protected from predators.

Within a few years bats started showing up. By the late 1980s, the colony was massive. The city initially considered exterminating them. Bat Conservation International (also based in Austin) intervened and convinced the city to leave them alone.

Now the colony is one of Austin's biggest tourist draws.

## Where they go in winter

The colony migrates to **central Mexico** in late October and returns in March. They follow insect populations south.

The bats you see in Austin from spring through fall are mostly females and their pups. The males stay further south.

## When pups are born

Late May through June. The mothers form maternity colonies and the pups are kept tucked into the bridge structure during the day. They start flying with the colony in late summer.

If you're at the bridge in July and August, the colony you're seeing is the largest size of the year because it includes the new pups.

## What they eat

Each night, the colony eats roughly **15-20,000 pounds of insects**. Mostly moths, mosquitoes, beetles, and agricultural pests like the corn earworm moth that costs Texas farmers millions per year.

The economic value of bat insect control in central Texas alone is estimated at $1.7 billion per year.

If you've enjoyed an outdoor patio in Austin without being eaten alive by mosquitoes, the bats are part of the reason.

## How fast they fly

Mexican free-tailed bats are the fastest-flying mammals on Earth. They've been clocked at **99 mph** in level flight. Most birds can't keep up.

When you watch them emerge from the bridge, that ribbon of bats heading east is moving fast.

## Common myths

**"The bats will get in my hair."** No. Bats are extraordinarily good at echolocation. They will not fly into you. Your hair is not a target.

**"Bats are blind."** They aren't. They have decent eyesight; echolocation is just better than vision in the dark.

**"Bats carry rabies."** Some bats can. The rate in healthy wild colonies is very low. You will never get close enough to a bat at Congress Bridge to be at risk. Don't pick up a bat on the ground (rule for any wild animal).

**"The colony is going to leave."** They've been there since the early 1980s and the population is stable. They aren't leaving.

## Conservation

Bat Conservation International is headquartered in Austin and runs research and protection efforts for bat colonies worldwide. The Congress Bridge colony is one of the most-studied urban bat populations in the world.

If you visit, the colony is free to watch. If you want to support the work, BCI takes donations.

## Common questions

**How many bats are at the Congress Avenue Bridge?**
Roughly 750,000 in winter, 1.5 million at peak summer.

**Are the Austin bats dangerous?**
No. They have no interest in humans and won't approach you.

**What kind of bats live under the Congress Avenue Bridge?**
Mexican free-tailed bats (Tadarida brasiliensis).

**Do the Austin bats have rabies?**
Wild bat populations have low rabies rates. You won't get close enough at the bridge to be at risk.

**Why did bats start living under the Congress Avenue Bridge?**
The 1980 bridge reconstruction created concrete expansion joints that turned out to be ideal bat habitat.

**When do the Austin bats migrate?**
South to Mexico in late October. Back in early March.
