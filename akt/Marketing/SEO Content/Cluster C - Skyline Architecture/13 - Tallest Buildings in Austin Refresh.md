# The Tallest Buildings in Austin (2026 List)

Austin's skyline changes every couple of years. A list from 2018 looks nothing like a list from 2026. Here's the current ranking, who built each one, and the best way to actually see them all in one shot.

## The list (as of 2026)

| Rank | Building | Height | Year |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Independent | 685 ft / 58 stories | 2019 |
| 2 | The Austonian | 683 ft / 56 stories | 2010 |
| 3 | 6 X Guadalupe | 601 ft / 47 stories | 2022 |
| 4 | Fairmont Austin | 590 ft / 37 stories | 2018 |
| 5 | 360 Condos | 581 ft / 44 stories | 2008 |
| 6 | Google Block 185 | 580 ft / 35 stories | 2022 |
| 7 | Frost Bank Tower | 515 ft / 33 stories | 2004 |
| 8 | The Bowie | 502 ft / 36 stories | 2014 |
| 9 | Northshore | 478 ft / 38 stories | 2017 |
| 10 | Block 71 | 462 ft / 32 stories | 2022 |

These rankings shift every 12-18 months. New towers in Rainey Street and east downtown are likely to crack the top 10 by the late 2020s.

## The Independent

The "Jenga Tower." 685 feet, residential. Designed by Rhode Architecture (Austin) and BVH Architecture. The stair-step shape is functional, every floor gets a different setback, which means more units have outdoor terraces.

When you see it from the water, it's the most distinctive shape in the skyline.

## The Austonian

For a long time this was the tallest in Austin. Now it's number two by 2 feet. Built in 2010, residential, with a gym, dog spa, and 56 floors of high-end condos.

## 6 X Guadalupe

Mixed-use tower opened 2022, hotel, residential, office. Helped redefine what's possible on Guadalupe.

## Fairmont Austin

Largest hotel in the city. If you've been to a downtown convention, you've probably stayed here.

## 360 Condos

Sits directly on the south shore of Lady Bird Lake. From a kayak, this is the building you're looking up at. Built 2008, kicked off the Austin condo boom.

## Frost Bank Tower

The "owl building." When the lights are on in the crown, the tower is supposed to look like an owl's face. Designed by Duda Paine. First major Austin skyscraper post-9/11.

## How to see them all at once

Honestly, there's no single street angle where you can see all 10 cleanly. Office towers block sightlines from Mount Bonnell and most rooftops.

The two best views:

1. **From a kayak on Lady Bird Lake.** You're below the buildings, the water reflects the skyline, and you cover the full downtown stretch in a 2-hour paddle. We run [the skyline kayak tour](/austin-skyline-tour/) for exactly this.
2. **From the Pennybacker Bridge overlook on Loop 360.** Drive up, walk a short trail, you get the western view of the skyline. Best at sunset.

The kayak version is slower, more immersive, and you get the architectural commentary. The Pennybacker view is faster and free.

## What you'll miss if you only see the skyline at street level

The scale. From the water, the cluster of towers rises a clean 600+ feet straight up. From street level, the buildings are obscured by other buildings, parked cars, and trees. You don't get the same visual impact.

## How fast Austin's skyline is growing

From 2010 to 2026, Austin added roughly 25 buildings over 300 feet. That's more than San Antonio's entire downtown. Most of the growth is residential, driven by the tech relocation boom.

If you're visiting now, take the photo. The skyline you're looking at will be different in 5 years.
