# The Austin Skyline From the Water: A Building-by-Building Guide

You can see the Austin skyline from a lot of places. From Mount Bonnell. From a hotel rooftop. From the SoCo overlook. None of them give you the view a kayak on Lady Bird Lake gives you, where the buildings rise straight up out of the reflection on the water and the scale of the city hits different.

Here's the building-by-building guide we use on the [skyline kayak tour](/austin-skyline-tour/), with the history we tell guests on the water.

## The Independent ("The Jenga Tower")

- **Height:** 685 feet, 58 stories
- **Built:** 2019
- **Why it's notable:** Tallest residential tower west of the Mississippi when it was completed. The stair-step shape (locals call it "the Jenga Tower") was designed to maximize views from each floor.
- **From the water:** unmistakable. It's the one that looks like stacked blocks slightly offset.

## Frost Bank Tower

- **Height:** 515 feet, 33 stories
- **Built:** 2004
- **Why it's notable:** First skyscraper built in Austin after 9/11. The crown design with three sloped panels is meant to evoke an owl's face when illuminated at night (intentional, by architect Duda Paine).
- **From the water:** the silver-and-glass tower with the distinctive crown.

## The Austonian

- **Height:** 683 feet, 56 stories
- **Built:** 2010
- **Why it's notable:** First "supertall" residential building in Austin. The penthouse sold for $11 million in 2010, an Austin record at the time.
- **From the water:** rectangular glass tower, slightly to the east of the Independent.

## 360 Condos

- **Height:** 581 feet, 44 stories
- **Built:** 2008
- **Why it's notable:** Helped kick off the Austin condo boom. Sits right on the water on the south shore of Lady Bird Lake.
- **From the water:** the curved glass tower closest to the lake itself.

## One Eleven Congress (formerly Frost Bank's neighbor)

- **Height:** Various stories
- **From the water:** part of the cluster directly above the Congress Avenue Bridge.

## Austin Marriott Downtown

- **Built:** 2020
- **Why it's notable:** A relatively recent addition; one of the largest hotels downtown.

## Fairmont Austin

- **Height:** 590 feet, 37 stories
- **Built:** 2018
- **Why it's notable:** Largest hotel in Austin. Hosts most of the big downtown conferences.

## The Travis (under construction in recent years)

Multiple new towers have gone up since 2020. The skyline you see today is bigger than it was 5 years ago. Building names change as they sell.

## Buildings you can't see from the water (but should know about)

- **The State Capitol** is north of downtown. You can't see it from Lady Bird Lake because the office towers block it. From the south shore looking north it pokes out occasionally.
- **The University of Texas Tower** is even further north. Same deal.

## What's actually changed in the last 10 years

The skyline you see in 2026 doesn't match the skyline from 2015. In that decade:
- The Independent (2019)
- Google Tower (2022)
- Block 71 / 6 X Guadalupe (2022)
- Multiple new residential towers

If you visited Austin in 2015 and came back today, the change from the water is dramatic. From street level you don't notice as much. From the lake, the difference is impossible to miss.

## The "fifth tallest building" question

The single most-Googled fact about the Austin skyline (we know because we get it in search data) is "what's the fifth tallest building in Austin?" The answer changes every year as new towers go up, but as of 2026 it's roughly:

1. The Independent (685 ft)
2. The Austonian (683 ft)
3. 6 X Guadalupe (601 ft)
4. Fairmont Austin (590 ft)
5. 360 Condos (581 ft)

These shift as new buildings open, so by the time you read this it may be different.

## What locals point out that tourists miss

- **The Pennybacker Bridge** is not visible from Lady Bird Lake (it's further west on Loop 360). People often ask about it.
- **The State Capitol's pink granite** comes from Marble Falls, west of Austin. Free shuttle buses still run from the Capitol to the granite quarry as a tourist thing.
- **The University of Texas Tower** turns orange after a major Longhorn win.

## Why the skyline view from water is different

You're at water level. Buildings rise straight up. The reflection on the lake doubles the visual scale. There's no foreground noise. Most importantly, you're moving slowly enough to take it in.

A car drive along Cesar Chavez gives you 30 seconds of skyline. A kayak gives you 90 minutes.

The Independent, at 685 feet. Built in 2019.

Yes, this is one of the best views in the city. Lady Bird Lake runs along the south side of downtown.

**What buildings can you see from Lady Bird Lake?**
The Independent, Frost Bank Tower, the Austonian, 360 Condos, the Fairmont, and dozens more.

**How tall is Frost Bank Tower?**
515 feet, 33 stories. Built in 2004.

Mostly no. The office towers block it. Occasionally you can spot the dome on a clear day from certain angles.
