# How Austin's Skyline Has Changed in the Last 10 Years

If you visited Austin in 2016 and came back today, the downtown skyline is almost unrecognizable. From a kayak on Lady Bird Lake, the change is impossible to miss. Roughly two dozen towers have gone up in a decade. This is the version of growth most cities haven't seen since the postwar boom.

## What the skyline looked like in 2016

In 2016, the Austin skyline had:
- **The Austonian** (2010) as the tallest at 683 feet
- Frost Bank Tower (2004)
- 360 Condos (2008)
- A handful of older 1980s and 90s office towers
- Several construction cranes

That was it. The "iconic" downtown was maybe 6-8 buildings.

## What's been built since 2016

A short list of what went up between 2016 and 2026:

- **The Independent** (2019) — 685 ft, took the tallest title
- **Fairmont Austin** (2018) — 590 ft, biggest hotel
- **Northshore** (2017) — 478 ft
- **6 X Guadalupe** (2022) — 601 ft
- **Google Block 185** (2022) — 580 ft
- **Block 71** (2022) — 462 ft
- **The Travis** and several other large residential towers
- **Multiple Rainey Street high-rises** that completely changed that neighborhood
- **Several East 6th and East downtown towers**

The pace was roughly 2-4 major towers per year throughout the late 2010s and early 2020s.

## Why it happened

A few reasons:

1. **The tech relocation boom.** Apple, Google, Tesla, Oracle, Meta, and dozens of smaller firms moved or expanded operations in Austin. The population grew faster than housing supply.
2. **Permitting and entitlement.** Austin's permitting environment, while not perfect, was faster than San Francisco's or LA's.
3. **Available downtown land.** Unlike most American downtowns, Austin had pockets of underdeveloped land near the core (especially Rainey, East 6th, and Seaholm).
4. **Capital flowing into multifamily.** Investor demand for residential towers was high, and Austin priced in growth.

## What the change feels like from the water

Honestly, it's the most visible from a kayak on Lady Bird Lake. From street level, you don't notice as much because you're looking at one tower at a time. From the water, you see the cluster, and the cluster has roughly doubled in density.

The view we tell guests on the [skyline tour](/austin-skyline-tour/): "If you've got a photo from 2015, hold it up. The right two-thirds of what you're looking at right now didn't exist."

## What's locked in for 2026-2030

Multiple towers are in construction or final entitlement that will hit the skyline by 2030:
- Several Rainey Street towers in the 500-700 foot range
- Mixed-use towers along Cesar Chavez and East 6th
- The Sixth and Guadalupe project completing additional phases
- A handful of new hotels in the 30-40 story range

By 2030, the skyline you see today will be missing a few more silhouettes from the right side.

## What's been lost

Not all of the 2016 skyline survived. A few notable losses:

- Several mid-century office buildings replaced by towers
- A handful of historic warehouses east of downtown demolished for new construction
- The character of certain neighborhoods (Rainey especially) traded for height

This is the trade-off, and Austin is still figuring out how to balance it.

## What's the same

A few things haven't changed:
- The State Capitol still dominates the north edge
- The University of Texas Tower still anchors the campus
- The historic core around Congress Avenue still has its 19th-century stock
- Lady Bird Lake itself is still the same calm urban paddle it always was

## How to see the change

If you want to see how much the skyline has grown:

- A [kayak tour on Lady Bird Lake](/austin-skyline-tour/) is the best single vantage point
- The Pennybacker Bridge overlook gives you the western view
- Mount Bonnell at sunset shows the eastern stretch
- The Long Center rooftop is free and faces downtown directly

Compare what you're looking at to a 2015 photo on Wikipedia. The change shows up immediately.

## Why this matters for tourists

If you visit Austin once a decade, you're seeing a different city each time. The 2026 skyline isn't the 2016 skyline isn't the 2030 skyline. The version you photograph today is going to look dated in five years.

This is a good time to take the picture.

## Common questions

**Has Austin's skyline changed a lot?**
Yes. Roughly 25 towers over 300 feet were built between 2010 and 2026, doubling the density of the downtown cluster.

**What's the newest tall building in Austin?**
Multiple towers from the 2022-2024 wave, including Google Block 185, 6 X Guadalupe, and Block 71.

**Is Austin still building downtown towers?**
Yes. Several major towers are in construction or planning for 2026-2030 delivery.

**Why is Austin's skyline growing so fast?**
Tech relocation, population growth, and capital flowing into multifamily housing. Roughly 50,000 new residents per year in the late 2010s and early 2020s drove demand for downtown housing.

**What's the best place to see Austin's skyline?**
From a kayak on Lady Bird Lake, the Pennybacker Bridge overlook, Mount Bonnell, or the Long Center rooftop. The kayak version gives you 2 hours of slow viewing.
