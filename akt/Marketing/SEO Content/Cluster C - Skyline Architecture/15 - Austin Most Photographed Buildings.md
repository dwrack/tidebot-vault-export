# Austin's Most Photographed Buildings (and How to Get the Shot)

You've seen most of these on someone's Austin trip Instagram already. Here's where to find them, and how to get a photo that doesn't look exactly like everyone else's.

## 1. The "I Love You So Much" wall (Jo's Coffee, South Congress)

Not technically a building. It's a wall on the side of Jo's Coffee on South Congress. The graffiti was added in 2010 by musician Amy Cook.

**Where:** Jo's Coffee, 1300 S Congress Ave
**Best shot:** late afternoon, when the sun is on the wall but soft. Mornings and midday are too harsh.
**The trick:** there's a line. Everyone wants the same photo. Wait your turn or come at 7:30am before the line forms.

## 2. The "Greetings from Austin" mural

Postcard-style mural on Roi Tan Cafe. One of the original Austin Insta murals.

**Where:** 1720 S 1st St
**Best shot:** mid-morning, the side gets even light.
**The trick:** there's no parking. Park nearby and walk.

## 3. Frost Bank Tower

The "owl building." The crown design with the three sloped panels lights up at night and creates the owl-face effect.

**Where:** Visible from anywhere downtown, but best photographed from Lady Bird Lake or from Mount Bonnell.
**Best shot:** From a kayak at sunset. The crown lights up, the building reflects on the water, you have a clean foreground.

## 4. The Texas State Capitol

The state capitol. Pink granite, taller than the U.S. Capitol in DC by 7 feet (which is something Texans bring up at every opportunity).

**Where:** 1100 Congress Ave
**Best shot:** from the south end of the Capitol grounds looking north. Or from above on a tour of the Capitol dome (free, no reservation).
**The trick:** the inside of the dome is a great photo spot most tourists skip.

## 5. The Driskill Hotel

Austin's oldest hotel (1886). The lobby and bar are richly detailed, full of cattle horns and old leather.

**Where:** 604 Brazos St
**Best shot:** the marble staircase and the lobby ceiling. It's a working hotel; be a respectful guest.

## 6. The Independent ("Jenga Tower")

The tallest residential tower in Austin. The stair-step shape is unmistakable.

**Where:** Visible from anywhere downtown. Best photographed from Lady Bird Lake or from across the river on the south shore.
**Best shot:** from a kayak on the lake, with the building rising above you.

## 7. The Long Center

Performing arts venue with a rooftop terrace that has one of the best free skyline views in Austin.

**Where:** 701 W Riverside Dr
**Best shot:** the rooftop terrace at sunset. Free public access most days.

## 8. UMLAUF Sculpture Garden

Outdoor sculpture garden tucked into Zilker Park. Not a building but architecturally curated.

**Where:** 605 Robert E Lee Rd
**Best shot:** late afternoon when the bronze sculptures catch warm light.

## 9. The 360 Condos (the "U" tower)

The curved residential tower on the south shore of Lady Bird Lake.

**Where:** 360 Nueces St
**Best shot:** from the north shore of the lake looking south, or from a kayak. The curve is most distinctive from the water.

## 10. The Pennybacker Bridge (Loop 360 Bridge)

Not in downtown but worth the drive. Steel arch bridge over Lake Austin. Great hike-up overlook.

**Where:** Loop 360 over Lake Austin
**Best shot:** sunset from the overlook on the east side. Hike up the trail (15 min).

## How to get shots tourists miss

Most "Austin Instagram spots" are saturated. The shots that actually stand out:

- **The skyline from the water at blue hour** (10-15 minutes after sunset). Lights on, sky still blue, water reflecting. We see this on every [skyline tour](/austin-skyline-tour/) and most guests don't realize how much better it is than the daytime version.
- **The bat colony in flight against the skyline**. Phones don't capture this well, but a real camera with a fast lens at f/2.8 and ISO 6400 can pull it off.
- **Mount Bonnell at sunrise**. Everyone goes at sunset. Sunrise has 10% of the crowd and equivalent light.
- **The boardwalk at 6:30am**. Glassy water, fog rising, no people. Best 30 minutes of light in Austin.

## What to bring for skyline photography

- A phone in a waterproof case if shooting from a kayak
- A real camera with a 24-70mm lens for everything else
- Polarizing filter for the daytime water shots
- A tripod for blue hour and night shots from land
- Patience for the crowd at popular spots

## Common questions

**What's the most Instagrammed building in Austin?**
The Jo's Coffee "I Love You So Much" wall is probably the single most Instagrammed spot in Austin.

**Where can I take photos of the Austin skyline?**
Mount Bonnell, Pennybacker Bridge overlook, the Long Center rooftop, and from a kayak on Lady Bird Lake are the four best vantage points.

**Can you photograph the Frost Bank Tower at night?**
Yes. It's lit nightly. Best vantage points are Lady Bird Lake or any rooftop bar downtown.

**Is the Austin skyline best at sunset or sunrise?**
Sunset for warm light and the city lighting up. Sunrise for fewer people and softer light.

**Where can I take photos of the Austin bats?**
From a kayak under or near the Congress Avenue Bridge. The [bat tour](/bat-bridge-kayak-tour/) puts you in the right spot.
