# Never Kayaked Before? An Honest First-Timer's Guide for Austin

If you've never been in a kayak and you're thinking about doing your first one in Austin, here's the honest truth: Lady Bird Lake is one of the easiest places in the country to learn. The water is flat, motorized boats are banned, and you're never more than 50 feet from a shoreline you can land on.

Most of our [first-time guests](/austin-skyline-tour/) are surprised by how quickly they relax. The first 5 minutes feel weird. The next 95 minutes feel normal.

## How hard is it actually?

If you can sit upright and hold a paddle with both hands, you can kayak Lady Bird Lake.

You're not whitewater kayaking. You're not going against current. You're sitting on calm water, moving at 2 mph, and you can stop whenever you want.

## What we tell first-timers in the safety brief

The actual content takes about 5 minutes. Here's the gist:

- **Hold the paddle correctly.** The flat side of each blade faces backward when you pull through the water. There's a slight curve. We show you in person, but most people get it in two strokes.
- **Sit up straight.** Lean back and you lose efficiency. Lean forward and you'll get a sore back after an hour.
- **Push the top hand, pull the bottom hand.** That's the whole stroke. Your core does most of the work, not your arms.
- **Don't fight the boat.** Kayaks are stable. They want to stay upright. Trust them.
- **If you flip:** you won't, but if you do, the boat floats, the paddle floats, and we're 5 feet away.

## How likely am I to flip?

On Lady Bird Lake in a sit-on-top recreational kayak, almost zero. We've had thousands of first-timers and the percentage who go in the water is roughly 1 in 200, and almost always because they tried to.

If you're doing whitewater or sea kayaking, it's a different story. Lady Bird Lake is the gentlest water you can kayak in.

## Single or tandem?

**Pick a single if:**
- You're an adult of average build
- You want to control your own boat
- You don't mind doing your own paddling

**Pick a tandem if:**
- You're with kids (5-12)
- You're with a partner who definitely doesn't want to paddle
- You want to chat the whole way and not worry about staying together

Tandems are more stable but harder to steer at first. Singles are easier to steer but take a tiny bit more skill.

## What to wear

- **Quick-dry shorts or athletic pants** — not jeans
- **A t-shirt or rash guard**
- **Sandals with a back strap** (Tevas, Chacos) or water shoes — no flip-flops
- **A hat with a strap**
- **Sunglasses with a strap or croakies**
- **Sunscreen, applied before you leave home**

You will get splashed. Plan accordingly.

For full breakdown by season, see [what to wear kayaking in Austin](/blog/what-to-wear-kayaking-in-austin-every-season/).

## What to bring

- **Water bottle** — at least 16 oz, more in summer
- **Phone in a dry bag or floating case** — phones in the lake are a real problem
- **A snack** if you're hungry
- **Cash for tipping** the guide if you take a tour ($5-10/person)

## Should I rent or book a tour?

**Rent if:**
- You want to figure it out as you go
- You're with friends and don't want a guide chatting
- Budget matters and you're staying short

**Take a tour if:**
- You want context (skyline, bats, history)
- You don't want to figure out routes or logistics
- It's your first time and you want someone with you

For the comparison, see [tour vs. rental on Lady Bird Lake](/blog/kayak-rental-vs-tour-on-lady-bird-lake-an-honest-comparison/).

## What it actually feels like the first 10 minutes

Awkward. You're getting used to the seat, the paddle feels too long, you're worried about flipping, and your strokes are uneven. By minute 10 you're paddling fine. By minute 30 you've stopped thinking about the technique and you're just on the water.

This is normal. Don't worry if you feel uncoordinated for the first quarter mile.

## Common first-timer mistakes

1. **Gripping the paddle too tight.** You'll get a sore wrist. Loose hands.
2. **Paddling only with your arms.** You'll be exhausted in 30 minutes. Twist your torso.
3. **Trying to go too fast.** Slow strokes track better. Fast strokes wobble.
4. **Forgetting sunscreen.** The reflection off the water is brutal.
5. **Putting the phone in a regular pocket.** Use a dry bag.

## What can actually go wrong

Realistically:
- **Sunburn.** Use sunscreen.
- **Phone overboard.** Use a dry bag.
- **Sore back.** Sit up straight.
- **Wind on open stretches.** Paddle harder, use shorter strokes.
- **Rain.** Light rain is fine. Lightning means we don't go.

Risk of injury or anything bad happening on calm water: very low.

## How long to plan for

- **Rental for an hour:** $20, gets you a basic taste
- **Two-hour tour:** $50, includes guide, route, and the iconic stuff (skyline, bats)
- **Half-day on your own:** if you have your own boat and want to explore

We'd recommend at least 90 minutes the first time. You need 30 to settle in and another hour to actually enjoy it.

## Common questions

**Is kayaking hard for beginners?**
Not on Lady Bird Lake. Calm water, no current, no boats. It's one of the easiest places in the country to learn.

**Do I need to know how to swim to kayak?**
You should be comfortable in water with a life jacket on. Confident swimming isn't required if you're wearing a PFD.

**What if I'm scared of the water?**
Lady Bird Lake is shallow near the shore (you can stand) and you'll have a guide if you take a tour. Mention the fear before you launch and the guide can stay close.

**Should I take a lesson first?**
Not necessary for Lady Bird Lake. The 5-minute brief covers everything. If you want a real lesson, [paddle board lessons](/paddle-board-lessons/) or a private kayak lesson can be arranged.

**Can I kayak alone as a beginner?**
Renting alone on Lady Bird Lake is fine if you can swim and you're paying attention. We'd recommend a tour or a friend for your very first time.
