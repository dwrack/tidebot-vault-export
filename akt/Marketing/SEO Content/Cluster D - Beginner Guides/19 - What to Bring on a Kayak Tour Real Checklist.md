# What to Bring on a Kayak Tour: The Real Checklist

Most "what to bring kayaking" lists tell you to pack a survival kit for a 2-hour paddle. This isn't that. Here's the actual list of what you need on Lady Bird Lake, ranked by how much you'll regret forgetting it.

## The "you'll regret forgetting it" tier

### 1. Phone in a dry bag or floating case
Phones in the lake happen every season. A floating case is $10. If you don't have one, we sell them at the launch.

### 2. Sunscreen
Apply 20 minutes before launch. Reapply at the halfway point if you can. The reflection off the water doubles the UV.

### 3. Water bottle
At least 16 oz in cool weather. 24+ oz in summer. We don't provide water on tours.

### 4. Hat with a strap
Without one, you'll burn the top of your head. With a regular hat (no strap), you'll lose it in the lake.

### 5. Sunglasses with a strap
Same as above. Polarized lenses help cut the water glare.

## The "nice to have" tier

### 6. A small snack
Granola bar, crackers, jerky. Especially on sunset tours where dinner gets pushed back.

### 7. A small dry towel
For wiping hands, your phone, your face. We provide one for the boat but a personal one is nice.

### 8. Cash for tipping
$5-10 per person for the guide is standard. We see a lot of guests forget cash and feel awkward at the end.

### 9. Bug spray (sunset and bat tours, summer)
Mosquitoes show up at dusk in summer. A small spray bottle in a dry bag.

### 10. A spare shirt
For after the tour. You'll be a little wet. A dry shirt in the car for the drive home is underrated.

## What we provide (don't bring duplicates)

- Kayak (single or tandem)
- Paddle
- USCG-approved life jacket
- A small dry bag for keys, wallet, sunscreen
- The guide (on tours)

## What you should NOT bring

### Glass containers
Water bottles must be plastic or metal. Glass on the water is a safety issue and a city ordinance issue.

### Alcohol
Open container is enforced on the water. Beers can wait until after.

### Big cameras with no protection
A DSLR with no waterproof housing is a bad idea on a kayak. A phone or a GoPro with a strap is fine.

### Backpacks
You don't have anywhere to put a regular backpack. A small dry bag fits in the cockpit. A backpack is in your way and doesn't float.

### Jewelry you care about
Wedding rings, watches, expensive earrings. The lake takes a few of these every season. If you're going to wear it, double-check it's secured.

### Pets (on most guided tours)
Dogs are allowed on rentals if calm. Tours are usually guides-only because of boat configuration.

## Optional but useful

### Buff or bandana
Soak it in cold water and wear around your neck. Cooling effect for the first 30 minutes in summer.

### Floating phone case (if not a dry bag)
Around your neck, you can shoot photos one-handed without worrying.

### Wet wipes or face wipes
For after the tour to clean off sunscreen and lake water.

### A small first-aid kit
We carry one, but if you have allergies or specific needs, bring your own.

## Tour-specific additions

### Bat tours (March-October)
- Light layer for after sunset
- Bug spray
- Phone with low-light camera capability if you want to try shots

### Sunrise tours
- Light jacket
- Coffee in a thermos (not glass)
- Spare layer, it's cooler at 6am

### Sunset skyline tours
- Light layer for the cool-off after sundown
- Phone fully charged for the blue hour shots

### Winter tours
- Beanie
- Insulated water bottle (water freezes is real)
- Spare dry layer in the car

## What people forget the most

In rough order:
1. Sunscreen (we always end up loaning ours)
2. Water bottle (we always end up loaning ours)
3. Cash for tipping
4. Phone case (most expensive mistake when forgotten)
5. A hat with a strap

## Common questions

**Do I need a dry bag for a kayak tour?**
We provide one for keys/wallet. Your phone benefits from a dedicated waterproof case.

**Can I bring a cooler on a kayak?**
Not on a guided tour. On longer rentals you can sometimes strap a small cooler to a kayak. Confirm with the rental shop.

**Should I bring my GoPro?**
Yes, with the wrist or chest strap. Mounted to the kayak with a no-slip leash works too.

**Do I need a waterproof phone case to kayak Lady Bird Lake?**
Strongly recommended. Floating cases run $10-20. We sell them at the launch.

**What snacks are good for a kayak tour?**
Crackers, granola bars, jerky, dried fruit. Anything that doesn't melt or need refrigeration.

**Can I bring my own paddle?**
You can, but ours are sized for the kayaks we rent and tested daily. There's no advantage.
