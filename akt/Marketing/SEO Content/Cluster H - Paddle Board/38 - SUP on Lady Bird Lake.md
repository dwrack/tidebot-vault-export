# Stand-Up Paddle Boarding on Lady Bird Lake (Everything You Need to Know)

Stand-up paddle boarding on Lady Bird Lake is one of the most popular ways to use the water in Austin. The lake is calm, motorboat-free, and shallow near the shore — basically ideal SUP conditions. If you can stand up, you can do this.

## SUP basics on Lady Bird Lake

- **Water:** flat, calm, no boat wakes
- **Shore:** shallow near the banks (you can step off)
- **Distance:** the full lake is 6 miles long
- **Cost:** rentals from $25/hour
- **Skill needed:** basic balance

## Why Lady Bird Lake is good for SUP

**No motorized boats** = no wakes that knock you off.

**Shallow shore** = if you fall, you can stand. The water is 4-6 feet near the banks in most places.

**Mild current** is essentially zero (it's a dammed lake).

**Year-round paddleable** weather and water conditions.

**Visual rewards:** skyline, bat colony (in season), boardwalk views.

## Standing for the first time

Most people fall in within their first 5 minutes. That's fine. Here's the technique:

1. **Start kneeling** on the board, paddle across your knees.
2. **Place hands on the board** in front of you.
3. **Stand up one foot at a time**, keeping your knees bent.
4. **Look at the horizon**, not at your feet.
5. **Bend your knees** and use your core, not your arms.

If you fall, fall to the side, not forward (the paddle can hit you).

## What to wear

Same as kayaking, with one note: SUP gets you wetter than kayaking because you'll likely fall in at least once.

- Quick-dry shorts and rash guard
- Hat with strap
- Sunglasses with strap (cheap pair, in case they go in)
- Sunscreen
- Sandals with back strap or barefoot (boards are non-slip)

## What to bring

- Water bottle
- Phone in waterproof case
- Dry bag for keys/wallet on the deck
- Cash for tip if you take a tour

## Best time to SUP

- **Sunrise:** glassy water, easiest balance
- **Mid-day:** warmer water (you'll fall in), but more crowded
- **Pre-sunset:** golden hour for photos
- **Sunset:** the bat colony emerges (in season)

**Avoid:** windy afternoons. SUP catches wind dramatically. 15+ mph winds make balance hard.

## SUP-specific safety

- **Leash on your ankle.** If you fall and the board gets blown away, the leash keeps it with you.
- **Life jacket.** Required by Coast Guard for SUP.
- **Don't paddle in lightning.** A vertical pole on water is a bad idea.
- **Watch wind.** It can pick up fast and push you toward the dam at the east end.

## Yoga on a paddle board

Lady Bird Lake hosts SUP yoga classes seasonally. We don't run them, but local instructors do. The combination of calm water and Austin yoga culture makes this work better here than in most cities.

## Fishing from a SUP

Yes, you can. The board is stable enough. You'll need:
- Texas fishing license
- A small dry bag for tackle
- A fishing-specific SUP if you plan to do this often (wider, more stable)

## SUP vs. kayak: which to pick

- **SUP if:** you want a workout, you want to stand, you want better visibility (looking down at fish, turtles, etc.)
- **Kayak if:** you want to cover more distance, you want a more relaxed experience, you don't want to fall in

For the comparison, see [kayak vs. paddle board on Lady Bird Lake](/blog/kayak-vs-paddle-board-which-is-better-for-lady-bird-lake/).
