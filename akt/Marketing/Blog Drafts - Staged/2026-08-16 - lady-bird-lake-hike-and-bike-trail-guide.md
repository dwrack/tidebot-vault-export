```
How long is the Lady Bird Lake hike and bike trail?
```

## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
We get guests who've run the trail three times this trip and still ask, at the dock, "wait, is this the same lake?" Yes. Same lake. You've been circling it on foot the whole time and only ever seen half of it.

If you're planning a trip around the trail, here's what it actually is, and here's the argument for spending one of your mornings on the water instead of beside it.

## What the trail actually is

The official name is the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, though almost nobody calls it that out loud. It's the roughly 10-mile paved loop that wraps the entire shoreline of Lady Bird Lake, including a floating boardwalk section on the south shore near I-35 that lets the trail continue over water where there used to be a gap. It's free, open daily, and it's genuinely one of the most-used pieces of park infrastructure in the city. Runners at 6am, dog walkers at 9, cyclists commuting through at rush hour, tourists strolling it at sunset. All day, every day.

Lady Bird Johnson's push to beautify the lakeshore in the 1970s is a big part of why this trail exists at all. She funded early tree planting along the banks with her own money and pushed the city toward exactly the kind of shoreline park the trail now runs through.

## Landmarks you pass (and what you're missing from the bank)

Walking or biking the loop, you'll pass most of downtown Austin's greatest hits: Auditorium Shores with its open lawn and skyline views, the Congress Avenue Bridge where 1.5 million bats roost seasonally, Lou Neff Point's overlook near Zilker Park, and the Pfluger Pedestrian Bridge connecting the north and south shores.

Here's the thing the trail doesn't give you: the water side of any of it. You see the skyline from the bank, one direction. You see the bat colony from the bridge railing, packed shoulder to shoulder with everyone else who had the same idea at sunset. You see the boardwalk from underneath it if you're lucky.

From a kayak, all three flip. The skyline reflects on the water around you instead of sitting across from you. The bat colony pours out directly overhead with room to actually watch it, not a crowded railing. And you can paddle under the boardwalk and bridges you've only ever walked over.

## Best access points if you want to do both

If you want to work the trail and the water into the same visit, a few spots make it easy:

1. **Auditorium Shores.** Right on the trail, close to downtown hotels, paid parking nearby. Good if you're walking from a hotel and want a launch within a few blocks.
2. **Festival Beach.** The busiest free public launch, also directly on the trail's east side. Fills up by 10am on summer weekends, so get there early if you want the free lot.
3. **200 E Riverside Dr.** Where Austin Kayak Tours actually launches guided tours and rentals, a short walk from the trail's south shore near the Congress Avenue Bridge.
4. **Roy G. Guerrero Park.** Quietest option, east end of the loop, if you'd rather skip foot traffic entirely before or after your paddle.

## A same-day plan that actually works

Here's a version of the day we see work well, over and over:

Walk or run a stretch of the trail in the morning while it's cool, say the loop from Auditorium Shores to the Pfluger Bridge and back, about 3 miles round trip. Grab lunch somewhere on South Congress or Rainey Street. Then head back down in the afternoon and either rent a kayak to paddle the stretch you just walked, or book the guided Skyline Tour and let someone else handle the route while you actually look up at the buildings instead of watching for cyclists.

You don't need to choose one or the other. The trail and the lake are the same trip, just two different ways to look at it.

## Get on the water

If you've already done the trail, or you're planning to, don't let the visit stop at the shoreline. [Book the guided Skyline Tour](/austin-skyline-tour/) for the full downtown view from the water, or [grab a kayak or paddle board rental](/rental/) from 200 E Riverside Dr and do it on your own schedule. Same-day bookings are the norm here, not the exception.
```
