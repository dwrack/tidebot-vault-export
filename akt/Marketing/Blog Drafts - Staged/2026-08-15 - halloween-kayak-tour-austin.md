Late October is a strange time to sell a bat tour. It's also exactly when people ask about it the most.

Every year around Halloween, we get some version of the same message: "Is this real? Is it actually spooky, or is it just the regular tour with a pumpkin sticker on it?" Fair question. Here's the honest version, prices, timing, and the one thing most sites won't tell you about bats in late October.

## What the Halloween Bat Tour actually is

The Halloween Bat Tour is a real, bookable trip that Austin Kayak Tours runs on Lady Bird Lake, not a marketing name slapped on our regular sunset paddle. It's $75 per person, runs 2.5 hours, and departs at 6:30pm. Ages 6 and up. It's seasonal, which means it only runs on select dates around Halloween, and availability is limited, not "sold out to create urgency" limited, actually limited.

You launch from our dock at 200 E Riverside Dr, get a paddling lesson and safety briefing, then paddle out toward the Congress Avenue Bridge as the sky goes orange and then dark. Same lake, same skyline, same launch point as our daytime tours. What changes is the hour, the crowd, and the reason everyone showed up.

## Will you even see bats on Halloween night

Here's the part nobody selling this tour wants to say out loud: Halloween lands right at the edge of bat season, and some years the colony has already started thinning out by then.

The Congress Avenue Bridge is home to roughly 1.5 million Mexican free-tailed bats at peak season, which usually runs March through October. They're not a permanent fixture. Come fall, that entire colony starts migrating south to Mexico for the winter, and the exact week they leave shifts year to year depending on temperature and food supply. Some Halloweens, you'll still get a strong emergence. Other years, it's a smaller, later, more spread-out show than the one you'd catch in July.

We're not going to promise you a wall of bats just because it's spooky season and the name of the tour has "bat" in it. What we will say: our guides run this stretch of water enough to read the signs, and even a thinner emergence against a dark October sky, paddling distance from downtown Austin, is still one of the stranger, better things you can do on a Tuesday night here. Just don't book this expecting the peak-July version. [Compare watching the bats from the water vs the bridge](/blog/austin-bat-kayak-tour-bridge-vs-water/) if the emergence itself, not the holiday, is the actual draw for you.

## What it costs, and why it's not the $50 tour

Our standard [Sunset Bat Bridge Kayak Tour](/bat-bridge-kayak-tour/) runs $50 per person for 2 hours, most nights the colony is active. The Halloween version is $75 for 2.5 hours. Same bridge, same lake, same guides.

The extra $25 isn't for a bigger show. It's the seasonal-date premium: fewer nights offered, smaller booking windows, and a half hour of extra paddling built into a night that already has less daylight to work with. If price is the deciding factor and the calendar date doesn't matter to you, the regular sunset bat tour is the better value most of the season. If it has to be around Halloween specifically, this is the one. We built [a full breakdown of what a kayak tour actually costs in Austin](/blog/how-much-does-a-kayak-tour-cost-in-austin/) if you want the wider comparison across all our tours.

## Costumes, cold water, and what to actually wear

Yes, people show up in costume. No, we don't have an official costume, but common sense applies once you're getting into a boat.

- **Face paint, ears, capes, headbands:** fine, easy, doesn't get in the way.
- **A costume top over swim clothes or leggings:** works. Layer it, October evenings on the water cool off fast.
- **Full masks or anything covering your face:** skip it, your guide needs to see you and hear you.
- **Wigs and anything with a lot of fabric:** they get heavy and cold if they end up in the lake.
- **Water shoes or old sneakers:** wear them regardless of costume. Flip-flops fall off a kayak faster than you'd think.
- **A dry bag for your phone:** $20 add-on, worth it for photos of the skyline lit up after dark.
- **Sunscreen during the day, a light jacket for the paddle back:** the launch is warm, the last twenty minutes back to the dock in October is not.

One thing that doesn't change for Halloween: no alcohol on Lady Bird Lake. That's a city ordinance, not our rule, and it applies every night of the year. If your group wants drinks worked into the evening, do them before or after, not on the water.

## What a late-October group looks like out there

Most of what we see this time of year isn't families. It's couples doing something other than dinner and a movie, groups of friends who've done the standard Austin bar crawl three times already, and a handful of people who specifically wanted "something different" for a birthday that happens to fall near Halloween. A few show up in full costume. Most just wear something warm and call it a night out.

The energy is different from a July sunset tour. Quieter, a little colder, everyone leaning forward trying to spot the first bats against a darker sky than they'd get in summer. It's the kind of tour where the conversation on the paddle back tends to be about whether they saw fewer bats than expected, not whether the tour itself was worth it. Those are two different questions, and we'd rather you know that going in.

## Should you book it

If what you actually want is the biggest, densest bat emergence Congress Avenue Bridge has to offer, book the regular sunset tour in July or August, not this one. If what you want is a genuinely different Austin night out around Halloween, on the water, downtown skyline lit up, costume optional, the timing risk explained honestly instead of buried in fine print, this is a real, worthwhile thing to do with a free evening in late October.
