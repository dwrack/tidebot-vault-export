## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
A guest called our booking line last month and asked, half apologetic, "does Austin Kayak Tours still run trips on Town Lake, or did y'all move?" We get some version of that question a few times a month. The answer's simple: nobody moved. Town Lake is Lady Bird Lake. Always has been, since 2007 anyway.

If you've bounced between the two names while searching for a kayak tour, a rental, or just trying to figure out where your Airbnb host meant when they said "walk down to the lake," here's the actual story, not the Wikipedia-summary version.

## Same water, two names

The lake running through downtown Austin, the one with the bats under Congress Bridge and the skyline reflected in it every evening, is a dammed stretch of the Colorado River between Tom Miller Dam on the west end and Longhorn Dam on the east. It's roughly two miles long, hugged the whole way by the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail. For nearly 40 years, from the 1960s until 2007, the city called it Town Lake. Then the name changed. The water didn't.

We paddle this lake daily during season, and honestly, guests use both names in the same sentence without noticing. "Is this Town Lake or Lady Bird Lake?" "Yes."

## What actually happened in 2007

The Austin City Council renamed Town Lake to Lady Bird Lake on July 26, 2007, just days after former First Lady Lady Bird Johnson passed away. It wasn't a spur-of-the-moment tribute. Johnson had spent decades pushing to beautify the lake and its shoreline, personally donating money, including roughly $19,000 of her own, to fund tree planting and the early stages of what became the hike-and-bike trail system. She'd reportedly turned down the idea of naming it after her while she was alive. The council waited until she was gone to make it official.

So the rename wasn't about erasing "Town Lake." It was about crediting the person whose work made the lake worth naming after anyone at all. That's a better story than most people get when they ask.

## Why "Town Lake" never really left

Names are sticky. Twenty years from now people will probably still call a renamed street by its old name, and this is the same phenomenon at lake scale. A few reasons it stuck around specifically here:

- **Nearly two generations grew up calling it Town Lake.** That's a lot of muscle memory to undo with a city council vote.
- **Old signage and business names lagged.** Some businesses near the water kept "Town Lake" in their names for years after 2007, and a few still do.
- **It's shorter to say.** Two syllables versus five. People default to the easy version, especially in casual conversation.
- **Search habits are slow to shift.** We still see "town lake kayak rules" and "town lake austin" show up in what people type when they're trying to find us, almost 20 years after the official rename.

None of that is wrong, by the way. Nobody's going to correct you at the launch if you say Town Lake. Half our guides probably say it themselves off the clock.

## Does it matter which one you Google

Not for finding a tour or a rental. Both names point to the same place, and any local business worth booking with will answer to either one. Where it can matter is if you're trying to research trail conditions, water levels, or city park info, since some official city sources use "Lady Bird Lake" exclusively now and a search for "Town Lake water level" can occasionally send you to outdated or third-party pages instead of the current city source.

For booking a kayak trip specifically, though, use whichever name feels natural. We've had guests book under "the Town Lake sunset tour" and "the Lady Bird Lake bat tour" in the same week for the literal same product.

## Where to launch either way

If you've got your own boat, the launches don't change based on which name you use. Festival Beach on the east side is the busiest free public option. Holly Shores and Auditorium Shores are smaller alternatives closer to downtown. Roy G. Guerrero Park sits at the quiet east end if you want to skip the crowd.

If you'd rather skip the logistics entirely, Austin Kayak Tours launches from 200 E Riverside Dr for both guided tours and rentals, kayaks and SUPATX paddle boards, starting at $20. About 40% of our bookings come in same-day, and the median lead time across the last 30 days was two days. Most people don't plan this trip weeks out. They decide over lunch and paddle by sunset.

Call it Town Lake. Call it Lady Bird Lake. [Book the guided Skyline Tour](/austin-skyline-tour/) or [grab a rental](/rental/) and go see it for yourself. The water doesn't care what you name it.
```
