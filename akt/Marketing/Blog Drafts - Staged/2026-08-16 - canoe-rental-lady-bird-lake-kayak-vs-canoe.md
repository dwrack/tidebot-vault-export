```markdown
"Do y'all have canoes?" We get asked this more than you'd guess, usually from someone who grew up canoeing at camp or on a lake back home and figures Austin works the same way. Short answer: no. We don't rent canoes, and neither does most of downtown. Here's why, and what you'd actually want instead.

## The honest answer: we don't rent canoes

Austin Kayak Tours rents sit-on-top kayaks, single and tandem, plus SUPATX stand-up paddle boards. That's the whole fleet. No canoes. If you've searched around Lady Bird Lake rentals already, you've probably noticed the same pattern everywhere downtown: kayaks and paddle boards dominate, canoes basically don't show up.

That's not an oversight. It's a fit issue, and it's worth explaining instead of just saying no.

## Kayak vs. canoe, the differences that actually matter here

People use these terms loosely, so let's be specific about what separates them:

- **Seating position.** Kayaks put you low, close to the water, seated. Canoes sit you higher, often kneeling or on a raised bench.
- **Paddle type.** Kayaks use a double-bladed paddle, alternating sides. Canoes use a single-bladed paddle, which takes more technique to keep a straight line solo.
- **Wind profile.** Canoes ride higher out of the water. On a breezy day that means more surface area for the wind to grab.
- **Stability for one person.** A sit-on-top kayak is nearly impossible to tip on flat water. A canoe with one paddler in the back can list if you're not centered.
- **Learning curve.** Kayaking solo is close to intuitive within the first five minutes. Canoeing solo, especially keeping a straight line, takes real practice.
- **Storage and dry goods.** This is the one place canoes win. Open hull, more room for coolers and gear, useful for a multi-day river trip. Less relevant for a two-hour paddle downtown.

## Why kayaks win on this specific lake

Lady Bird Lake runs east to west through a corridor of high-rise buildings, and Austin wind funnels right down that corridor most afternoons. We've had guests in a canoe on a windy day (borrowed, brought from home) spend more energy fighting a crosswind than actually enjoying the paddle. A kayak sits lower and tracks straighter through the same wind without the fight.

There's also the beginner math. Something like 40% of our bookings come in same-day, guests who decided over lunch and want to be on the water by 2pm. Nobody's got time for a canoe-steering lesson on a spontaneous afternoon. A sit-on-top kayak, you get in, we point you at the water, you're paddling within a minute.

We're not knocking canoes generally. On a slow, protected river with a group and gear to haul, they're great. Lady Bird Lake, open and occasionally gusty, just isn't that kind of water.

## What we actually have (and what it costs)

Here's the real rental lineup:

1. **Single sit-on-top kayaks**, starting at $20.
2. **Tandem sit-on-top kayaks**, good for couples or a parent-and-kid pair.
3. **SUPATX stand-up paddle boards**, same starting price.
4. **Dry bag add-on**, $20, worth it if you're bringing a phone or wallet.

You'll need a photo ID, a credit card, and a signed waiver at the counter. No prior kayaking experience required. If you'd rather have a guide handle the route and the history, [book the guided Skyline Tour](/austin-skyline-tour/) instead of renting solo.

## If you're set on a canoe

If a canoe is genuinely what you want, whether for the nostalgia or the extra storage, your best bet is looking outside downtown Lady Bird Lake, at spots along the Colorado River or nearby lakes where slower, more protected water and canoe-specific outfitters are more common. For a downtown Austin afternoon on Lady Bird Lake specifically, though, a kayak is going to be the easier, more enjoyable call, and it's what's actually sitting on the rack when you show up.
```
