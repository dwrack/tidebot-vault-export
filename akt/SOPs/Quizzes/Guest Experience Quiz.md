# Guest Experience Quiz
## Austin Kayak Tours

**Passing score: 80% (8 of 10 correct)**

### Question 1
A guest arrives for a tandem kayak tour with their partner. One of them mentions they're terrified of water but came because their partner really wanted to do it. How do you handle this?

A) Tell them they shouldn't have booked if they're scared
B) Take extra time to reassure them: explain that sit-on-tops are very stable, the lake is calm, and you'll be right there the whole time. Offer to put them in the front of the tandem so their partner controls steering.
C) Suggest they sit on shore and watch
D) Ignore it and proceed with the normal briefing

**Answer: B**
A scared guest needs extra attention before launch. If you take 2 minutes to genuinely reassure them and give them the less intimidating seat, they often end up loving it. Dismissing fear or suggesting they skip the tour is a lost guest and a bad review.

### Question 2
A group of 8 books a tour, but only 6 show up. Two cancelled last minute but didn't notify you. What do you do?

A) Wait 20 minutes past the start time in case they show
B) Launch with the 6 who are here on time, note the no-shows in FareHarbor
C) Call the no-shows repeatedly
D) Cancel the tour because the group size changed

**Answer: B**
Don't delay the experience for guests who showed up on time. Launch with who's there, note the discrepancy in FareHarbor, and let management handle the no-show follow-up.

### Question 3
Mid-tour, a guest complains that the tour isn't what they expected. They thought it would be a more adventurous, rapids-type experience. What do you say?

A) "You should have read the description more carefully"
B) "Lady Bird Lake is a flat-water lake, so it's more of a scenic paddle. Let me make the most of it for you. There's some great wildlife and views coming up."
C) "I agree, it's kind of boring"
D) "You can file a complaint on our website"

**Answer: B**
Acknowledge the mismatch without being defensive. Redirect their attention to what IS good about the experience. Flag the feedback to management in case the listing needs clearer language.

### Question 4
A guest asks you to take a group photo with their phone. You're holding 6 boats together in a mild current. What do you do?

A) Refuse, it's too dangerous to hold a phone on the water
B) Raft the group up (boats side by side), take the photo, then get moving again
C) Tell them to take selfies instead
D) Say you'll do it at the end when you're back on shore

**Answer: B**
Photo ops are part of the experience. Raft up safely, take the photo, hand the phone back (in a dry bag ideally), and keep going. It takes 30 seconds and makes the trip memorable.

---

### Question 5
A guest with a physical disability arrives and their booking didn't mention any accessibility needs. They use a wheelchair and have limited upper body mobility. What do you do?

A) Tell them they can't do the tour
B) Assess what they can do, discuss options (tandem kayak with them in front, partner or guide doing most of the paddling), and make it work if it's safe
C) Refund them immediately
D) Call management and make the guest wait while you figure it out

**Answer: B**
Figure out how to make it work safely. Many people with mobility challenges can paddle with adaptations. A tandem kayak with a strong partner or a guide in the back seat can open up the experience. Only cancel if it's genuinely unsafe.

---

### Question 6
A guest asks if they can tip you. What's the right response?

A) "Tips aren't expected but are appreciated!"
B) "You'll need to add it through the booking system"
C) "I can't accept tips"
D) "Minimum 20%"

**Answer: A**
Tips are always appreciated and never expected. Be gracious, not awkward about it. Don't bring up tipping yourself, but if they ask, a simple acknowledgment is perfect.

---

### Question 7
It's a bat tour and the bats don't show. Maybe the colony shifted timing, maybe something spooked them. Guests are disappointed. What do you do?

A) Blame the bats and call it bad luck
B) Acknowledge the disappointment, share what you know about bat behavior, and pivot: "The sunset from here is incredible and not many people get to see it from the water"
C) Promise they'll definitely see bats if they come back
D) Offer a full refund on the spot

**Answer: B**
You can't control wildlife. But you can control the narrative. Acknowledge, educate, and redirect to what IS happening. Don't make promises about future tours.

---

### Question 8
A guest shows up 15 minutes late for a tour that's already launched. What do you do?

A) Tell them they missed it and to rebook
B) If you haven't left the launch area yet, get them checked in quickly. If the group has already departed, explain the situation, apologize, and help them reschedule through FareHarbor.
C) Launch them solo to catch up with the group
D) Make the whole group come back for them

**Answer: B**
If you can still include them, do it quickly. If the group is gone, don't send someone out alone to chase down a tour. Help them reschedule and be empathetic about it.

### Question 9
A parent shows up with a child who looks younger than expected. The child seems about 5 or 6. Is there a concern?

A) No, kids of any age can kayak
B) Yes. Assess the situation: the child must wear a PFD at all times (Texas law for under 13), must ride in a tandem with the parent, and the guide needs to assess whether the child is comfortable and safe on the water.
C) Children under 12 aren't allowed on tours
D) Just put them in a single kayak, kids are natural paddlers

**Answer: B**
Young kids can absolutely join, but they need to be in a tandem with a parent, PFD on the whole time, and the guide needs to use judgment about whether the child is calm enough for the water. If the kid is screaming and terrified, that's a different conversation.

---

### Question 10
After a tour, a guest approaches you privately and says they had a bad experience because another guest in the group was rude to them during the paddle. What do you do?

A) "Sorry about that, some people are just like that"
B) Apologize that their experience was impacted, ask if they'd like you to pass the feedback to management, and thank them for telling you
C) Say you didn't notice anything
D) Offer to ban the other guest

**Answer: B**
Take it seriously, validate their experience, and route it to management. You can't fix what already happened, but you can make sure they feel heard. Management can follow up with a gesture if appropriate.
