### Question 1
During your morning inspection, you find a PFD with a buckle that won't click shut. The rest of the PFD looks fine. What do you do?

A) Use it anyway and tell the guest to hold the strap
B) Pull it from rotation immediately, mark it for repair, and grab a replacement
C) Try to fix it with a zip tie
D) Use it for the smaller guests who won't need as snug a fit

**Answer: B**
A PFD with a broken buckle is not a functional PFD. Pull it, mark it, replace it. There's no workaround.

---

### Question 2
You're about to launch a tour and one guest says they already signed a waiver "last time they came." What do you do?

A) Take their word for it and launch
B) Check FareHarbor for a signed waiver on today's specific booking
C) Have them sign a generic waiver on paper
D) Ask another guide if they remember the guest

**Answer: B**
Waivers are tied to specific bookings. A waiver from a previous trip doesn't cover today. Check FareHarbor for the current booking.

---

### Question 3
You check the weather and see sustained winds at 14 mph with gusts to 18 mph. What's the call?

A) Launch normally, 14 mph sustained is under the threshold
B) Hold the trip. Gusts are hitting 18 mph, which exceeds the 15 mph threshold
C) Launch but stay close to shore
D) Ask the guests if they're comfortable with the wind

**Answer: B**
If gusts are exceeding 15 mph, that's a hold. The threshold applies to what you'll actually encounter on the water, not just the sustained average.

---

### Question 4
A guest capsizes in Lady Bird Lake. They're conscious, holding onto the kayak, but look scared. What's your first action?

A) Paddle over and flip the kayak back upright immediately
B) Call 911
C) Verbally confirm they're okay and coach them through holding on while you approach
D) Tell them to swim to shore

**Answer: C**
First, communicate. Confirm they're breathing, conscious, and secure on the kayak. Then approach and assist. Don't rush in and potentially create a second problem.

---

### Question 5
You hear thunder in the distance. You estimate the lightning is about 12 miles away based on the delay. What do you do?

A) Keep paddling, 12 miles is outside the 10-mile threshold
B) Get off the water immediately, it's close enough
C) Finish the tour quickly and head back
D) Wait and see if it gets closer

**Answer: B**
Lightning within 10 miles is the hard threshold, but 12 miles is close enough that the storm could be on you in minutes. Use good judgment. Get off the water. The protocol says 10 miles, but waiting until exactly 10 miles to react means you're already cutting it too close.

---

### Question 6
What type of PFD does Austin Kayak Tours use?

A) Type I — offshore
B) Type II — near-shore
C) Type III — flotation aid
D) Type IV — throwable device

**Answer: C**
Type III PFDs. They're designed for calm inland water where rescue is likely to be quick. Type IV throwables are kept at the launch site as supplemental.

---

### Question 7
A tandem kayak has a combined weight limit of 450 lbs. A couple arrives and you estimate they're close to or at the limit. What do you do?

A) Load them up and hope for the best
B) Offer them each a single kayak or a SUP instead, explaining that it's about performance and comfort
C) Ask them how much they weigh
D) Refuse to let them on the tour

**Answer: B**
Don't ask people their weight. Offer alternatives (singles, SUPs) and frame it around comfort and performance on the water. Handle it with tact.

---

### Question 8
During a tour, you notice one of your kayaks has developed a slow leak. The guest is sitting in a few inches of water. What do you do?

A) Finish the tour, it's not sinking
B) Get the guest into a spare kayak or onto another boat, then pull the leaking vessel
C) Tell the guest to keep paddling and bail water
D) Call 911

**Answer: B**
A leaking vessel gets pulled from service immediately. Transfer the guest safely and tow the damaged kayak back. 911 isn't needed unless someone's in danger.

---

### Question 9
TPWD conducts an on-water inspection and asks to see registration on one of your kayaks. The sticker is missing. What happens?

A) Nothing, they'll just give you a warning
B) Potential fine and the vessel could be pulled from the water
C) They'll check your permit instead
D) You can show them the registration paperwork at your office later

**Answer: B**
TPWD can fine you and potentially pull an unregistered commercial vessel off the water on the spot. Registration stickers must be visible on every vessel, every day.

---

### Question 10
After an incident where a guest cut their hand on a sharp edge of a kayak, what's the reporting timeline?

A) End of the week
B) Within 24 hours
C) Within 2 hours
D) Only if the guest asks you to report it

**Answer: C**
All incidents must be documented and reported to management within 2 hours. Even minor injuries. The report needs to be fresh and accurate.
