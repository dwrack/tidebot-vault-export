# How We Run Things — The Quiz
### Austin Kayak Tours

*For new hires. Read each scenario. Pick your gut answer. Then read the takeaway. No wrong answers, just honest ones.*

*Managers: walk through these together on orientation day. The conversation matters more than the "score."*

### Scenario 1: The Empty Slot

It's a Tuesday in June. You check the schedule and see a 2pm Skyline Tour with only 2 people booked. Your buddy texts asking if you want to grab tacos on South Congress. The weather is perfect.

**What do you do?**

A) Text your manager asking if the tour will cancel
B) Show up ready to give those 2 people the best tour of their summer
C) Suggest combining it with the morning tour so you can have the afternoon off

<details>
<summary>The takeaway</summary>

**Own Your Patch.** Those 2 guests might have driven from San Antonio to be here. They picked your tour. They might tip better than a group of 12, write the review that books your next 50 tours, or come back for the bat tour next month. The tour is the tour, whether it's 2 people or 14. If you're only excited when the group is full, this isn't the right fit.
</details>

### Scenario 2: The Bat Bridge Selfie

A guest on your Sunset Bat Bridge Tour is barely paying attention to the colony emergence because they're filming everything for Instagram. They keep asking you to "say that again but louder" and want you to pose with the bats in the background. The rest of the group is getting annoyed.

**What do you do?**

A) Play along. Content is good for business.
B) Ignore them and focus on the rest of the group
C) Find a middle ground. "Hey, I love that you're capturing this. How about I set you up with a killer angle in just a minute, and right now let's all take in this moment together?"

<details>
<summary>The takeaway</summary>

**Be Real.** You're not a content creator's prop. You're also not a jerk about it. The move is being genuine: acknowledge what they want, redirect with something better, and protect the experience for everyone. A guide who performs for one person's phone at the expense of the group has lost the plot. A guide who shuts it down harshly isn't being real either. Find the human middle.
</details>

### Scenario 3: The Waiver Rush

You're at the launch site on a Saturday morning. Full tour, 12 guests. A couple walks up and says they already signed the waiver online. You pull up the FareHarbor manifest and their names show as unsigned. The group is ready to go and you're already running 5 minutes late.

**What do you do?**

A) Take their word for it. They probably signed and the system is just slow.
B) Pull up their booking-specific waiver link on your phone, hand it to them, and have them sign right now before anyone gets on the water
C) Send them a generic QR code so they can sign while you launch the rest of the group

<details>
<summary>The takeaway</summary>

**Safety Is the Job.** Option B. No signed waiver, no tour. Pull up their booking-specific link and make it fast. Blame the tech if you need to ("system's being weird, takes 30 seconds, sorry about that"). A generic QR code doesn't tie the signature to their booking, so it doesn't count. And taking someone's word for it when the manifest says otherwise is a liability you don't want. Five minutes late is nothing compared to an unsigned waiver on the water.
</details>

### Scenario 4: The Quiet Problem

You overhear another guide complaining to guests about the schedule, saying things like "they always overbook us" and "management doesn't really get what it's like out here." The guests look uncomfortable. You know this guide has been frustrated but hasn't brought it up with anyone.

**What do you do?**

A) Stay out of it. Not your business.
B) Talk to the guide privately after the tour. "Hey, I heard some of that. You should bring it up with the manager. Venting to guests isn't going to fix it."
C) Report it to management immediately

<details>
<summary>The takeaway</summary>

**Respect the Work.** Option B. Direct, private, peer-to-peer. You're not tattling, you're looking out for them. Complaining to guests doesn't fix anything and it makes everyone look bad. The guide has a real frustration, and it deserves a real conversation with someone who can actually change things. If they won't bring it up, you can offer to back them up when they do. That's respect.
</details>

### Scenario 5: The Better Way

You've been running tours for a few weeks. You realized that if you adjust the launch order, putting beginners in the water first and experienced paddlers last, the group stays tighter on Lady Bird Lake and the tour flows way better. Your manager hasn't mentioned this approach.

**What do you do?**

A) Just do it your way. It works better.
B) Keep doing it the old way. The SOP says what it says.
C) Share it. Drop a message in the channel: "Tried something different with launch order today, here's what happened." Let the team decide if it's worth adopting.

<details>
<summary>The takeaway</summary>

**Get It Out of Your Head.** Option C. You found something better. Now share it so everyone benefits. The SOPs aren't sacred texts carved in stone. They're living documents built by the people doing the work. But "just doing it your way" without telling anyone means it disappears when you're not on the schedule. And blindly following a process you know is worse isn't loyalty, it's laziness. Share what you learned. That's how we all get better.
</details>
