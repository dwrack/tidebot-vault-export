# Weather Hold Protocol
## Austin Kayak Tours

### Why This Exists

Austin weather can shift fast, especially in spring and fall. A clear morning can turn into a lightning storm by noon. This protocol removes guesswork from weather decisions so no one's debating whether a trip should go out while guests are standing at the launch site.

The rule is simple: if a threshold is hit, follow the protocol. No freelancing, no "it looks like it might clear up."

### Thresholds

| Condition | Threshold | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Wind speed | >15 mph sustained | Hold or reschedule |
| Lightning | Within 10 miles | Cancel immediately, shelter |
| Visibility | <0.25 miles (fog, rain) | Hold until clear |
| Water temperature | <50°F | Require wetsuit or cancel |
| Severe weather warning | NWS warning issued for area | Cancel, do not launch |

### How to Check

**Wind:** Use a weather app with real-time local data (Weather Underground, Windy, or NOAA). Check at the launch site, not just online, because Lady Bird Lake can funnel wind differently than what a station a mile away reports. If you feel sustained gusts that are making it hard to stand a SUP paddle upright, it's probably too windy.

**Lightning:** The 30-30 rule works. If the time between seeing lightning and hearing thunder is 30 seconds or less, lightning is within 6 miles. But for our purposes, we use a 10-mile radius as the trigger. Lightning apps (WeatherBug, MyLightning) give real-time strike proximity.

**Visibility:** If you can't see the far bank of the lake clearly, visibility is compromised. Use your judgment, but if there's any question, hold.

**Water temp:** Relevant mainly in winter months. Cold water immersion is far more dangerous than cold air. Check water temp via USGS gauges for Lady Bird Lake.

### Decision Authority

Guides can make a hold call on their own. You don't need permission to keep people off the water.

Only management can override a hold call, and they should only do so if conditions have genuinely changed (not just because there's revenue at stake).

Guests don't get a vote on weather decisions. Be polite, be empathetic, but be firm. "I know this is disappointing, but I'm not comfortable putting the group on the water in these conditions" is the right thing to say. Offer to reschedule through FareHarbor.

### If You're Already on the Water

1. Monitor conditions throughout the tour, not just at launch
2. If conditions deteriorate past thresholds, head to the nearest safe shore immediately
3. Keep the group together. No one paddles ahead or falls behind.
4. If lightning is observed, get off the water and away from the shore edge. Seek shelter in a vehicle or solid structure.
5. Do not wait for the storm to "get closer." By the time it's overhead, you're already in danger.
6. Call management and coordinate next steps (shuttle, reschedule, partial refund)

### Communication

When a hold or cancellation is called:

1. Text management immediately with the reason and current conditions
2. Contact affected guests as early as possible (use FareHarbor contact info)
3. Offer rescheduling first, refund if rescheduling doesn't work
4. Document the hold/cancel: date, time, conditions, who was affected

### Bat Tour Specifics

Bat tours happen at dusk, which means weather assessment happens later in the day when afternoon storms are more likely. Check conditions at 3 PM and again at 5 PM for evening departures. If storms are forecast for the evening window, make the call early so guests aren't driving to the site for a cancellation.

### The Bottom Line

No trip is worth a safety incident. A cancelled tour costs revenue. An accident costs everything. Make the safe call and the business will back you up every time.
