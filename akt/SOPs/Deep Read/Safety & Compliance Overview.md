# Safety & Compliance Overview
## Austin Kayak Tours

### Regulatory Framework

Austin Kayak Tours operates on Lady Bird Lake in Austin, Texas. The primary regulatory authority is the **Texas Parks & Wildlife Department (TPWD)**, which oversees vessel registration, safety equipment requirements, and commercial operation standards on Texas waterways.

The City of Austin also has local ordinances governing watercraft on Lady Bird Lake. Motorized boats are prohibited on the lake, which simplifies some things, but commercial paddle operations still need to stay current on city park rules and launch site agreements.

### Vessel Registration

All commercial kayaks and SUPs must carry current TPWD registration. Registration stickers must be visible on each vessel. Expired or missing stickers can result in fines during an on-water inspection, and TPWD officers can and do conduct random checks.

If a commercial outfitter permit is required for the specific operation area, that must be current as well. Check with management if you're unsure about the permit status.

### PFD Requirements

Every person on the water must have a USCG-approved Type III PFD. For Austin Kayak Tours, that means:

- One PFD per guest, properly fitted before launch
- Guides wear PFDs at all times on the water (no exceptions, even if you're a strong swimmer)
- PFDs must be in serviceable condition: buckles working, straps intact, foam not degraded
- Children under 13 must wear a PFD at all times per Texas law (our policy is everyone wears one, so this is already covered)

A Type IV throwable device should be available at the launch site.

### Sound-Producing Devices

Every guide must carry a whistle on the water. This is both a TPWD requirement and a practical safety tool. One blast to get attention, three blasts signals distress.

### Incident Reporting

All incidents must be documented and reported to management within 2 hours. This includes:

- Any capsize, even if no one is hurt
- Any injury, no matter how minor
- Any equipment failure on the water
- Any encounter with emergency services
- Near-misses that could have resulted in injury

Texas requires reporting of boating accidents to TPWD if there's death, disappearance, injury requiring medical treatment beyond first aid, or property damage exceeding $2,000. Management handles the formal TPWD report, but the guide's incident report is the foundation for that filing.

### Waiver and Liability

Every guest must have a signed waiver before getting on the water. Waivers are managed through FareHarbor and tied to specific bookings. Do not use generic QR codes or unsigned waiver workarounds.

The business carries liability insurance, but waivers are the first line of legal protection. A missing waiver turns a defensible situation into an expensive one.

### Guide Certifications

All guides must maintain current CPR/First Aid certification. Copies are kept on file. If your cert expires, you're off the schedule until it's renewed. No grace period.

### Weather Compliance

Weather decisions aren't optional judgment calls. The Weather Hold Protocol sets clear thresholds:

- Wind >15 mph: hold or reschedule
- Lightning within 10 miles: cancel and shelter immediately
- Visibility <0.25 miles: hold
- Water temp <50°F: require wetsuit or cancel

When in doubt, cancel. No tour is worth a safety incident. Guests can be rescheduled. Lawsuits can't.

### Equipment Standards

Kayaks and SUPs must be inspected daily before use. Any vessel with structural damage (cracks, holes, delamination) is pulled from service immediately. Paddles with cracked blades or bent shafts get replaced.

Gear maintenance isn't glamorous, but it's the foundation of safe operations. A hull that fails mid-lake is a crisis. A hull that gets caught in morning inspection is a 5-minute equipment swap.
