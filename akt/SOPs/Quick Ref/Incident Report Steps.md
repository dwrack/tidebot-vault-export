# Incident Report Steps
**Safety first, documentation second, but both are non-negotiable.**

## Medical Emergency or Serious Injury
1. Call 911 immediately
2. Administer first aid within your training level
3. Designate someone to flag down EMS at the nearest road access point
4. Get all other guests off the water and to a safe location
5. Call management as soon as the situation is stabilized
6. Do not move an injured person unless they're in immediate danger

## Capsizing
1. Stay calm and call out to confirm the guest is conscious and breathing
2. Guide the guest to hold onto the kayak (sit-on-tops won't sink)
3. Assist guest back onto the kayak or tow to shore if needed
4. Check for injuries, hypothermia signs, or panic
5. Offer to end the tour early if the guest is shaken, no pressure either way
6. Log the capsize even if no one was hurt

## Weather Emergency (Lightning/Severe Storm)
1. If lightning is spotted or thunder heard, get everyone off the water immediately
2. Head to nearest shore and move away from the water's edge
3. Seek shelter in a vehicle or solid structure (not under trees)
4. Do not return to water until 30 minutes after the last thunder/lightning
5. Call management and coordinate rescheduling if the tour can't continue

## Vehicle or Trailer Incident
1. Check for injuries first, call 911 if needed
2. Exchange insurance info with any other parties involved
3. Take photos of all vehicles, damage, and the scene
4. Call management immediately
5. File a police report if there's any injury or significant damage
6. Do not admit fault or make statements beyond basic info exchange

## Documentation (All Incidents)
1. Write down what happened while it's fresh: time, location, what you saw
2. Note names and contact info of anyone involved or who witnessed it
3. Take photos if appropriate and safe to do so
4. Send written report to management within 2 hours
5. Do not post anything on social media about the incident
6. Preserve any equipment involved (don't clean, repair, or discard it)

> [!warning]
> If anyone is in the water and unresponsive, that's a 911 call before anything else. Every second matters.

> [!tip]
> Keep your phone in a waterproof case or dry bag at all times. You can't call for help with a dead phone.
