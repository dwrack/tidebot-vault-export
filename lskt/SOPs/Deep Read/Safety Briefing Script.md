# Safety Briefing Script

## Before You Start Talking

The safety briefing happens after check-in, after PFD fitting, and before anyone gets in a kayak. Guests should be standing together, able to hear you, PFDs already on. Don't rush through it. This is 5-7 minutes and it's the most important 5-7 minutes of the tour.

In Houston, you're competing with heat and excitement. People want to get on the water and out of the sun. Acknowledge that, but don't let it speed you past the information that keeps them safe. "I know it's hot standing here. Five minutes of this and we're on the water."

## The Briefing

### PFDs Stay On

"Your PFD stays on for the entire tour. Buckled, the whole time. I know it's hot. I know it feels bulky. But if you go in the water, this is what keeps you floating while your brain catches up to what just happened. We don't take them off on the water, not even for a minute."

This is the number one pushback in Houston because of the heat. Be empathetic but firm. "I get it, it's warm. But the PFD is non-negotiable. You'll be surprised how fast you stop noticing it once we're paddling."

### The Kayaks

"We're using sit-on-top kayaks. You're sitting on the deck, not inside a cockpit. If you flip, and it's unlikely, you slide off and climb back on. No getting trapped. These are very stable, but keep your weight centered. Don't reach way out to one side or stand up."

For tandems: "Person in the back steers, person in the front powers. If you disagree, back wins. That's just how the boat works."

### Paddle Technique

Demo while you talk. Hold the paddle up.

"Grip about shoulder-width apart. The concave side of the blade, the scooped side, faces you. Reach forward, plant the blade in the water near the front of the kayak, pull back to your hip. Switch sides. You don't need to dig deep or pull hard. Smooth beats strong."

"To turn, paddle on the opposite side from where you want to go. To stop, drag a blade in the water."

Keep it brief. Most people figure paddling out within the first few minutes. The demo is so they're not completely lost at launch.

### Staying Together

"We stay together as a group. I'll be up front setting the pace. If you fall behind, no worries, I'll see it and we'll wait. If you're way ahead, I'll ask you to ease up. We move at the speed of the group, and that's fine."

"If you need to stop or need help, raise your paddle overhead. That's the signal. I'll come to you."

### Wildlife

"This is Texas, so yes, there are alligators in these waterways. Before anyone panics: they want nothing to do with you. They're usually sunning on the bank or cruising at a distance. Don't approach them, don't feed them, don't splash at them. If you see one, point it out and we'll enjoy it from a distance."

"You'll also see turtles, herons, egrets, maybe a kingfisher. We might see a water moccasin along the banks. Same rule: look, don't approach. Stay away from overhanging vegetation at the water's edge."

"Part of why this tour is cool is the wildlife. We're guests in their space."

### Heat and Hydration

"Houston heat is real and it's sneaky on the water. You feel the breeze and think you're fine, but you're sweating, the sun is reflecting off the water, and dehydration creeps up fast. Drink water before you feel thirsty. I'll call hydration breaks. Take them seriously."

"If you start feeling dizzy, nauseous, or like the heat is getting to you, tell me immediately. Don't tough it out. Heat exhaustion turns into heat stroke faster than people expect, and that's a medical emergency."

This section matters more in Houston than the cold water section matters in northern operations. Treat it with the same weight.

### Lightning Rule

"If we hear thunder or see lightning, the tour pauses immediately. We head to the nearest shore, get out of the kayaks, and seek shelter. Kayaks on open water in a thunderstorm is the one scenario where there's no discussion. We just go."

"Houston gets afternoon thunderstorms regularly in summer. If it happens, it's not a surprise, and I already have a plan for getting us to shore quickly."

### Questions

"That's the safety stuff. Questions before we hit the water?"

Give it a real pause. People need a moment. Common Houston questions: "Will we see alligators?" (Probably, and it'll be great.) "How hot is it going to get?" (Hot. Drink your water.) "What if I fall in?" (You'll be fine. PFD keeps you up, kayak doesn't sink, and I'm right there.)

## Seasonal Adjustments

**Summer (June-September):** Heavy emphasis on heat and hydration. Mention that afternoon storms are common and you're monitoring. Sunscreen reminder.

**Spring/Fall (March-May, October-November):** Most comfortable paddling weather. Standard briefing. Water is warm, air is pleasant. Still mention hydration since dehydration happens even in mild weather.

**Winter (December-February):** Lighter tours, cooler air, potentially cold water after fronts. If water temp is borderline, mention it. "The water is colder than it looks right now. If you go in, it'll be a shock. PFD is even more important today."

## What the Briefing Isn't

It's not a legal disclaimer read at speed. It's not optional for returning guests. It's not something you skip because the group "looks experienced." It's a conversation between a guide who knows these waters and guests who are trusting you with their safety. Every guest, every tour, every time.
