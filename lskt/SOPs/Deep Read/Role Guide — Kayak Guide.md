# Role Guide — Kayak Guide

## What This Role Is

You're a 1099 independent contractor working with Lone Star Kayak Tours LLC, which is the same entity that operates Houston Booze Cruise. That shared entity is an administrative detail, but it's worth knowing so you're not confused when you see it on paperwork.

As a 1099 contractor, the relationship works differently than a traditional job. You set your own availability. We don't control your schedule hour by hour. But when you're on a tour, you follow our safety protocols, our SOPs, and our guest experience standards without exception. That's the deal. The flexibility of contracting comes with the responsibility of showing up prepared and operating at the standard every single time.

## Before You Start

Texas Parks & Wildlife Department (TPWD) regulates commercial kayak operations in the state. We operate in compliance with TPWD requirements, and you need to understand the basics even though you're not handling the regulatory filings.

Pre-hire requirements:

- CPR and First Aid certification (current, not expired)
- Background check clearance
- Swim proficiency demonstration
- W-9 form (required for 1099 tax reporting)
- Signed contractor agreement
- Kayak or paddle instructor certification is preferred but not required
- Swiftwater or open water rescue training is preferred but not required

The CPR/First Aid and swim test are hard requirements. No cert, no tour. The paddle and rescue certs give you priority for scheduling and we'd rather you have them, but we'll work with you on getting them if you don't have them yet.

## Training Progression

Even experienced paddlers go through our training progression. We've seen guides with ten years of personal kayaking experience who had never managed a group of eight novice paddlers in current. Different skill set.

### Shadow Phase (minimum 2 tours)
You ride along with an experienced guide and watch everything. How they run check-in, deliver the briefing, manage group pacing, handle the nervous first-timer in the back, and read changing conditions. Pay attention to the Houston-specific stuff: how they manage heat, how they deal with boat traffic on busier waterways, how they handle the afternoon thunderstorm that rolls in on schedule from June through September.

### Co-Guide Phase (minimum 3 tours)
You run portions of the tour with a senior guide present. Safety briefing, leading the paddle, managing guest questions. The senior guide gives you feedback after each tour. This is where you find your rhythm. Some people nail the briefing but struggle with group management. Others are natural leaders but rush through safety information. The co-guide phase surfaces your gaps so you can close them.

### Solo Clearance
Management signs off after reviewing feedback from your co-guide tours. The criteria: Can you deliver a complete, confident safety briefing? Can you manage a full group in variable conditions? Can you make the right weather call? Can you handle an emergency without freezing? There's no set number of tours that guarantees clearance. You're ready when you're ready.

## The 1099 Reality

Since you're a contractor:

- **Taxes:** We don't withhold taxes. You receive a 1099 at year-end and are responsible for your own tax obligations, including self-employment tax. Set aside roughly 25-30% of your earnings. Seriously. April surprises are no fun.
- **Availability:** You tell us when you're available. We offer tours. You accept or decline. If you decline a lot, you'll naturally get fewer offers. That's not punitive, it's just how scheduling works when other guides are saying yes.
- **Insurance:** You're not covered by company health insurance or workers' comp. Make sure you have your own coverage.
- **Equipment:** We provide the kayaks, PFDs, paddles, and safety equipment. You bring yourself, your certifications, and your attitude.

## Code of Conduct

Professional, sober, on time. Treat guests well. Don't trash competitors. Don't use your phone during tours except for safety. Don't flirt with guests. Don't post about incidents online.

Houston is a big city but the outdoor recreation community is surprisingly tight. Your reputation follows you. Guides who are reliable, safe, and good with guests get more tours. Guides who aren't, don't.

## Ongoing Development

Even after solo clearance, you're expected to stay current. CPR/First Aid certs need to stay active. If TPWD updates regulations, we'll brief you on changes. If we add new routes or launch sites, there's orientation for that.

We also listen to guest feedback. If a pattern shows up in reviews that relates to your tours, you'll hear about it. That's not a write-up. It's information that helps you improve. The guides who take feedback well are the ones who build a strong book of tours over time.
