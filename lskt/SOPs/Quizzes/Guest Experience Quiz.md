# Guest Experience Quiz — Lone Star Kayak Tours

*Pass: 80% (8 of 10 correct). If you score below 80%, re-read the Role Guide and retake.*

---

### Q1. A guest booked for 2 people shows up with 4. You have the kayaks and PFDs to handle it. What do you do?

A) Turn the extras away since they didn't book
B) Welcome them, add them to the booking in FareHarbor on the spot (they'll need to sign waivers), and adjust your kayak assignments
C) Let them join but skip the waiver since they're just tagging along
D) Call management and wait for instructions before doing anything

**Correct: B**
Extra guests mean extra revenue and a better experience for the group. Add them in FareHarbor so the waiver is tied to their booking, get them signed, fit PFDs, and go. If you don't have enough equipment, then call management. But if you can accommodate them, do it smoothly.

---

### Q2. A guest is visibly nervous about getting in the kayak. They keep saying "I've never done this before." How do you handle it?

A) Tell them it's easy and they'll be fine
B) Spend extra time with them: show them how the kayak works on shore, let them sit in it on land first, pair them in a tandem with a confident paddler, and check on them early in the tour
C) Suggest they sit this one out and wait on shore
D) Put them in a single kayak so they can go at their own pace

**Correct: B**
Nervous guests need confidence built through action, not words. "You'll be fine" doesn't help. Letting them feel the kayak on land, pairing them with someone stable, and checking in early shows you actually care. Most nervous guests end up having the best time because the contrast is so big. A single kayak is the worst option for an anxious beginner.

### Q3. Halfway through the tour, a guest complains that the tour is "boring" and "not what they expected." What do you do?

A) Apologize and offer a refund
B) Ignore it and keep going
C) Acknowledge their feedback, adjust your energy and content delivery, engage them directly with something specific ("check out that osprey diving"), and note it for your post-tour report
D) End the tour early since the group isn't having fun

**Correct: C**
One bored guest doesn't mean a failed tour. Acknowledge without being defensive, then redirect with specifics. Often "boring" means "I'm not being engaged." Pull them into the experience: give them a task, point out something only they can see from their position, ask them a question. Note it for your report so the feedback gets back to management.

### Q4. A guest pulls out a flask and starts drinking on the water. What do you do?

A) Tell them alcohol isn't allowed and confiscate it
B) Ignore it as long as they're not causing problems
C) Monitor them. If they remain in control and aren't creating safety issues, let it go. If they become impaired (slurred speech, loss of coordination, reckless behavior), address it directly and firmly.
D) End the tour for everyone immediately

**Correct: C**
Adults make their own choices, but you're responsible for everyone's safety. A sip from a flask on a calm paddle isn't an emergency. Someone who can't hold a paddle straight or is leaning dangerously is. Know the line and act decisively when it's crossed. "Hey [name], I need you to put that away for the rest of the trip. Safety first."

### Q5. At the end of the tour, how do you ask for a review without it feeling forced?

A) Hand them a card with a QR code and walk away
B) Thank them specifically for something real from the tour, then mention that a Google review takes 2 minutes and helps the business more than anything. Keep it genuine and brief.
C) Don't ask. If they liked it they'll leave one on their own.
D) Text them the review link during the tour so they can do it while it's fresh

**Correct: B**
The review ask works when it follows a genuine moment. "That was a great catch spotting that eagle nest. Hey, if you had a good time today, a Google review honestly means everything for a small operation like ours." Specific, human, brief. Don't be a robot about it. Most guests are happy to do it if you don't make it weird.

### Q6. A guest with a visible physical disability arrives for the tour. They didn't mention anything when booking. What do you do?

A) Tell them the tour might not be suitable for them
B) Privately and respectfully ask about their comfort level, discuss what the tour involves physically, and work together to figure out the best setup (tandem placement, which seat, any modifications needed)
C) Treat them exactly the same as everyone else without asking any questions
D) Call management to decide if they can participate

**Correct: B**
You don't assume someone can't do something, but you also don't ignore it. A brief, private, respectful conversation: "Hey, I want to make sure you have the best setup. The tour involves about 2 hours of paddling. Want to talk through what'll be most comfortable?" Let them lead. Most people with disabilities know exactly what they need.

### Q7. A family with kids (ages 8 and 10) is on your tour alongside a couple clearly on a romantic getaway. How do you manage the group dynamic?

A) Separate them on the water and alternate your attention
B) Set the tone early: this is a nature tour for everyone. Keep your content family-appropriate, engage the kids (they're often the most enthusiastic wildlife spotters), and make the couple feel like they're getting a premium experience through your knowledge and storytelling.
C) Focus on the kids since they need more supervision
D) Focus on the couple since families are easier to manage

**Correct: B**
Mixed groups are normal. Kids actually make tours more fun when you engage them. "Who can spot the first bird?" turns them into your best scouts. The couple gets a great tour because your content is sharp and the atmosphere is lively. Everyone wins when you set the right tone early.

### Q8. A guest asks you directly "How much should I tip?" What do you say?

A) "Whatever you think is fair"
B) "Tips are totally optional but appreciated. Most folks do $10-20 per person if they had a good time. But seriously, a Google review is just as valuable to us."
C) "$20 per person is standard"
D) "We don't accept tips"

**Correct: B**
Be honest and low-pressure. Give a range so they have a reference point, emphasize it's optional, and redirect to the review. Never make anyone feel bad for not tipping. The review ask is real too: a 5-star Google review with a photo is worth more than $20 over time.

---

### Q9. Post-tour, a guest messages you directly complaining about something (the drive was too long, the water was murky, etc.). How do you respond?

A) Apologize and offer them a discount on their next tour
B) Acknowledge their feedback, thank them for sharing it, and let them know you've passed it to management. Don't make promises about refunds or discounts yourself.
C) Ignore it since the tour is over
D) Defend the experience and explain why their complaint isn't valid

**Correct: B**
You're the guide, not the manager. Acknowledge, don't get defensive, and pass it up. "Hey, thanks for letting me know. I'll make sure management hears this. I appreciate you coming out today." You don't have authority to offer refunds or discounts, and you shouldn't promise things you can't deliver.

### Q10. It's 95 degrees and humid. Two hours into the paddle, a guest says they feel lightheaded. What do you do?

A) Tell them to drink some water and keep paddling
B) Stop the group, get the guest into shade if possible, give them water, check for other heat exhaustion signs (heavy sweating, pale skin, nausea, rapid pulse). If symptoms don't improve in 10-15 minutes or get worse, call 911.
C) End the tour immediately and rush back to the launch
D) Pour water on their head to cool them down

**Correct: B**
Heat exhaustion is real and it escalates. Stop, shade, hydrate, assess. Don't rush back to the launch because that means more exertion for the person who's already overheating. If they recover with rest and water, great. If they get confused, stop sweating (paradoxically worse), or vomit, that's heat stroke territory and you need 911.
