# Safety & Compliance Quiz — Lone Star Kayak Tours

*Pass: 80% (8 of 10 correct). If you score below 80%, re-read the Safety & Compliance Overview and retake.*

---

### Q1. You're fitting PFDs before launch and a guest says "I'm a strong swimmer, I don't need one." What do you do?

A) Let them skip it since they accepted the risk on the waiver
B) Explain it's required by law and company policy, no exceptions, and fit them properly
C) Give them the PFD but say they can take it off once you're on the water
D) Tell them they can hold it in their lap instead of wearing it

**Correct: B**
Every guest wears a PFD for the entire time on the water. This isn't a suggestion. TPWD requires it for commercial operations, and our policy goes further: no PFD, no paddle. Swimming ability is irrelevant when you're disoriented underwater after a capsize.

### Q2. You check the weather the morning of your tour and see sustained winds at 17mph. What's the right call?

A) Run the tour as normal, kayaks handle wind fine
B) Hold the tour, contact management, and inform guests of a possible reschedule
C) Cancel immediately and issue refunds
D) Run the tour but shorten the route

**Correct: B**
Our threshold is 15mph sustained. At 17mph, you hold. Don't cancel outright without talking to management first since conditions may improve, but don't launch either. Inexperienced paddlers in wind lose control fast, and a fun tour turns into a rescue operation.

### Q3. A guest's waiver shows unsigned in FareHarbor. They say "I signed it last time I came." What do you do?

A) Take their word for it and let them on
B) Pull up their booking-specific waiver on your phone and have them sign now
C) Send them a generic QR code to sign quickly
D) Call management to verify their previous waiver

**Correct: B**
Every waiver must be tied to that guest's specific booking. Previous waivers don't carry over. Generic QR codes are never acceptable because they aren't linked to the booking. Pull up their specific waiver, hand them your phone, get it signed before they touch a kayak.

### Q4. Mid-tour, you see lightning in the distance but haven't heard thunder yet. What do you do?

A) Keep paddling but watch it closely
B) Head to shore immediately and get everyone out of the water
C) Wait until you hear thunder to make the call
D) Ask the group what they want to do

**Correct: B**
If you see lightning, you move. Don't wait for thunder. Don't gauge distance. Water is the most dangerous place to be in a lightning storm. Get everyone to shore, into the van if accessible, away from tall isolated trees. Contact management. Guests are not charged if the tour is cut short for weather.

### Q5. You notice a PFD has a broken buckle during morning inspection. You have exactly enough PFDs for today's guest count with no spares. What do you do?

A) Use it anyway and tie it with a knot
B) Run the tour short one PFD and have the strongest swimmer go without
C) Contact management immediately, do not launch until you have enough functional PFDs
D) Borrow one from another guide's stock without telling them

**Correct: C**
Every guest gets a properly functioning PFD. No exceptions, no workarounds. A knotted buckle isn't a functioning PFD. A tour short on safety equipment doesn't launch. Contact management so they can solve it (spare stock, delay, reassign equipment).

### Q6. A guest capsizes and is panicking in the water, thrashing and not grabbing their kayak. What's your first action?

A) Paddle to them as fast as possible and pull them onto your kayak
B) Yell instructions from where you are so you can keep watching the group
C) Signal the group to stop (horizontal paddle), then paddle to the guest calmly and talk them through it
D) Call 911 immediately

**Correct: C**
Secure the group first (horizontal paddle = stop), then go to the guest. Approaching calmly matters because a panicking person can flip your kayak too if you rush in. Talk to them: "Stay with your boat, I'm coming." Get them to grab the kayak first. If they can't re-enter, tow to shore. 911 is for medical emergencies, not a standard capsize.

### Q7. During guest check-in, someone mentions they have Type 1 diabetes. How do you handle it?

A) Tell them they can't participate for liability reasons
B) Note it, confirm you have granola bars and water in the guide bag, ask if they have their own supplies, and check in on them periodically during the tour
C) Have them sign an additional medical waiver
D) Ignore it since they already signed the standard waiver

**Correct: B**
You don't refuse guests for manageable conditions. You prepare. Confirm your guide bag has granola bars (it should always), ask if they brought their own glucose supplies, and make a mental note to check on them during the tour. If they seem off at any point (confused, shaky, sweating unusually), address it immediately.

### Q8. You're loading kayaks back on the trailer after a tour and notice a crack in one hull that wasn't there before launch. What do you do?

A) Load it normally and mention it to management when you get back
B) Flag it to management immediately by phone, photograph it, and note it in your end-of-day report. Do not use that kayak again until cleared.
C) Try to patch it with duct tape from the guide bag
D) Leave it at the launch site and come back for it later

**Correct: B**
Equipment damage gets reported immediately, not at the end of the day. Photograph it for documentation. That kayak is out of rotation until management inspects and clears it. A cracked hull can take on water and become a safety issue. Don't improvise repairs, don't delay reporting.

### Q9. TPWD requires commercial kayak operations in Texas to maintain what documentation?

A) Just a business license
B) Current watercraft registration (if required), state-mandated safety equipment on each vessel, and any required commercial outfitter permits
C) Only a captain's license
D) Federal USCG certification

**Correct: B**
Texas Parks & Wildlife oversees commercial paddle operations. You need current registration, proper safety equipment per vessel (PFDs, whistles, throw bags), and applicable commercial permits. USCG certification is for motorized vessels, not kayaks. Know your state regulatory body.

### Q10. A guest's young child (age 6) is on the manifest. What's the PFD requirement?

A) Children under 13 must wear a PFD at all times, but this is already our policy for everyone
B) Children are exempt if a parent signs off
C) Only children under 5 need PFDs
D) PFDs are optional for all ages on kayaks

**Correct: A**
Texas law requires PFDs for children under 13 on any vessel, but our policy is simpler: everyone wears a PFD, every time, full stop. For kids, make sure the PFD is properly sized (not an adult PFD on a child) and fitted snugly. Check that it can't be pulled over the child's head.
