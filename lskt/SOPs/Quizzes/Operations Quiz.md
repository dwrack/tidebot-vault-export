# Operations Quiz — Lone Star Kayak Tours

*Pass: 80% (8 of 10 correct). If you score below 80%, re-read the Quick Ref checklists and retake.*

---

### Q1. You arrive for your morning inspection and discover you're short 3 PFDs for today's guest count. What do you do?

A) Run the tour and have the 3 strongest swimmers go without
B) Contact management immediately. Do not launch until you have enough functional PFDs for every guest.
C) Check if any PFDs from yesterday are still drying and use those
D) Ask guests if anyone brought their own

**Correct: B**
No shortcuts on PFDs. Every guest gets one, every time. Contact management so they can pull from another location, delay the tour, or adjust the booking. Yesterday's drying PFDs might work if they're functional, but check with management first and inspect them properly.

---

### Q2. You're at the launch site and it starts raining steadily. No lightning, no wind. What do you do?

A) Cancel the tour
B) Run the tour. Rain without lightning or dangerous wind isn't a safety issue for kayaking. Warn guests they'll get wet (they were going to anyway), and keep energy high.
C) Wait 30 minutes to see if it passes
D) Call management for a decision

**Correct: B**
Rain alone isn't a cancel condition. You're on kayaks. Guests are already going to get wet. The key factors are lightning, wind, and visibility. If those are fine, you paddle. Adjust your energy since rain can either kill the mood or make it an adventure. Your attitude sets the tone.

### Q3. A guest left their phone in your vehicle at the launch site and realizes it mid-tour. They want to go back immediately. What do you do?

A) Turn the whole group around
B) Acknowledge their concern, reassure them the vehicle is locked and the phone is safe, and let them know you'll get back to it as soon as the tour wraps up. Don't cut the tour short for one phone.
C) Let them paddle back alone to get it
D) End the tour early so they can retrieve it

**Correct: B**
One guest's phone doesn't override everyone else's experience. The vehicle is locked, the phone isn't going anywhere. Reassure them calmly and keep the tour moving. Never let a guest paddle alone back to the launch.

### Q4. Your guide bag is missing the first aid kit when you check it during morning inspection. First tour is in 90 minutes. What do you do?

A) Run the tour without it, injuries are rare
B) Contact management immediately. A tour does not launch without a first aid kit. Get a replacement from another location, buy one at a nearby store, or delay until one is sourced.
C) Bring some band-aids from your car instead
D) Ask guests if anyone has medical supplies

**Correct: B**
The first aid kit isn't optional equipment. You can't predict when you'll need it, but when you do, nothing else substitutes. 90 minutes is enough time to solve this. Contact management, get a replacement, and don't cut corners.

---

### Q5. You're running 20 minutes behind schedule because a guest was late to pickup. You still have a full tour ahead. What do you do?

A) Skip the safety briefing to make up time
B) Shorten the tour content (fewer stops, tighter pace) but never skip safety procedures. Briefing, PFD fitting, and paddle demo happen in full every time.
C) Rush through everything to get back on schedule
D) Extend the return time by 20 minutes

**Correct: B**
Safety procedures are non-negotiable. The briefing, fitting, and demo take the time they take. If you need to shorten something, trim a content stop or reduce paddle time. Guests would rather have a slightly shorter paddle than a rushed, unsafe experience. And a calm, confident guide who adjusts smoothly is better than one who's visibly stressed about the clock.

### Q6. Between tours, you notice a kayak has standing water inside from the morning tour. What do you do?

A) Ignore it, sit-on-tops are supposed to have water in them
B) Drain the scupper plugs, tip the kayak to empty it, and check that the drainage system isn't clogged. Standing water adds weight and makes the kayak sluggish for the next group.
C) Assign it to the lightest guest
D) Leave it and grab a different kayak

**Correct: B**
Standing water means the scuppers aren't draining properly. This affects performance and guest experience. Takes 2 minutes to fix. Don't just swap kayaks because the problem will still be there for the next guide. Fix it, or if you can't, flag it to management and pull it from rotation.

### Q7. FareHarbor shows 12 guests booked for your tour, but 14 show up claiming they all booked. What do you do?

A) Turn away the 2 extras
B) Check FareHarbor carefully (refresh the manifest since last-minute additions happen). If 2 are genuinely unbooked, add them if you have equipment and capacity. If you don't have capacity, contact management.
C) Let everyone on without checking
D) Make 2 people share a tandem kayak meant for one

**Correct: B**
Refresh first. Manifests change. If they're legitimately unbooked, accommodate if you can (enough kayaks, PFDs, and you can safely manage the group size). If you're maxed out on equipment or can't safely guide that many, management needs to help. Never compromise safety to avoid an awkward conversation.

### Q8. End of day: you realize you forgot to spray PFDs with MiraZyme after the afternoon tour. What do you do?

A) Skip it today, you'll do double tomorrow
B) Go back and do it now, even if it means an extra 20 minutes. PFD maintenance happens after every tour, not when it's convenient.
C) Text the next guide to do it before their morning tour
D) It's not that important, they'll be fine

**Correct: B**
PFD odor is a real guest experience problem and a health/hygiene issue. MiraZyme after every tour is the standard because that's how you prevent the buildup. Skipping it once becomes skipping it twice. Go back and do it. 20 minutes now saves a guest complaint tomorrow.

### Q9. During a tour, your phone dies. You have no way to contact management or call 911. What's your priority?

A) End the tour immediately and head back
B) Continue the tour but stay on the most conservative route, keep the group tight, and be extra vigilant about conditions. Head back at the normal time. Get your phone charged or replaced before the next tour.
C) Ask a guest to be your emergency contact for the duration
D) It's fine, you know the route

**Correct: B**
A dead phone isn't an emergency that ends the tour, but it does change your risk calculus. Stay conservative, keep the group close, don't take any chances you'd normally take knowing you have a phone backup. Borrowing a guest's phone for a genuine emergency is acceptable, but don't make it their job to be your safety net. Fix the problem before the next tour.

### Q10. You get a flat tire on the trailer while driving back from the launch with guests in the van. What do you do?

A) Keep driving slowly to the nearest service station
B) Pull over safely, ensure guest safety, assess the situation. If you can change it and have a spare, do it. If not, contact management for roadside assistance. Keep guests informed and comfortable.
C) Unhitch the trailer and leave it, come back for it later
D) Call 911

**Correct: B**
A flat tire isn't a 911 situation, but it requires you to stop and assess. Driving on a flat damages the trailer and is unsafe. Pull over where it's safe, let guests know what's happening (transparency builds trust), and solve it. If you have a spare, great. If not, management handles roadside assistance. Guests will remember how you handled the problem more than the problem itself.
