# Culture & Values Quiz
**Lone Star Kayak Tours | Davey Jones' Locker**

**Passing score: 80%**
**Format: Choose the best answer for each scenario.**

---

## Question 1

A guest asks you how long you've been doing this. You've only been guiding for three weeks. What do you say?

- A) "I'm brand new, honestly. But I've been trained well and I'm excited to show you the bayou."
- B) "A while now!" (keep it vague so they don't worry)
- C) "This is basically my first tour, so bear with me."
- D) "Long enough to know what I'm doing."

**Best answer: A**
Honesty builds trust faster than vagueness. You're new, and that's fine. Pairing honesty with confidence ("I've been trained well") reassures the guest without making them feel like a test subject. B and D are evasive. C undersells you and makes guests nervous for no reason.

---

## Question 2

You overhear another guide trash-talking a competitor kayak company to guests during a tour. What's your move?

- A) Jump in and agree so the guests feel like they picked the right company.
- B) Pull the guide aside after the tour and let them know that's not how we operate.
- C) Ignore it. It's their tour, not yours.
- D) Report them to management immediately without saying anything to them.

**Best answer: B**
We don't build our reputation by tearing others down. A direct, respectful conversation after the tour is the right call. If it keeps happening, then loop in management. Going straight to a report without talking to them first (D) skips a step. Ignoring it (C) lets a bad habit spread.

---

## Question 3

A guest tips you $100 on a sunset tour and asks if the other guide on the trip gets any of it. The other guide helped load kayaks but isn't officially co-guiding. What do you do?

- A) Pocket the full tip. You ran the tour.
- B) Tell the guest you'll split it, then keep it all.
- C) Split it with the other guide and thank the guest.
- D) Tell the guest "tips are individual" and keep it.

**Best answer: C**
If someone helped make the tour happen, they deserve a cut. This isn't about policy, it's about being the kind of person people want to work alongside. B is obviously dishonest. A and D are technically defensible but miss the point of being a team.

---

## Question 4

You're running 10 minutes late to your launch site because of traffic. You haven't contacted anyone. What should you have done?

- A) Just drive faster and hope nobody notices.
- B) Text the ops manager as soon as you realized you'd be late.
- C) Wait until you arrive and apologize in person.
- D) Text the guests directly to let them know.

**Best answer: B**
The ops team can manage guest expectations if they know early. Waiting until you arrive (C) means guests are standing around wondering what's happening. You don't have guest contact info to text them directly (D), and that's not your role anyway. Driving faster (A) is dangerous and stupid.

## Question 5

A regular guest who's done three tours with you asks if you can run a private tour "off the books" for their family this weekend and pay you directly. What do you say?

- A) "I'd love to, but let me check with the office first."
- B) "Sure, I'll give you my personal rate."
- C) "I can't do that, but I can help you book a private tour through Lone Star."
- D) "Let me think about it and get back to you."

**Best answer: C**
Running side tours using the company's brand, equipment, or reputation is a hard no. You're a 1099 contractor, but when you're working under the Lone Star name, bookings go through FareHarbor. C is honest and redirects them to a legitimate option. A sounds like you might do it. D is just a delayed version of B.

## Question 6

You notice a fellow guide consistently shows up in ratty clothes, unshowered, with gear thrown loosely in their car. Their reviews are fine. Is this a problem?

- A) No. If reviews are good, nothing else matters.
- B) Yes. How you show up reflects on the whole brand, even if individual reviews haven't caught it yet.
- C) Only if guests complain.
- D) It's their business as a 1099 contractor.

**Best answer: B**
Presentation matters before the first word is spoken. A guide who looks like they don't care about their own gear signals they might not care about guest safety either. Reviews lag reality. Waiting for complaints (C) means the damage is already done. Being a 1099 doesn't exempt you from brand standards (D).

---

## Question 7

A guest posts a 3-star Google review saying the tour was "fine but nothing special." You know you guided that trip and it went smoothly. How do you feel about this?

- A) Frustrated. "Fine" tours are the enemy. You should be creating memorable experiences, not just adequate ones.
- B) Satisfied. No complaints means a good tour.
- C) Annoyed at the guest for being ungrateful.
- D) Indifferent. You can't control what people write.

**Best answer: A**
"Fine" is the most dangerous word in the tour business. A 1-star review tells you what went wrong. A 5-star review tells you what went right. A 3-star review means the guest had no reason to remember you. Our job isn't to avoid problems, it's to create moments people talk about.

## Question 8

During a tour, a guest makes a mildly racist joke. Nobody laughs. The guest seems to realize it landed badly. What do you do?

- A) Laugh politely to ease the tension and move on.
- B) Ignore it completely and redirect the group's attention to something on the water.
- C) Confront the guest directly in front of the group.
- D) End the tour immediately.

**Best answer: B**
You don't reward bad jokes with laughs, and you don't need to create a bigger scene than the moment warrants. Redirecting moves everyone past an awkward moment without validating it. If it escalates or happens again, you address it directly but privately. Confronting in front of the group (C) punishes everyone. Ending the tour (D) is a nuclear option for a single comment.

---

## Question 9

You're guiding a bachelorette party and they want to blast music from a Bluetooth speaker on the water. What's your call?

- A) Let them. They're paying customers and it's a celebration.
- B) Allow it at low volume but explain you'll need to turn it down if it disturbs wildlife or other paddlers.
- C) Say no. Music isn't allowed on tours, period.
- D) Let them play it but complain about it to the ops manager after.

**Best answer: B**
We're in the business of good times, and a bachelorette party with zero music is a buzzkill. But we're also on shared waterways with wildlife and other people. Setting a reasonable expectation upfront ("keep it at conversation level, and if we're near nesting areas I'll ask you to pause it") respects everyone. A blanket ban (C) is unnecessarily rigid for a celebration.

## Question 10

It's your day off but another guide calls in sick for a sold-out sunset tour. The ops manager asks if you can cover. You have dinner plans. What's the right mindset?

- A) You're a 1099 contractor. You have zero obligation to say yes, and you shouldn't feel guilty about it.
- B) You should always say yes. The company comes first.
- C) You're not obligated, but if you can make it work, stepping up when the team needs you is how you build a reputation worth having.
- D) Say yes, then resent it the whole tour.

**Best answer: C**
Nobody's forcing you to cover. That's real, and A isn't wrong on the facts. But the guides who become indispensable are the ones who show up when it's inconvenient. It's not about obligation. It's about the kind of guide you want to be. If you genuinely can't, say so without guilt. If you can swing it, your team will remember. D is worse than saying no.

---

## Question 11

A guest asks you to take a photo of them with their phone. You take one quick shot and hand it back. Is that enough?

- A) Yes. You took the photo they asked for.
- B) No. Take 3-4 shots from slightly different angles. Check for good lighting. Show them a quick preview. This is a memory they'll share.
- C) Only if the lighting is good. Otherwise one shot is fine.
- D) Offer to take a video instead since it's more shareable.

**Best answer: B**
A single rushed photo says "I'm doing you a favor." Multiple shots with a quick preview says "I want this to be great for you." Those photos end up on Instagram, in group chats, on Google reviews. Every good photo is free marketing and a genuine gift to the guest. This takes 30 extra seconds.

## Question 12

You finish a tour and realize you gave one of your best performances. The group loved it. Energy was high. As they're leaving, one guest quietly tells you they're going through a divorce and this was the first time they've smiled in weeks. How does this land for you?

- A) Nice to hear, but you move on to the next tour.
- B) This is the whole point. We're not just running kayak tours. We're giving people a break from their real lives, even if just for two hours.
- C) You feel awkward and don't really know how to respond.
- D) You tell them about a good therapist you know.

**Best answer: B**
This is the job beneath the job. Paddles and PFDs are the vehicle, but what we're actually selling is two hours where someone's problems can't reach them. When you understand that, you guide differently. You're more present, more intentional. You stop thinking about tips and start thinking about impact. C is human and honest, but B is the growth mindset we want.
