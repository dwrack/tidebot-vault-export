# Role Guide — Kayak Guide

## What This Role Is

You're a W-2 seasonal employee of Davey Jones' Locker, operating under Door County Kayak Tours. That distinction matters. You're not a contractor picking up shifts. You have a schedule, you follow our processes, and we handle your taxes through Homebase. You'll fill out a W-4 and I-9 before your first day, not a W-9.

The job is simple to describe and hard to do well: get guests on the water, keep them safe, and make sure they have the kind of experience that ends up in a five-star review. In Door County, that comes with a twist most kayak operations don't deal with. The water is cold. Lake Michigan doesn't care that it's July. You could be guiding in 55-degree water on a day that feels like summer everywhere except the lake. That reality shapes everything about how we train, brief, and operate.

## Before You Start

Wisconsin DNR regulates commercial kayak operations. We comply with all state requirements for guided watercraft tours, and you need to understand those requirements even if you're not the one filing paperwork.

Pre-hire requirements:

- CPR and First Aid certification (current, not expired)
- Background check clearance
- Swim proficiency demonstration
- Kayak or paddle instructor certification is preferred but not required
- Swiftwater or open water rescue training is preferred but not required

If you don't have the paddle or rescue certs, that's fine. We'll get you trained. But the CPR/First Aid and swim test are non-negotiable before you touch a kayak with guests in it.

## Training Progression

Nobody goes solo on day one. The progression is intentional and we don't skip steps.

### Shadow Phase (2-3 tours)
You ride along with an experienced guide. You watch how they run the briefing, handle nervous paddlers, manage the group on open water, and deal with wind shifts. Your job here is to absorb, not perform. Pay attention to how they read the water conditions, because that's the skill that takes longest to develop in Door County. The difference between a calm morning and a sketchy afternoon can be about two hours and a wind direction change.

### Co-Guide Phase (3-5 tours)
You run portions of the tour with a senior guide present. You'll do the safety briefing, lead sections of the paddle, and handle guest questions. The senior guide is there to catch anything you miss and give you feedback after. This phase is where most people either click or realize they need more time. Both outcomes are fine.

### Solo Clearance
Management signs off on your solo readiness based on feedback from your co-guide tours. There's no set number of tours that guarantees clearance. Some people are ready after three co-guide runs. Others need six or seven. The deciding factors are: Can you deliver the safety briefing completely and confidently? Can you read changing water conditions and make the right call? Can you handle a capsized guest in cold water without panicking?

## Code of Conduct

This is the short version: Be professional. Be on time. Be sober. Treat guests like you'd treat friends visiting your town. Don't trash-talk other tour companies. Don't use your phone during tours except for safety communication. Don't flirt with guests. Don't post about incidents on social media.

The longer version: Door County is a small community. Tourism is the economic engine. The reputation of this operation directly affects whether we get to keep running here. Every interaction you have, on or off the water, reflects on the business. That's not corporate talk. It's practical reality in a place where the coffee shop owner knows the marina manager knows the county board member.

## Seasonal Scheduling

Door County is a seasonal operation. The bulk of our tours run from late May through early October, with the peak being June through August when tourist traffic is highest. Schedules come through Homebase, and you'll know your shifts in advance.

Peak season means early mornings and sometimes back-to-back tours. Shoulder season (May, September, October) means fewer tours but colder water, which actually requires more experience and preparation per trip. Late season guides need to be especially sharp on cold water protocols.

There's no off-season work. When the season ends, it ends. Some guides come back year after year, and those returning guides get scheduling priority.

## Ongoing Development

Even after solo clearance, you're still learning. We do informal check-ins throughout the season. If we notice a pattern in guest feedback, you'll hear about it. If conditions change (say we add a new launch site or start running evening tours), there's additional training for that.

Wisconsin DNR can update regulations between seasons. Whatever changes come down, we'll brief you before they affect your tours. You're expected to stay current on any updates that affect how you guide.

The guides who last more than one season are the ones who treat every tour like it matters, even the Tuesday afternoon trip with two guests. That's the standard.
