# Weather Hold Protocol

## The Basic Idea

A weather hold means we pause or cancel a tour because conditions are unsafe or trending unsafe. It's not a judgment call you make in the moment with guests standing there. It's a structured decision process with clear thresholds, and it starts well before launch time.

Door County weather is more variable than people expect. You can wake up to a perfect morning and have three-foot waves on Lake Michigan by early afternoon. Green Bay and the lake side of the peninsula often have completely different conditions on the same day. Wind direction matters enormously. A southwest wind that barely ripples an inland lake can stack serious waves on the Lake Michigan shore.

## Thresholds

These are not guidelines. They're hard lines.

| Condition | Threshold | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Wind speed | >15 mph sustained | Hold: do not launch. Reassess in 30 minutes. |
| Lightning | Within 10 miles | Cancel immediately. If on water, get to shore now. |
| Visibility | <0.25 miles | Hold: fog, heavy rain, or smoke. Do not launch. |
| Water temperature | <50°F | Wetsuits required. If no wetsuits available, cancel. |

### Wind

15 mph is the line because that's roughly where sit-on-top kayaks with novice paddlers start becoming hard to control. Experienced kayakers can handle more, but our guests are mostly vacationers, many of them first-time paddlers. On Lake Michigan, 15 mph sustained wind can produce 2-3 foot waves depending on fetch. On Green Bay or inland waters, the same wind has less effect, but we hold the same threshold across all locations for simplicity and safety.

When wind is borderline (12-15 mph), check the trend. If it's been building all morning and the forecast says it continues, treat it as a hold even if you're technically below threshold at launch time. You don't want to be a mile from shore when it crosses 20.

### Lightning

The 10-mile rule is standard across outdoor recreation. If you see lightning or hear thunder, you're within range. Get off the water. Period. Kayaks on open water are about the worst place you can be during an electrical storm. Flat water, you're the tallest object, holding a metal or carbon paddle.

If lightning appears mid-tour, paddle to the nearest shore. Do not continue to the planned takeout if it's farther away. Get people off the water, away from the water's edge, and into a vehicle or solid structure. Not under a tree. Wait 30 minutes after the last observed lightning or thunder before considering returning to the water. Most of the time, a lightning cancel means the tour is done for the day.

### Visibility

Fog is common in Door County, especially early morning in spring and fall. Thick fog on open water is disorienting and dangerous. You can't see boat traffic, you can't navigate reliably, and guests panic when they can't see shore. Quarter-mile visibility is the minimum. If you can't clearly see landmarks you'd normally use for navigation, don't go.

Morning fog often burns off by 10 or 11 AM. If the tour is early and fog is present, delay launch and reassess every 30 minutes. Communicate with guests that you're holding for their safety and give them a realistic timeline.

### Water Temperature

Below 50°F, the cold shock risk from an unexpected immersion is serious enough that we require wetsuits. If we don't have wetsuits available or the guest doesn't have one, we cancel. This mostly affects early season (May, early June) and late season (October) tours.

Check the actual water temp that morning. Don't rely on yesterday's reading or a regional forecast. Water temp can swing several degrees overnight, especially if there's been an upwelling event on the Lake Michigan side.

## Lake Michigan vs. Green Bay vs. Inland

This matters more than people think.

**Lake Michigan (east side of peninsula):** The most exposed water we operate on. Larger fetch means bigger waves for the same wind speed. Colder water earlier in the season due to depth. More susceptible to sudden condition changes. When in doubt on the lake side, hold.

**Green Bay (west side):** Generally calmer, warmer earlier in the season, and more protected. But it's not immune. Strong south or southwest winds can build significant chop on Green Bay. Don't get complacent just because it's "the bay side."

**Inland waters:** Most protected. Wind has less effect, water warms faster, no significant wave development. But lightning and visibility rules still apply 100%.

## Communication Protocol

When you call a weather hold or cancel:

1. **Tell management first.** Call or text. Don't just decide and then inform them after the fact. If conditions are borderline, management may want input on the call.
2. **Notify guests.** Be direct and friendly. "Conditions on the water aren't safe for today's tour. We're going to reschedule you." Don't apologize excessively or hedge. You're keeping them safe and that's the right call.
3. **Offer alternatives.** Reschedule for another day, different time, or a different route that might have more protection from current conditions. Work through FareHarbor for rebooking.
4. **Document it.** Log the hold/cancel, the conditions that triggered it, and the time. This helps with refund processing and tracks weather patterns across the season.

The guides who make the best weather calls are the ones who've spent enough time on Door County water to read the signs before hitting the threshold. That instinct develops over time. Until then, lean conservative. Nobody has ever regretted canceling a tour for safety. People have definitely regretted launching when they shouldn't have.
