# Guest Experience Quiz — DCKT
**Score 80% or higher to pass. One best answer per question.**

---

**1. A family of four arrives for a tour. The youngest is about 8 years old and visibly scared. The parents say "she'll be fine once we get going." What's your approach?**

A) Trust the parents and proceed normally
B) Talk directly to the child, find out what's scaring her, and address it while fitting her PFD
C) Tell the parents the child is too young for the tour
D) Pair the child with a parent in a tandem and hope for the best

**Best answer: B.** Kids respond to being talked to, not talked about. Maybe she's scared of the water, maybe of tipping, maybe it's just unfamiliar. A minute of direct conversation and showing her how stable the kayak is usually does it. Parents saying "she'll be fine" isn't a plan. Engaging with the child is.

---

**2. A couple on their honeymoon booked the tour and they're clearly more interested in each other than kayaking. How do you make this a great experience for them?**

A) Keep the group together and don't give them special treatment
B) Give them space when safe to do so, point out scenic spots for photos, and let the tour feel romantic rather than instructional
C) Speed through the tour so they can get back to their vacation
D) Suggest they cancel and do something else instead

**Best answer: B.** Reading what the guest actually wants is a big part of the job. A honeymoon couple doesn't need you lecturing about paddle technique for 90 minutes. Point out the beautiful spots, offer to take their photo with the bluffs in the background, and let them enjoy being on the water together. Safety standards don't change, but the vibe can.

---

**3. It's early June and a guest shows up in shorts and a tank top. Water temp is 56°F. They don't have a change of clothes. What do you do?**

A) Let them go as-is since we can't control what people wear
B) Explain that the water is much colder than the air, mention the risk if they capsize, and offer any extra layers or gear you have available
C) Refuse to let them on the tour
D) Tell them to buy a wetsuit from a local shop before the tour

**Best answer: B.** You can't force people to dress differently, but you can inform them clearly about the risk. If you have spare layers, a windbreaker, or a splash jacket, offer it. Make sure they understand that getting wet in 56-degree water in a tank top is a very different situation than it would be in August. Document that you informed them.

---

**4. A guest says they booked the tour thinking it would be a "party kayak" experience with drinks on the water. They brought a cooler of beer. How do you handle this?**

A) Let them bring the beer, it's their vacation
B) Explain that alcohol is not permitted on tours, it's a safety issue on cold water, and the cooler stays on shore
C) Let them have one beer each as a compromise
D) Tell them they should have read the booking description more carefully

**Best answer: B.** Alcohol and cold water don't mix. Impaired paddlers capsize more, and alcohol accelerates hypothermia. Be friendly about it but firm. No lectures about reading the fine print. Just: "We don't allow alcohol on the water for safety reasons. We can keep your cooler here and you can enjoy it after the tour."

---

**5. Mid-tour, a guest complains that the tour is "too slow" and "boring." What do you do?**

A) Speed up the whole group to match their pace
B) Acknowledge their energy, explain the group pace, and engage them by pointing out something interesting or giving them a mini-challenge like a navigation task
C) Ignore them and hope they stop complaining
D) Tell them they can paddle ahead on their own

**Best answer: B.** Some guests are athletic and want more stimulation. You can't change the group pace, but you can redirect their energy. Point out something they might have missed, challenge them to spot wildlife, or explain the geology of the shoreline. An engaged guest isn't a bored guest.

---

**6. A group of 6 friends arrives. One person is clearly the organizer and keeps speaking for the group. During PFD fitting, you notice one of the quieter members looks uncomfortable but hasn't said anything. What do you do?**

A) Ask the organizer if everyone is OK
B) Find a moment to check in directly with the quiet person, one-on-one, and ask how they're feeling
C) Assume they're fine since they haven't complained
D) Give a general announcement that anyone with concerns should speak up

**Best answer: B.** Group dynamics suppress individual concerns. The quiet person in a friend group isn't going to raise their hand in front of everyone. A quick, private "Hey, how are you feeling about this? Anything I can help with?" takes 15 seconds and can prevent a bad experience or a safety issue.

---

**7. It's a foggy morning. Visibility is marginal but improving. You've held the launch for 30 minutes and guests are getting antsy. One asks "Can't we just go? It's not that bad." What do you say?**

A) "You're probably right, let's go"
B) "I hear you. The fog is clearing and I think we'll be good to go in another 15-20 minutes. On the water, visibility matters more than it does on land because of boat traffic and navigation."
C) "Company policy says no"
D) "If you want a refund, I can arrange that"

**Best answer: B.** Acknowledge their frustration, give them a realistic timeline, and explain the why. Most people are reasonable when they understand the reason. "Company policy" is a conversation-ender that makes you sound like you're hiding behind rules instead of making a professional judgment.

---

**8. A guest with limited English proficiency is on the tour. They seem to understand some of what you're saying but not all. How do you handle the safety briefing?**

A) Deliver it at normal speed and assume they got the key points
B) Slow down for critical safety points, use physical demonstrations, and check understanding by asking them to show you (not tell you) key actions like the distress signal
C) Skip the briefing and just keep a closer eye on them during the tour
D) Ask another guest to translate

**Best answer: B.** Physical demonstration bridges language gaps. Showing is more reliable than telling. Asking them to demonstrate the paddle signal (raise paddle overhead) confirms they understood the most critical piece. You can't skip the briefing, and you can't assume someone else speaks their language.

---

**9. A guest asks "What's the best restaurant in Door County?" What's the ideal response?**

A) "I'm not really sure, I just work here"
B) Give a genuine personal recommendation or two, maybe mention what type of food they serve
C) Say you can't recommend businesses
D) Pull up Yelp on your phone and read reviews

**Best answer: B.** You're a local guide, not just a kayak operator. Personal recommendations make the experience feel authentic and build the kind of connection that leads to five-star reviews. If you don't have strong opinions on restaurants, at least know two or three good spots to recommend.

---

**10. A guest takes a selfie and accidentally drops their phone in the water. They're panicking. The phone sinks. What do you do?**

A) Dive in after it
B) Acknowledge that it sucks, confirm you can't retrieve it safely, and keep the tour moving so the experience doesn't become entirely about the lost phone
C) Stop the entire tour while everyone looks for it
D) Tell them they should have brought a dry bag

**Best answer: B.** You're not diving into cold water for a phone. Empathy is appropriate. "That's rough, I'm sorry." But the tour continues. Stopping everything and making the whole group search accomplishes nothing except ruining everyone else's experience. After the tour, you can help them think through insurance options or next steps.

---

**11. A guest asks you to take a group photo. It's a reasonable spot but you're in a section with light current. What do you do?**

A) Refuse because you need to keep moving
B) Find a calm spot nearby, raft the kayaks together, take the photo, and then continue
C) Take the photo while everyone is spread out in the current
D) Tell them to take it themselves

**Best answer: B.** Guest photos are part of the experience, especially in a scenic place like Door County. But safety first. A light current with spread-out kayaks while you're distracted with a camera isn't great. Raft up in a calm spot, take a good photo, and you've given them a memory and a potential social media post that markets the business.

---

**12. At the end of a tour, a guest says "That was fine, I guess. Not really what I expected." How do you respond?**

A) Get defensive about the tour quality
B) Ask what they were expecting, listen to their answer, and take the feedback to management if it's useful
C) Apologize profusely
D) Offer a discount on their next tour

**Best answer: B.** "Fine, I guess" is mild negative feedback and it's actually valuable. Maybe their expectation was set wrong by the booking description. Maybe the route wasn't what they pictured. Ask a brief follow-up question, thank them for being honest, and pass useful feedback along. You don't need to fix it in the moment or apologize for something that wasn't broken.
