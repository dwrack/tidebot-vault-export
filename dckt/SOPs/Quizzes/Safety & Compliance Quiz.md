# Safety & Compliance Quiz — DCKT
**Score 80% or higher to pass. One best answer per question.**

---

**1. What type of PFD is required for all guests on DCKT tours?**

A) Type I (offshore)
B) Type II (near-shore)
C) Type III (flotation aid)
D) Type V (special use)

**Best answer: C.** Type III PFDs are the standard for paddle sports. They provide adequate buoyancy while allowing full arm mobility for paddling. Every guest wears one, buckled, for the entire tour.

---

**2. You're about to launch a morning tour and the wind is reading 14 mph sustained with gusts to 18 mph. The forecast says wind will increase through the afternoon. What do you do?**

A) Launch since you're technically under the 15 mph threshold
B) Hold the launch, reassess in 30 minutes, and factor in the increasing trend
C) Launch but shorten the route to stay close to shore
D) Launch and plan to come back in early if it gets worse

**Best answer: B.** 14 mph with gusts to 18 and an increasing forecast is a borderline situation trending the wrong way. The protocol says to consider the trend when wind is 12-15 mph. Launching when you know conditions are getting worse puts you on the water when it crosses the threshold.

---

**3. Lightning is spotted in the distance during a tour. You estimate it's about 8 miles away. What's the correct action?**

A) Keep paddling but monitor it
B) Head to the nearest shore immediately
C) Finish the tour quickly before the storm arrives
D) Call management and wait for their decision

**Best answer: B.** Lightning within 10 miles triggers an immediate cancel. Eight miles is within that range. Get everyone off the water and to the nearest shore, then seek shelter in a vehicle or solid structure. Don't try to make it to the planned takeout if the nearest shore is closer.

---

**4. A guest capsizes in late June. Water temp is about 58°F. After you help them back on their kayak, they say they're fine. What do you watch for?**

A) Nothing, they said they're fine
B) Shivering, confusion, slurred speech, and blue lips over the next several minutes
C) Just check if their PFD is still buckled and continue
D) Only worry if they went fully underwater

**Best answer: B.** 58°F water triggers cold shock response. Even brief immersion can lead to hypothermia symptoms. "I'm fine" is what everyone says right after a capsize. Monitor them for the next 10-15 minutes for signs that say otherwise. Shivering, confusion, slurred speech, and blue lips are the indicators.

---

**5. It's early May and the water temperature is 48°F. A tour is booked for this morning. What's the protocol?**

A) Cancel the tour, full stop
B) Run the tour with extra caution
C) Require wetsuits for all participants. If wetsuits aren't available, cancel.
D) Run the tour but stay close to shore

**Best answer: C.** Below 50°F, wetsuits are required. Not recommended, required. If you don't have enough wetsuits for the group, the tour doesn't go. This is a hard policy line, not a judgment call.

---

**6. Which of the following is NOT standard equipment a guide carries on every DCKT tour?**

A) Throw bag
B) Whistle
C) Flare gun
D) First aid kit

**Best answer: C.** Throw bag, whistle (attached to PFD), first aid kit, phone in dry bag, and emergency blanket (shoulder season) are standard. Flare guns are not part of the kayak guide kit.

---

**7. A guest arrives for a tour and says they signed a waiver "last time they came." Their current booking shows no signed waiver. What do you do?**

A) Accept the old waiver since they've signed one before
B) Have them sign a new waiver for this specific booking before they get on the water
C) Let them paddle and have them sign after the tour
D) Call management to look up the old waiver

**Best answer: B.** Waivers are booking-specific. A waiver from a previous visit doesn't cover today's tour. Every person on every booking signs before they paddle. No exceptions, no "we'll do it after."

---

**8. You're guiding on the Lake Michigan side and notice the wind has shifted to come directly onshore, building waves at the launch site. Current readings show 13 mph. What's your primary concern?**

A) The waves will make launching difficult but paddling will be fine
B) Lake Michigan's fetch means 13 mph onshore wind produces larger waves than the same wind on Green Bay, and conditions can build quickly
C) 13 mph is well under threshold so there's no concern
D) Switch to Green Bay since it's always calmer

**Best answer: B.** Lake Michigan has more fetch than Green Bay, so the same wind speed produces bigger waves. 13 mph onshore and building is a situation that needs monitoring. The conditions at launch could be significantly worse by mid-tour. Also, Green Bay isn't always calmer. It depends on wind direction.

---

**9. During the safety briefing, a guest says "I've kayaked a hundred times, I don't need to hear this." What do you do?**

A) Skip the briefing for them and let them launch early
B) Deliver the full briefing to the group, including them, while acknowledging their experience
C) Quiz them on cold water safety to prove they need it
D) Ask them to step aside while you brief the others

**Best answer: B.** The briefing is mandatory for every guest on every tour. Acknowledging their experience is polite ("Great, you'll probably know most of this"), but the cold water information and Door County-specific details are almost certainly new to them. Don't skip it, don't single them out, don't argue.

**10. After a capsizing incident where the guest was fine and uninjured, what's the documentation requirement?**

A) No documentation needed since no one was hurt
B) Log it within 2 hours with time, location, and what happened
C) Only report it if the guest complains
D) Mention it to management verbally next time you see them

**Best answer: B.** All capsizes get logged, even if no one was hurt. Written report to management within 2 hours. These reports help spot patterns (same location, same conditions, same kayak) that prevent future incidents. A capsize today with no injury could be a capsize next week with hypothermia if the conditions repeat and nobody noticed.

---

**11. What's the wait time after the last lightning strike or thunder before returning to the water?**

A) 10 minutes
B) 20 minutes
C) 30 minutes
D) Once the rain stops

**Best answer: C.** 30 minutes after the last observed lightning or thunder. Rain stopping is not the standard. Lightning can strike from clouds well after rain passes. This is the industry standard for outdoor recreation.

---

**12. A DNR inspector arrives at your launch site while you're about to run a tour. What do you do?**

A) Ask them to come back later so you don't delay guests
B) Cooperate fully, be polite, and call management immediately
C) Tell them they need to talk to the business owner, not you
D) Show them everything and handle it yourself without involving management

**Best answer: B.** Full cooperation, polite demeanor, and an immediate call to management. You don't have to handle inspections solo, and management needs to know about it right away. Don't obstruct, don't dismiss, don't try to manage it entirely on your own.
