# Culture & Values Quiz — DCKT
**Score 80% or higher to pass. One best answer per question.**

---

**1. A guest asks why they have to wear a PFD when "it's 85 degrees out and I'm a strong swimmer." What do you do?**

A) Let them take it off since they seem experienced
B) Explain the water is cold regardless of air temp, PFDs are required the entire tour, no exceptions
C) Tell them it's a Wisconsin state law and leave it at that
D) Suggest they loosen the straps so it's more comfortable

**Best answer: B.** The air temp is irrelevant. Lake Michigan can be in the upper 50s in July. Cold shock from an unexpected immersion is the risk, and a strong swimmer loses motor control fast in cold water. Citing the law alone (C) isn't wrong, but it doesn't help the guest understand why. You want compliance and understanding.

---

**2. You're a returning seasonal guide. A brand-new guide asks you how they should handle a guest who's clearly nervous about kayaking. What's your best advice?**

A) Tell the guest they'll be fine, it's totally safe
B) Acknowledge their nerves, ask what specifically worries them, and walk them through how you'll address it
C) Suggest the guest skip the tour if they're that worried
D) Pair them with a confident paddler and hope it works out

**Best answer: B.** Dismissing someone's fear (A) doesn't build trust. Acknowledging it and getting specific lets you actually address what's going on. Maybe they're worried about tipping, maybe about deep water, maybe about cold. Each one has a real answer you can give them.

---

**3. It's a slow Tuesday in September with only two guests booked. How do you approach this tour?**

A) Run it faster since it's a small group and you can cut some corners
B) Skip the safety briefing since it's just two people
C) Run it at the same standard as a fully booked summer tour
D) Use the time to be on your phone since it's low stress

**Best answer: C.** Every tour gets the full treatment. Small groups in shoulder season actually deserve more attention, not less. September water is getting cold, conditions are more variable, and these two guests are just as likely to leave a review as anyone in July.

---

**4. You overhear another local tour company badmouthing your operation to a group of tourists at a restaurant. What do you do?**

A) Confront them publicly
B) Badmouth them right back to your next group of guests
C) Ignore it and focus on running your tours well
D) Post about it on social media

**Best answer: C.** Door County is a small community. Getting into a public spat with competitors makes everyone look bad. Your reviews and reputation speak for themselves. If it's serious enough to matter, mention it to management privately.

---

**5. A guest is struggling to keep up with the group and seems embarrassed about it. What's the right move?**

A) Tell them to paddle harder
B) Slow the entire group's pace without making a big deal of it
C) Leave them behind and come back to check on them later
D) Suggest they turn back early

**Best answer: B.** The group moves at the speed of the slowest paddler. That's stated in the briefing for exactly this reason. Slowing down casually, maybe pointing out some wildlife or a shoreline feature while the slower paddler catches up, keeps the experience positive for everyone.

**6. What documents do you fill out as a W-2 seasonal employee of DCKT?**

A) W-9 and a non-compete agreement
B) W-4 and I-9
C) 1099 form and a contractor agreement
D) Just a direct deposit form

**Best answer: B.** DCKT guides are W-2 employees through Homebase. W-4 is for tax withholding, I-9 verifies work eligibility. W-9 is for independent contractors, which you are not.

**7. You finished the season last year and are coming back this year. When you arrive, the Night-Before Checklist has been updated with a new step. What's the right attitude?**

A) Ignore it because you know how things work from last year
B) Read the updated checklist and follow it as written
C) Do it the old way but tell management you saw the update
D) Ask other returning guides what they think before deciding

**Best answer: B.** Processes get updated between seasons for good reasons, sometimes regulatory, sometimes based on incidents or feedback. Returning guides who assume nothing has changed are a liability. Read the updates, follow them.

---

**8. A guest asks if you personally enjoy kayaking in Door County. What kind of response fits the culture?**

A) A generic "yes it's great" and change the subject
B) A genuine, specific answer about what you like about paddling here
C) Redirect to the company's marketing talking points
D) Complain about the cold water

**Best answer: B.** Guests connect with authenticity. If you genuinely like watching the eagles along the bluffs or the way the light hits the water in the morning, say that. Canned responses and marketing-speak kill the vibe.

---

**9. A guest tips you $50 after the tour. Another guest in the group didn't tip. How do you handle it?**

A) Thank the tipper and make a comment about the non-tipper
B) Thank the tipper genuinely and treat the non-tipper exactly the same
C) Refuse the tip because it might seem unfair
D) Mention tipping to the whole group before they leave

**Best answer: B.** Tips are appreciated but never expected, and you never guilt or hint. Your service quality is the same regardless of tips. Gratitude for the one who tipped, zero change in attitude toward the one who didn't.

---

**10. It's your day off and you run into a group of tourists at a Door County shop who ask about kayak tours. What do you do?**

A) Ignore them, you're off the clock
B) Give them a friendly recommendation to check out DCKT and maybe mention what makes it a good experience
C) Complain about your job to show you're "keeping it real"
D) Promote a competitor because you're not working today

**Best answer: B.** You represent the business whether you're clocked in or not, especially in a small tourist community. You don't have to do a full sales pitch, but a genuine, friendly mention goes a long way. Small-town word of mouth is real.

---

**11. A guest with a visible physical disability arrives for a tour. They didn't mention anything when booking. How do you handle it?**

A) Ask them directly whether they can handle the tour
B) Quietly assess if any accommodations are needed, ask them privately if there's anything you can do to make the experience comfortable, and proceed with the tour
C) Suggest they might want to do something else instead
D) Call management and ask if they should be allowed on

**Best answer: B.** Don't make assumptions about what people can or can't do. A private, respectful conversation lets them tell you what they need, if anything. Many people with disabilities are experienced outdoor recreators. Your job is to make the tour accessible, not to gatekeep it.
