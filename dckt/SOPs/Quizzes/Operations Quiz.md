# Operations Quiz — DCKT
**Score 80% or higher to pass. One best answer per question.**

---

**1. It's the night before a busy Saturday with three tours booked. You check FareHarbor and see 28 guests total across the three tours, but only two guides are confirmed. What's your first action?**

A) Run all three tours with two guides since that should work
B) Immediately contact backup guides and notify management that you're short-staffed
C) Cancel the smallest tour to make the numbers work
D) Wait until morning and see if someone else shows up

**Best answer: B.** Night-before is when staffing problems get solved, not morning-of. Contact backup guides immediately and loop in management. Canceling tours or running understaffed are both bad outcomes that a few phone calls tonight might prevent.

---

**2. During Morning Inspection, you find a PFD with a buckle that won't click. What do you do?**

A) Use it anyway and tell the guest to hold the strap
B) Pull it from rotation immediately, mark it for repair, and grab a replacement
C) Set it aside and fix it after today's tours
D) Only use it if you run out of other PFDs

**Best answer: B.** A PFD with a broken buckle is not a functional PFD. It comes out of rotation right now. Mark it clearly so nobody accidentally grabs it. If pulling it drops you below the count you need for today's tours, that's a problem to solve before launch, not a reason to use broken gear.

**3. It's early October. You're checking water temp before a late-season tour. The water is reading 51°F. What do you need to verify?**

A) Nothing, 51°F is above the 50°F wetsuit threshold
B) Confirm wetsuit availability just in case, since you're only 1 degree above the threshold
C) Cancel the tour since it's close enough to the threshold
D) Check the forecast to see if it'll warm up by afternoon

**Best answer: B.** 51°F is technically above the 50°F hard line, but one degree of margin in late season means conditions could dip below threshold during the tour. Knowing that wetsuits are available and ready to go is responsible preparation. You don't have to require them at 51°F, but having them on hand is smart.

**4. A guest calls the night before asking to add two more people to tomorrow's morning tour. FareHarbor shows the tour at capacity. What do you do?**

A) Add them anyway and figure out equipment later
B) Check if another time slot has availability and offer that instead, or add them to a waitlist
C) Tell them no and hang up
D) Overbook by two since someone usually cancels

**Best answer: B.** Capacity limits exist because of equipment counts and guide ratios. Overbooking creates safety and logistics problems. But you can still help them. Another time slot might work, or you can waitlist them in case of cancellations. Turning them away without offering alternatives loses revenue and leaves a bad impression.

**5. You're mid-season and notice that three kayaks have developed small cracks in the hull. They're still functional but the cracks are visible. What's the right approach?**

A) Keep using them until the cracks affect performance
B) Flag them for repair, assess whether they're safe for continued use, and report to management with a replacement timeline
C) Retire them immediately and run with fewer kayaks
D) Cover the cracks with tape so guests don't notice

**Best answer: B.** Small cracks need assessment, not panic or cover-ups. Some cracks are cosmetic and can be repaired in rotation. Others are structural and need immediate retirement. Flag them, get eyes on them, and give management the information they need to decide on repairs or replacements. Tape over cracks is not a repair and looks unprofessional.

**6. It's peak July weekend. You have back-to-back tours with 30 minutes between them. What's the most critical thing to do in that 30-minute window?**

A) Take a break and eat lunch
B) Reset equipment (kayaks back in position, PFDs re-counted), quick-check weather, and verify the next group's details in FareHarbor
C) Just wait for the next group to show up
D) Deep-clean all equipment

**Best answer: B.** Thirty minutes between tours is tight. You need to get equipment back in launch position, verify PFD count and condition (a guest might have left one on the beach), check weather for any changes, and pull up the next booking in FareHarbor so you know headcount and any notes. Eating matters too, but the operational reset is the priority in a short window.

**7. A guide calls in sick at 7 AM for a 9 AM tour. What's the protocol?**

A) Cancel the 9 AM tour immediately
B) Call backup guides, notify management, and assess whether you can cover with available staff before deciding to cancel
C) Run the tour yourself even though you're already scheduled for a different tour at 9 AM
D) Ask a guest to help co-lead

**Best answer: B.** Canceling is the last resort, not the first. Work the backup list, coordinate with management, and see if coverage is possible. There might be a guide available who wasn't scheduled today. If nobody can cover and the ratio won't work solo, then you cancel and rebook, but you exhaust options first.

**8. End of season. You're helping with equipment storage. Which of the following is most important for kayak winterization?**

A) Stack them outside under a tarp
B) Clean, dry, inspect for damage, and store in a covered space away from UV and extreme temps
C) Just put them wherever they fit
D) Leave them at the launch site until spring

**Best answer: B.** Door County winters are harsh. UV, freeze-thaw cycles, and moisture will destroy equipment left exposed. Clean the kayaks to prevent mold, dry them completely, inspect for damage that needs repair over the winter, and store them properly. This is the difference between starting next season with a fleet that's ready versus one that needs expensive replacements.

**9. You're running Morning Inspection and the first aid kit is missing two items: the SAM splint and the burn gel. Today's first tour is in 45 minutes. What do you do?**

A) Go without those two items since you probably won't need them
B) Restock from backup supplies or make a quick run to replace them before the tour launches
C) Cancel the tour until the kit is fully stocked
D) Borrow from another guide's kit

**Best answer: B.** The first aid kit needs to be complete. "Probably won't need it" isn't a safety strategy. If backup supplies are on hand, restock immediately. If not and there's time, get replacements. Borrowing from another guide's kit just moves the problem. You don't need to cancel a tour over two items if you can solve it in 45 minutes.

**10. FareHarbor is down and you can't access bookings or waivers electronically. Guests are arriving for a tour. What do you do?**

A) Turn everyone away until FareHarbor comes back online
B) Verify bookings from any confirmation emails guests have, collect waivers on paper, and run the tour while coordinating with management to reconcile records later
C) Run the tour without checking anything and sort it out later
D) Wait indefinitely for FareHarbor to come back

**Best answer: B.** Tech goes down sometimes. You still have a tour to run. Guests usually have booking confirmation emails. Paper waivers are a backup, not ideal, but they work. The key is: nobody goes on the water without a waiver, and you need to know who's on the tour. Communicate with management so records get reconciled once the system is back.

**11. You're reviewing the Night-Before Checklist and notice tomorrow has a tour on the Lake Michigan side and a tour on the Green Bay side scheduled 30 minutes apart. Same guide. What's the problem?**

A) No problem, Door County is small
B) Transit time between launch sites plus equipment setup makes this scheduling conflict a potential problem that needs to be flagged to management tonight
C) Just run both from the same launch site
D) Cancel the later tour

**Best answer: B.** Door County feels small until you're driving between launch sites with a trailer, setting up equipment, and trying to be ready for guests. This is a scheduling conflict that needs resolution tonight, not at 8 AM when you're supposed to be at two places. Management can reassign a guide, adjust timing, or find another solution.

**12. A guest left their car keys in their kayak. You discover them during Post-Tour Closeout after the guest has already left. What do you do?**

A) Put them in the lost and found and hope the guest calls
B) Check FareHarbor for the guest's contact info and call or text them immediately, then secure the keys until they pick them up
C) Drive to their hotel and drop them off
D) Post about it on social media hoping they see it

**Best answer: B.** FareHarbor has guest contact info. A quick call or text right now saves someone from a very bad afternoon. Secure the keys, reach out directly, and arrange pickup. This is the kind of post-tour service that turns a "good experience" into a "this company is amazing" review.
