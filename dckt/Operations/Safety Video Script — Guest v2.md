# Safety Video Script — Guest v2
*~6 minute video. Pre-arrival watch via FareHarbor email + in-shop replay. Scout voice. Replaces existing video.*

---

## Production notes

- **Length:** ~6 minutes (5:30-6:15 target)
- **Format:** 16:9, 1080p minimum, captions burned in (most viewers watch with sound off)
- **Voiceover:** Female or male, Wisconsin or Midwest accent, warm without being folksy. NOT corporate. If David or a senior guide can voice it, even better — authenticity beats production polish.
- **Visuals:** DCKT B-roll from the YouTube pipeline (`~/Projects/dckt-youtube/pipeline/`). Real guides, real water, real caves. No stock footage.
- **Music:** Sparse. One acoustic track that comes in around minute 2 and stays low. No swelling cinematic score — that's the wrong energy for a safety video.
- **Hosting:** Vimeo Pro private (so it embeds in FareHarbor confirmation email + plays on shop screen without ads).
- **Where it appears:**
  - FareHarbor booking confirmation email (pre-arrival, T-minus 3 days and re-sent T-minus 1)
  - In-shop screen during Beat 4 (loop or per-group replay)
  - Optional: embedded on website on the booking page

## Script

### COLD OPEN — 0:00 to 0:25

**Visual:** Slow drone shot pulling back from a kayak entering a sea cave. Just the kayak, the water, the rock.

**VO:**
> *"You're about to do something most people who come to Door County never do."*

*(beat)*

> *"You're going to paddle into a 10,000-year-old sea cave on Lake Michigan, and you're going to do it with us."*

*(text on screen: "Door County Kayak Tours — Cave Point Sea Caves")*

> *"Before you get on the water, we want to walk you through a few things. This isn't a legal disclaimer. It's how we run a good tour."*

---

### THE BOAT — 0:25 to 1:10

**Visual:** Close-up of a sit-on-top kayak at the dock, guide demonstrating.

**VO:**
> *"We use sit-on-top kayaks. They're stable, they're easy to get in and out of, and they don't trap you in the boat. That's the good news."*

*(beat)*

> *"The other news: they can flip. If a wave catches you wrong, if you lean too far, if another paddler bumps you — you might end up in the water. It's not common, but it happens."*

*(visual cuts to: paddler in the water next to their kayak, calm, holding paddle)*

> *"If it does happen — and we'll cover this again at the dock with your guide — the rule is simple. Stay with the kayak. Hold your paddle. Don't swim for shore. Your guide will come to you."*

---

### THE WATER — 1:10 to 2:15

**Visual:** Wide drone shot of Lake Michigan / Green Bay. Beautiful, calm.

**VO:**
> *"This water is cold."*

*(beat — let the line land)*

> *"It looks like every other lake. It's not. Lake Michigan stays in the 40s and 50s well into June. Even in August it can drop fast with a wind shift."*

*(visual cuts to: shore-side shot, paddler putting on a layer)*

> *"If you go in cold water, your body does something called cold shock. You gasp, your heart races, your breathing goes ragged. It's involuntary, and it happens in seconds — even to strong swimmers, even with a PFD on."*

> *"That's why we say: dress for the water, not the air. If it's a 75-degree day and the water is 55, you still want layers. Your guide will tell you what to wear when you book."*

> *"And if it's a day where conditions don't work for us, we won't take you out. We'd rather reschedule than push it."*

---

### THE LAUNCH — 2:15 to 3:00

**Visual:** Dock, kayaks lined up, guide demonstrating dock launch.

**VO:**
> *"Getting in and out of the kayak at the dock is the most likely place to get hurt on this tour."*

*(visual: close-up of hands on paddle shaft, hands inside kayak — clearly NOT on the rail near the dock)*

> *"Your hands stay on the paddle or inside the kayak. Never on the edge near the dock. If a wave moves the kayak, a finger between the dock and the kayak loses every time."*

*(beat)*

> *"Your guide will assist every launch. Let them. This isn't the part of the tour to be heroic."*

---

### THE CAVES — 3:00 to 4:00

**Visual:** Slow pan inside a sea cave. Low ceiling, water against rock.

**VO:**
> *"The caves are what you came for. They are stunning. They are also unforgiving rock."*

*(beat)*

> *"Inside, the ceiling can drop. Walls can scrape kayaks. Waves push you against rock harder than you'd think. We enter one kayak at a time, only when the guide signals, and you do what your guide says when they say it."*

*(visual: kayaker ducking under low rock at guide's hand signal)*

> *"Duck when they say duck. Stop when they say stop. The caves have been there for ten thousand years. We can wait twenty seconds for the right entry."*

> *"One more thing on the rocks: we do not cliff jump. The water depth and the rocks underneath are not what they look like from above. People have died. We're not doing it. Don't ask, don't joke."*

---

### THE WEATHER — 4:00 to 4:30

**Visual:** Stormy water, then quick cut back to calm.

**VO:**
> *"Lake Michigan weather changes fast. It can be a clear morning and a wall of weather by 1pm."*

> *"If your guide calls 'return to shore' — every paddler turns and follows. No questions on the water. We tell you that calmly because we've gotten good at watching for it, and the decision is the right one every time we make it."*

---

### THE SIGNALS — 4:30 to 4:55

**Visual:** Guide demonstrating: whistle, paddle raise, hand signals.

**VO:**
> *"Three whistle blasts is an emergency. Paddle raised above your head means you need your guide. Your guide will use hand signals for stop, follow, return, and gather up. You'll catch on fast."*

---

### THE SOBER PART — 4:55 to 5:15

**Visual:** Two kayakers, smiling, paddling.

**VO:**
> *"No alcohol or anything else that impairs your judgment before or during the tour. If you show up impaired, we won't take you out, and we won't refund. That's not negotiable. We've seen it go bad."*

> *"After the tour? Different conversation."*

*(small smile in VO — this is the only beat of levity in the video)*

---

### THE INVITATION — 5:15 to 5:50

**Visual:** Guide and group at the dock, group photo moment.

**VO:**
> *"That's the safety part. Everything else is what makes this fun."*

*(beat)*

> *"Your guide is going to walk you through all of this again at the dock — with today's actual conditions, today's wind, today's water temp. They'll record that briefing. That's standard for how we run tours."*

> *"Bring water. Wear something you can move in. Sunscreen and a hat are smart. Sunglasses on a strap if you've got them."*

> *"We'll see you at the shop."*

---

### TAG — 5:50 to 6:05

**Visual:** DCKT logo, simple. Calm water in background.

**VO:**
> *"Door County Kayak Tours. We don't treat returning guests like strangers. That starts the first time you paddle with us."*

*(end card)*

---

## What this video does NOT do

- It does not read the waiver. Guests already read the waiver.
- It does not list every risk. The waiver lists every risk. The video covers what guests need to *internalize*.
- It does not make safety look fun, gritty, or epic. Safety is just true. The fun is on the water with the guide.
- It does not end with "Have fun!" or "See you out there!" — both are off-brand and weak.

---

## Captioning

Burn in clean captions. Most pre-arrival viewers will watch on phone with sound off. The captions need to land the key safety beats even silent.
