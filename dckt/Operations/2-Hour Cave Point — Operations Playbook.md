# 2-Hour Cave Point — Operations Playbook
*The 11-beat staff playbook for our flagship tour. Safety + experience + record in one flow.*

---

## How This Doc Fits

This is the **tour-day operations playbook** for the 2-hour Cave Point Sea Caves tour. It does three jobs at once:

- **Layer A — Staff knowledge:** What every guide must internalize from the waiver so they can speak to it on the dock without reading legalese.
- **Layer B — Guest interaction:** The 11-beat pathway in Scout voice. Three of those beats are designed peak moments; the rest stay clean and warm.
- **Layer C — Operational record:** What gets posted to `#dckt-tour-logs` in Slack at each beat, so every tour is documented and coachable.

For the broader marketing/CRM/post-tour arc (discovery → booking → 7-day re-engagement → seasonal return), see `DCKT — Guide Training & Customer Journey Playbook.md`. This doc picks up at arrival and ends at the Paddler's Circle closing ritual. The two docs share DNA — same Scout voice, same Paddler's Circle, same "make them feel like they mattered" north star — but this one is tour-day execution, not the full customer journey.

For the legal source-of-truth on risks, see `Waiver — Kayaking (Wisconsin, Draft for Attorney Review).md`. This playbook maps each waiver section to what the guide says and checks.

---

## The Standard

A guest finishes the 2-hour tour and says one of three things to whoever they go home with:

1. **"That was the highlight of the trip."**
2. **"You have to do this."**
3. **"We're going back."**

If those three sentences aren't naturally falling out of guests' mouths at the end of the tour, the playbook isn't working. Adjust accordingly.

## Layer A — What Every Guide Knows From the Waiver

The waiver has 10 sections. Guides don't recite them. Guides know what's in each section well enough to speak to it in plain Scout voice during the dock briefing and on the water when something comes up. The table below is the cheat sheet.

| Waiver Section | What it's about | What the guide says (anchor phrase) | When it gets reinforced |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Scope | What this tour includes | "Today covers the dock launch, the paddle, the caves, and the return." | Shop pre-brief |
| 2. Inherent risks | Water, weather, sit-on-tops, sea caves, pinch points, paddle strikes, wildlife | "These are sit-on-top kayaks. They can flip. There's no spray skirt. Stay with your paddle if you go in." | Dock briefing |
| 2. Cliff jumping NOT authorized | No jumping or diving from cliffs/rocks | "We don't do cliff jumping. The rocks underwater are not what you think they are. Don't even joke about it." | Dock briefing + on water if anyone hints |
| 3. Cold water and cold shock | Lake Michigan/Green Bay are dangerously cold; PFD doesn't solve cold shock | "Even when it's warm out, that water is colder than it looks. If you flip, your body's first reaction is going to be a giant gasp — that's normal, that's your body, and a PFD doesn't stop it. Stay with your kayak, hold your paddle, I'll come to you." | Dock briefing (always); SAFETY VIDEO does the heavy lifting |
| 4. Loading/unloading | Lifting, carrying, pinching, slipping with gear | "When we're moving kayaks, hands stay on the handles, not the edges. I'll show you the lift." | Shop pre-brief + at the dock |
| 5. Health and fitness | Adequate health, sober, swim ability, disclosed conditions | "If anything's going on today — heart, back, medication, pregnancy — tell me before we launch. I'm not going to judge it, I just need to know." | Shop pre-brief (private check) |
| 6. Pregnancy | Additional risks; physician decision; harm to mother and unborn child | (Private 1-on-1 if disclosed) "We've had pregnant paddlers do this tour. The decision is yours and your doctor's. Here's what to know..." | Shop pre-brief, separately |
| 7. Express assumption of risk | Includes DCKT's own ordinary negligence | (Don't speak to this directly to guests; the waiver speaks for itself.) | Already signed at check-in |
| 8. Release of liability | Including DCKT ordinary negligence | (Same — already signed.) | Already signed |
| 9. Indemnification | Defense costs if they bring barred claim | (Don't bring this up unless asked.) | N/A at briefing |
| 10. Wisconsin venue + final ack | Wisconsin law, Door County venue | (Don't bring up unless asked.) | N/A at briefing |

**Two rules for talking about the waiver:**

1. Never say "the waiver." Say "what you signed" or "what you read." It feels less defensive.
2. Never read it aloud. Talk to the substance. Reading sounds like a CYA performance, which is exactly the energy you don't want.

## Layer B — The 11-Beat Pathway

Three beats are designed peak moments. The rest stay clean, warm, and unscripted.

**Peak moments:** Arrival (Beat 1), Dock briefing (Beat 7), Paddler's Circle closing (Beat 11).
**Power moment on the water:** Cave entry (Beat 9) gets Disney treatment too, but happens organically each tour.

---

### Beat 1 — [PEAK] Arrival & greet

**Owner:** Guide on tour if available. Lea if guide is unloading kayaks.
**Tone:** Scout-warm. Name use. Eye contact within 30 seconds of arrival.
**6 Human Needs:** Certainty (they know they're in the right place) + Connection (someone knows their name).

**What happens:**

- Greeter spots the arriving group, walks toward them within 30 seconds — does not let them stand in the parking lot wondering where to go.
- Uses their name from the manifest: *"You must be the Tompkins party. Welcome."*
- For returning guests (flagged in FareHarbor manifest): *"[Name]! Good to have you back. This your fourth?"* Let them answer.
- Offers something physical immediately: water bottle, sunscreen, hat from the rack. **A hand-off, not a directive.** Robbins note: small physical gesture in the first 60 seconds anchors certainty.

**Anti-script (never say):**

- "Welcome aboard!" (corporate)
- "Are you here for the kayak tour?" (you should already know)
- "Did you find us okay?" (sets a low bar; sounds like a strip mall)

**Slack action:** None yet. Pre-tour shop check post should already exist in the day's thread.

---

### Beat 2 — Check-in & waiver

**Owner:** Front desk / Lea / whoever is at the counter.
**Tone:** Warm-efficient. Not friction.

**What happens:**

- Confirm names match the manifest in FareHarbor.
- Confirm digital waiver is signed for every guest. If anyone hasn't signed, they sign now on a tablet — not paper.
- **Private check for the health/pregnancy disclosures (Sec 5/6 of waiver).** This is one-on-one, away from the group, conversational: *"Anything we should know health-wise before we head out?"* Allergies, heart issues, recent surgery, pregnancy. If yes, flag in the day's Slack thread and to the guide before launch.
- Capture safety video acknowledgment — confirm whether they pre-watched via FareHarbor email. If not, they watch in-shop now (Beat 4).

**Anti-script:**

- "Just sign here real quick" (dismissive of what they're signing — and bad legally too)
- "It's just a formality" (it's not)

**Slack action:** Front desk updates the day's pre-tour check post with each completed check-in (names, waivers signed, special notes).

---

### Beat 3 — Comfort prep

**Owner:** Guest self-directed. Lea or staff available.
**Tone:** Hospitality. Don't hover.

**What happens:**

- Bathroom, water, change room, hat/sunglasses/sunscreen, gear shop browse.
- Dry bag sale moment: if they brought a phone or keys, offer a dry bag *as a question*, not a pitch. *"Want a dry bag for the phone? They're at the counter."*
- Aim for 10-15 minutes here. Not rushed.

**Anti-script:**

- "Would you like to add..." (sales-y; just point and ask)

**Slack action:** None.

---

### Beat 4 — Safety video

**Owner:** Plays on shop screen. Guide or Lea ensures it's playing and visible.
**Tone:** Set by the video itself (warm-directive-warm, Scout voice).

**What happens:**

- ~6-minute video. Covers PFD, dock pinch, sit-on-top behavior, cave hazards, cold water, what to do if you flip, weather change, sober rule.
- Guests who pre-watched via FareHarbor email get the option to skip; in practice, leave it playing and let them re-watch if they want.
- After the video, guide is at the front for questions. **Guests almost always have one. Be there.**

**Anti-script:**

- "You don't have to watch the whole thing." (Then why are we showing it?)

**Slack action:** None (the video acknowledgment was captured at booking via FareHarbor).

---

### Beat 5 — Shop pre-brief by guide

**Owner:** Guide.
**Tone:** Scout intro. Group bond.

**What happens:**

- Guide introduces themselves *as a person*, not a job title: *"I'm Bella. I've been paddling Cave Point for [X] seasons. The thing I love about today is..."*
- Names every guest in the group out loud once. Eye contact each time.
- Reads the group (per the existing customer journey playbook): who's nervous, who's been before, who's celebrating something, who's got the camera.
- Covers: what to expect on the drive, what to leave in the car, restroom one more time before we go.
- Confirms anyone with disclosed health/pregnancy is comfortable with the day's conditions (private aside if needed).

**Anti-script:**

- "Has everyone been kayaking before?" (Generic. Use FareHarbor data and the personal questions instead.)

**Slack action:** None.

---

### Beat 6 — Drive/shuttle to launch

**Owner:** Guide leads caravan.
**Tone:** Building anticipation.

**What happens:**

- Caravan to the dock. Guide vehicle leads.
- Optional: short story on the way (Death's Door history, Cave Point geology, why we run sit-on-tops). The point isn't education — it's signaling that there's more to this place than they think.
- Confirm headcount at the lot before walking to the dock.

**Anti-script:**

- "Just follow me!" (Confidence-undermining. They know how to follow a car.)

**Slack action:** None.

---

### Beat 7 — [PEAK] Dock briefing

**Owner:** Guide. **This is the moment guests will remember most for the next decade.**
**Tone:** Warm welcome → directive safety → warm send-off.
**6 Human Needs:** Certainty + Significance.

**What happens:**

Guide opens the day's Slack thread by starting a video recording on their phone. Briefing is roughly **4-5 minutes total**:

**Warm open (30 sec):**

> *"Alright. We're at the launch. Before we get on the water, I want to walk you through a couple things so this is fun and not scary. I'm recording this — that's standard for us, just part of how we do tours."*

(Wisconsin one-party consent — recording disclosure isn't legally required but is best practice and audibly takes 4 seconds.)

**Conditions check (15 sec):**

> *"Today: air is [X], water is [X], wind is [X] from the [direction], next two hours we're expecting [forecast]. We are in [GREEN / YELLOW] conditions."*

If YELLOW: spend an extra 30 seconds on the cold-water section. If RED: you wouldn't be here.

**Directive safety segment (3 min):**

Cover each of the 10 numbered items below. Pace is brisk. **Show, don't only tell.** Demonstrate the dock-launch hand position with an actual kayak. Hold up the PFD when you talk about it. Point at the cave entrance when you describe cave hazards.

1. **PFD.** "Yours is fitted. It stays on the entire time."
2. **Dock pinch.** "Hands on the paddle shaft or inside the kayak — never on the edge near the dock. I've seen fingers crushed. We are not doing that today."
3. **Sit-on-top behavior.** "These can flip. There's no spray skirt holding you in. If you come off, stay with your paddle, signal me, do not swim for shore."
4. **Cave hazards.** "Low ceilings, walls that can scrape, waves that push you into rock. Enter only when I signal. Duck when I say duck."
5. **Cold water.** "Even today, that water is cold enough to take your breath. If you go in, your body will gasp. That's normal. Stay calm, hold your paddle, I will come to you."
6. **Cliff jumping.** "We don't do that. Don't ask, don't joke. The rocks underneath are not what you think."
7. **Weather change.** "If I call us back to shore, every paddler turns and follows. No questions on the water."
8. **Signals.** "Three whistle blasts = emergency. Paddle up = you need me. I'll use hand signals for the rest."
9. **Spacing.** "Stay one kayak length back from the paddler in front. Don't pass me. Don't fall behind."
10. **Sober.** "Anyone under the influence, you can't paddle today. No exceptions."

**Verbal acknowledgments (60 sec):**

> *"Last thing — I'm going to ask each of you to say your name and 'I understand' for the recording. Helps us run a clean operation."*

Go around the group. Each guest, name + "I understand."

**Warm close (15 sec):**

> *"Photo at the dock — get close. [snap]. Alright. We're launching. Hands inside, stay with me, this is going to be a great two hours."*

**Slack action:** Guide posts the video to `#dckt-tour-logs` immediately after recording stops, with caption:

```
2hr Cave Point | [date] [launch time] | guide [name]
Group: [size] | Conditions: [GREEN/YELLOW] | Air [X]°F / Water [X]°F / Wind [X] mph
[video attached] [group photo attached]
```

This post is the **thread starter** for the tour. Everything else for this tour goes in this thread.

### Beat 8 — Guide-assisted launch

**Owner:** Guide. Sweep guide assists if double-teamed.
**Tone:** Directive, hands-on, calm.

**What happens:**

- Guide stabilizes each kayak at the dock. Guest steps in with paddle in one hand, opposite hand on the dock-side of the kayak (not the edge).
- Confirm each guest is balanced and has paddle in hand before pushing off.
- Final headcount before the group paddles out of the launch area.

**Anti-script:**

- "Just hop in!" (Casual energy → broken ankle.)

**Slack action:** None.

---

### Beat 9 — [PEAK] On-water tour & cave entry

**Owner:** Guide.
**Tone:** Scout storytelling, then peak-state.
**6 Human Needs:** Variety + Growth + Significance.

**What happens (the long version is in the existing customer journey playbook, the short version here):**

- Visual headcount every 15 minutes.
- One designed peak moment: the cave entrance. Stop the group. Let them drift. Then name it:

> *"Look at where you are right now. You're floating inside a 10,000-year-old sea cave. This rock was being carved by water while humans were still using stone tools. Most people drive past this on Hwy 57 and never see it."*

10 seconds of silence. Let it land.

- Rotate one ecological reveal per group: geology, shipwrecks, invasive species, Indigenous history (Death's Door, Potawatomi + Ho-Chunk — see `reference_deaths_door_history.md` for sensitivity notes), wildlife. Returning guests should hear something different than their last visit.
- Mid-trip check-in to Slack thread ~15 min before tour end, while the group is gathering for return paddle (post from a sheltered cove or dry spot):

```
Mid-trip check | quality [1-5] | mechanical: NO | incident: NO | reviews asked: YES
```

If YES on mechanical or incident, `@manager @owner` in the same post.

**Anti-script:**

- "Isn't this amazing?" (Tells them what to feel. Let them feel it.)

**Slack action:** Mid-trip check-in post, threaded under the briefing video.

---

### Beat 10 — Return & take-out

**Owner:** Guide.
**Tone:** Calm, grounding. The peak just happened; this is the breath after.

**What happens:**

- Guide-assisted exit at the dock. Same rules as launch: hands inside, paddle in hand, guide stabilizes.
- Gear stow: PFDs, paddles, dry bags returned.
- Don't rush. The last 5 minutes set the post-tour anchor.

**Slack action:** None until Beat 11 closeout.

---

### Beat 11 — [PEAK] Paddler's Circle closing ritual

**Owner:** Guide.
**Tone:** Significance + Connection.
**6 Human Needs:** Significance + Connection + Contribution.

See `Operations/Paddler's Circle Closing Ritual Scripts.md` for first-timer / 3-visit / 5-visit / 10-visit scripts. Guide picks the right one based on the FareHarbor visit count (already checked at Beat 5).

After the ritual, guide collects favorite moments per the existing customer journey playbook ("What was your favorite moment?"), takes a final group photo if not already done, asks for the review.

**Slack action:** Closeout post in the tour's thread:

```
Closeout | return [time]
Incidents: NONE / [detail]
Gear: OK / [damage flagged]
Paddler's Circle: [first-timer induction / 3rd visit / 5th visit / 10th visit / none]
Reviews asked: YES / NO
Photo filed: YES / NO
Notable moment for the team: [optional one-line note]
```

## Layer C — The Operational Record

Per tour, the Slack thread in `#dckt-tour-logs` contains:

1. **Pre-tour shop check post** (Beat 2, by front desk) — manifest confirmed, waivers signed, video acknowledged, allergies/pregnancy flagged
2. **Dock briefing post** (Beat 7, by guide) — video + conditions + group photo (thread starter)
3. **Mid-trip check post** (Beat 9, by guide) — quality, mechanical, incident, reviews
4. **Closeout post** (Beat 11, by guide) — incidents, gear, Paddler's Circle, photo

Each thread is one tour, retrievable by date and launch time. David and Bella scan the channel daily for coachable moments and weekly for systemic patterns.

**Channel retention:** Slack alone is not safe long-term. Slack → Google Drive auto-backup to `DCKT > Guide Logs > YYYY > MM` runs weekly. 7-year retention.

## The Scout Voice — Anti-Script

From `Brand Story & Values.md`: *"If a guide is reciting the same monologue word-for-word every paddle, something has gone wrong. Know your material, then be a human being about it."*

**Never say:**

- "Welcome aboard!" — corporate
- "Safety is our number one priority" — meaningless, every operator says it
- "Hop on in!" — casual energy at the wrong moment
- "Just relax and enjoy" — sounds like a hostage script
- "Isn't this amazing?" — tells them what to feel
- "Did you have fun?" — invites a polite yes; ask for the favorite moment instead
- "Drive safe!" — generic farewell; use their name and the experience

**Use instead:**

- Their name
- A specific reference to something that happened on the water
- A peak moment named, then silence
- An offer of something physical (water, photo, postcard) over a verbal flourish

## Cold Water Special Handling

When the day is YELLOW per the cold-water protocol (see `Operations/Cold Water Go-No-Go Protocol.md`):

- 2 guides required on the tour
- Every guest in synthetic layers (no cotton) — verify at Beat 2 check-in
- Guide spends extra 30 sec on cold-shock in the dock briefing
- Sweep guide carries the throw bag and tow line at the ready
- Manager green-lights the launch via Slack before the briefing video goes up

When the day is RED: no tour. Reschedule or refund. This is a guide-empowered call.

## Group Size Adjustments

- **6-8 guests, solo guide:** Standard flow. Everything in this playbook applies.
- **9-12 guests, solo guide (standard summer):** Standard flow. Briefing scales fine; cave entry is sequenced one kayak at a time.
- **13-14 guests:** Guide discretion. Strongly prefer a sweep guide. Briefing time goes from 4 min to ~5 min (more verbal acknowledgments).
- **Early-season:** 2 guides + 1 tour group is the goal. If only 1 guide is available, cap at 8 guests and verify YELLOW protocol.
