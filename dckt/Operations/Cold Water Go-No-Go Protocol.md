# Cold Water Go-No-Go Protocol
*The rule a guide can run in 60 seconds before launch. Concrete numbers. Guide can always pull the plug.*

---

## Why this exists

Cold water is DCKT's highest-likelihood serious-injury risk and the source of our worst real-world close call. A PFD does not solve cold shock. We need a rule that's concrete enough to be applied consistently across 9+ guides and 5 months of season, but loose enough that the guide on duty can always say no.

This protocol applies to the 2-hour Cave Point tour. Half-day and other tours have their own variant (TBD).

---

## The Rule

Before every tour, the guide checks four numbers and the sky. The result is GREEN, YELLOW, or RED.

### GREEN — Run normally

- **Air temperature** ≥ 65°F
- **Water temperature** ≥ 55°F
- **Sustained wind** ≤ 12 mph (gusts okay up to 20)
- **Sky** clear or partly cloudy
- **No storms** in the next 3 hours per forecast

Brief cold water as a standard topic. No additional handling required.

### YELLOW — Run only if all three conditions are met

Any one of:
- Air 55-64°F
- Water 50-54°F
- Sustained wind 13-18 mph
- Overcast or rain in forecast within 3 hrs

Run **only if all three** of these are true:

1. **2 guides on the tour** (lead + sweep)
2. **Every guest is in synthetic layers** (no cotton; verify at shop check-in, Beat 2)
3. **Guide-on-duty AND manager both green-light** via Slack before the briefing video goes up

Additional handling:
- Spend an extra 30 seconds on cold-shock in the dock briefing
- Sweep guide carries throw bag + tow line at the ready
- Guide notes YELLOW in the briefing video Slack post

### RED — No-go

Any one of:
- Air < 55°F
- Water < 50°F
- Sustained wind > 18 mph
- Storms in forecast within 3 hours
- Lightning detected within 10 miles in past 30 min

Tour is canceled. Reschedule or refund. **Guide does not need manager approval to call a RED.**

---

## Guide Override

The guide on duty can call any tour RED regardless of the numbers. Reasons can include:

- Gut feeling about the group's experience level
- A guest visibly unwell or impaired at check-in
- Wave action that doesn't match the wind reading
- Fog or visibility issues
- Anything else

Numbers are the floor, not the ceiling. **Pulling the plug is never a wrong call.** No guide will be questioned for canceling a tour they didn't feel right about. Period.

---

## What to tell guests when calling a YELLOW or RED

### YELLOW (running with conditions)

> *"Conditions today are cool — water's at [X]°F. We're going. Here's what changes: we're running two guides, I need everyone in non-cotton layers, and we're spending an extra minute on cold water in the briefing. The tour is the same; we just take it a little more seriously today."*

### RED (canceling)

> *"I'm pulling the tour. Water is [X]°F, wind is [X], and that combination is not a good day for us. We're going to reschedule you to [date] or refund your booking — your call. I know that's not what you wanted to hear, but this is the right call. Thanks for being flexible."*

Don't apologize for the call. Don't soften the why. The honesty is part of the brand.

## Where to get the numbers

- **Air temp + forecast:** weather.gov hourly forecast for Sturgeon Bay or Sister Bay, or any reliable phone weather app
- **Water temp:** NOAA Lake Michigan buoy data, or `https://seatemperature.org/north-america/united-states/door-county.htm` as a fallback
- **Wind:** weather.gov sustained wind reading, not the gust
- **Lightning:** any standard weather app with radar

Guide is responsible for checking these in the hour before launch and posting the reading in the dock briefing video.

---

## When to recheck during a tour

- **Every 20 minutes** the guide does a visual sky check (building clouds, darkening to the west, wind picking up).
- If conditions change to RED mid-tour, the guide calls *"return to shore"* — every paddler turns and follows without question (this is covered in Beat 7 briefing).
- Sweep guide watches the rear of the group for any sign of cold distress (shivering, confusion, slurred speech, blue lips). Any sign = immediate return.

---

## Cold-shock and cold-water immersion response

If a guest goes in the water:

1. **Talk to them.** "Stay with the kayak. Hold your paddle. I'm coming to you."
2. **Get them out fast.** Cold shock is 1-3 minutes. Swim failure is 10-30 min. Hypothermia is 30+ min. Speed matters at every stage.
3. **Assisted T-rescue** is the standard for sit-on-tops. Guide pulls alongside, stabilizes guest's kayak, helps them back on.
4. **If they can't re-enter:** tow them on the back of your kayak to the nearest shore. Don't let them swim.
5. **Once back at the launch:** dry layers, warm shelter, food/hot drink. Watch for delayed hypothermia symptoms for the next 1-2 hours.
6. **If unconscious or unresponsive:** 911 immediately, then begin care.

This protocol is also in the Guide Training Video.
