# Guide Training Video Script
*~15 minute video. Internal staff only. New guide watches before first solo tour. Annual rewatch for returning guides.*

---

## Production notes

- **Length:** 14-16 minutes
- **Format:** 16:9, 1080p. Captions optional (internal use).
- **Voiceover/host:** David or Bella on-camera. This one should NOT be a slick voiceover — it's a senior team member walking a new guide through the system. Conversational, slightly imperfect, real.
- **Visuals:** Real Cave Point footage, real dock, real shop, real guides at work. Show the 11-beat pathway as it actually happens.
- **Hosting:** Vimeo Pro private, password-protected. Link distributed to new guides during onboarding.
- **Companion docs:** Every section references a written doc that's the source of truth. Video is for the texture; docs are for the detail.

---

## Script Outline (the structure to film against)

### CHAPTER 1 — Why this exists — 0:00 to 1:00

David on camera at the shop.

> *"Welcome. If you're watching this, you're guiding for DCKT this season. I want to walk you through how we run a tour, because we don't do it the way most outfitters do."*

> *"There are three things every tour does at the same time. It keeps people safe. It gives them a great experience. And it leaves us a record of what happened so we can get better. Most outfitters do one of those well. We're trying to do all three."*

> *"This video is about 15 minutes. Watch it before your first solo tour. Watch it again at the start of every season. We'll keep updating it."*

---

### CHAPTER 2 — The waiver (in plain English) — 1:00 to 3:30

David at a table with the waiver in hand.

> *"Every guest signs a waiver. They read it. You don't recite it. But you need to know what's in it so you can talk to it when something comes up on the water."*

Walk through the 10 sections from `Operations/2-Hour Cave Point — Operations Playbook.md` Layer A table.

> *"Section 2 is inherent risks. The big ones: sit-on-top behavior, the dock pinch, cave hazards. You'll cover these in the dock briefing. They sign saying they understand. Your briefing makes sure they actually do."*

> *"Section 3 is cold water. Most important section in here. Cold shock kills strong swimmers wearing PFDs. We have a separate doc on the cold-water protocol — go read it after this video. Your job: never let anyone think a PFD is enough."*

> *"Section 4 is loading and unloading. Pinched fingers and back strains. Cover this when you're handing out paddles. Show them the lift before they need it."*

> *"Section 5 is health and fitness. Section 6 is pregnancy. These are private conversations. We do them at the shop, one-on-one, when they're filling out the waiver. Not in front of the group. Anyone with a heart issue, a recent surgery, a pregnancy — you need to know before launch, and you do not bring it up at the dock briefing."*

> *"Two rules for talking about the waiver with guests: never say 'the waiver,' say 'what you signed' or 'what you read.' Never read it aloud. You talk to the substance like a human being."*

---

### CHAPTER 3 — The 11-beat pathway — 3:30 to 9:00

Pacing through the actual sequence. Use real footage.

> *"A 2-hour Cave Point tour has 11 distinct moments. Three of them are designed to be memorable — peak moments. The rest just need to be clean and warm. I'm going to walk you through them fast, but the full doc is in the Operations folder."*

**Beat 1 — Arrival.** *"First peak. You greet them by name within 30 seconds. You hand them water or a hat. You scan the group. Who's nervous, who's been before, who's celebrating something, who's got the camera. Three minutes of attention here pays off for the rest of the tour."*

**Beat 2 — Check-in & waiver.** *"Front desk or you, depending on the day. Confirm names. Confirm waivers. Do the private health check one-on-one. Anyone disclose a pregnancy or heart condition, you flag it in our day's Slack thread before they get to the dock."*

**Beat 3 — Comfort prep.** *"Bathroom, water, gear shop. Don't hover. 10-15 min."*

**Beat 4 — Safety video.** *"Plays on the shop screen. After it ends, you're at the front for questions. Be there."*

**Beat 5 — Pre-brief.** *"You introduce yourself as a person. Name every guest out loud once. Set what's coming on the drive."*

**Beat 6 — Drive.** *"Caravan. Optional short story about the place. Don't lecture."*

**Beat 7 — Dock briefing.** *"Second peak. Most important 4 minutes of the day. We're going to spend a whole chapter on this one."*

**Beat 8 — Launch.** *"Guide-assisted, hands inside the kayak, paddle in hand. Do not let anyone get cute at the dock. This is where injuries happen."*

**Beat 9 — On water.** *"Third peak happens organically — the cave entrance. You stop, the group drifts, you name what they're seeing, you go silent for 10 seconds. That's the photograph their brain takes. About 15 minutes before return, you post the mid-trip check to the Slack thread."*

**Beat 10 — Return.** *"Calm exit. Same dock rules as launch. Don't rush gear stow."*

**Beat 11 — Paddler's Circle ritual.** *"Fourth peak. Last 60 seconds. We have separate scripts for first-timers and returning guests. This is the moment they take home. Do not skip this."*

---

### CHAPTER 4 — The dock briefing (Beat 7 deep dive) — 9:00 to 11:30

Out at the actual dock, demonstrate.

> *"This is the briefing. You're going to record this on your phone. We post it to Slack as the start of the tour's thread. It's both proof that we briefed them and a coaching tool for ourselves."*

> *"Structure: warm open, conditions check, 10 numbered safety items, verbal acknowledgments, warm close. Total time about 4 to 5 minutes."*

Demonstrate the warm open with a stand-in group.

> *"You're going to tell them you're recording, that it's standard for us. Wisconsin law doesn't require you to disclose, but we do anyway. Sets the tone right."*

Show the conditions check.

> *"Air temp, water temp, wind, forecast. You announce whether we're in GREEN or YELLOW. If we're in YELLOW you spend an extra 30 seconds on cold water and you make sure every guest is in non-cotton."*

Walk through each of the 10 safety items quickly, showing the demo move for each.

> *"After the safety items, you go around the group and each guest says their name and 'I understand' for the recording. Yes it feels a little weird the first time. Yes it's effective. The guests usually crack a smile. Hold the moment."*

> *"Group photo. Recording stops. You post the video and photo to Slack. Now you're ready to launch."*

---

### CHAPTER 5 — Cold water — the rule and the response — 11:30 to 13:00

David back at the shop, holding a printed cold-water protocol.

> *"Cold water is our highest-likelihood serious-injury risk. We had a close call. I'm not going to be cute about this."*

> *"There's a separate doc — Cold Water Go-No-Go Protocol. Concrete numbers. GREEN, YELLOW, RED. You'll read it as part of onboarding. Two things to know now."*

> *"One: you can pull any tour at any time. You don't need my approval to call a RED. If your gut says no, the answer is no. I will back that play every single time."*

> *"Two: if a guest goes in the water, the order of operations is talk to them, get them out fast, assisted T-rescue, tow them in if they can't re-enter. Watch for delayed hypothermia for the next hour. We'll drill this at training week."*

---

### CHAPTER 6 — The Slack channel and how we use it — 13:00 to 14:00

Screen recording of Slack `#dckt-tour-logs` channel.

> *"Every tour gets one thread. The thread starts when you post your dock briefing video. The mid-trip check goes in the same thread. The closeout goes in the same thread. One tour, one thread, retrievable forever."*

> *"This isn't surveillance. It's how we get better. Bella and I scan the channel daily for coachable moments. If we see something great, we'll call it out. If we see something to teach, we'll DM you. Nobody's name gets shamed in the channel."*

> *"If you have a real incident, you tag manager and owner in the same Slack post. We'll get to you fast."*

---

### CHAPTER 7 — The brand voice — 14:00 to 14:45

Brief.

> *"One last thing. We call our voice 'the Scout.' Think the friend who actually lives in Door County and knows where to go. Not a tourism account. Not a hype machine."*

> *"Things we never say: 'Welcome aboard.' 'Safety is our number one priority.' 'Isn't this amazing?' 'Did you have fun?' These are all things every other outfitter says. They flatten the experience."*

> *"Things we do say: their name, specific reference to something on the water, a peak moment named then silence, a physical hand-off."*

> *"Full anti-script is in the Operations Playbook. Read it. Don't recite it — just don't talk like a corporate account."*

---

### CHAPTER 8 — The one standard — 14:45 to 15:00

David, close.

> *"Last thing. At the end of every tour, a guest should be able to answer yes to one question: did someone here make me feel like I mattered?"*

> *"That's the job. Keep them safe. Run the system. But the standard is that one feeling. Get that right and everything else follows."*

> *"Welcome to the team. Let's have a good season."*

*(end card: DCKT logo)*
