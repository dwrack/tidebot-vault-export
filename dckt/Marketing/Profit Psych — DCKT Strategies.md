# Profit Psych -- DCKT Strategies

*Psychological triggers applied to Door County Kayak Tours, April 2026.*

---

## 1. The Hook

*How to capture attention in the crowded Door County tourism market.*

**1.1 The Cave Point Cold Open.** Start every Instagram Reel and TikTok with 3 seconds of raw Cave Point footage, no logo, no text, just the turquoise water hitting the cliffs. The landscape does the selling. Add context after the scroll-stop moment.

**1.2 "First Timer or Lifer?" Quiz on the Website.** A quick 4-question quiz ("Have you kayaked before? How adventurous is your group?") that routes visitors to the right tour or e-bike option. Interactive content converts 2-3x better than static pages because people want to see their result.

**1.3 The Weather Bet.** Post real-time conditions on Instagram Stories every morning during season. "Water temp: 58F. Visibility: 12 feet. Wind: calm. Today's a 10/10 paddle day." Followers start checking your stories like a forecast, which builds habitual engagement before they ever book.

**1.4 Guide Takeover Days.** Hand the Instagram account to a different guide each week. Their personality, their favorite spots, their real voice. Eight guides means eight distinct flavors of Door County content. Tourists connect with people, not brands.

**1.5 The "What You'll Actually See" Post.** Side-by-side: the stock-photo version of Door County kayaking vs. what it actually looks like from your kayak. Honesty is a pattern interrupt in a feed full of over-filtered tourism content.

**1.6 Geo-Targeted Ads at the Ferry.** Run Meta ads geo-fenced to the Washington Island ferry terminal and the Sturgeon Bay bridge. People arriving in Door County right now are making activity decisions right now. Hit them with "You're here. The water's ready." and a direct FareHarbor link.

**1.7 The "Locals Know" Angle.** Create a recurring Instagram series where Isabella or a guide shares a Door County spot that tourists usually miss. Position DCKT as insiders, not just another tour operator. The subtext: book with us and you get the real Door County.

**1.8 Partner Content Swaps.** Trade one Instagram post per month with Door County wineries, restaurants, and lodges. Their audience wants activities; your audience wants dining and lodging. Both lists grow without ad spend.

**1.9 The Sunset Kayak Thumbnail.** For any paid ads, always lead with the golden hour shot from the water. Testing across tourism brands consistently shows that water + warm light + human silhouette outperforms every other creative format. Make it the default hero image on the website too.

**1.10 "Bring Your Dog" Content.** If any tours allow dogs, that's your most shareable content category by a wide margin. One golden retriever in a kayak will outperform a month of regular posts. If dogs aren't allowed, this is worth reconsidering for at least one tour slot.

**1.11 The E-Bike Discovery Hook.** Most visitors don't associate Door County with e-bikes yet. Lead with "You've done the kayak. Now ride to Cave Point." Position the e-bike as the thing they didn't know they needed, the second-day activity.

**1.12 Review Screenshot Carousels.** Pull the best 1-2 sentence FareHarbor/Google reviews, screenshot them, and post as Instagram carousels with the location tagged. Real words from real people beat any copy you could write. Post these weekly.

**1.13 The "We're Fully Booked" Post.** When a tour sells out, post about it. "Saturday morning Cave Point tour: sold out. A few spots left Sunday." Scarcity is its own hook, and it signals demand to anyone on the fence.

**1.14 Email Subject Lines That Name the Month.** "Your July Door County trip needs this" will always outperform "Book a kayak tour." Time-specific subject lines create urgency and make the email feel relevant to their actual planning timeline.

**1.15 The 15-Second Guide Intro.** Film each guide giving a 15-second intro ("I'm Jake, I've been paddling Door County for 6 years, my favorite moment is when first-timers see the caves"). Pin these to the website's tour pages. People book experiences with people, not companies.

**1.16 Google Business Profile Posts.** Most competitors ignore GBP posts entirely. Post weekly with a photo and a FareHarbor link. When someone searches "Door County kayak tours," your listing shows fresh content while competitors show stale profiles.

**1.17 The Arrival Day Email.** Partner with local lodges or Airbnb hosts to get included in their "welcome to Door County" info. Even a mention in a check-in packet with a QR code to your FareHarbor page puts you in front of people at the exact decision moment.

## 2. The Retention

*Why some customers come back every summer and others forget you exist.*

**2.1 The Post-Tour Photo Drop.** Email every group their tour photos within 24 hours. Not a link to a gallery they'll forget, a direct attachment or small album. This becomes their vacation highlight reel and keeps DCKT in the memory. Include a "Book next summer" link at the bottom.

**2.2 The "Your Guide Remembers You" Note.** When repeat customers book, have the guide who led their last tour send a short personal text or email. "Hey, the Johnsons are back! I've got you on Saturday, the caves have been incredible this week." This costs nothing and creates fierce loyalty.

**2.3 Seasonal Email Drip (4 Emails, Not 12).** Send exactly four emails per year: (1) "Season is open, early birds get first pick" in March, (2) "Peak season reminder" in June, (3) "Fall colors from the water" in September, (4) "Gift cards for the holidays" in November. Don't over-email.

**2.4 The Anniversary Ping.** FareHarbor has the booking date. One year later, send an automated email: "It's been a year since your Cave Point paddle. The water misses you." Simple, personal, and it hits right when families are planning their next Door County trip.

**2.5 The "Level Up" Upsell.** If someone did the standard kayak tour, their next booking email should pitch the Door Bluff Shipwreck, the Eco Estuary, or the e-bike combo. If they did the e-bike, pitch the kayak. Never offer the same thing twice to a repeat customer, show them what's next.

**2.6 Kids Grow Up, Tours Scale.** Track family bookings. If the Millers brought a 10-year-old last year, this year that kid is 11 and ready for a longer route or a different tour. A "your kids are ready for the next adventure" email is incredibly specific and lands hard.

**2.7 The Guide Roster Page.** Put guide bios on the website with real photos and a sentence about what makes each one's tours different. Repeat customers will request specific guides, which builds personal loyalty that competitors can't replicate.

**2.8 The Post-Season "Thank You" Video.** At the end of October, film a 60-second team video thanking customers for the season. Post it on Instagram, send it via email. It humanizes the off-season gap and gives people a reason to remember you exist in January.

**2.9 Loyalty Pricing for Repeat Bookers.** Offer a 10% "welcome back" code to anyone who's booked before. Don't advertise it publicly. Send it directly via email. The exclusivity matters more than the discount amount.

**2.10 The "Your Door County Crew" Group.** Create a private Facebook group or email list for past customers. Share off-season Door County content, early booking access, and behind-the-scenes prep. This only works if it's genuinely low-volume and interesting, not a spam channel.

**2.11 Review Follow-Up Sequence.** 48 hours after the tour, send a review request with a direct Google Review link. If they leave one, send a genuine thank-you reply within 24 hours. If they don't, a second nudge 5 days later. Two asks max, never three.

**2.12 The "We Fixed It" Update.** If a customer mentioned something in a review or feedback form (cold wetsuits, confusing parking, whatever), email them when you fix it. "You mentioned the parking was tricky. We added signs this season." This turns critics into advocates.

**2.13 Guide Tips and Venmo.** If guides have personal Venmo/CashApp for tips, include it in the post-tour email. Customers who tip feel more invested in the relationship. It also gives guides a financial reason to deliver exceptional experiences.

**2.14 The Off-Season Content Calendar.** Don't go dark November through March. Post once a week: winter Door County beauty shots, gear prep, guide off-season adventures, "countdown to May." Staying visible in the off-season is what separates businesses that retain from businesses that restart every year.

**2.15 The Combo Booker Advantage.** Anyone who books both a kayak tour AND an e-bike rental gets a small perk: priority booking window next season, a free photo package, or a DCKT sticker/hat. Reward the behavior you want to see.

**2.16 The Feedback Text.** Send a simple text (not email) the evening after the tour: "Hey, this is Isabella from DCKT. How was the paddle today? Anything we can do better?" Texts get 90%+ open rates. The ones who reply become your most loyal customers because you actually listened.

## 3. The Suspense

*Building anticipation and demand so customers book before they're even sure of their dates.*

**3.1 The Season Opener Countdown.** Start a 30-day countdown on Instagram Stories in April. Day 30: gear inspection. Day 20: guides arriving. Day 10: first test paddle. Day 1: "We're live." Give followers a reason to watch the buildup like a show they're invested in.

**3.2 Early Access Booking Window.** Email your past-customer list 48 hours before public booking opens. "You're getting first pick of summer dates before we open to the public." This rewards loyalty and creates urgency simultaneously.

**3.3 The Waitlist as a Feature.** When tours sell out, don't just show "unavailable" on FareHarbor. Add a waitlist option. People who waitlist are more committed than people who browse, and the waitlist itself signals that this experience is worth waiting for.

**3.4 Limited-Availability Tours.** For any tour that could support more capacity than we run (Door Bluff Shipwreck, Inland Fall Paddle, private routes), lean into scarcity messaging: "limited availability this week" on the website and Instagram. Scarcity makes each departure feel like a special event rather than just a time slot. (Note: we do NOT run a Sunset Tour — do not create one just to run this play.)

**3.5 The "Secret" Tour.** Once per month, run a tour to a route you don't normally offer, something off-menu. Announce it only via email to past customers with 72 hours notice. It'll sell out in hours, and the exclusivity creates word of mouth that money can't buy.

**3.6 Weather-Dependent Flash Bookings.** On days with exceptional forecasts, post an Instagram Story at 8am: "Today is going to be a 10. We just opened 4 extra spots on the afternoon paddle. Link in bio." Real-time urgency tied to something people can see out their window.

**3.7 The "Sold Out Saturday" Tracker.** On the website, show which dates are sold out vs. which have limited availability. A calendar view where half the Saturdays in July are red creates more urgency than any "book now" button.

**3.8 Pre-Season E-Bike Teaser.** Film someone riding the e-bike route to Cave Point in early May before tourists arrive. Empty roads, no crowds, pristine views. Post it with "This is what June looks like before you get here. Book now." The implication: this sells out.

**3.9 The "Last 3 Spots" Story.** When a tour is almost full, post it on Instagram Stories with the FareHarbor link. "Tomorrow's 9am Cave Point tour: 3 spots left." Do this consistently and followers start watching your stories with a fear-of-missing-out reflex.

**3.10 Holiday Gift Card Drops.** In November, release gift cards with a limited bonus ("Buy $100, get $120 in value through December 15"). Frame it as a seasonal drop, not a permanent offer. The deadline does the work.

**3.11 The New Tour Reveal.** If you're adding a new tour or route for the season, don't just list it. Tease it. Week 1: "Something new is coming." Week 2: a 5-second clip of the route. Week 3: the full reveal with booking link. Three weeks of content from one announcement.

**3.12 The Group Booking Countdown.** For bachelorette parties, corporate outings, and family reunions, show a "group dates filling up" banner on the website starting in February. Groups book early when they believe they'll miss out, and they actually will.

**3.13 Guide Assignment Reveals.** A week before their tour, email customers: "Your guide for Saturday is Jake. Here's a quick video of what he's got planned." This turns the days before the tour into anticipation instead of just calendar logistics.

**3.14 The Returning Species Post.** "The eagles are back at Cave Point" or "Water clarity just hit peak levels." Tying bookings to natural events that are genuinely time-limited creates urgency that doesn't feel manufactured, because it isn't.

**3.15 The "Members Book First" Model.** If you build any kind of loyalty program or email list tier, the single most valuable perk is early access to peak dates. Let members book July 4th week before the public. That alone justifies the list.

**3.16 Off-Season Booking Incentive.** Open next season's bookings on November 1 with a small early-bird discount (5-10%) that expires January 31. Families planning summer vacations in winter will lock in dates, giving you revenue visibility months ahead.

**3.17 The "We Added a Date" Drop.** When you add an extra tour due to demand, frame it as a limited release. "Due to demand, we just opened one extra Cave Point departure this Friday. It won't last." This trains your audience to act fast on any announcement.

## 4. The Reward

*Creating reciprocity and loyalty that turns customers into your unpaid marketing team.*

**4.1 The Free Photo Package.** Take great photos during every tour. Give them away for free, no watermark, no paywall. Customers who get free photos post them to their own Instagram, tag the location, and do your marketing for you. The ROI on a guide with a GoPro is enormous.

**4.2 The "Tag Us" Incentive.** Tell every group at the end of the tour: "If you post and tag @doorcountykayaktours, we'll repost the best ones every week." People love being featured. This is a reward loop that generates UGC at zero cost.

**4.3 The Handwritten Card.** For groups of 6+ or repeat customers, mail a handwritten postcard after their tour. "Thanks for paddling with us, the Johnson family killed it out there." A $1 postcard creates a physical artifact that sits on a fridge for months.

**4.4 The Local Discount Network.** Partner with 5-10 Door County businesses (restaurants, wineries, ice cream shops) to give DCKT customers a small discount card after their tour. The card says "DCKT Friends" and gets them 10% off at partner spots. You become the gateway to their whole Door County experience.

**4.5 The "You Were Here" Map.** Email customers a simple illustrated map of the route they paddled, with their tour date on it. Frameable, shareable, and a physical reminder that they did something cool. This could also be a printed handout given at tour end.

**4.6 The Referral Code.** Give every past customer a unique referral code worth $15 off for their friend and $15 off their next booking. Track it in FareHarbor. This turns happy customers into recruiters with a tangible incentive.

**4.7 The Guide Shoutout.** When a customer leaves a great review mentioning a specific guide, screenshot it and post it on Instagram with the guide tagged. The guide feels valued, the customer feels famous, and future customers see that your team is worth booking for.

**4.8 The "100th Tour" Celebration.** Track milestones. When you hit your 100th tour of the season (or 500th customer, or whatever), celebrate it publicly. Give that group a surprise (free t-shirts, a bottle of local wine). Post about it. Milestones create shareable moments.

**4.9 The End-of-Tour Surprise.** After the paddle, offer something small and unexpected: a cold local beer, a handful of Door County cherries, or just cold water and a local snack. The surprise element matters more than the cost. Unexpected rewards trigger reciprocity harder than expected ones.

**4.10 The "Best Photo of the Week" Contest.** Each week during season, pick the best customer-submitted photo and feature it on Instagram. Winner gets a DCKT hat or sticker shipped to them. This incentivizes tagging, creates weekly content, and costs maybe $5 per week.

**4.11 The Review-to-Reward Pipeline.** After someone leaves a 5-star Google review, send them an exclusive early-access link to next season's bookings or a small discount code. Don't announce this publicly (that would feel transactional). Just surprise the people who took the time.

**4.12 The Anniversary Gift.** For customers who've booked 3+ seasons in a row, send them something in the mail during winter: a DCKT beanie, a Door County candle, anything small. The gesture is absurdly disproportionate to its cost, and those families will never book with a competitor.

**4.13 The Group Leader Perk.** Whoever organizes a group booking (bachelorette, family reunion, corporate) gets their tour free if the group is 8+. This rewards the person doing the organizational labor and incentivizes them to fill the tour to the threshold.

**4.14 The "Your Guide Picked This" Recommendation.** At tour end, have the guide give one personal Door County recommendation: "If you like wine, hit up Door Peninsula tonight. Tell them Jake sent you." This creates a genuine personal connection and positions the guide as a local expert, not just a paddle instructor.

**4.15 Social Proof Stacking.** Display total reviews and average rating prominently on the FareHarbor booking page and the website header. "1,200+ happy paddlers. 4.9 stars." Social proof reduces booking friction and rewards the collective customer base by making their reviews visible.

**4.16 The E-Bike Upgrade Surprise.** Occasionally upgrade a standard e-bike rental to a longer route or throw in an extra 30 minutes at no charge. Don't announce it in advance. Random upgrades create stories: "They just gave us extra time for free." Those stories get told at dinner, at the lodge, on social media.

**4.17 The "Bring Back a Friend" Challenge.** At tour end, hand each person two business cards with a personal discount code. "Bring a friend next time, you both save $15." Physical cards work better than digital codes here because they sit in wallets and get handed to real people.

**4.18 The Year-End Impact Email.** In December, send a short email: "This season, DCKT took 3,200 people to Cave Point. Our guides logged 800 hours on the water. We cleaned 140 lbs of trash from the shoreline." Real numbers make customers feel like they were part of something, not just consumers of a service.
