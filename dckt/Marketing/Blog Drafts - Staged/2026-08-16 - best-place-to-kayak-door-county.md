## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
Every Door County kayak guide has an opinion about where the best paddling actually is, and most of them will tell you the same top pick before you finish the question. Here's the honest ranking, including the spots we don't personally guide, because pretending otherwise wouldn't be much of a "best of" list.

## The short answer

Cave Point County Park, hands down. Sea caves, dolostone cliffs, water clear enough to watch the bottom drop away underneath you. It's the paddle that shows up in almost every Door County photo you've seen before you got here, and it's the one our guides paddle most, by a wide margin.

Everything below it is real and worth knowing about, but nothing else on this peninsula competes with Cave Point for a first-time visitor's one kayak trip.

## #1 Cave Point County Park

Sea caves cut into 420-million-year-old dolostone, cliffs, and the kind of water clarity, 15 to 20 feet on a good day, that people don't expect from the Midwest. This is the flagship, and it's the spot our guides paddle underneath almost daily through the summer.

Door County Kayak Tours runs the [2-hour Cave Point tour](https://www.doorcountykayaktours.com/kayak-tours/cave-point-county-park/) ($69) and the [4-hour Cave Point and Whitefish Dunes half-day](https://www.doorcountykayaktours.com/kayak-tours/cave-point-whitefish-dunes-half-day-kayak-tour/) ($145), which adds a picnic and more time in the caves on calm days. Both depart from our shop in Jacksonport.

## #2 Cana Island

A working lighthouse on a small tied-island, reachable by an extended paddle through calmer, more protected water than Cave Point's open lake exposure. Different mood entirely, quieter, more historical, better suited to a slower day.

We run a [Cana Island Lighthouse tour](https://www.doorcountykayaktours.com/kayak-tours/cana-island-lighthouse-kayak-tour/) ($110, about 4 hours) that pairs the paddle with a locally sourced picnic. Good pick for an anniversary trip or anyone who wants scenery over cliffs.

## #3 Peninsula State Park

Sheltered bay water, shipwreck sites near the park, and a setting that's calmer and more forgiving than the open lake conditions at Cave Point. This is a real Door County paddling destination, and we'll say plainly: it's not currently one of our guided kayak tours. If Peninsula State Park is the plan, expect a self-guided or rental paddle rather than a booked DCKT tour, at least for now.

## #4 Garrett Bay

A quieter, less-visited bay on the peninsula's northern reaches, sheltered and scenic, popular with paddlers who've already done Cave Point and want somewhere with fewer people around. Like Peninsula State Park, this one's typically a self-guided trip rather than a current DCKT tour offering.

## #5 Sister Bay and Ephraim

Town-adjacent water, easy access, and a built-in reason to combine a paddle with dinner or a sunset walk through either town afterward. Good for a low-key evening on the water rather than a big-ticket adventure. Also self-guided territory today.

## #6 Sturgeon Bay

Convenient if you're staying in the southern part of the county and don't want to drive 30-plus minutes north for a paddle. Less dramatic scenery than Cave Point or Cana Island, but it's an easy, low-commitment option, especially useful for a short window between other plans.

## How to choose, by experience level

**First-timer:** Cave Point, guided. The scenery does the work of making it memorable, and a guide handles the parts a first-timer shouldn't have to figure out alone, the route, the safety talk, the launch.

**Comfortable paddler, wants something calmer:** Cana Island. Protected water, slower pace, still genuinely scenic.

**Experienced, wants to explore on their own:** Peninsula State Park, Garrett Bay, or Sturgeon Bay with a rental kayak.

## How to choose, by what you want to see

**Cliffs and caves:** Cave Point, no contest.
**A lighthouse:** Cana Island.
**A shipwreck:** [Door Bluff County Park](https://www.doorcountykayaktours.com/kayak-tours/door-bluff-county-park-shipwreck/), near Death's Door, where our shipwreck kayak tour runs.
**Calm, sheltered bay water:** Peninsula State Park.
**A quiet afternoon with fewer people:** Garrett Bay.
**Something you can pair with dinner in town:** Sister Bay or Ephraim.

## The bottom line

If you're picking one spot and one spot only, it's Cave Point. If you've got a few days in Door County and want to see more of what the peninsula's shoreline actually offers, and it's more shoreline than any county in the country, working through this list in order isn't a bad plan. Book the [Cave Point tour](https://www.doorcountykayaktours.com/kayak-tours/cave-point-county-park/) or [browse all our guided kayak tours](https://www.doorcountykayaktours.com/kayak-tours/) to start, and grab a [self-guided rental](https://www.doorcountykayaktours.com/kayak-rentals/) for the spots we don't currently guide. Door County Kayak Tours has been getting people onto this water long enough to know which of these actually earns the "best" label and which ones are just good, and we'd rather tell you the difference than pretend every spot on this list is our tour.
```
