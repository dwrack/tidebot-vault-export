## FAQ SCHEMA
_(5 Q&As)_

**Q1:**
```
What is the difference between Cave Point and Whitefish Dunes?
```
**A1:**
```
Cave Point County Park is a free, no-sticker-required county park with dolostone cliffs, sea caves, and a hiking trail along the bluff, best experienced by kayak or on foot along the cliff edge. Whitefish Dunes is a Wisconsin state park next door with a sand swimming beach, dune trails, and Old Baldy, the tallest dune on the Wisconsin Lake Michigan shoreline. You need a state park vehicle sticker to park at Whitefish Dunes. You don't at Cave Point.
```

**Q2:**
```
Can you swim at Cave Point or Whitefish Dunes?
```
**A2:**
```
Whitefish Dunes has the actual designated swimming beach, sand bottom, gradual entry, the kind of swimming most people picture. Cave Point's shoreline is mostly rock and cliff with limited safe swim entry, and the water near the caves can get rough fast. If swimming is the point of your day, go to Whitefish Dunes.
```

**Q3:**
```
Can you kayak at Whitefish Dunes or do you need to go to Cave Point?
```
**A3:**
```
Both parks border the same stretch of Lake Michigan shoreline, and paddlers sometimes launch from the Whitefish Dunes beach area to reach the Cave Point sea caves, since Cave Point itself has no easy self-launch spot. Most people skip the logistics entirely and book a guided kayak tour with Door County Kayak Tours, which handles the launch and route from a dedicated put-in.
```

**Q4:**
```
Do you have to pay to get into Cave Point or Whitefish Dunes?
```
**A4:**
```
Cave Point County Park has no entrance fee and no state park sticker requirement, just a small parking lot that fills early on summer weekends. Whitefish Dunes is a Wisconsin state park, so it requires a state park vehicle admission sticker (daily or annual), posted at the gate.
```

**Q5:**
```
Which is better for kids, Cave Point or Whitefish Dunes?
```
**A5:**
```
Whitefish Dunes, if you want them in the water. Sand beach, shallow gradual entry, lifeguards on duty in season, and dune trails that are an easy walk. Cave Point's cliff-edge trail is stunning but has real drop-offs close to the path, better suited to kids who are steady on their feet and staying close to an adult. Many families do both in the same afternoon.
```

## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
Every summer, our guides get some version of the same question at the shop: "Are we at the right park?" Cave Point and Whitefish Dunes share an access road, sit five minutes apart, and confuse first-timers constantly. They are not the same place, and they are not interchangeable.

Here's the actual difference, from people who launch kayaks off one and drive past the other almost every day.

## The 30-second answer

**Cave Point County Park:** dolostone cliffs, sea caves, a cliffside hiking trail, free entry, best seen from a kayak. Go here for the view that makes Door County famous.

**Whitefish Dunes State Park:** a real sand swimming beach, dune trails, Old Baldy (the tallest dune on the Wisconsin Lake Michigan shoreline), requires a state park sticker. Go here to actually get in the water and lie on a towel.

They're connected by a short stretch of road and most people who visit one should visit both. But if you only have an hour, the answer depends on what you actually want to do, not just what looks good in photos.

## Cave Point: cliffs, caves, and free parking

Cave Point County Park is a Door County park, not a state park, which means no sticker and no entrance fee. Just a small lot that fills up fast on summer weekends.

What you get: a walking trail along the bluff with sweeping Lake Michigan views, dolostone cliffs carved into sea caves over roughly 420 million years, and the single most photographed stretch of shoreline in the county. The caves themselves are only reachable from the water. Standing at the top, you can see the cliff edges and hear the waves working underneath you, but you can't actually get into the caves without a kayak.

**The catch:** it's a lookout point and a trail, not a beach. The shoreline is mostly rock, entry into the water is limited and can get rough, and there's real drop-off right next to the trail in several spots. Not the place to bring a cooler and spend the afternoon swimming.

## Whitefish Dunes: sand, dunes, and a state park sticker

Whitefish Dunes State Park sits just south of Cave Point, and it's a completely different kind of park. Sand beach, gradual water entry, lifeguards on duty in peak season, and dune trails that climb up to Old Baldy for a view back down the shoreline.

It's a Wisconsin state park, which means a vehicle admission sticker (daily or annual, posted at the gate) instead of free entry. Bigger lot than Cave Point's, though it also fills on the busiest summer days.

**The catch:** no sea caves, no cliffs like Cave Point's, and the dune trail system takes real time if you want to see Old Baldy properly. It's the beach day. It's not the dramatic photo.

## Side-by-side, the stuff that actually matters

- **Entry fee:** Cave Point, free. Whitefish Dunes, state park sticker required.
- **Swimming:** Whitefish Dunes has an actual designated beach. Cave Point mostly doesn't.
- **The photo everyone wants:** Cave Point's cliffs and caves, hands down.
- **Hiking:** both have trails. Whitefish Dunes' dune trails are gentler underfoot. Cave Point's cliff trail has more exposure and better views.
- **Kayaking:** Cave Point's sea caves are the draw. Whitefish Dunes doesn't have caves, but paddlers sometimes use its beach area as a launch point to reach them.
- **Dogs:** rules differ by park and by trail, always check current signage at the gate before you bring one.
- **Best souvenir:** a photo from inside a sea cave (Cave Point) versus sand in your shoes for the next three days (Whitefish Dunes, affectionately).

## Best for swimming

Whitefish Dunes. It's not close. If the plan is towels, a cooler, and actual time in the water, that's the park.

## Best for families with young kids

Also leans Whitefish Dunes, mostly because of the shallow, gradual beach entry and lifeguards in season. Cave Point's cliff trail is genuinely beautiful with kids, but the drop-offs sit close enough to the path that you'll spend the walk holding a hand instead of taking photos. Plenty of families do both in one day: sand and swimming first, cliffs and caves second, timed around nap schedules.

## Best for first-timers to Door County

Cave Point. If you're new to the peninsula and only have one park in you, this is the one that actually looks like Door County in every photo you've seen before you got here. Our guides paddle guests underneath these exact cliffs on the [internal link: Cave Point kayak tour → /cave-point-kayak-tour/], right into the [internal link: sea caves → /cave-point-sea-caves/], which is the version of this park most day visitors from the overlook trail never get to see. Door County Kayak Tours has run that exact route long enough to carry a 4.9-star average across 800+ reviews, mostly from guests who started out just as confused about which park was which as you might be right now.

## Do both, they're a five-minute drive apart

You don't actually have to choose. Most guests who ask us this question end up doing Cave Point in the morning, when the light's better for photos and the crowds are thinner, then Whitefish Dunes in the afternoon once the sun's higher and swimming sounds better than hiking.

If you want the version of Cave Point that most visitors standing on the overlook trail never see, from inside the caves, at water level, book the guided [internal link: Cave Point kayak tour → /cave-point-kayak-tour/] with Door County Kayak Tours. Bring the swimsuit for Whitefish Dunes after. That's the full day, and it's the one we'd plan for our own family if we were visiting instead of living here.
```
