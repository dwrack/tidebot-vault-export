# VSL Script — Cave Point Kayak Tour

**Built:** 2026-05-06
**Audience:** Avatar from `Customer Avatar — From Google Voice Data.md` — 35–55 woman from WI / Chicago burbs, in a Door County cottage, never kayaked, kids 7–12, deciding today, watching the weather, worried about tipping over.
**Use case:** Landing page hero video (primary), YouTube pre-roll cutdown (secondary), Facebook/Instagram video ad (60-sec cutdown).
**Target length:** 2:45–3:15 spoken
**Voice:** David Rack (owner, on camera). Authentic > polished. He's the one with 24 years in the business; he should be the one talking.
**Tone:** Conversational, warm, direct. No salesman energy. The voice of "the guy who actually owns the kayak shop, talking to you across a counter."

## Production notes

**Lines that should NOT be cut from any version:**
- "Most of the people who book this tour have never kayaked before in their lives." (positions DCKT correctly for the avatar)
- "If the weather isn't safe, we reschedule for free." (top objection)
- "You can't really tip them." (#1 fear, named directly)
- "Twenty-four years." (the trust signal that doesn't require a stat dump)

**Lines lifted from real customer voicemails (verbatim phrasing patterns):**
- "What if I tip over?"
- "My daughter's nine, is she going to be okay?"
- "It looks windy tomorrow, do we get a refund?"
- "We've never done this before, is it too hard?"

**B-roll priority list** (build a shot list for the next shoot):
1. Drone shot pulling back from sea caves with a kayak entering an arch
2. David at the launch, kayaks in background, talking to camera
3. Kid (7–12) paddling in front of tandem with parent in back, both grinning
4. Sit-on-top kayak being held up to camera, David tapping it
5. GoPro POV entering a sea cave, daylight in the cave
6. Wide group shot of 5–6 kayaks moving along the bluffs
7. Happy family photo on the beach after a tour
8. Phone screen showing the booking page (or recreate)

**Music:** Light acoustic / pop-folk underbed. Not "epic adventure" trailer music. This is a calm-water tour, not whitewater rafting. The music should reinforce "you'll be fine," not "this will challenge you."

**On-camera direction for David:** Don't memorize line-for-line. Read it once for cadence, then say it back in your own words. The audience trusts the guy who sounds like he's actually saying this for the first time, not reading off a teleprompter. Your voice is the asset.

**Avoid in delivery:**
- Over-enunciating
- "Hi guys"
- Looking past the camera
- Smiling through the fear-naming section (let those questions land)

**The CTA matters most.** Test "Book today" vs "Spots fill the night before — book now" vs "Text us at [number] if you have questions." The voicemail data says half the audience would text rather than commit on a booking page; making "text us" a primary CTA might convert better than "book now."
