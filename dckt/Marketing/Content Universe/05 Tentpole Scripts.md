# The Quiet Bottom

## Door County Kayak Tours Content Universe, Tentpole Scripts 05

This file is the production bible for the four tentpole event episodes named in World Bible 01 and the canon brief. These are the big swings of Season 1. Everything here obeys the canon. If a beat in here contradicts the World Bible, the World Bible wins and this file is wrong.

House style is strict and non-negotiable. No em dashes anywhere, use commas or colons. No double dashes. Plain ASCII in every caption. Short direct sentences. Every spoken word is captioned on screen. Hook lands on frame 1. Vertical 9:16. Loop ending. A save trigger or a send trigger near the close. We take positions, we do not hedge.

The four tentpoles, in canon order:

1. Why They Call It Death's Door (lore and dread)
2. The Cold Water Initiation (warm comedy turning tender)
3. The Loudest Quiet Morning (pure heart, the emotional center)
4. Birgit Finally Moves (the finale, every device cashes out)

Ansel is the only narrator. Real footage where we can get it, AI for the talking animals and anything we cannot legally or safely shoot. Every AI beat carries a paste-ready Seedance 2.0 prompt block for Higgsfield. Voice is captured voice-agnostic so any VO talent or synth can read it.

A note on the founding-year contradiction in the vault: no founding year goes on camera in any of these four. We use guide hours, group size, geology age, and the 1888 shipwreck date as our Bencivenga proof instead. Those are confirmed and they hit harder anyway.

A note on the sturgeon: Ansel is folklore. Real wildlife only on screen. We never imply a guest will see a sturgeon. When Ansel appears we show a shadow, a tail, a wake, a slow shape in deep water, never a clear full body.

## How to read a script in this file

Each tentpole has the same eleven sections so a producer, an editor, and a VO reader can all work from one page:

1. Logline and runtime
2. Tone register and the cinematography mode driving it
3. Schwartz awareness stage and the one Blair Warren lever
4. Copy masters and directors anchoring the piece
5. Beat-by-beat shot list (framing, lens, move, light, grade, real vs AI)
6. Voice-agnostic VO and on-screen text, beat by beat

9. Production routing (what we really shoot vs what we generate)

Two matrices close the file: a device matrix proving every canon device has a plant and a payoff, and a connection matrix proving every tentpole links to every other.

# TENTPOLE 1: Why They Call It Death's Door

## 1. Logline and runtime

Ansel tells the real story of the strait that swallowed more than 200 ships, while the camera drifts over the Fleetwing schooner resting in twelve feet of clear water. He admits, almost as an aside, that he watched it settle. Runtime: 31 seconds. This is the save-and-send monster, the longest of the four because narrative buys the retention.

## 2. Tone register and cinematography mode

Lore and dread. Maritime Gothic, mode 3: Robert Eggers in The Lighthouse, and Spielberg's Jaws inverted. The menace here is not a shark. It is the cold water and the name. Slow, low, patient. The water is the antagonist and it never raises its voice. We borrow Deakins for the fog and the scale, the strait as a cathedral nobody asked to enter.

## 3. Schwartz stage and Blair Warren lever

Schwartz awareness stage: Problem Aware moving to Solution Aware. The viewer knows Door County is pretty. They do not know it is haunted in the specific, factual, 1888 way we are about to show them. We move them from "nice lake" to "I need to see this with someone who knows the story."

Blair Warren lever: Confirm their suspicions. People half-sense that the pretty tourist places have a darker history underneath. We confirm it with a real date and a real wreck, and we put the audience on the side of the people who know.

## 4. Copy masters and directors anchoring it

Copy master: Gary Bencivenga for proof, and Gene Schwartz for the awareness ladder. Every dread line is nailed to a verifiable fact so the viewer cannot say "yeah, right." The schooner is 136 feet. It ran aground September 26, 1888. The strait is the only direct route between Lake Michigan and Green Bay. More than 200 wrecks over roughly 200 years. Real numbers carry the goosebumps.

Directors: Robert Eggers (dread, period weight, patience) and Roger Deakins (fog, scale, the cold light on water).

## 7. SFX and music

No music for the first 11 seconds. Just water, a low underwater hum, a single distant creak of wood. Music enters at beat D, a single sustained cello note, very low, Eggers-style, no melody. It does not resolve. At beat F the music cuts to silence on the paddle lift, the loudest moment is the absence. Beat G brings back only water and the low hum, looping clean to frame 1. Use real ambient wave and paddle sound on the live footage beats wherever possible.

## 8. Rewatch devices planted and paid off

- Death's Door, the Name: this episode is the primary payoff. The name is dropped as mystery in early lore clips, and here the full weight lands over the real Fleetwing. Ansel's "I watched her settle" is the canon line.
- The Glass-Water Cold Open: planted across the season's opener, echoed here in beat B's glassy fog strait, so the look rhymes.
- The Engine Before The Boat: planted by absence. There is no engine in this entire episode, on purpose. Garrett Bay has no power boats, which is a real fact and a quiet setup for Act 3's silence payoff.

## 9. Production routing

Real: the kayak POV over the Fleetwing in clear shallow water (beat C), the paddle-blade lift (beat F), drone B-roll of the strait as cutaway (beat B). These are real because the wreck in twelve feet of clear water is the single most credible asset we own. Do not fake what we can actually shoot.

AI: the underwater up-look hull silhouettes (beats A and G), the fog-scale strait establishing shot (beat B primary), Ansel's unresolved dark shape (beat D), and the painterly wreck-timber depth shots (beat E). These are AI because we cannot safely or legally dive-shoot the wreck at depth, and Ansel is folklore.

Safety and legal note: never imply guests dive on the wreck. The tour paddles over it. Min age 4 on this tour, calmer than Cave Point, history-focused, that is the real product.

# TENTPOLE 2: The Cold Water Initiation

## 1. Logline and runtime

Every summer the lake greets newcomers with a gasp, because it is genuinely that cold, and Ansel explains the gasp is the price of admission to the only sea caves on Wisconsin's Lake Michigan coast. The Hagens gasp. Then we cut to the quiet payoff. Runtime: 18 seconds.

## 2. Tone register and cinematography mode

Warm comedy turning tender. Two modes braided: Deadpan Comedy (mode 2, Wes Anderson and The Life Aquatic, symmetry and a chapter card) for the gasp, then Beauty (mode 1, Malick and Lubezki) for the turn. The joke earns the heart.

## 3. Schwartz stage and Blair Warren lever

Schwartz awareness stage: Most Aware to Product Aware. The viewer already pictures kayaking. Our job is to reframe the one thing that scares them, the cold, as the entry fee to something only we can give them. We name the product and the place plainly.

Blair Warren lever: Allay their fears. The cold water is the fear. We do not pretend it is warm. We tell them the gasp is normal, everybody does it, and then it is over and you are somewhere nobody else can reach. Honesty about the cold is the trust.

## 4. Copy masters and directors anchoring it

Copy master: Joseph Sugarman for the slippery slide (short opener, each line pulling the next) and Bencivenga for proof (the only sea caves on Wisconsin's Lake Michigan coast, 420-million-year-old dolomite, 40 to 60 foot cliffs). The proof makes the payoff feel earned rather than sold.

Directors: Wes Anderson (the symmetrical gasp gag, a clean chapter card) and Terrence Malick with Emmanuel Lubezki (the wide, golden, hand-of-god turn into the cave).

## 8. Rewatch devices planted and paid off

- Tollef's One Good Look is gently teed up: if we want, a cormorant sits on the cliff in beat D drying its wings, a quiet plant of Tollef's world without him speaking.

## 9. Production routing

This is our strongest real-footage tentpole and it should feel like genuinely captured tour footage, because that is the credibility. Use actual guest footage or a staged real paddle, not AI.

AI: only if we cannot get a clean cave-interior light shaft on the shoot day, generate a backup cave-mouth beat. Prefer real. The whole point of this one is that it is true.

Brand safety: the kid is a minor, use a hired family or get signed releases, never use unconsented guest footage of children. Group size stays small on camera, that is real and on-brand.

3. Loads two devices that cash out in Birgit Finally Moves: the cold-water gasp (kid does not gasp) and the wrong shoes (kid ends up barefoot like a local). This episode is where the finale's payoffs get their plants.

# TENTPOLE 3: The Loudest Quiet Morning

## 1. Logline and runtime

On one glass-calm dawn the engines never start. For the length of a single paddle stroke the whole peninsula holds still, and even Tollef the motormouth cormorant shuts up. Ansel barely speaks. The scenery carries it. Runtime: 19 seconds. This is the one people send to someone they love.

## 2. Tone register and cinematography mode

Pure heart, almost no jokes. Beauty mode (mode 1, Malick and Lubezki) for the landscape, with one breath of Deadpan Comedy mode for Tollef going quiet. The emotional center of the season. Less is the whole strategy.

## 3. Schwartz stage and Blair Warren lever

Schwartz awareness stage: Most Aware, and beyond. This is not a sell. It is a feeling clip for people who already get it, designed to be sent, which is the strongest distribution signal we have. We are not converting a skeptic here, we are arming a believer to share.

Blair Warren lever: Encourage their dreams. Everyone watching wants this exact morning, the glass water, the quiet, the person beside them. We show them the dream so cleanly they have to send it to the person they would want there.

## 4. Copy masters and directors anchoring it

Copy master: Eugene Schwartz on restraint (when the market is most aware, get out of the way) and David Ogilvy on respecting the viewer's intelligence (no hard sell, the image is the argument). The fewest words of any tentpole, on purpose.

Directors: Terrence Malick and Emmanuel Lubezki (the dawn, the held breath, the natural-light reverence), with a single Wes Anderson beat for Tollef's comic silence so the heart does not curdle into syrup.

# TENTPOLE 4: Birgit Finally Moves

## 1. Logline and runtime

The eagle who has not moved all season turns her head once. Ansel admits, only to the water, that he was rooting for the quiet ones the whole time. Every device cashes out. The clip closes on the exact opening glass-water shot and the loop snaps shut. Runtime: 24 seconds. The finale.

## 2. Tone register and cinematography mode

Payoff and quiet triumph. Three modes converge: Beauty (Malick and Lubezki) for the season's closing light, The Standoff and Reveal (mode 5, Sergio Leone) for Birgit's head turn (extreme close on the eye, slow zoom), and one last Deadpan Comedy beat for Tollef. This is the only place all season we spend Birgit's head turn. Spend it nowhere else, ever.

## 3. Schwartz stage and Blair Warren lever

Schwartz awareness stage: Most Aware, season-payoff register. By the finale the audience is fully inside the world. This is not acquisition, it is the emotional close that makes the whole season worth rewatching and worth following for Season 2.

Blair Warren lever: Confirm their suspicions and quietly throw a rock at the enemy. The audience suspected all along that the quiet way was the right way and that Ansel secretly cared. We confirm both. The rock at the enemy is gentle, the loud way already left, it missed everything, and we let the silence say so without naming a competitor.
