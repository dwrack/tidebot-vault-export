# Storyboard 08, First Tentpole to Produce

## "Why They Call It Death's Door" (Ansel over the Fleetwing)

This is a shot-ready production bible for the very first tentpole we shoot and cut. One video, frame by frame. Everything below obeys World Bible 01 and the canon brief. If a shot here breaks a canon rule, the shot is wrong, not the rule.

---

## Why this one goes first

Of the four tentpoles, this is the most self-contained. Here is the reasoning, plainly.

- **Birgit Finally Moves** is the finale. It cashes out seven planted devices from across a whole season. You cannot shoot the payoff before you have planted anything. Disqualified for first.
- **The Loudest Quiet Morning** is the emotional center of Act 2. It leans on the Hagens and Tollef already being established. It needs the season's setup to land. Not first.
- **The Cold Water Initiation** is good and fairly standalone, but its punch (the kid does not gasp in Act 3) depends on the Act 1 gasp being planted first. The full joke needs two episodes.
- **Why They Call It Death's Door** needs nothing before it. One location (Garrett Bay, protected water, not open lake), one wreck (the Fleetwing), one narrator (Ansel), one real date (September 26, 1888). It plants the "Death's Door, the Name" device for later but does not depend on any prior plant. It is also the explicit save-and-send monster in the canon, which is exactly the signal we want to prove the format on. Lore carries retention, so we get to run long enough (30s) to teach the algorithm the account does story, not just scenery.

Decision: shoot this first.

---

## The spec sheet

- **Target runtime:** 30.0 seconds (lore zone is 21 to 34s; 30 gives the story room and still clears the completion bar).
- **Aspect:** vertical 9:16, 1080x1920, 30fps minimum, shoot 60fps where we want slow-mo headroom.
- **Hook:** lands on frame 1. The wreck and the words "Death's Door" are both on screen inside 2 seconds.
- **Captions:** every spoken word, white text, black outline, Montserrat or Helvetica, 3 to 7 words per line, active word highlighted in a warm amber (#E8B04B) so it reads against cold blue water.
- **Loop:** the last shot rhymes with the first (glass water, the schooner's shadow), so it auto-replays clean.
- **Save and send triggers:** save trigger at ~24s, send trigger at ~27s, both as on-screen text, neither spoken (Ansel would never tell you to share something, that is the loud way).
- **Audio bed:** real Garrett Bay ambient (wind, hull, a single distant gull) under a low sustained cello drone. No trending pop sound on this one. Lore posts carry on narration plus ambient. Original audio also earns the 40 to 60 percent distribution bump.
- **Primary search term:** "Death's Door Wisconsin" / "Door County shipwrecks." In on-screen text by 0:02, in caption line 1.

### Strategy tags (one of each, per the brief)

- **Blair Warren lever:** Confirm their suspicions. Everyone half-suspects the pretty tourist spots have a darker history underneath. Ansel confirms it. We are not selling fear, we are confirming a feeling they already had looking at that cold water.
- **Schwartz awareness stage:** Problem-aware sliding to Solution-aware. The viewer knows Door County, maybe knows the name "Death's Door" sounds ominous, does not know why. We name the problem (the strait killed people), then reveal that the quiet way (paddle over it, slow, with someone who knows the story) is how you actually meet it.
- **Bencivenga proof:** Real, falsifiable facts do the persuading. 136-foot schooner. Ran aground September 26, 1888. Rests in about 12 feet of clear water. The strait is the only direct route between Lake Michigan and Green Bay. 200-plus shipwrecks over roughly 200 years. None of it invented. The real numbers are scarier than anything we could make up.

### Real footage vs AI, per beat

- **AI (talking animal, underwater perspective):** Ansel's shots. We never show a clean full sturgeon (canon: shadow, tail, wake, voice from below). AI gives us the moody bottom-of-the-lake perspective we cannot safely film.
- **Real footage:** the Fleetwing wreck from above, Garrett Bay surface, the kayak paddling over the wreck, the bald eagle, the bluff. Shoot these on the actual Death's Door Shipwreck tour out of 1310 Garrett Bay Rd. Real wreck footage is the whole credibility of the piece. Do not fake the wreck.

### SHOT 1: The cold open (loop anchor)

- **Source:** REAL footage (drone). This is the master loop frame. Shot 10 must match it.
- **Frame:** Top-down aerial directly over Garrett Bay on a dead-calm dawn. Water is glass. The Fleetwing reads as a faint dark shape under the surface, not yet clear. Hook is the contrast: a beautiful, still, almost holy frame, captioned with the words DEATH'S DOOR.
- **Lens / angle:** Drone, 24mm equivalent, nadir (straight down). Altitude ~40m, descending slowly.
- **Move:** Slow push-in / descent. The wreck shadow grows very slightly more defined over three seconds. Never resolves fully here, we save the reveal.
- **Light / grade:** Deakins for fog and scale. Cold blue-steel grade, lifted blacks, a thin band of warm dawn on the far shore so the cold reads as a choice. Highlight roll-off soft.
- **Audio:** Low sustained cello drone fades up from silence. One hull creak (foley) at 0:02. No VO yet, let the image and the title card do the work.
- **On-screen text:** DEATH'S DOOR, WISCONSIN. Centered, lower third, holds full three seconds. This is the SEO term landing inside 2 seconds, as required.
- **VO:** none.
- **Editing note:** Frame 1 must be the strongest single frame in the cut. No fade-in from black, open on the image already there. Cut hard to shot 2 on the cello's first swell.
- **Seedance prompt:** n/a (real drone footage). If a plate is unavailable, see AI fallback in the asset checklist.

### SHOT 2: The wreck, named

- **Source:** REAL footage (underwater) preferred. AI fallback prompt provided.
- **Frame:** Underwater wide of the Fleetwing hull resting on the bottom in about 12 feet of clear Garrett Bay water. Green-blue light, particulate in the beam. The schooner's keel runs diagonally across frame.
- **Lens / angle:** Wide, ~16-20mm, low angle looking slightly up along the hull so the wreck has mass.
- **Move:** Slow drift along the keel, left to right.
- **Light / grade:** Maritime Gothic. Eggers' Lighthouse coldness, the menace is the water, not a creature. Desaturated, green cast, a single shaft of surface light. The wreck should feel like it is sleeping, not staged.
- **Audio:** Ansel VO begins, dry and unhurried. Muffled underwater ambient under it.
- **On-screen text:** caption of the spoken line, active word in amber.
- **VO (Ansel):** "They named this strait"
- **Editing note:** This is the first time we hear him. Keep it flat. He is not narrating a horror movie, he is telling you something he has known for a hundred years. Let the line run slightly under the image.
- **Seedance prompt (AI fallback only):**
```
Vertical 9:16, cinematic underwater wide shot of an old wooden shipwreck, a 136-foot schooner hull resting on a silty lake bottom in roughly 12 feet of clear cold green-blue freshwater, diagonal keel across frame, soft shafts of dawn light from the surface, fine particulate drifting in the beams, no fish in frame yet, desaturated Robert Eggers maritime palette, slow lateral drift left to right, eerie still calm, Great Lakes cold water, photoreal, 30fps, no text.
```

### SHOT 3: Ansel passes (the Leone creep)

- **Source:** AI (Ansel shot). Canon: never a clean full sturgeon.
- **Frame:** Extreme close, mostly darkness. A single ancient eye, or the slow shadow of a long tail, passes between camera and the wreck. We feel something old is down here, watching, we never get a clean look.
- **Lens / angle:** Macro to the eye, or a long lens flattening the shadow. Sergio Leone standoff framing applied to a fish: the eye fills frame, the zoom is slow and patient.
- **Move:** Slow lateral drift plus the faintest push toward the eye. Leone creep.
- **Light / grade:** Near black with one catchlight in the eye. Cold. The catchlight is the whole shot.
- **Audio:** Ansel VO continues. One low cello note under it, nothing else.
- **On-screen text:** caption, active word amber.
- **VO (Ansel):** "after the people it kept."
- **Editing note:** "kept" is the gut-punch word. Hold the eye on "kept," then cut. This is the line that confirms the suspicion (Blair Warren). Do not oversell it, the flatness is what sells it.
- **Seedance prompt:**
```
Vertical 9:16, extreme close-up underwater, the single ancient eye and dark shadowed flank of a very old lake sturgeon passing slowly between camera and a faint shipwreck in the murky background, mostly black frame, one cold catchlight in the eye, we never see the whole fish, only shadow tail and eye, Sergio Leone slow patient creep, ominous and still, deep cold Great Lakes water, photoreal, moody, 30fps, no text.
```

### SHOT 4: 1888, the storm (real wreck from above)

- **Source:** REAL footage (drone or shallow top-down over the wreck).
- **Frame:** Top-down on the surface, a single kayak paddling over the wreck line. The wreck is visible under the boat. This is the proof frame: a real person, a real wreck, real clear water.
- **Lens / angle:** Drone nadir, ~25m, or a high shore-side long lens if drone wind is bad.
- **Move:** Locked frame, the kayak crosses through.
- **Light / grade:** Slightly warmer than the underwater beats, we are back on the surface with the living. Still cold blue water, but daylight.
- **Audio:** Paddle dip, real ambient wind. VO rests here, let the fact card breathe.
- **On-screen text:** 1888. A storm. (Two short beats, big type. Bencivenga proof, the real date.)
- **VO:** none (the card carries it).
- **Editing note:** Pacing reset. After two heavy underwater beats, this clean surface shot lets the viewer breathe and trusts them to read the date. Slippery-slide: the open earned this line, this line earns the next.
- **Seedance prompt:** n/a (real footage). AI fallback in checklist if needed.

### SHOT 6: Still down there

- **Source:** REAL footage (underwater detail) or AI fallback.
- **Frame:** Underwater detail of the Fleetwing's wood ribs, a few real fish (perch, smallmouth, not invented species) moving through the frames of the hull. Life inside the wreck.
- **Lens / angle:** ~24mm, slow truck right along the ribs.
- **Move:** Slow truck right.
- **Light / grade:** Same cold Gothic palette as shot 2 for continuity.
- **Audio:** Ansel VO.
- **On-screen text:** caption.
- **VO (Ansel):** "still down there. 12 feet."
- **Editing note:** "12 feet" is the access promise hiding inside a lore line. It is shallow. It is close. You could paddle over it tomorrow. We never say that out loud yet, the number plants it.

### SHOT 7: Birgit's stillness (the dread holds)

- **Source:** REAL footage (bald eagle at Garrett Bay).
- **Frame:** Low wide of a real bald eagle on a weathered snag over the bay. Dead still. In canon this reads as Birgit: the fixed star, the judge. She does not move (her one head turn is saved for the finale, spend it nowhere else).
- **Lens / angle:** ~200mm from a distance, low, eagle small-ish in frame against big sky and cold water.
- **Move:** Locked. Zero camera move, zero subject move. The stillness is the content.
- **Light / grade:** Cold, wide, Deakins scale. Let the bird be small against the strait.
- **Audio:** Wind drops almost to silence. This is the quietest beat. The near-silence is the loudest thing (plants the Act 3 device tonally).
- **On-screen text:** 200+ wrecks. 200 years. (Bencivenga proof, the scale of the strait.)
- **VO:** none.
- **Editing note:** Resist the urge to cut fast here. Three full seconds of a still eagle and a real number is a pattern interrupt in a fast feed. It says: this account is not afraid of quiet. That IS the brand.
- **Seedance prompt:** n/a (real eagle footage, required, do not fake wildlife).

### SHOT 8: The loud way roars past

- **Source:** REAL footage (POV paddle over the wreck).
- **Frame:** First-person POV, hands on the paddle, bow of the kayak, gliding directly over the wreck line. The viewer is now on the water, doing the quiet thing.
- **Lens / angle:** Chest-mounted or bow-mounted action cam, ~16mm wide, low to the water.
- **Move:** Forward glide, smooth.
- **Light / grade:** Natural, cold, slightly warmed. We want this to feel like the good way to be here.
- **Audio:** Ansel VO. Real paddle and water.
- **On-screen text:** caption.
- **VO (Ansel):** "The loud way roars past it."
- **Editing note:** This is the moral axis landing. The villain (The Wake) is named only by what he does, never shown, never branded (canon: faceless). We do not show a jet ski here. We show the quiet POV and let Ansel name the loud way in absentia. The contrast is the whole point.
- **Seedance prompt:** n/a (real POV). AI fallback:
```
Vertical 9:16, first-person POV kayaking, hands gripping a paddle, bow of a sit-on-top kayak gliding forward over clear cold blue-green lake water with a faint shipwreck visible below the surface, low to the water, calm and quiet, cold Great Lakes morning light, photoreal action-cam feel, smooth forward motion, 30fps, no text.
```

### SHOT 9: The stop (save trigger)

- **Source:** REAL footage (wide, kayak at rest over the wreck).
- **Frame:** Wide calm shot, the kayak drifts to a stop directly over the wreck, paddle still, the paddler just looking down. The reward of the quiet way: you get to stop and actually see it.
- **Lens / angle:** ~35mm from shore or a second boat, kayak small in a big calm frame.
- **Move:** Slow drift to rest, then nothing.
- **Light / grade:** The warmest of the cold grade. We are paying off the dread with stillness.
- **Audio:** Ansel VO, low ambient.
- **On-screen text:** Save this for Door County (save trigger, canon-mandated, near the close).
- **VO (Ansel):** "You can only meet it slow."
- **Editing note:** "You can only meet it slow" is the thesis of the entire universe in five words (quiet is the price of admission). The save trigger sits in the corner, not over Ansel's caption. Two text elements, never overlapping.
- **Seedance prompt:** n/a (real footage).

### SHOT 10: Loop close (send trigger)

- **Source:** REAL footage (the identical drone frame as shot 1).
- **Frame:** Top-down aerial, glass water, the faint wreck shadow. Identical composition to shot 1 so the video loops cleanly into its own open.
- **Lens / angle:** Drone nadir, matches shot 1 exactly. Same altitude, same framing.
- **Move:** Slow rise (the inverse of shot 1's descent), so the motion hands off cleanly to the loop.
- **Light / grade:** Identical to shot 1.
- **Audio:** Cello drone resolves to the same note it opened on. One last hull creak. This is what makes the loop feel inevitable, not accidental.
- **On-screen text:** Send to who'd paddle it (send trigger, canon-mandated). The send trigger is the single most powerful distribution signal we have, so it gets the last frame.
- **VO:** none. Ansel does not sign off. The loud way needs the last word, Ansel never does.
- **Editing note:** The cut from shot 10 back to shot 1 must be invisible on auto-replay. Match the frame, match the drone speed, match the grade. If the loop is clean, completion and replay rate (the heaviest TikTok and Shorts signals) climb on their own.
- **Seedance prompt:** n/a (real footage, must match shot 1 plate).

Read flat. Old fishing-shack deadpan. He is not trying to scare you, he is just telling you what is true. Total spoken time should land near 9 to 11 seconds across 30, the rest is image and ambient.

3. (shot 5) A 136-foot schooner
4. (shot 6) still down there. 12 feet.
