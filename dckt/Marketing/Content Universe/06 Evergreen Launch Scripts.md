# The Quiet Bottom

## Door County Kayak Tours Content Universe, Production Bible 06: Evergreen Launch Scripts

These six clips launch the account. They are the always-on, search-driven, non-arc videos from the World Bible slate. They run BEFORE the four tentpoles and before the Season 1 arc, because their job is to introduce the cast, plant the devices, and bank a library of search-indexed clips that keep working forever. By the time the arc episodes drop, the audience already knows Ansel's voice, has met Tollef and Birgit and the Hagens, has heard the engine over the horizon, and has the words Cave Point, Death's Door, and sea caves stuck in their head. The arc then pays off everything these six planted.

House style is strict and non-negotiable. No em dashes anywhere, use commas or colons. No double dashes. Plain ASCII in every caption. Short direct sentences. Every spoken word is captioned on screen. Hook lands on frame 1. Vertical 9:16. The primary search term is in the on-screen text inside the first 2 seconds and in the first line of the caption. End on a loop, plus a save or send trigger near the close.

Ansel is the only narrator that frames a video. Other characters speak inside a scene, Ansel holds the world together. We never put an unconfirmed fact on camera: no founding year, no ACA claim, no review count, no promise you will see a sturgeon. Real wildlife only on screen. Villain stays faceless.

Every line below was run through the three-test filter: can I visualize it, can I falsify it, could nobody else say it. Kill-list words (unlock, unleash, seamless, elevate, leverage, robust, holistic, utilize) are purged. Each script names its Blair Warren lever and its Schwartz awareness stage, because the launch slate spans a cold audience that does not know us yet, so awareness stage is the whole game here.

## How to read a script in this bible

Each script block has: title, length, mode, role in launch, the device or cast it plants, awareness stage, Blair Warren lever, a beat-by-beat table marked REAL footage or AI per beat, the full VO, every on-screen text card, sound design, the caption, the paste-ready Seedance prompt for any AI beat, and the proof facts used. The five cinematography modes are the World Bible house modes:

- BEAUTY: Malick and Lubezki for natural-light grace, Deakins for fog and scale, the lake as a cathedral.
- DEADPAN COMEDY: Wes Anderson symmetry and chapter cards, The Life Aquatic is literally nautical.
- MARITIME GOTHIC: Robert Eggers, The Lighthouse, plus Jaws inverted, the menace is the cold water and Death's Door, not a shark.
- MOCKUMENTARY DRY REALISM: Nathan Fielder and Taika Waititi, guides and creatures to camera.
- THE STANDOFF REVEAL: Sergio Leone, extreme-close eyes, slow zoom, saved for Ansel the narrator creature.

Real guide and tour footage carries wherever possible. AI carries the talking animals and any shot we cannot safely or legally get in the cold open lake. Mark every beat.

## Launch Order and Cadence Note

Run all six BEFORE any arc episode or tentpole. The launch window is roughly the first three to four weeks of posting, two to three clips a week. Order is deliberate, it warms a cold dormant account from zero, banks search-indexed evergreen first, then escalates into lore and positioning, then hands off to the Season 1 arc.

**Recommended order and why:**

1. **Clip 1, POV: Paddling Into the Cave Point Sea Caves.** Lead with the strongest pure-visual thesis. Lowest barrier, highest shareability, sets the brand's whole promise in 14 seconds. First impression of Ansel's voice, soft.
2. **Clip 2, Caribbean or Door County?** A known winner. Drop it second to ride the reach off Clip 1 and bank an early viral hit while the account has momentum. Question hook in the first three words, proven pattern.
3. **Clip 5, The Blowhole Money Shot.** Third, because by now we need a replay-rate machine to juice the algorithm's completion signal and keep distribution climbing. Quick dopamine, plants Tollef quietly.
4. **Clip 3, The Best Time to Kayak Cave Point.** Fourth, once we have reach, convert it to saves and bookings. This is the trip-planner that lives in saved folders. Introduces a real guide for trust.
5. **Clip 4, What Nobody Tells You About Death's Door.** Fifth, the lore monster. By now the audience trusts the account enough to sit for 28 seconds. Plants the Death's Door name and the dread the first tentpole pays off. Heaviest save-and-send driver of the slate.
6. **Clip 6, Sea Caves You Can Only Reach by Kayak.** Sixth and last of the launch, the positioning and full cast introduction. Plants the Wrong Shoes and the moral axis, throws the rock at the loud way, and hands cleanly into Act 1 of the arc, which opens on the same quiet-water world this clip closes on.

**Cadence specifics:**

- Two to three posts per week through the launch window, never daily, the account is dormant and a sudden daily blast looks like a bot to the algorithm and to people.
- Best windows for this niche: early morning local time (trip-planners scroll at breakfast) and Sunday evening (people plan the week's getaway). Lore clips (Clip 4) do better at night.
- Pair each clip with a fresh trending sound only if it is inside the 24 to 48 hour rising window, otherwise run the real ambient audio. Lore clips (Clip 4) always run original ambient, never a trend.
- Watch Clip 2 and Clip 5 for the first breakout. If one pops, post the next clip within 24 hours to catch the spillover audience while reach is hot.
- Do not post a tentpole until at least four of these six have run and the account has a baseline. The tentpoles assume the viewer already knows the cast and the words Cave Point, Death's Door, sea caves. These six are what teach them.
- After the six launch clips, hold a few days, then open Season 1 Act 1 with the Glass-Water Cold Open. The handoff from Clip 6's quiet cave to the arc's empty dawn water should feel like the same world exhaling.

**Standing reminders for every clip above:** Hook on frame 1. Primary search term on screen inside 2 seconds and in the first caption line. Every spoken word captioned, white text, black outline, 3 to 7 words a line, active word highlighted. Loop ending on unfinished motion. Save or send trigger near the close. No em dashes, no double dashes, plain ASCII captions. No founding year, no ACA claim, no review count, no sturgeon-sighting promise on camera. Villain stays faceless. Real footage carries wherever we can shoot it, AI only for the talking animals and shots we cannot safely get.
