# The Viral Playbook

## Door County Kayak Tours Content Universe, Production Bible 04

This file is the rulebook the content obeys. World Bible 01 says what the world is. This file says how a clip survives contact with the 2026 algorithm. If a script is beautiful and breaks a rule in here, the clip dies in the feed and the beauty never gets seen. So the rules come first.

House style is strict and non-negotiable. No em dashes anywhere, use commas or colons. No double dashes. Plain ASCII in every caption, no curly quotes, no ellipsis character, no symbols the caption renderer will choke on. Short direct sentences. Every spoken word is captioned on screen. Hook lands on frame 1. Vertical 9:16. Loop ending. A save trigger or a send trigger near the close. We take positions, we do not hedge.

One thing to hold in your head the whole time you read this: @admirenola is a small, basically dormant account. 489 followers, dark since late 2025, traffic was 100 percent Search and 0 percent For You. That is not a failure, that is a clean slate. But it means we play this like a new account, not a big one. Account safety beats reach for the first month. A throttled account posts to nobody no matter how good the clip is.

## Part 1: The Numbers That Actually Move Distribution

### Length, the money zone

8 to 30 seconds is where this niche wins. That is the whole game for most clips. Inside that band:

- **Scenery, POV, before-after, money-shot clips: 8 to 19 seconds.** Short on purpose. The job of these is completion rate above 80 to 90 percent, because completion and replay are the heaviest signals TikTok and Shorts weight. A glass-water reveal does not need 30 seconds. It needs you to watch the whole thing and loop.
- **Lore and story clips (Death's Door, the Fleetwing, sea-cave history): 21 to 34 seconds.** Narrative buys you the extra seconds. Ansel's voice carries retention the way scenery cannot. But only if the hook holds, and only if the story actually goes somewhere by second 5.

Hard rule: never get near the 60-second cap. Completion craters as length climbs and the algorithm reads low completion as a weak clip and chokes the distribution. A 90 percent watch-through on a 20-second clip beats a 30 percent watch-through on a 60-second clip every single time. We are optimizing the percentage, not the runtime.

Per platform, same idea, slightly different dials:

| Platform | Scenery / POV | Lore / story | Weighted hardest by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TikTok | 15 to 30s | 21 to 34s | Completion rate |
| Reels | 8 to 21s | up to ~30s | Sends, then saves, then watch time |
| YouTube Shorts | 20 to 35s | 20 to 35s | Replay / loop strength |

### Hook timing, the first second is the whole audition

Land the hook at or before 1 second. Outer bound is 1.3 seconds on TikTok, 2.1 seconds on Reels, and you should treat those as the edge of a cliff, not a target.

Frame 1 is the payoff teaser, never a logo, never a slow drone push-in, never a title card that fades. The viewer decides to stay or swipe before they finish reading the first line, so the first line has to be the most interesting thing in the clip. Our two best-ever TikToks both led with a claim or a question paired with Cave Point water: "#1 Door County Activity" and "Caribbean or Door County?" Lead the first three words of on-screen text with a claim or a question. Not a montage. Not a vibe. A claim or a question.

### The signals, ranked by how hard they push reach

This is the order to engineer for. Build the clip to manufacture the top two and the rest follow.

1. **Sends / DM shares.** The single strongest distribution signal on Reels for reaching people who do not follow you yet, weighted roughly 3 to 5 times a like. A send is one human telling another human "you have to see this." Engineer the moment that makes someone do that. "Send this to the person you'd paddle with" is not a tagline, it is a distribution mechanic.
2. **Saves.** Weighted roughly 3 times a like. Signals the clip has lasting, planning value. This is the niche's secret weapon, because travel content is inherently save-bait. Anyone planning a Door County trip saves the how-to, the best-time, the fall-color-timing clip. Trip-planning content gets saved harder than anything else we can make.
3. **Completion / replay rate.** Heaviest on TikTok and Shorts. Short length plus a loop ending (end on unfinished motion or return to the opening shot) farms auto-replays, and an auto-replay reads as a completion plus.
4. **Watch time.** One of Mosseri's three confirmed Reels signals. Punchy short clips win this on percentage, not on raw seconds.
5. **Likes per reach.** Third confirmed Mosseri signal, lowest weight of the three, but still counts as early-engagement velocity in the first hour.

Two more that are not engagement but quietly decide your ceiling:

- **Original content gets 40 to 60 percent more distribution than reposts.** Always post original guide and tour footage. This is free reach we already own.
- **Accounts with 10-plus reposts in 30 days get cut from recommendations entirely.** So do not repost other people's clips to fill the calendar. Ever. One bad month of reposting can shadow-ban the account out of For You.

### The trending-audio lifecycle, brutally short in 2026

A trending sound peaks in about 72 hours and is functionally dead in about 5 days. The window is tiny.

- Posting within 24 to 48 hours of a sound rising gets 3 to 5 times more algorithmic push than using that same sound at peak or saturation. Early is everything. Using a sound everybody already used is worse than using original audio.
- Trending audio alone can add up to about 48 percent more reach and about 68 percent more views versus original sound, when you catch it early.
- Cross-platform arbitrage: Reels trends usually lag TikTok by 3 to 7 days. A sound blowing up on TikTok with under roughly 5,000 Reels uses is a head-start window for @admirenola on Instagram. Watch TikTok, post the Reel before the sound crosses over.
- The exception, and it matters here: on lore clips where Ansel's narration and real ambient sound (the blowhole spray, the paddle in glass water) carry the emotional weight, do not bury that under a trending pop song. Mix real ambient under the voice. Save trending audio for scenery, POV, and before-after clips where there is no narration to protect.

## Part 2: The Winning Formats For This Niche

These are the shapes that work. Each one maps to a signal it is built to farm. Mark every beat real footage or AI when you build the shot list (see Production Bible on shot lists). Real guide and tour footage wherever possible, AI only for the talking animals.

1. **POV: paddling into the sea cave.** First-person GoPro, the water reveal on frame 1, 8 to 15 seconds, trending audio. Farms completion. This is the most repeatable format we have and the launch slate leads with it.
2. **The blowhole money shot.** The dolomite blowholes spray 15 to 30 feet on the right swell. That is a natural "wait for it" loop, the spray is the payoff and the buildup is the retention. Farms replay rate. End on the spray, cut back to the calm before it, loop.
3. **Lore / mystery narration.** "Why they call it Death's Door," shipwrecks under the surface. Start at the climax, plant a mid-clip cliffhanger, Ansel voiceover plus captions plus real ambient, 21 to 34 seconds. Farms saves and sends. This is the save-and-send monster of the whole account.
4. **Trip-planning list.** "Best time to kayak Cave Point and what nobody tells you." Explicit save-bait, list format, 5 to 7 word caption chunks on screen. Farms saves. Travel planners save these and come back to them in March when they book.
5. **Before / after, expectation vs reality.** The rocky launch warning cut against the Caribbean-blue payoff. Or the calm-morning glass water vs the choppy afternoon. Doubles as a quiet safety and credibility signal, which answers a booking objection without sounding like it is answering one.
6. **Fall-color drone reveal.** Over the bluffs and dunes, post late September into October. Leans on searchable "things to do in Door County in fall" demand, which spikes seasonally. Farms saves from people planning a fall trip.
7. **Guide-credibility / safety POV.** "What your guide watches for on this water." Builds trust, quietly handles the "is this safe" objection. Safe on-screen language only: local, naturalist-led, CPR and first-aid current. No ACA claim. No founding year.

### Captions, the one mechanic on every single clip

85 percent watch with sound off. So the captions are not an accessibility nicety, they are the primary read. Every clip, no exceptions:

- Word-by-word karaoke captions, active word highlighted in a contrast color.
- White text, black outline, sans-serif (Montserrat or Helvetica).
- 3 to 7 words per line, each line on screen 1 to 3 seconds.
- The primary search term (Cave Point, Door County sea caves, Death's Door) appears in the on-screen text inside the first 2 seconds. Every time.

## Part 3: The Caption Formula

Reels and TikTok are search engines now. The caption is not decoration, it is the index entry. Build it in this order, every time.

**Line 1, the search term plus the hook.** Lead with the primary search term in plain words, fused to the hook. This line gets indexed and it gets read. Example: "Cave Point sea caves are the only ones on Wisconsin's Lake Michigan coast." The term is doing double duty: SEO and curiosity.

**Line 2 to 3, the payoff or the proof.** One concrete fact or one specific claim that makes someone want to save or send. Use real proof, not vibes. 420-million-year-old dolomite. The Fleetwing in 12 feet of clear water. The only sea caves you can reach by kayak. A line that invites a "yeah, right" needs a real fact right behind it.

**Line 4, the trigger.** One save trigger or one send trigger, written like a human, not a marketer. "Save this for your Door County trip." Or "Send this to the person you'd paddle with." Pick one per clip, do not stack both in the caption (you can put the other one in the on-screen text near the close).

**Line 5, hashtags.** 3 to 5 specific ones, never 30. Buried walls of hashtags read as spam and dilute relevance. Put them on their own line at the very bottom, never in the middle of the readable copy.

Two hard caption rules: the search term goes in line 1, not buried under the hashtags, or it does not get indexed where it matters. And plain ASCII only, because curly quotes and Unicode symbols break the caption on some renders and look like a glitch.

### A worked example, the Death's Door lore clip

```
Death's Door, Wisconsin earned its name from the boats that never came back.
A 136-foot schooner called the Fleetwing still rests in 12 feet of clear water,
right where it ran aground in an 1888 storm. You paddle straight over it.
Save this for your Door County trip.

#deathsdoor #doorcounty #shipwreck #kayaking #lakemichigan
```

That caption indexes for "Death's Door Wisconsin," "Door County shipwrecks," and "Lake Michigan kayaking," it carries real proof (136 feet, 12 feet of water, 1888, the Fleetwing by name), it lands a save trigger, and it stays under 5 hashtags. No founding year, no review count, no ACA claim, no promise of a sturgeon. Clean.

Put the primary term in line 1 and in the on-screen text inside 2 seconds. Rotate a couple of secondary terms naturally into lines 2 and 3. Do not stuff. Pick the one or two that actually fit the clip.

The name in the text is not just for the algorithm, it is the thing that turns a viewer into a booking.

## Part 5: The Posting Cadence For A New, Small Account

This is the part people get wrong and it is the part that can kill the account. @admirenola is small and was dormant. We treat it like a brand-new account waking up, because to the algorithm, a long-dark account coming back online basically is one. Reach is the reward. Account safety is the price of admission. We pay the price first.

### The rules, in order of importance

**1. One post per day, maximum. Never a burst.** A dormant account that suddenly dumps 5 clips in an afternoon looks like a bot or a hijacked account, and that is the fastest way to get throttled or flagged. One quality clip a day, every day, is a stronger signal than ten clips on Monday and silence the rest of the week. Consistency reads as a real account. Bursts read as spam.

**2. Ramp in slow, do not launch at full speed.** Week 1, post 3 to 4 clips across the week, not daily. Let the account re-establish a heartbeat. Week 2 onward, move toward the steady 1-per-day rhythm if the clips are ready. Better to post 4 great clips in week 1 than 7 rushed ones. Quality and steadiness both beat volume here.

**3. Original footage only. Zero reposts.** Covered above and it bears repeating as a safety rule: 10-plus reposts in 30 days gets an account cut from recommendations. We are nowhere near that and we never will be, because every clip is original guide and tour footage or our own AI. This also protects the 40 to 60 percent original-content reach bonus.

**4. Hold all commenting and outbound engagement until a few posts are live.** Do not have the account out commenting on other accounts, following 50 people in a day, or DMing, while it has almost no content of its own. A near-empty account doing a lot of outbound looks like exactly the spam pattern the platforms hunt. Get at least 4 to 6 real clips live first. Then, slowly, start replying to comments on our own posts. Outbound commenting on other accounts comes later, and gradually, never in a batch.

**5. No follow/unfollow, no engagement pods, no bought anything.** None of it. One flag on a small account can end it before it ever gets going. We have a clean slate and we are not gambling it for a vanity number.

**6. Post into the window, not at random.** Aim for when the planning audience is actually scrolling: late evening on weeknights and weekend mornings skew well for travel and outdoor content. Pick a couple of consistent slots and hold them. Consistency of timing is itself a positive signal.

### The first 30 days, concretely

- **Days 1 to 7:** Post 3 to 4 clips, lead with the evergreen launch slate (POV sea cave, Caribbean or Door County, best time to kayak Cave Point). No outbound engagement. Reply to comments on our own clips only, and only after a clip has a couple.
- **Days 8 to 21:** Move toward 1 per day if clips are ready, do not force it. Mix the launch slate with the first lore clip (Death's Door). Start replying to all comments on our own posts within a few hours.
- **Days 22 to 30:** Steady 1 per day. Begin light, genuine outbound commenting on relevant Door County and Wisconsin travel accounts, a few a day, real comments, never copy-paste. Watch saves and sends in the analytics and lean the next batch toward whatever format is earning them.

The whole point of the slow ramp: we would rather this account be quietly alive in 90 days than briefly loud and then throttled in week 2. Account safety first, always. The clips are good enough to wait for.

## Part 6: The One-Screen Checklist (pin this)

Before any clip ships, it passes all of these or it does not ship.

- [ ] Hook on frame 1, payoff teaser, no logo or slow intro
- [ ] Primary search term in on-screen text inside 2 seconds
- [ ] Primary search term in caption line 1, not under the hashtags
- [ ] Length in the money zone: 8 to 19s scenery, 21 to 34s lore, never near 60
- [ ] Every spoken word captioned, karaoke style, white text black outline
- [ ] Loop ending built in (unfinished motion or return to opening shot)
- [ ] One save trigger OR one send trigger near the close
- [ ] Original footage only, zero reposts
- [ ] Plain ASCII caption, no em dashes, no curly quotes, no symbols
- [ ] 3 to 5 specific hashtags, on their own line at the bottom
- [ ] Trending audio caught early (under ~5,000 uses) OR real ambient on lore
- [ ] No founding year, no review count, no ACA claim, no sturgeon promise on camera
- [ ] Villain stays faceless, no named competitor
- [ ] Posting 1 per day, no burst, account-safety rules honored
