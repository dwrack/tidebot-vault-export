# Cave Point + Whitefish Dunes Fact Sheet

Researched 2026-10-14

Purpose: fact-check for DCKT educational Instagram carousels (e-bike rental, Jacksonport shop to Cave Point and Whitefish Dunes). Every item has a source. Anything not sourced is marked UNVERIFIED. Official = DNR, Door County government, WGNHS.

## Cave Point County Park

**1. Size and relation to Whitefish Dunes**
- 18.6 acres, including 900 feet of shoreline (Door County official page). The county's draft 2026-2030 Parks Plan says 19.3 acres. Use "about 19 acres".
- Address: 5360 Schauer Road, Jacksonport. County-owned since gifts from three families in 1943 and 1945.
- Adjacent to Whitefish Dunes State Park (DNR: "nearby Cave Point"; DNR geology page says Cave Point once defined Clark Bay). Wikipedia calls it an "enclave inside the state park" (secondary source, not DNR; DNR does not use the word enclave). Say "next door to" not "inside".
- Sources: https://www.co.door.wi.gov/554/Cave-Point-County-Park ; https://www.co.door.wi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/9511/2026-2030-draft-Co-Parks-Open-Space-Plan_2-12-26 ; https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish/geology

**2. Fee and hours**
- Hours: all Door County parks are "open 1/2 hour before sunrise to 11:00 PM unless otherwise posted at park." Not seasonal per the county rules page. Source: https://www.co.door.wi.gov/469/Door-County-Parks
- Fee: no entry fee is stated on any official page I could load. "Free" is inferred, not quoted. The county's DRAFT 2026-2030 Parks Plan budgets a "Park pass collection system" at Cave Point ($35,000, 2027), so a fee may be coming. It is a draft, not adopted. Source: the draft plan above (Figure 9, Proposed Improvements). Also in the plan: survey respondents mostly opposed an annual entrance fee. Status: free appears true today, UNVERIFIED as an official statement. Call Facilities and Parks, 920-746-9959.
- Do not use the state park sticker for Cave Point; it is county land.

**3. Geology**
- Rock: Niagara Escarpment dolomitic limestone ("dolostone"). DNR: bedrock "is part of the Niagara Escarpment which underlies all of Door County and forms the rock ledges of nearby Cave Point." Source: https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish/recreation/hiking
- Age: DNR secondary-reported figure of about 425 million years (Silurian, shallow warm sea) came back from a search summary, but I did not get the exact DNR sentence on the page I loaded. The DNR geology page says only "clues to the age of the rock are found in the fossils." Safe wording: "about 400 million years old (Silurian)". 425 million = UNVERIFIED as exact quote. Source: https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish/geology
- Sea caves: Wisconsin Geological and Natural History Survey: "Littoral, or sea, caves are formed by wave action ... Well-known examples occur at Cave Point County Park." Waves erode and loosen stone along joints; frost probably helps enlarge them. At Cave Point most entrances are at water level and run roughly perpendicular to shore; one cave has three entrances and runs about 100 ft parallel to shore. "Most of these are best seen from the water." Source: https://data.wgnhs.wisc.edu/pubshare/GS22-a05.pdf (Geoscience Wisconsin, Zachariasen)

**4. Bike racks, restrooms, picnic**
- Official county page lists: restrooms, picnic tables, cooking grills, a gazebo/shelter. Fun fact there: it has the county system's "first solar power restroom." Draft plan facility types: trails, picnic areas, shelter, interpretive features. Source: https://www.co.door.wi.gov/554/Cave-Point-County-Park
- Bike rack: NOT mentioned by any official source. UNVERIFIED. A public survey comment in the draft plan asked for "bike racks" in county parks generally, which hints there are not many. Do not promise racks.
- Winter restroom closure: no official statement either way. UNVERIFIED. Do not say restrooms are open or closed in winter.

**5. Safety**
- County page: "weather conditions can change rapidly" and "Use caution, as rocks are slippery when wet or covered with ice." Source: https://www.co.door.wi.gov/554/Cave-Point-County-Park
- County rule, all parks: "No lifeguard on duty - Swim at your own risk." Source: https://www.co.door.wi.gov/469/Door-County-Parks
- Jumping: no written county rule found. But Door County Emergency Services (director Aaron LeClair, July 2022) urged people not to cliff jump: "There have been deaths there over the years"; "extremely dangerous due to the shallow water"; swift currents, large waves. A woman was injured jumping about 20 ft into about 6 ft of water. His agency answers six or seven injury or distress calls at the park per year. Source: https://www.wbay.com/2022/07/19/there-have-been-deaths-authorities-warn-against-cliff-diving-cave-point/
- Winter: doorcounty.com (tourism bureau) warns the ground is slippery and uneven, wear proper footwear, stay several feet back from edges and ledges (I could not load that page directly, search summary only; treat as UNVERIFIED wording). County page also says wave action forms icicles on the rocks and the trail can be cross-country skied ungroomed.

## Whitefish Dunes State Park

**6. Size, Old Baldy, fragile dunes**
- Size: 867 acres (DNR history page: "In 1982, 230 acres of the 867-acre park were designated as a state natural area"). Some sources say 865; use 867 or "about 865 acres". The DNR State Natural Area page says 256 acres for the natural area itself (different figure, do not mix them up). Sources: https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish/history ; https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/statenaturalareas/WhiteFishDunes
- Old Baldy: "the tallest dune in the park, which rises 93 feet above lake level," observation platform over Lake Michigan and Clark Lake, boardwalk and stairs to the top. Source: https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish/recreation/hiking
- "Tallest in Wisconsin": the DNR hiking page says the steep forested dunes here are "the highest dunes on the western shore of Lake Michigan." A DNR event listing says a hike "to the tallest sand dune in Wisconsin." Official claims differ, so the safest line is "the tallest dune in the park, and the park's dunes are the highest on Lake Michigan's western shore." Sources: hiking page above ; https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/events/50316
- Fragile: the park "protects the fragile dune environment on the eastern Door County Peninsula." DNR asks: "Please do not climb on the dunes and stay on the park's designated trails and stairs to protect the rare plant and animal species." Sources: https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish ; hiking page above
- Winter: Old Baldy boardwalk "not maintained in winter," use at your own risk. Source: https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish/recreation/winter

**7. Admission for someone on a bike**
- DNR: "A vehicle admission sticker is required." (info page). The statute and rule are written for motor vehicles: NR 45.12(1)(a): "No person may operate or park any motor vehicle ... unless the vehicle displays a vehicle admission receipt." Wis. Stat. 27.01(7)(a)2 defines "vehicle" as an automobile, motor truck, motor delivery wagon, motor bus, motorcycle or other similar motor vehicle. DNR admission page: "A vehicle admission pass is required on all motor vehicles stopping in state parks."
- So the written rule attaches to motor vehicles, which implies a person arriving by bicycle or on foot does not need the sticker. No DNR page says this in plain words about bicycles. Mark as: rule-based inference, UNVERIFIED as a DNR plain-language statement. Confirm with park office, 920-823-2400.
- State trail pass (separate): required for ages 16+ biking on listed trails only. Whitefish Dunes does not appear on the DNR state trail pass property list or the DNR bicycle trail table I checked. UNVERIFIED whether a trail pass is needed for the park's bike routes.
- Sources: https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/code/admin_code/nr/001/45/12 ; https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/27/01/7 ; https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/admission ; https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/admission/trailpass ; https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish/info

**8. Bicycling and e-bikes**
- Park bike rule (DNR): "Biking is allowed on designated bike trails only; near the office along the Red Trail to S. Cave Point Drive, the Third Beach access to Clark Lake Road, and from the office to Schauer Road. Biking is not allowed on the beach/dunes or hiking trails." Source: https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish/recreation/biking
- Note: that page says the Red Trail bike segment is near the office, but the hiking page describes the Red Trail as a 1.2 mile out-and-back hiking trail to Old Baldy. Wording is confusing; do not tell guests to ride the Red Trail to Old Baldy. Sticking with "bike only on the designated bike routes."
- E-bikes, statewide rule NR 45.05(3)(em): Class 1 and class 3 e-bikes may run under 15 mph with motor engaged on trails listed in NR 51.73(1) that are open to bicycles, and on other trails that are open to nonmotorized bikes, named in the property master plan, and posted as open to e-bikes. DNR "may prohibit operation of electric bicycles on specific trails ... by posted notice." Source: https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/code/admin_code/nr/001/45/05
- DNR bike page: e-bikes "are not allowed on any other bicycle trails other than those indicated in the table"; "Electric bicycles used without the motor engaged are allowed on all bicycle trails." Class 2 is not in the rule text. Source: https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/recreation/biking
- Whitefish Dunes is NOT in the DNR e-bike table I could load and its park biking page says nothing about e-bikes. So: no source says power is allowed there. The rental page line "power not permitted inside the state park" is CONSISTENT with the rule (not listed means not allowed with motor on) but no source says it in those words. Safer phrasing: "Motor off inside the state park: e-bike motors are only allowed on trails the DNR lists or posts for e-bikes, and Whitefish Dunes isn't one of them." Still UNVERIFIED as an explicit Whitefish Dunes statement. Recommend one phone call to 920-823-2400 and get the answer in writing.

**9. Trails (DNR, all lengths from the hiking page)**
- Black Trail 2.1 mi (1.2 mi short loop). Forest and exposed rock, the Niagara bedrock "forms the rock ledges of nearby Cave Point."
- Brachiopod Trail 1.5 mi, interpretive, accessible loop, rock shelf shore, Leopold bench, boardwalk.
- Green Trail 1.8 mi. Yellow Trail 3.7 mi. Yellow Spur 0.6 mi. Red Trail 1.2 mi out and back to Old Baldy. Red Pine Trail 0.25 mi. Clark Lake Spur 0.3 mi. Whitefish Creek Spur 0.3 mi (winter page says 0.4; hiking page says 0.3).
- Total: 14.5 miles of hiking trails and 1.5 miles of sandy beach.
- Which trail connects to Cave Point: the DNR hiking and biking pages name no walking trail that goes to Cave Point County Park. Wikipedia (secondary) says a shoreline trail connects the two parks. Official bike route: Red Trail to S. Cave Point Drive (see 8). UNVERIFIED which hiking trail and its length.
- Source: https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish/recreation/hiking ; https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish/recreation/winter

**10. Beach**
- 1.5 miles of sandy beach (DNR hiking page).
- Swimming: "No lifeguards are present." "Please use caution in the lake." Lake Michigan can produce "dangerous rip currents," most dangerous when waves are large; if caught, "swim parallel to the shore." Source: https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish/recreation/water
- Context (news, not DNR): Aug 2022 a young swimmer was pulled from a rip current at the beach; the Friends group later donated a warning flag and signs. Source: https://kaukaunacommunitynews.com/2022/09/11/new-riptide-warning-flag-added-to-dangerous-stretch-of-door-county-beach/

**11. History and archaeology (DNR)**
- "Evidence of eight separate occupations ranging in age from 100 B.C. to the late 1800s." Earliest: North Bay people (100 B.C. to A.D. 300). Heins Creek occupation about A.D. 500-750. "Around A.D. 900 the Oneota people appeared on the Door Peninsula." The site is on the National Register of Historic Places. Fishing (lake sturgeon, walleye, lake trout, whitefish) is believed to be a major reason for repeated occupation; Cave Point, Whitefish Bay and Clark Lake gave fishing variety.
- A replica indigenous settlement with wigwams is in the park per doorcounty.com tourism pages (not DNR page). Keep wording respectful: "people lived and fished here for roughly 2,000 years."
- Sources: https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish/history ; https://www.doorcounty.com/experience/indigenous-peoples-historical-sites

**12. Clark Lake**
- 865 acres, max depth 25 ft, very clear water (DNR lake page). Second-largest lake in Door County (secondary). Next to the park: the park has "frontage on Clark Lake, a bay mouth bar lake" (DNR natural area page), and the park entrance is on Clark Lake Road. DNR geology: "accumulating sand has closed off Clark Bay to create Whitefish Dunes and Clark Lake."
- Sources: https://apps.dnr.wi.gov/lakes/lakepages/LakeDetail.aspx?wbic=97700 ; https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/statenaturalareas/WhiteFishDunes ; https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish/geology

**13. November and hunting**
- Open in November: yes. "The park is open year-round from 6 a.m. to 8 p.m. The main gate opens at 8 a.m." Source: https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/parks/whitefish/info
- Hunting: DNR info page says only "Hunting and fishing licenses apply." The park hunting map (PDF, rev. 8/20/2014, hosted on a third-party site, not dnr.wisconsin.gov) says gun and archery hunting and trapping are allowed in the property's open areas Nov. 15 to Dec. 15. So Whitefish Dunes appears to be in the Nov 15 - Dec 15 window, but UNVERIFIED against the current official DNR map. Open vs closed zones are graphics I could not read. Source: https://flbezvotyyjruhqdwgst.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/park-maps/wi/whitefish-dunes-hunting.pdf
- Cave Point County Park: the county's draft Parks Plan lists "bow hunting" among Cave Point's current uses. Source: draft plan above. Surprise for any "safe to ride in November" content.

## Safe to say on Instagram

1. Cave Point County Park is about 19 acres with 900 feet of Lake Michigan shoreline.
2. The ledges are Niagara Escarpment dolomite, the same rock ridge that runs under all of Door County.
3. The sea caves were carved by waves working along cracks in the rock, and frost helps widen them.
4. Cave Point is county land, not part of the state park, so the state park sticker does not apply there.
5. Door County parks open 30 minutes before sunrise and close at 11 p.m.
6. Cave Point has restrooms, picnic tables and grills. The restroom was the county's first solar-powered one.
7. The rocks are slippery when wet or icy, and weather can change fast.
8. Door County emergency officials say do not cliff jump here: shallow water, big waves, swift currents.
9. There are no lifeguards at county parks. Swim at your own risk.
10. Whitefish Dunes State Park is about 867 acres, open year-round, 6 a.m. to 8 p.m.
11. Old Baldy is the tallest dune in the park, 93 feet above lake level, with an observation platform over Lake Michigan and Clark Lake.
12. Whitefish Dunes' dunes are the highest on the western shore of Lake Michigan.
13. The dunes are fragile. Stay on the boardwalks and stairs and do not climb the dunes.
14. The park has 14.5 miles of hiking trails and 1.5 miles of sandy beach.
15. The Brachiopod Trail is a 1.5-mile loop, named for fossils found throughout Door County.
16. People have lived and fished at what is now Whitefish Dunes on and off from about 100 B.C. to the late 1800s. The Oneota arrived around A.D. 900. The site is on the National Register of Historic Places.
17. Clark Lake is 865 acres, up to 25 feet deep, and was cut off from Lake Michigan by drifting sand over thousands of years.
18. Whitefish Dunes beach has no lifeguards and can have dangerous rip currents. If caught, swim parallel to shore.
19. Biking inside Whitefish Dunes is allowed only on designated bike routes, never on the beach, dunes or hiking trails.
20. The ride from our Jacksonport shop to Cave Point is about 4 miles one way (map-derived, ride it first).

## Surprises and flags

- 4.5 miles: the map router says 4.1 miles via Hwy 57, Lake Shore Rd, Cave Point Dr, Schauer Rd. Not a contradiction, but "about 4 miles" is safer unless someone has ridden it.
- "Power not permitted inside the state park": no source states this for Whitefish Dunes. The statewide rule (NR 45.05(3)(em)) allows e-bike motors only on trails the DNR lists or posts for e-bikes, only class 1 and 3, under 15 mph, and Whitefish Dunes is not on the DNR e-bike table I could load. Consistent with our line, but unconfirmed. Class 2 is not in the rule at all. Call 920-823-2400 and ask for it in writing before we say it as fact.
- Cave Point fee: free today as far as any official page shows, but the county's draft 2026-2030 plan budgets a "Park pass collection system" at Cave Point for 2027. Re-check before any 2027 evergreen content says "free".
- "Tallest dune in Wisconsin": DNR itself is inconsistent (park page says tallest in the park; one DNR event page says tallest in Wisconsin; hiking page says highest on the western Lake Michigan shore). Use the western-shore wording.
- Sticker for cyclists: no DNR page says bikes are exempt. The rules are written for motor vehicles, so exemption is an inference. Get park office confirmation.
- Cave Point has no officially listed bike rack and no official winter-restroom statement. Cave Point also lists bow hunting as a current use.
- Not Cave Point: the county plan's October 1 to May 15 "cave closed to protect bats" refers to Horseshoe Bay Cave at Frank E. Murphy park, not the Cave Point sea caves. Do not mix them up.
- Acreage is inconsistent across sources (Cave Point 18.6 / 19.3 / 19; Whitefish 865 / 867). Use "about".
