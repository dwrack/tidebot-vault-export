# How We Run Things — The Quiz
### NOLA Bike Bar

*For new hires. Read each scenario. Pick your gut answer. Then read the takeaway.*

*Managers: walk through these together. The conversation matters more than the "score."*

---

### Scenario 1: The Local Connection

A regular at a bar on Frenchmen Street mentions their staff has never done the bike bar tour. You've been meaning to pitch the local business outreach but nobody told you exactly how.

**What do you do?**

A) Wait until your manager sets up the official program
B) Tell your friend about it casually and let it die there
C) Bring it up in the Slack channel: "Hey, I've got a contact at [bar]. They're interested in a staff outing. What's our move?"

<details>
<summary>The takeaway</summary>

**Own Your Patch.** Option C. You don't need a finished program to make a connection. Surface the opportunity so the team can act on it.
</details>

---

### Scenario 2: The Safety Question

A guest on the bike bar seems visibly intoxicated before departure. They're part of a bachelorette party and the group is excited.

**What do you do?**

A) Let it slide, they're having fun
B) Quietly check in with them: "Hey, just want to make sure you're good to ride. We can adjust your seat or have you ride up front where it's easier."
C) Refuse to let them on in front of the whole group

<details>
<summary>The takeaway</summary>

**Safety Is the Job.** Option B. Handle it privately and with care. If they truly can't safely participate, that's a firm but private conversation. Never embarrass a guest in front of their group, but never compromise safety either.
</details>

---

### Scenario 3: The Equipment Rush

You're about to depart. One guest hasn't signed their waiver. The group is ready and getting antsy.

**What do you do?**

A) Let them on. They'll sign later.
B) Have them sign on your phone right there. "Sorry about the tech glitch, this'll be quick."
C) Leave without them

<details>
<summary>The takeaway</summary>

**Safety Is the Job.** Option B. No signed waiver, no tour. But make it fast and keep the energy up.
</details>

---

### Scenario 4: The Checked-Out Teammate

You notice a more experienced team member has been showing up late and not cleaning the bike bar after tours. Nobody's said anything.

**What do you do?**

A) Keep your head down
B) Talk to them one-on-one: "Hey, you good? Noticed you've been a little off lately."
C) Go straight to the manager

<details>
<summary>The takeaway</summary>

**Respect the Work.** Option B is the human move. Peer-to-peer, private, genuine concern. If nothing changes, then loop in the manager.
</details>

---

### Scenario 5: The Great Night

End of a killer tour. The bachelorette party was amazing, one guest tipped huge, and a local photographer happened to snap some shots of the bike bar rolling through the Quarter.

**What do you do?**

A) Enjoy it and head home
B) Drop a note in the channel: the photographer's handle, the energy of the group, what made it work
C) Text your manager later

<details>
<summary>The takeaway</summary>

**Get It Out of Your Head.** Option B. The photographer contact becomes marketing gold. The energy notes help the next captain set up a great night. None of that happens if it stays in your head.
</details>

---

## How'd You Do?

No grade. No pass/fail. The point is getting these principles in your head before your first shift.

If you disagreed with anything, bring it up. The best crew members push back.

**Welcome aboard.**
