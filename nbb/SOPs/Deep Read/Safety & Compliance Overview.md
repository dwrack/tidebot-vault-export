# Safety & Compliance Overview
### NOLA Bike Bar

---

## This Is a Street Vehicle Operation

Let's be very clear about what NOLA Bike Bar is: three open-air, multi-passenger vehicles that operate on public streets in New Orleans. This is not a boat. Not a barge. Not a vessel. There is no USCG involvement, no Coast Guard licensing, no maritime law.

The regulatory framework here is traffic law, commercial vehicle insurance, and City of New Orleans business permits. If you came from one of our boat operations and your instinct is to think about life jackets and VHF radios, reset. Think traffic lights, brake checks, and seatbelt-equivalent passenger restraints.

## Regulatory Framework

### City of New Orleans
- Valid business permits for commercial operation
- Vehicle registration for each of the 3 vehicles
- Compliance with any city-specific ordinances for party bikes / pedal pubs (these change, so stay current)
- Noise ordinances: know the neighborhoods and times where volume matters

### Traffic Law
- All standard traffic laws apply. Stop signs, traffic lights, speed limits, right of way.
- Drivers must have a valid license with a clean record. If the city requires a commercial endorsement for this vehicle class, that's required too.
- DUI laws apply to the driver. Obviously. The driver must be completely sober. Passengers may be drinking, but the driver is operating a commercial vehicle on public roads.
- Lane usage: know which lanes you can legally use, which streets have restrictions on oversized vehicles, where you need to yield to other traffic patterns.

### Insurance
- Commercial vehicle insurance covering all 3 vehicles
- Liability coverage for passengers
- Coverage for property damage (other vehicles, structures, etc.)
- Policies must be current. Lapsed insurance means the vehicle doesn't go out. Period.

## Vehicle Inspection Standards

Two layers of inspection keep the vehicles safe:

**Daily (Morning Inspection Checklist):** Every vehicle, every day it operates. Tires, brakes, steering, lights, passenger area, safety equipment. Takes 10-15 minutes per vehicle. Non-negotiable.

**Monthly/Seasonal (Vehicle Safety Checklist):** Deep inspection of frame integrity, electrical systems, structural components, documentation status. This catches things that daily checks might miss: slow tire degradation, frame fatigue, wiring corrosion.

## Passenger Safety on an Open-Air Vehicle

This is the core safety challenge. You have an open-air vehicle with passengers who may be drinking, moving through city traffic alongside cars and trucks. No doors, no windows, no airbags. The safety comes from:

1. **Passenger rails and restraints.** These are the primary physical safety barrier. They must be inspected daily and pass monthly deep inspection.
2. **The safety briefing.** Every group, every ride. Stay seated while moving. Keep hands and arms inside. Hold the rail during stops and starts.
3. **The driver's judgment.** You decide the speed, the route, the stops. If a passenger is standing, you stop until they sit. If conditions feel unsafe, you modify the route or end the ride.
4. **Stop/start procedures.** Announce stops before they happen so passengers can brace. Accelerate smoothly. Brake gradually unless emergency. Sudden movements are when people get hurt.

## DUI and Liability Considerations

The driver is always sober. There's no gray area, no "just one beer," no exceptions.

Passengers drinking on the vehicle creates a liability environment that requires extra caution:
- Intoxicated passengers are less stable, less likely to follow safety instructions, and more likely to do something unpredictable.
- If a passenger is too impaired to safely ride (can't stay seated, can't hold the rail, combative), they don't ride. You remove them from the vehicle with management support if needed.
- If a passenger gets injured and they were intoxicated, the signed waiver is your documentation. This is why waiver compliance is zero-tolerance.

## What Happens If There's an Accident

Follow the Incident Report Steps. But here's the bigger picture:

A collision or injury involving a commercial vehicle with passengers in New Orleans will involve:
- NOPD (police report)
- Insurance claims (potentially multiple parties)
- Possible litigation
- Media attention (party bike accidents make the news)

This is why daily inspection, sober driving, safety briefings, and signed waivers aren't bureaucratic box-checking. They're the things that determine whether an accident is a terrible day or a business-ending event.

## How This Differs from a Boat Operation

If you've worked on one of our barge or boat operations, here's the mental shift:

| Boat Operation | Bike Bar |
|---|---|
| USCG regulated | City traffic law regulated |
| Captain's license required | Driver's license required |
| Life jackets, flares, VHF radio | Reflective gear, lights, first aid |
| Weather = wind, waves, lightning | Weather = rain, heat, storms (open-air) |
| Isolated on water | Sharing roads with other vehicles |
| Controlled boarding at dock | Street-level boarding, public environment |
| Coast Guard inspections | City permits and vehicle registration |

The safety mindset is the same. The regulatory framework is completely different.
