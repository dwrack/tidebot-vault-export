# Safety Briefing Script
### NOLA Bike Bar

---

## Why This Matters

The safety briefing is the single most important 90 seconds of every ride. It's the moment where you set expectations, establish that you're in charge of safety, and give passengers the information they need to keep themselves safe on an open-air vehicle in city traffic.

Don't read it off a card. Don't rush through it. Don't treat it like a formality. Internalize it, deliver it naturally, and make eye contact while you do it.

## The Briefing

Here's what passengers need to hear before the vehicle moves. The exact words are yours, but every one of these points must be covered:

### Stay Seated While We're Moving
"Once we start rolling, I need everyone to stay in their seats. I know it's tempting to stand up and dance, but we're on the road with other vehicles and I need you seated so I can keep everyone safe. When we stop, you can stand, stretch, whatever you need."

### Keep Hands and Arms Inside
"This is an open vehicle. We're going to be passing close to other cars, signs, poles, trees. Keep your hands and arms inside the rails at all times. Don't reach out to high-five people on the street. Don't hang anything over the side."

### Hold On During Stops and Starts
"When I'm slowing down or speeding up, hold onto the rail in front of you. I'll try to keep everything smooth, but sometimes I have to brake for traffic and I want you braced for that."

### The Driver Controls the Route
"I've got a great route planned. I know the best streets, the best sights, and I know which roads to avoid. If you've got a request, I'm happy to hear it, but the final call on where we go is mine. That's a safety thing, not a control thing."

### If You Need to Stop
"If anyone needs me to stop for any reason, just call out. Shout 'STOP' and I'll pull over as soon as it's safe. Bathroom break, someone not feeling well, anything at all."

### First Aid Kit
"First aid kit is right here [point to it]. If anyone needs it during the ride, let me know."

### Emergency Procedure
"If anything goes wrong, I'm going to pull over to a safe spot, and I'll give you instructions from there. Your job in an emergency is to stay seated and listen for my directions."

## Delivery Tips

**Make it conversational.** You're talking to a group of people who are excited to have fun. Don't sound like a flight attendant reading a card. Sound like someone who does this every day and genuinely wants them to be safe.

**Make it quick.** 60-90 seconds. If it drags past two minutes, people tune out. Hit every point, but don't belabor any of them.

**Use humor if it fits.** "I know nobody came here for a safety lecture, so I'll keep this quick" is a fine opener. Just don't undermine the seriousness of the content.

**Watch their eyes.** If someone is on their phone or clearly not listening, call them in gently. "Hey, I need everyone's ears for about 60 seconds, then we're rolling." You need everyone to hear this, not just the front row.

**End with energy.** "Alright, safety's done. Let's have the best ride of your trip. Everybody ready?" Transition from safety mode to party mode cleanly.

## Non-Negotiable

Every point in the briefing is delivered on every ride. There's no "this group seems chill, I'll skip it" or "it's my fifth ride today, they'll figure it out." If a ride goes out without a full safety briefing, that's a write-up on the first offense and termination on the second.
