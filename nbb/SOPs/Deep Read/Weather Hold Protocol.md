# Weather Hold Protocol
### NOLA Bike Bar

---

## The Core Principle

This is an open-air vehicle on public streets. Your passengers have no roof, no walls, no protection from weather. And wet roads, high winds, and poor visibility create real safety risks when you're sharing lanes with cars. The threshold for cancellation is lower than you might think.

## Decision Maker

The driver scheduled for the ride makes the initial call. If it's borderline, escalate to management. When in doubt, cancel. A refund is always cheaper than an accident.

## Cancellation Thresholds

### Heavy Rain — Cancel
Open-air vehicle + rain = miserable for guests and dangerous on the road. Wet roads reduce braking performance and visibility. Passengers will be soaked and unhappy. If rain is expected during the ride window, cancel.

Light drizzle is a judgment call. If it's a brief passing shower and the group is game, you can offer to wait it out. But don't pressure anyone into riding in the rain.

### Lightning or Thunderstorms — Cancel
If there's lightning anywhere in the area, the ride doesn't go out. Open-air vehicle, metal frame, elevated passengers. Cancel immediately and don't wait for it to pass unless the storm is clearly moving through quickly and the ride window allows for delay.

### Extreme Heat (Heat Index >105F) — Evaluate
New Orleans summers are brutal. An open-air vehicle with no shade (or limited shade) in 105+ heat index is a heat illness risk, especially for passengers who have been drinking.

Options:
- Cancel outright
- Modify route to include more shaded streets and more frequent stops for water
- Move the ride to a cooler time slot if available

If you run the ride in extreme heat, watch your passengers closely. Anyone looking flushed, confused, or excessively sweaty needs water and a stop immediately.

### Extreme Cold (<40F) — Inform and Evaluate
This is New Orleans, so genuine cold is rare. But it happens. Open-air vehicle in sub-40 temps is uncomfortable at best.

If cold weather is forecasted:
- Inform guests in advance, let them decide
- Offer blankets if available
- Consider cancellation if wind chill makes it genuinely unsafe (hypothermia risk on a 90-minute ride is real)

### High Winds (>30mph Sustained) — Cancel
Sustained winds over 30mph create stability concerns for the vehicle and make the ride genuinely unpleasant. Gusts can be worse than sustained wind because they're unpredictable. If wind is forecast above 30mph sustained or gusts above 40mph, cancel.

### Poor Visibility — Cancel
Fog or any condition that reduces visibility to the point where other drivers can't see you easily. You're a large, slow-moving vehicle in traffic. If drivers can't see you, that's a collision waiting to happen.

## Communication Protocol

**Timing:** Make the call by 7am for morning rides, by noon for afternoon rides, by 3pm for evening rides. Don't wait until the last minute.

**Guest communication:**
1. Call or text the booking contact directly. Don't rely on email alone.
2. Offer to reschedule to another date/time if available.
3. If no reschedule works, process refund per cancellation policy.
4. Log the cancellation in FareHarbor with the reason.

**Internal communication:**
1. Post in the group chat that the ride is cancelled and why.
2. Notify any other drivers who were scheduled.
3. If it's a full-day weather cancel, post once for the whole day rather than ride by ride.

## Borderline Situations

The forecast says 40% chance of rain. Or the heat index is 103, not 105. Or the wind is 25mph and gusty.

In these cases:
- Check radar, not just the forecast. Is rain actually approaching or is it a generic percentage?
- Call the guest and be honest: "Weather looks borderline. We can try it, but I want you to know there's a chance we cut it short. Or we can reschedule." Let them choose.
- If you go out and conditions worsen mid-ride, you bring it in. Safety over schedule, every time.
