# Role Guide — Driver & Host
### NOLA Bike Bar

---

## The Job Is Hosting, Not Just Driving

If you think this gig is about steering a big bike from point A to point B, you'll be mediocre at it. The driving is the easy part. The real job is making 10-15 people feel like they just had the best two hours of their trip to New Orleans.

You're the DJ, the tour guide, the bartender (sort of), and the energy of the whole experience. You're also the person responsible for getting everyone home safe on a vehicle that shares the road with cars, trucks, and distracted Uber drivers. That combination requires someone who can hold two mindsets at once: party host and safety-first operator.

## What a Great Ride Looks Like

The group shows up. You greet them like friends. You do the safety stuff without making it feel clinical. You read the room fast: bachelorette party? Crank the energy. Corporate team outing? Start mellow and let them warm up. Birthday group with grandma along? Find the sweet spot.

The music hits. You're moving through the neighborhood and pointing things out. Not a scripted tour, just real stuff. "That corner right there, that's where Louis Prima used to play." Or "See that house? That's been the same family since before the Civil War." You don't need to be a historian. You need to know enough to make the ride feel like New Orleans, not just any city.

You know your routes cold. You know which streets have potholes, which intersections are sketchy at 4pm, where construction popped up this week. You adjust on the fly without the group even noticing.

## Route Knowledge

Learn the neighborhoods. Not from a script, from riding them.

**The essentials:**
- French Quarter: the obvious one, but know the less-trafficked streets, not just Bourbon
- Marigny/Bywater: great vibe, good music venues to point out, less traffic
- Garden District: beautiful houses, oak trees, slower pace for groups that want that
- Treme: the oldest African American neighborhood in the country, rich history

Know where to stop for a quick photo. Know which bars will wave at your group. Know where the potholes will rattle your passengers' drinks. These details are what make a ride feel local vs. tourist.

## Managing the Party

People on your vehicle are often drinking. That's the deal. But you have to manage the energy so it stays fun without becoming unsafe or obnoxious.

**Things that work:**
- Controlling the music is controlling the energy. You pick the playlist, not the loudest guest.
- If someone wants to stand up while moving, that's a firm no. Friendly but absolute.
- If someone is getting too drunk and becoming a hazard, you address it directly. "Hey, I need you to sit down and hold on. I want everyone to have a good time, but I need you safe." If they can't comply, you stop the ride and call management.
- If a group is getting rowdy and bothering people on the street, bring the energy down. You're the thermostat.

**Things that don't work:**
- Ignoring a problem hoping it goes away
- Getting into an argument with a drunk passenger
- Letting one person hijack the vibe for the whole group

## De-Escalation

Most situations never get past a firm, friendly redirect. But if someone gets aggressive:

1. Stay calm. Lower your voice, don't raise it.
2. Stop the vehicle if needed. You're not having a confrontation while driving.
3. Name the behavior, not the person. "I can't have standing while we're moving" not "You're being an idiot."
4. Give them a choice. "I need you to sit down so we can keep going, or I'll need to end the ride here."
5. If someone is threatening or violent, stop the vehicle, call 911, and call management. You're not a bouncer.

## Music and Energy Management

Your playlist is your most powerful tool. Have multiple playlists ready:

- **High energy**: bachelorette parties, birthday groups, young crowd
- **Classic New Orleans**: jazz, brass band, Rebirth, Trombone Shorty, for groups that want the local flavor
- **Chill/R&B**: corporate groups, older groups, afternoon rides
- **Mixed**: your general-purpose list that works for most groups

Read the group in the first 5 minutes and pick accordingly. If it's not working, switch. Nobody will notice the transition, but they'll notice if the music doesn't match the mood.

## Requirements

- Valid driver's license with clean driving record
- Background check cleared
- CPR/First Aid preferred
- Complete shadow rides (2-3) and assisted rides (1-2) before solo
- Know all routes and be able to adjust for road conditions
- Deliver the safety briefing from memory

## Prohibited Behaviors

These are immediate termination:

- Operating while impaired by alcohol or any substance
- Allowing a guest to ride without a signed waiver
- Skipping the safety briefing
- Using a phone while driving (music control excepted, and only when stopped)
- Reckless driving, road rage, or aggressive behavior toward other motorists
- Any form of harassment toward guests
- Operating a vehicle that failed morning inspection

## Violation Protocol

**First offense (non-termination items):** Conversation with management. Documented. Specific corrective action agreed on.

**Second offense:** Final warning in writing. One more and you're done.

**Third offense:** Contract terminated.

For the termination-level items listed above, there's no progression. One instance and the contract ends.
