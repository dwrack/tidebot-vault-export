### Question 1

A bachelorette party of 14 shows up, but the booking was for 12. The extra 2 say they "just decided to come last minute." What do you do?

A) Let them on, it's only 2 extra people
B) Check if the vehicle can safely hold 14, have the extra 2 sign waivers and pay through FareHarbor before they ride, and only proceed if capacity allows
C) Tell the extra 2 they can't ride, no exceptions
D) Call management and wait for their decision

**Best answer: B.** You need to check actual vehicle capacity first. If 14 is within safe limits, the extras need waivers signed and payment processed before they get on. If it exceeds capacity, they can't ride regardless. This isn't about being rigid, it's about safety and liability.

### Question 2

Halfway through a ride, one passenger is clearly very drunk. They're not being aggressive, but they keep trying to stand up despite being told to sit. Other passengers are starting to look uncomfortable.

A) Keep telling them to sit each time they stand
B) Stop the vehicle, have a direct conversation: "I need you seated for the rest of the ride. If you can't stay seated, I'll need to end the ride here for everyone's safety." Follow through if they can't comply.
C) Ask their friends to handle them
D) Speed up the ride to get it over with faster

**Best answer: B.** Repeated standing is a safety issue that escalates. One firm, direct conversation with a clear consequence. If they sit and stay seated, great. If they can't, you stop the ride and call management for next steps. Relying on their friends rarely works, and speeding up is dangerous.

### Question 3

A guest in a wheelchair wants to join the ride. Your vehicle isn't wheelchair accessible.

A) Tell them you can't accommodate wheelchairs, sorry
B) Acknowledge the situation honestly, explain the vehicle's limitations, offer to help them board if physically possible and they're comfortable with it, and if not, offer a full refund and help them find an alternative
C) Try to lift them onto the vehicle with the help of other guests
D) Tell them to contact the office for ADA accommodations

**Best answer: B.** Handle it in person, with honesty and genuine effort. Some wheelchair users can transfer to a seat with assistance. Ask them what they need and what they're comfortable with. If the vehicle genuinely can't accommodate them, be upfront, refund immediately, and help them have a good day anyway if you can. Don't punt it to "the office."

### Question 4

The group wants to play their own music through a Bluetooth speaker they brought. Your sound system is set up and ready.

A) Let them connect their speaker, the customer is always right
B) Explain that you use the vehicle's sound system for the ride, but you're happy to take song requests or play their playlist through your system
C) Let them play their speaker alongside yours
D) Tell them no outside speakers, period, with no alternative offered

**Best answer: B.** You control the sound system. That's part of controlling the energy and the experience. But meeting them halfway by taking requests or playing their playlist through your system makes them feel heard without losing control of the ride.

### Question 5

A guest complains that the ride is "boring" 20 minutes in. The rest of the group seems to be enjoying it.

A) Ignore them, the rest of the group is fine
B) Acknowledge them, adjust slightly: change the music, take a more interesting street, engage them directly in conversation, but don't overhaul the whole ride for one person
C) Ask the whole group if they want to change the route
D) Tell the complainer that this is the standard route and they signed up for it

**Best answer: B.** One unhappy guest doesn't mean the whole ride needs to change. But ignoring them guarantees a bad review. Small adjustments, some direct attention, maybe a fun detour or story. Often the "bored" person just wants to be engaged directly.

### Question 6

A group asks you to stop at a specific bar so they can grab drinks. It's not on your route and it's on a busy street with no safe stopping point.

A) Stop there anyway, the customer wants what they want
B) Explain why you can't stop at that specific spot (no safe pull-over), and offer a nearby alternative where you can stop safely
C) Tell them you can't make any stops that aren't on the route
D) Drive by the bar slowly so they can at least see it

**Best answer: B.** Safety dictates where you can stop, not customer requests. But a flat "no stops" kills the experience. Offering an alternative shows you're trying to give them what they want within the constraints of keeping everyone safe.

### Question 7

Two groups back to back have left bad reviews mentioning the same thing: the safety briefing felt "preachy" and "killed the vibe." What should you take away from this?

A) Shorten the briefing and skip the less important parts
B) Work on your delivery, make it more conversational and quicker, but keep every safety point intact
C) Stop doing the briefing since guests don't like it
D) Add humor to the briefing by making fun of the safety rules

**Best answer: B.** The content is non-negotiable. Every point gets covered on every ride. But delivery matters. If it feels like a lecture, people tune out and resent it. Make it feel like a quick, natural conversation. 60 seconds, friendly tone, make eye contact, move on. The briefing should feel like something you say because you care, not because you have to.

### Question 8

You notice a guest in the group is noticeably quiet while everyone else is partying. They're not engaging and look a little anxious.

A) Leave them alone, not everyone is a party person
B) Check in with them casually and privately: "Hey, you doing alright? Anything I can do?" Give them space if they want it, but make sure they know you noticed them
C) Try to get them involved by putting them on the spot with the group
D) Ask their friends if something is wrong

**Best answer: B.** A quick, low-pressure check-in shows you're paying attention without forcing anything. Some people are just quiet. Some people are anxious on an open vehicle in traffic. Some people got dragged to a party they didn't want to attend. Acknowledge them without making it a thing.

### Question 9

A guest asks you to take a group photo using their phone. While you're taking the photo, you notice they're also filming a TikTok and clearly positioning you as part of the content.

A) Refuse to be filmed and hand the phone back
B) Take the photo, and if you're comfortable being on camera, play along. If not, politely ask them to keep you out of the video
C) Ham it up for the camera, this is great marketing
D) Tell them you need written permission to be filmed at your workplace

**Best answer: B.** Social content from guests is generally good for business, but you also have a right to your own comfort. If you're fine with it, lean in. If you're not, a simple "Hey, happy to take the photo but I'd rather not be in the video" is perfectly fine. No need to make it weird or legalistic.

### Question 10

It's 95 degrees out, high humidity. Your ride is about to start and the group is already sweating. No one has water bottles.

A) Start the ride as scheduled, they'll be fine
B) Mention the heat, offer to wait a few minutes while anyone grabs water from a nearby store, remind them to stay hydrated during the ride, and keep an eye on everyone for signs of heat-related illness
C) Cancel the ride due to heat
D) Drive faster to create more airflow

**Best answer: B.** 95 degrees is hot but likely below the cancellation threshold (heat index >105). But you still have a responsibility to look out for your passengers. Encourage hydration, watch for heat illness (flushed skin, confusion, dizziness), and be ready to stop if someone's struggling. Driving faster is not a cooling strategy.
