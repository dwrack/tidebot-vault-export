### Question 1

You're assigned to Vehicle 2 for a 2pm ride. During morning inspection, Vehicle 2 passes everything except the sound system, which won't power on. Vehicle 1 is available. What do you do?

A) Run the ride on Vehicle 2 without music, just explain the situation to the group
B) Switch to Vehicle 1 if it passes inspection, report the Vehicle 2 sound system issue to management
C) Cancel the ride since the assigned vehicle has an issue
D) Borrow a portable Bluetooth speaker and use that instead

**Best answer: B.** A dead sound system isn't a safety failure that grounds the vehicle, but it significantly degrades the experience. If Vehicle 1 is available and passes inspection, switch. Report the issue on Vehicle 2 so it gets fixed. Canceling is overkill for a sound system issue. A portable speaker is a workable backup if no other vehicle is available, but switching is better.

### Question 2

Mid-ride, your vehicle develops a mechanical issue: the steering feels loose and is pulling to the right. You have 8 passengers on board. What do you do?

A) Carefully drive back to the starting point and report the issue
B) Pull over safely and immediately, secure the vehicle, call management, and arrange alternative transportation for the passengers. Do not continue driving.
C) Finish the ride since you're more than halfway done and drive extra carefully
D) Ask the passengers to shift their weight to the left to compensate

**Best answer: B.** Steering issues are safety-critical. You don't "carefully drive back." You stop. A vehicle with compromised steering is a danger to your passengers and everyone else on the road. Pull over, secure it, and call management to figure out how to get the passengers where they need to go.

### Question 3

It's a Saturday and all 3 vehicles are booked back-to-back. Vehicle 3's 1pm ride runs 15 minutes long, and the 2pm group is already waiting. What do you do?

A) Rush through the closeout on Vehicle 3 and get the next group on as fast as possible
B) Communicate with the 2pm group that you'll be a few minutes late, do a proper closeout (trash, cooler, quick wipe), then greet the new group and check them in fully
C) Skip the post-tour closeout entirely, the next group won't notice
D) Have the 2pm group start boarding while you're still doing closeout

**Best answer: B.** The 2pm group sees you rushing and feels like an afterthought, or they board a dirty vehicle. Neither is acceptable. A quick, honest "we're just a few minutes behind, I want to make sure everything's fresh for you" buys goodwill. Closeout doesn't need to be perfect, but trash removed, cooler reset, and a quick wipe are the minimum.

### Question 4

At 6:30am you check the weather for your 10am ride. Forecast says 60% chance of thunderstorms starting at noon, but the morning looks clear. What do you do?

A) Cancel the 10am ride proactively, storms are coming
B) Run the 10am ride as planned since the morning is clear, but monitor radar closely and be ready to bring it in early if conditions change. Cancel or decide on any afternoon rides by noon.
C) Wait until 9am to make any decisions
D) Run everything as scheduled and only cancel if lightning is actually visible

**Best answer: B.** A 10am ride with storms starting at noon should be fine, but you stay on radar. The protocol is to make decisions early: morning rides by 7am, afternoon rides by noon. Canceling a morning ride because of an afternoon storm is unnecessary. Waiting until lightning is visible is too late.

### Question 5

You arrive for your shift and the driver from the previous shift didn't complete the End-of-Day Sign-Off. Coolers are dirty, there's trash in Vehicle 1, and keys were left in the ignition of Vehicle 3.

A) Clean everything up, do your morning inspection, and don't say anything
B) Clean up what you need to for your rides, report the previous driver's failure to management, and do your normal morning inspection
C) Refuse to start your shift until management addresses the previous driver's behavior
D) Text the other driver and tell them to come back and clean up

**Best answer: B.** You need to get your rides ready regardless of what the last person did. But management needs to know so they can address it. This is a pattern they need to see, not something you should handle by covering for it or by being confrontational. Your guests are coming at 10am whether the last driver cleaned up or not.

### Question 6

A road closure pops up mid-ride on your planned route. You can see the barriers two blocks ahead. What do you do?

A) Turn around and go back the way you came
B) Take the next safe turn onto an alternate street you know, continue the ride without making it feel like a disruption, and mention something interesting about the new street
C) Stop and pull up Google Maps to figure out a new route while passengers wait
D) Ask the passengers which way they want to go

**Best answer: B.** This is why route knowledge matters. You should know 2-3 alternate paths for any section of your primary route. Make the turn, keep the energy going, and point out something on the new street. If you stop to stare at your phone, the group's momentum dies. Asking passengers to navigate for you doesn't inspire confidence.

### Question 7

You're scheduled to drive Vehicle 1 and Vehicle 3 today (two rides, different vehicles). During morning inspection, Vehicle 3 has a flat tire. Vehicle 2 is not scheduled but is parked at the lot. What do you do?

A) Report the flat on Vehicle 3, inspect Vehicle 2, and if it passes, use it for the second ride
B) Cancel the second ride since Vehicle 3 is out
C) Try to change the tire on Vehicle 3 before the second ride
D) Run both rides on Vehicle 1 with a quick turnaround

**Best answer: A.** Vehicle 2 is available. Inspect it. If it passes, use it. There's no reason to cancel a ride when you have a backup vehicle. Changing a tire yourself might work but isn't guaranteed, and running back-to-back on the same vehicle might not leave enough turnaround time.

### Question 8

A guest calls 30 minutes before their ride asking if they can bring a large cooler with alcohol for the group. Your BYOB policy allows guests to bring drinks. What do you tell them?

A) Yes, bring whatever they want
B) Yes, guests can bring drinks, but check on cooler size since space on the vehicle is limited. Confirm what the BYOB policy actually covers (cans/cups only? no glass?) and let them know you have a cooler on board they can use
C) No, outside coolers aren't allowed on the vehicle
D) Tell them to call the office

**Best answer: B.** BYOB policies vary, so know yours. The practical concerns are: will a large cooler fit? Is glass allowed? Where will it go safely during the ride? Give them useful info so they show up prepared, not confused. "Call the office" is lazy when you can answer the question.

### Question 9

It's your first solo ride after being signed off. You're nervous and forget to deliver the full safety briefing, you only mentioned staying seated. You realize your mistake 5 minutes into the ride. What do you do?

A) Keep going, delivering the briefing mid-ride would be awkward
B) At the next safe stop, pause and deliver the remaining safety points you missed. Better late than never.
C) Make up for it by being extra vigilant about safety during the ride
D) Text management that you forgot and ask what to do

**Best answer: B.** A partial briefing is better than none, but a complete briefing is required. At your next stop, just say "Hey, one more thing I should've mentioned before we started" and cover the rest. Passengers won't think it's weird. Skipping it entirely because it'd be "awkward" means passengers are riding without critical safety information.

### Question 10

End of the day. All rides are done. You notice the first aid kit on Vehicle 2 is missing gauze and only has 1 bandage left. The next day's rides start at 10am.

A) Leave a note for the morning driver
B) Restock it now if supplies are available. If not, report it to management tonight so it gets handled before tomorrow's first ride.
C) It's fine, first aid kits rarely get used
D) Move the kit from Vehicle 1 to Vehicle 2 since Vehicle 1 isn't scheduled tomorrow

**Best answer: B.** First aid kits need to be stocked and ready on every vehicle that goes out. Restocking tonight is ideal. If you don't have supplies, reporting tonight means management has time to handle it before 10am. Leaving a note puts the burden on tomorrow's driver who might not see it. Moving kits between vehicles means Vehicle 1 is now unequipped if plans change.
