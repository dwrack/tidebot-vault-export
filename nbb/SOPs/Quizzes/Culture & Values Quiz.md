### Question 1

You're about to start a ride and you notice the group is a bachelorette party. They're loud, excited, and already a few drinks in. How do you approach the energy?

A) Match their energy immediately, crank the music, let them set the tone
B) Read the group, start with good energy, but you control the playlist and the pace
C) Keep things calm and professional, this is a safety-first operation
D) Let the maid of honor decide what music to play and where to go

**Best answer: B.** You're the thermostat, not the thermometer. Match their excitement but stay in control of the music, the route, and the pace. Letting the group dictate everything leads to chaos. Keeping it too buttoned-up kills the experience they paid for.

---

### Question 2

A guest gives you feedback mid-ride that a certain street was really bumpy and uncomfortable. What do you do after the ride?

A) Nothing, bumpy streets are part of New Orleans
B) Share it in the group chat so other drivers can avoid that street or at least warn passengers
C) Email management a formal report
D) Fix the route yourself and don't bother anyone with it

**Best answer: B.** "Get it out of your head" is the culture. Quick note in the channel, other drivers benefit. You don't need to write a formal report, but keeping it to yourself helps no one.

---

### Question 3

You show up 5 minutes before a ride and realize the cooler still has yesterday's standing water in it. What's the right move?

A) Dump it, wipe it quick, load ice, and get going
B) Skip the cooler for this ride since you're short on time
C) Show up earlier next time, but for now, clean it properly even if it means a 5-minute delay
D) Text management about the previous driver not cleaning up

**Best answer: C.** The guest experience starts with a clean vehicle. A 5-minute delay with a quick heads-up to the group beats serving drinks out of a gross cooler. And yeah, show up earlier. That said, texting management about the previous driver isn't wrong, but it's not the priority in the moment.

### Question 4

A passenger asks you to take a detour through a neighborhood you know has heavy construction and a tricky intersection. They really want to see it because their grandma's house is there.

A) Take the detour, the customer is always right
B) Explain that the route is set for safety reasons and you can't modify it
C) Offer to drive past the closest safe point to that neighborhood and point it out, but explain why you can't take the full detour
D) Tell them to Uber there after the ride

**Best answer: C.** You own the route for safety reasons, but you're also a host. Finding a middle ground shows you care without compromising safety. A flat "no" without context feels cold. Telling them to Uber is dismissive.

### Question 5

It's your third ride of the day and you're tired. The next group is a corporate team that seems low-energy. Your instinct is to just drive the route and let them talk amongst themselves.

A) That's fine, not every group needs high energy
B) Reset your energy. Every group gets the same quality ride regardless of how many you've done today
C) Ask them what vibe they want and match it
D) Play loud music to pump them up whether they want it or not

**Best answer: B.** Every group pays the same price and deserves the same experience. "Respect the work" means showing up ready for every ride, not just the first one. You can absolutely read their vibe and adjust, but phoning it in isn't an option.

### Question 6

A regular guest has been on 3 rides before and says "I know the safety stuff, you can skip it for me." What do you do?

A) Skip the briefing for them since they already know it
B) Give them a shorter version, just the highlights
C) Deliver the full briefing to the whole group, including them
D) Ask them to deliver the briefing to the group since they know it so well

**Best answer: C.** Full briefing, every ride, every guest. No exceptions. You can acknowledge their experience with a smile ("I know you've heard this before, bear with me") but the briefing happens in full. This is a non-negotiable safety item.

### Question 7

You notice that one of the other drivers has been skipping the morning vehicle inspection and just eyeballing things. Their rides have been fine so far.

A) Mind your own business, they haven't had any problems
B) Say something to them directly, and if they don't fix it, escalate to management
C) Report them to management immediately
D) Start doing their inspection for them so at least it gets done

**Best answer: B.** "Own your patch" includes looking out for the operation, not just your own rides. Direct conversation first is the right move. If they blow you off, management needs to know. Doing their job for them enables the problem.

---

### Question 8

A group of tourists asks you where to eat after the ride. You know a great spot but it's your buddy's restaurant and he gives you a kickback for referrals.

A) Recommend your buddy's place without mentioning the kickback
B) Give them a few honest recommendations including your buddy's place if it's genuinely good, but disclose any financial relationship
C) Only recommend places you have no financial connection to
D) Tell them to check Yelp

**Best answer: B.** Be real. If your buddy's spot is legitimately great, recommend it. But if you're getting paid to send people there, be upfront or at least give balanced options. Telling people to check Yelp is lazy and unhelpful.

---

### Question 9

You discover a small crack in one of the passenger rails during your morning inspection. It doesn't seem structural, the rail is still solid, but the crack is visible.

A) Note it in the chat and run the ride, it's not structural
B) Pull the vehicle from service and report it to management immediately
C) Wrap it with tape so guests don't cut themselves and run the ride
D) Wait until the monthly deep inspection to address it

**Best answer: B.** You can't determine on the spot whether a crack is structural or not. A crack in a passenger rail is a safety item. The vehicle gets pulled until someone qualified evaluates it. Tape and hope is not a safety strategy.

### Question 10

A passenger is posting videos of the ride on Instagram and tags your business. The video shows everyone having a great time, but you can also see that one passenger is standing while the vehicle is moving.

A) Great content, share it to the business page
B) Don't share it, the standing passenger is a liability issue on camera
C) Share it but crop out the standing person
D) Ask the guest to take it down

**Best answer: B.** Social media content showing safety violations is a liability nightmare. It documents that a rule was broken on your watch. Don't share it, don't amplify it. You should also reflect on why that passenger was standing while you were driving, because that shouldn't have happened in the first place.
