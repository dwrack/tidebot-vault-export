### Question 1

During your morning inspection, you notice the right brake light is out on Vehicle 2. Everything else checks out fine. What do you do?

A) Run the rides on Vehicle 2, it's just one brake light and you still have the left one
B) Pull Vehicle 2 from service and reassign rides to Vehicle 1 or 3
C) Run the rides but drive extra carefully and tell passengers to hold on tighter
D) Replace the bulb yourself if you have one, and if it works, the vehicle can go out

**Best answer: B.** A non-functional brake light means other drivers can't see you braking properly. On a slow-moving vehicle in traffic, that's a serious risk. The vehicle is pulled until the light is fixed. If you have the ability and parts to fix it and verify it's working, that's fine, but "just drive careful" isn't a substitute for functioning safety equipment.

### Question 2

You're mid-ride when a car rear-ends your vehicle at a stoplight. It's a minor bump, no one seems injured, and the car driver is apologetic. What's the correct sequence?

A) Check if passengers are okay, exchange info with the car driver, finish the ride, file a report later
B) Check passengers, call 911, move out of traffic if safe, contact management, exchange info, document everything, file report same day
C) Check passengers, exchange info, take photos, call management, continue the ride if everyone's fine
D) Call management first, let them tell you what to do

**Best answer: B.** Any collision gets the full protocol. Even a minor bump can result in delayed injury claims. Call 911 (police report required for any collision), document everything, contact management. Do not continue the ride as if nothing happened. Passengers may feel fine now and have whiplash tomorrow.

### Question 3

What regulatory framework governs NOLA Bike Bar operations?

A) USCG regulations and maritime law
B) City of New Orleans business permits, vehicle registration, traffic law, and commercial vehicle insurance
C) Department of Natural Resources boating regulations
D) Federal commercial vehicle regulations (DOT/FMCSA)

**Best answer: B.** NOLA Bike Bar is a street vehicle operation. No boats, no water, no USCG, no maritime anything. City permits, vehicle registration, traffic law, and commercial insurance are what govern this business.

### Question 4

A passenger trips getting off the vehicle at the end of the ride. They say they're fine, wave it off, and walk away with their group. What do you do?

A) Nothing, they said they're fine
B) Ask if they need first aid, then document the incident in an internal report
C) Only document it if they ask for medical attention
D) Mention it to management the next day if you remember

**Best answer: B.** Every incident gets documented, even "minor" ones. A trip-and-fall today becomes a lawsuit next month when the ankle swelling hasn't gone down. Offer first aid, note their response, and file the internal report same day.

### Question 5

You're checking waiver status in FareHarbor and one guest in a group of 12 hasn't signed. The group is getting antsy and the unsigned guest says "I'll sign it after the ride, I promise." What do you do?

A) Let them ride and have them sign after, it's just one person
B) Pull up their booking-specific waiver on your phone and have them sign right now before the ride starts
C) Use a generic QR code to speed things up
D) Have another group member sign on their behalf

**Best answer: B.** No one rides without a signed waiver. No exceptions. No "I'll do it later." Pull up their specific booking waiver and hand them the phone. Takes 60 seconds. Generic QR codes are not permitted. Someone else cannot sign for them.

### Question 6

During a ride, a passenger reaches out and slaps a street sign as you pass it, nearly falling out of their seat. Other passengers laugh. What's your response?

A) Laugh it off but tell them not to do it again
B) Stop the vehicle, address it directly and firmly, explain that hands must stay inside at all times, and confirm everyone understands before continuing
C) Keep driving and mention it at the end of the ride
D) Pull over and call management

**Best answer: B.** This is a near-miss safety incident. Stop the vehicle. Address it without anger but with zero ambiguity. "I need everyone to keep their hands and arms inside the vehicle at all times. What just happened could have resulted in a serious injury. I need your agreement before we keep going." Then resume.

### Question 7

Which of the following is an immediate termination offense?

A) Being 10 minutes late to a ride
B) Playing music a guest complained about
C) Operating a vehicle that failed morning inspection
D) Forgetting to offer a photo opportunity before the ride

**Best answer: C.** Operating a vehicle that failed inspection is a termination-level safety violation. Being late, music choices, and skipping a photo op are performance issues that get addressed through coaching, not termination.

### Question 8

Your vehicle insurance policy expired yesterday and you just noticed. You have a ride in 2 hours and the renewal is "being processed."

A) Run the ride since the renewal is in process, you're technically covered during the grace period
B) Cancel the ride. No current insurance means the vehicle doesn't go out, regardless of what's "being processed"
C) Call the insurance company and get verbal confirmation, then run the ride
D) Transfer the ride to a different vehicle that has current insurance

**Best answer: B.** Lapsed insurance means the vehicle doesn't move. "Being processed" is not current coverage. If another vehicle has current insurance and is available (answer D), that could work, but the correct first instinct is: no insurance, no ride. Don't rely on verbal confirmations or grace period assumptions.

### Question 9

What's the purpose of the monthly Vehicle Safety Checklist compared to the daily Morning Inspection?

A) They check the same things, the monthly one is just more formal
B) The monthly goes deeper into structural, mechanical, and documentation items that daily checks don't cover, like frame integrity, tread depth measurements, and permit/registration currency
C) The monthly is for management, the daily is for drivers
D) The monthly replaces the daily for that day

**Best answer: B.** The daily check catches obvious, visible issues. The monthly goes deeper: frame stress, electrical system condition, precise tread depth, steering linkage, and verifying that all documentation (insurance, registration, permits) is current. They're complementary, not redundant.

### Question 10

You're at an intersection and a drunk pedestrian stumbles in front of your vehicle. You brake hard. A passenger spills their drink and bumps their elbow on the rail. The pedestrian walks away. What do you do?

A) Check on the passenger, offer first aid, continue the ride, and file an incident report at the end of the day
B) Nothing, no collision occurred and the passenger just bumped their elbow
C) Stop, check on the passenger, offer first aid, ask if they want to continue or end the ride, file an incident report same day regardless of their answer
D) Call 911 since a pedestrian was involved

**Best answer: C.** A passenger was hurt (even mildly) during an emergency braking event. That gets documented. Check on them, offer the first aid kit, and give them the option to continue or not. The incident report captures what happened in case the elbow turns into something more later. No need for 911 since no one was struck and the injury is minor.
