## The top 5, in plain language

**Contractor estimate + invoice (23, Fit 3).** The incumbent (Invoice Simple, Invoice2go, Joist, SimplyWise) makes a plumber type line items into a form after a job. The AI-native version photographs the job site or listens to the walkthrough and produces the invoice. It wins because the entire interface is a form — delete it. What kills it: Intuit owns QuickBooks, which owns small-business accounting distribution already, and they will ship this feature into the product he already uses rather than let a standalone app win. Highest merit on the board. Not the easiest to actually land as an outsider.

**Camera roll cleaner (22, Fit 2).** The incumbent makes you swipe through thousands of photos one at a time, bribing you with a free trial that auto-renews. The AI-native version just does it — sort, dedupe, suggest deletes, done. Wins because Cleanup, Cleaner Guru, and three more clones all do the same job the same bad way and none of them is meaningfully smarter than the others. What kills it: Apple could ship this straight into Photos, and winning it as an outsider means buying consumer App Store traffic against funded incumbents, which David has never done.

**GPS audio tour (22, Fit 5).** The incumbent (GuideAlong, Parkwolf) sells a pre-recorded narration track triggered by GPS location for a specific park or city. The AI-native version generates the narration live from whatever's actually around the guest, in whatever depth they ask for, for any route — not just the dozen cities GuideAlong bothered to record. It wins because David already has the thing that's expensive to build: real routes, real local knowledge, 12 GBP listings, and guides who've narrated these exact waters hundreds of times. What kills it: the category's small. Two apps in the top 18 isn't a stack, it's two solo operators splitting a modest market. This is a smaller prize than the invoice or cleaner opportunity. It is also the only one where distribution isn't the hard part.

**Recipe capture + meal planner (20, Fit 2).** The incumbent makes you manually re-type a recipe from a screenshot or a cookbook photo. The AI-native version reads the photo and builds the plan. It wins because this chart just proved nobody's won it — four of nine names turned over in six weeks. What kills it: there's no consumer recipe audience David owns, and this field is now actively volatile, which usually means a well-funded entrant is about to eat it.

**Scanner that skips the PDF (20, Fit 2).** The incumbent (CamScanner, Adobe Scan, Tiny Scanner) scans a document into a PDF you then have to read yourself. The AI-native version skips the PDF and gives you the extracted answer: the total, the date, the clause. It wins because five apps are grossing doing the same scan-to-PDF job with no differentiation. What kills it: this field already has aggressive AI feature-shipping from Adobe, and it's cold consumer distribution either way.

## What I'd do

Build the GPS audio tour. It is not the biggest number on this board, Contractor invoice and Camera roll cleaner both score higher, and I'd be lying if I said otherwise. But those two require beating Intuit or buying App Store traffic against funded clones, and David has never won either game. The audio tour is the one spot on the whole chart where the moat is distribution, and distribution is the one thing David already has sitting in four markets. Two apps splitting a small, stable, un-grown category for six straight weeks means there's room and no urgency. Say the word and I'll write the PRD.
