# Sentry Tower — Scan 001 (Aug 28, 2026)

Hunted the App Store top-grossing charts across 12 categories, the Product Hunt August leaderboard, and Show HN launches over 100 points. Looking for one thing: proven paid demand sitting on top of a job that is now a single model call.

## The headline finding

The overall top charts are worthless for this. YouTube, Netflix, Monopoly Go, Peacock. Nothing rebuildable.

The money is in the **category** charts, and one pattern shows up over and over: **clone stacks**. A category chart where four, five, seven near-identical apps all grossing in the top 18 is not fragmentation. It is a demand signal so loud that nobody has won yet, and every one of them is running a pre-LLM interface.

Three clone stacks stood out:

| Category | Clone stack | Count in top 18 |
|---|---|---|
| Business | Invoice / estimate makers | 5 |
| Utilities | Phone + photo storage cleaners | 5 |
| Food & Drink | Recipe savers / meal planners | 7 |

Plus a fourth in Business: six separate document-scanner apps (CamScanner, iScanner, Adobe Scan, Tiny Scanner, Scanner App, Smart Printer).

## Proof the thesis already prints money

**Cal AI** is #10 top-grossing in Health & Fitness. It is a photo-of-your-plate calorie tracker built by a solo-ish team, sitting nine slots below MyFitnessPal, which has a 2012 barcode-and-search interface and a decade of brand. Cal AI did not out-feature MyFitnessPal. It deleted the interface. That is the whole play.

**PictureThis** is #3 top-grossing in Education. It is a plant identifier. One vision call, wrapped in a subscription. It out-grosses Quizlet, Pimsleur, and Babbel.

Two data points, same shape: take a category where the incumbent makes you do the work, and replace the work with a model.

## The frontier, for timing

Product Hunt's August leaderboard is entirely agents: Hey Noah (proactive AI EA, 614), Clipto MCP (604), AdAnt AI (595), Astute (582), Grok Bot (547). Top of Show HN is **Needle2, a 14MB agentic LLM that runs on phones and wearables** (537 points). That matters here specifically. The cleaner and scanner plays need on-device vision to avoid uploading a user's entire camera roll, and 14MB on-device just became real.

Also on PH: **x1**, "Lovable for iPhone apps, go from idea to App Store" (500). The build cost for the thing below is falling while you read this.

## 1. Contractor estimate + invoice — 23

**What's there now:** Invoice Maker (Labhouse) #8, Invoice Simple #9, SimplyWise Cost Estimator #10, Invoice Maker/GetPaid #13, Invoice2go #17, Joist #16. Six apps doing the same thing in the Business top 18. Plus QuickBooks at #4 sitting above all of them.

**What they make you do:** Open the app on the job site. Tap "new invoice." Type the client. Type line items. Type quantities. Type prices you have to remember. Pick a tax rate. Send.

**AI-native version:** The contractor walks the job and talks. "Master bath, tear out the tile, forty square feet, new subfloor, supply and install porcelain, they picked the mid-grade." Phone listens, prices it against his own history of past jobs, produces a line-itemed estimate with a photo of the space attached, sends it before he is back in the truck. When the job is done, "invoice the Miller bathroom, add the two extra hours Thursday" and it goes out.

**Why it wins:** The competitor's entire product is a form. Forms are the single most collapsible interface in software. And the buyer is a trade with real money whose alternative is doing paperwork at 9pm.

**Risk:** QuickBooks. If Intuit ships this, it is over. They probably will, badly and slowly.

## 2. Camera roll cleaner — 22

**What's there now:** Cleanup #1, Cleaner Guru #3, AI Cleaner #10, Cleanup App #12, Swipewipe #14. Five of the top 18 grossing Utilities apps, and #1 in the whole category.

**What they make you do:** They mostly find duplicates and big videos. They are also, as a class, notorious subscription traps. Bad apps making a fortune off a real pain.

**AI-native version:** On-device vision reads every photo. Not "these two files are identical" but "these are the eleven near-identical shots of your kid on the swing, here is the one where both eyes are open." Screenshots you never looked at again. Receipts you photographed in 2023. Blurry, dark, accidental pocket shots. It shows you 400 photos to delete in one pass, ranked by confidence, and you swipe.

**Why it wins:** #1 grossing in Utilities with a product that barely works. The 14MB on-device model from Show HN means you can do this without ever uploading someone's photos, which is also the marketing.

**Risk:** Apple ships it into Photos. Also the hardest one to get on the charts without paid UA.

## 3. Recipe capture + meal planner — 20

**What's there now:** ReciMe #1, mise #3, Osta #13, MealPrepPro #14, Pepper #15, Mealime #16, Mob #17. Seven meal planners in the top 18 Food & Drink grossing.

**The tell:** ReciMe is #1, above NYT Cooking. ReciMe's pitch is saving recipes off social. That means the job people are paying for is not "give me recipes." It is "I saw a thing on TikTok and I will never find it again."

**AI-native version:** Send it anything. A TikTok link, a screenshot of a cookbook page, a voice note of your grandmother's directions. It extracts the actual recipe, normalizes units, scales it, plans the week around what you already have, and builds one grocery list. Then, mid-week: "I have chicken thighs, half a cabbage, and 30 minutes."

**Why it wins:** Extraction from messy multi-modal input is the thing models got good at and the thing seven incumbents are all doing with brittle scrapers.

## 4. Scanner that skips the PDF — 20

**What's there now:** CamScanner #7 Productivity, iScanner #3 Business, Adobe Scan #12, Tiny Scanner #18, Scanner App #5, Smart Printer #15.

**The insight:** Nobody wants a PDF. A PDF is the format you accept because the app cannot understand the paper. Photograph a lease and the honest product is not a scan, it is "your rent goes up 4% in March and there is a 60-day notice clause." Photograph a receipt and it is a filed, categorized expense, not a file named Scan_004.pdf.

**Risk:** This one is genuinely crowded with AI entrants already.

## 5. GPS audio tour — 21, and the one you should actually consider

**What's there now:** GuideAlong (GyPSy Guide) #11 in Travel grossing. Parkwolf #17. Wanderlog #7. Small category, real revenue.

**How it works today:** A production company drives a route, hires a voice talent, records narration, cuts it, GPS-triggers the clips. One route costs real money and months. That is why GuideAlong covers a few dozen parks and nothing else.

**AI-native version:** Generate the route. Pull the local history, the geology, the weird stories. Voice it. Trigger it by GPS. A route costs an afternoon instead of a quarter. Then it is not one narration, it is the version for a family with a bored 9-year-old, or the version that is all food, or the version in Spanish.

**Why this one is different for you:** Every other target on this list requires you to win at consumer App Store distribution, which you have never done and have no list for. This one you already own the inputs to. You run tours in New Orleans, Houston, Austin, and Door County. You have the local knowledge, the routes, the guides who know the stories, the review-generating machine, twelve GBP listings, and past-customer lists across every one of those cities. You could ship Door County and New Orleans as real products, sell them to your own past guests, and have revenue before you ever fight for a chart position.

It is also the smallest market on the list. That is the honest tradeoff: #1 has ten times the ceiling and you would be starting from zero on distribution.

## 6-10, briefly

**6. Voice-to-action assistant.** Pocket AI is #6 in Utilities grossing and Hey Noah was the #1 Product Hunt launch of August. A top-grossing incumbent and the month's biggest launch doing the same job is a live category, which cuts both ways.

**7. Money coach.** Five budgeting apps in the Finance top 18. But the moat here is Plaid plumbing and bank connections, not UX, and the AI layer is the easy part. Weakest "Open" score on the list.

**8. Identify-anything camera.** PictureThis at #3 in Education proves people pay for one vision call. But that means everyone already knows, and the differentiation is zero.

**9. AI study companion.** Gauth is already AI-native at #9. Crowded, and the buyer is a teenager.

**10. Fax and print utilities.** Three of them in Business grossing. Pure legacy rent. Almost no AI content, so no wedge. Listed because the demand is funny, not because you should build it.
