### 1. movie-gen: full AI films on the stack you already run
- **What changed:** A single open-source pipeline (MIT) now chains Seedance 2.0 video, Nano Banana Pro images, Sonilo music, ElevenLabs voice, and Gemini quality-control through Higgsfield's CLI and ffmpeg. It's built to be driven by Claude Code, not clicked by hand. The maintainer has ~10 finished films already, one is 11 minutes and 80 shots with characters that stay consistent across cuts.
- **Who it's for:** Anyone making branded video who's stuck at the 8-second single-clip ceiling. That's you across 12 businesses.
- **Why now:** The jump from "generate a clip" to "generate a multi-shot film with continuity, orchestrated by an agent" is the line that just got crossed. Consistent characters across shots was the wall; this steps over it.
- **Own-the-ground angle:** It's your pipeline on your machine producing your footage. The paid pieces (Seedance, Higgsfield, ElevenLabs) are durable APIs you already pay for, and the orchestration + the finished films + the distribution are 100% yours. Nobody can revoke a film you already rendered.
- **Cheap first test:** Clone it, point Claude Code at it, hand it one real DCKT or NKST shot list plus a few frames of actual tour footage as reference, and let it build one 2-3 minute branded piece this week. See how close the character/scene consistency gets before you trust it on a real campaign.
- **Fit:** This is the rare one where your operator edge is the input, not a nice-to-have. You have real drone shots, real guides, real locations across four states. A software-only founder generating tour videos has to fake all of that. You feed it the real thing. Doesn't close the desk-to-mobile gap, but it's the sharpest content-engine unlock on the board.
- Source: [Hacker News](https://github.com/dawndrain/movie-gen) · scores: newly-possible 4/5, momentum 3/5, own-ground 4/5, fit 5/5, run-with-it 5/5

### 2. Kimi-K3 open weights: frontier quality you can't get evicted from
- **What changed:** Moonshot published the full Kimi-K3 weights on HuggingFace today under Apache 2.0. It's the first open-weight model in the ~3-trillion-parameter class, MXFP4 quantized, and benchmarks put it neck-and-neck with Fable 5 on agentic and coding work. HN had it on the front page (540 points) within hours, and Fireworks and Artificial Analysis both corroborate the quality.
- **Who it's for:** Anyone building agent loops who's nervous about being locked to one lab's pricing and terms. That nervousness is rational.
- **Why now:** Open weights at frontier quality is the actual event. Last week Kimi was hosted-only benchmark chatter. Today the weights are downloadable, so any provider can serve it and no single vendor owns the off switch.
- **Own-the-ground angle:** This is the own-the-ground story. You can run your SMS/voice loops on a frontier brain that Moonshot, Google, or Anthropic can't price-hike or cut off, because the weights are out and portable across hosts. You're not self-hosting 2.8T parameters on your own box (nobody is), but you're also not renting from a single landlord.
- **Cheap first test:** Swap it in as the model behind one existing Twilio SMS or voice loop via an open-weight host like Fireworks or Together. Pennies to run. Compare its answers to whatever you're paying more for now on a real booking-FAQ task.
- **Fit:** Your edge here is partial, any builder benefits from a cheap open frontier model. It ranks this high on newly-possible and momentum, not on David-specificity. Pairs directly with last week's Flash-Lite spark: same play, more durable ground.
- Source: [Hacker News](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3) · scores: newly-possible 5/5, momentum 5/5, own-ground 4/5, fit 3/5, run-with-it 3/5

### 3. OpenWorker: an ops agent that lives on your machine
- **What changed:** Andrew Ng's team released OpenWorker, an open-source (MIT) desktop agent that delivers finished work, real documents, spreadsheets, calendar updates, not just chat. It plugs into 25+ tools (Gmail, Notion, Slack, Jira), runs entirely on your machine, works from your local files, supports scheduled automations, and asks for approval before anything consequential goes out. Bring your own model key or run fully local through Ollama.
- **Who it's for:** An operator drowning in back-office across a dozen entities. Your data stays home, which matters when it's booking sheets and customer contacts.
- **Why now:** "Open, local, ships finished artifacts, from a credible name" is a combination that wasn't sitting there a week ago. Most agents either live in someone's cloud or stop at a chat reply.
- **Own-the-ground angle:** MIT license, runs on hardware you own, optional fully-local models, data leaves only through the integrations you choose. About as owned as an agent gets.
- **Cheap first test:** Install from source, wire up one business's Gmail plus Notion, and set one scheduled morning task, draft overnight-inquiry replies, or update a booking tracker. Keep the approval gate on. See if it saves a real 20 minutes before you scale it.
- **Fit:** Strong but not unique, any coder could stand this up. Your fit is having 12 businesses' worth of repetitive ops to point it at. This is the "handle the implementation details, save David's energy for big thinking" play in a box.
- Source: [TLDR](https://github.com/andrewyng/openworker) · scores: newly-possible 4/5, momentum 3/5, own-ground 5/5, fit 4/5, run-with-it 4/5
