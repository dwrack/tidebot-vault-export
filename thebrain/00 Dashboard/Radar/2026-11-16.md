## Sparks (run with these)

### 1. Give agents a front door on your own pages
- **What changed:** Agent traffic is real and concentrated. Meta and OpenAI agents made about 90% of agent requests in HUMAN Security's October data, 85% of them landing on product and search pages. Big sellers are splitting: United blocks agents, Shopify invites them.
- **Who it's for:** Small tour operators whose site is a FareHarbor embed plus thin pages. An agent asked "kayak tour near New Orleans Saturday under $80" has little to read.
- **Why now:** Muse crossed into mass agent browsing and calling this month, and Black Friday gift-card season is two weeks out.
- **Own-the-ground angle:** The readable layer lives on your own WordPress sites: price, duration, what's included, cancellation, and the FareHarbor link with `ref=`. FareHarbor stays the rented checkout, the facts stay yours. This matches the Website Standards (visible all-in prices).
- **Cheap first test:** Pull Cloudflare or server logs for NPB and DCKT, count Muse and OpenAI agent hits this week, then rewrite one top tour page so every fact an agent needs sits in plain text above the book button. Compare agent hits and referrals in two weeks. One catch: Muse moved its Web Bot Auth signatures from meta.com to muse.ai, so a bot rule that trusts only meta.com may block it.
- **Fit:** An operator knows the facts agents get wrong (meeting point, weather policy, group size). A software-only founder doesn't.
- Source: [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/meta-muse-ai-agent-requests-human-security/597388/) · scores: newly-possible 4/5, momentum 5/5, own-ground 4/5, fit 4/5, run-with-it 4/5 (46)

### 2. Make the phone line ready for AI callers
- **What changed:** Muse can call US businesses for $20/month and up. Gemini already could. The article says many receptionists hang up when they hear an AI caller.
- **Who it's for:** Any business where the phone is how people book or ask a weather question, which is most of your portfolio. NPB missed calls is already an open project.
- **Why now:** Calling moved from early-adopter access to anyone who pays on Nov 12.
- **Own-the-ground angle:** Your Twilio numbers and OpenCX phone agents. An agent that answers any caller, human or not, with the same facts and logs it as a lead.
- **Cheap first test:** Use Gemini's calling feature to ask an NPB line about Saturday availability. Note who answers, what they say, and whether anything lands in OpenCX. Fix the answers, not the tech.
- **Fit:** You own the numbers and the phone-agent stack already. Also feeds the desk-to-mobile gap, since the callers are people on phones.
- Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/muse-can-now-place-calls-to-u-s-businesses-if-you-pay/) · scores: newly-possible 4/5, momentum 4/5, own-ground 4/5, fit 5/5 (+1 desk-mobile bonus, capped), run-with-it 4/5 (46)

### 3. Audit which shipped changes moved a number
- **What changed:** AppSumo's founder found only 4 of 67 shipped changes moved a key metric, and shared the Claude prompt he used. Age note: it is a prompt, not a capability, so newly-possible is low. It earns the slot on run-with-it.
- **Who it's for:** Anyone shipping constantly across many brands, which is you.
- **Why now:** The Website Standards rollout (visible prices, short checkout, newsletter opt-in) is exactly the kind of batch you want proof on.
- **Own-the-ground angle:** Your GA4 and your change log. Nothing rented.
- **Cheap first test:** Pick DCKT, list the last 30 days of site changes with dates, pull GA4 conversions before and after each, and let Claude rank them. Kill whatever didn't move.
- **Fit:** Strong. Twelve businesses means shipped work you can't remember.
- Source: [Noah Kagan on X, via TLDR Founders](https://x.com/noahkagan/status/2121379356207464887) · scores: newly-possible 2/5, momentum 3/5, own-ground 5/5, fit 4/5, run-with-it 5/5 (40)
