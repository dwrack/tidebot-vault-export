### 1. Grok Voice Agent Builder puts a phone agent on your own line in 2 minutes

Plain-language setup, ~2 minutes to a working agent, sub-second latency, 25+ languages with mid-call switching, and SIP so it rides a number you already control. All-in cost is about $0.06/min.

- **Why now:** Voice agents were a developer-only API stack six months ago. The no-code builder plus direct SIP integration landed this month, which is what moves this from "engineering project" to "set it up Tuesday."
- **Own-the-ground angle:** Do not build on xAI's provisioned number. Wire it into David's existing Twilio spine and DIDs. The agent, the call logs, the knowledge docs, and the phone numbers stay his. xAI is a swappable brain behind a line he owns.
- **Cheap first test:** Take one spare Twilio number, feed the builder a single business FAQ (hours, launch points, booking link), and let it field the NPB or DCKT after-hours line for a weekend. Pennies per call. Listen to the recordings Monday.
- **Fit:** This is the desk-to-mobile gap closing. David's AI leverage has been trapped behind a keyboard while the business happens on the water and on the phone. He already owns the Twilio backbone. An operator who knows exactly what a booking call sounds like will out-configure a software-only founder here.

### 2. On-device AI that knows when it's wrong (Cactus Hybrid)
- **What changed:** Cactus embedded confidence probes into a small local model (Gemma 4 E2B) that read a correctness signal straight from the hidden state. It scores 0-1 on every answer and only escalates to the cloud when it is unsure. AUROC 0.814 versus 0.549 for the usual token-entropy trick. Result: 45-85% of queries stay on the device while matching Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite quality.
- **Who it's for:** Anyone who wants a private, offline-capable assistant that does not phone home for every question. Field ops, spotty-signal locations, privacy-sensitive workflows.
- **Why now:** "Small local model" used to mean "wrong and confident about it." A model that flags its own uncertainty is the piece that made on-device trustworthy enough to ship. Open source, runs on llama.cpp / MLX, quantized down to 3-bit.
- **Own-the-ground angle:** This is the most ownable thing on the board. Open weights, runs on hardware David buys once, no API that can raise prices or cut him off. The whole point is that the cloud is optional.
- **Cheap first test:** Load it on a phone or a cheap edge box and point it at one real DCKT task, tide and trip-time Q&A. Measure what fraction never leaves the device. If it is 70%+, a private guide-in-your-pocket assistant is suddenly a hardware problem, not a research problem.
- **Fit:** Closes the desk-to-mobile gap from the other direction: intelligence that lives on the device in the guide's hand, not on a server. David's edge is knowing which questions guides and guests actually ask.
- Source: [GitHub](https://github.com/cactus-compute/cactus-hybrid) · scores: newly-possible 5/5, momentum 3/5, own-ground 5/5, fit 4/5, run-with-it 3/5

### 3. Real-time inference got cheap enough to leave running (Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite)
- **What changed:** Google shipped Flash-Lite at $0.30 per million input, $2.50 per million output, at 350 tokens/sec, with real jumps in coding and long context. That is roughly a nickel to hold a long conversation. Always-on agent loops just stopped being a budget question.
- **Who it's for:** Anyone who wanted an agent watching a stream of events (bookings, texts, inbox) but balked at the token bill.
- **Why now:** Price and speed both crossed a line the same week. At this rate you can run a loop 24/7 per business without thinking about cost, which changes what is worth automating.
- **Own-the-ground angle:** It is a rented API, so keep the logic, prompts, and data in David's own code and treat the model as a swappable engine. The moat is the workflow and the customer data, not the model.
- **Cheap first test:** Drop it in as the brain behind a lightweight SMS or voice loop on the Twilio line for one business and watch a week of real spend. It will be trivial.
- **Fit:** Partial. Any coder can call the API, so the edge is not the model, it is David's operator knowledge of what the loop should actually do. Pairs naturally with Spark 1.
