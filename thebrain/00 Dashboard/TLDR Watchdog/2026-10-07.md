## 1. AI Overviews are measurably cutting organic clicks

**Source:** "The evidence is clear: AI search is costing publishers" (Digital Content Next, 4 min read), via TLDR Marketing 2026-10-07. [Link](https://digitalcontentnext.org/blog/2026/10/05/the-evidence-is-clear-ai-search-is-costing-publishers/?utm_source=tldrmarketing)

**The numbers:** One study found AI Overviews reduced outbound organic clicks by 39.8%. Pew found traditional-result clicks fell from 15% to 8% when an AI summary appeared on a search result page. Separately, ChatGPT generated just 0.02% of publisher referral traffic compared with 7.3% from Google Search — AI referrals aren't making up the gap.

**Why it matters here:** NKST and DCKT both depend on blog-driven organic traffic as a core acquisition channel — NKST's entire watched-page strategy (the "Airboat vs Kayak" comparison post) is a bet on ranking for informational queries. If AI Overviews are increasingly answering those same questions before a user ever clicks through, holding position 1-10 on GSC stops being a reliable proxy for traffic. The brief currently reports GSC position and clicks together, but a page can hold its position and still lose the click to an AI summary — that distinction isn't currently visible in any of our reporting.

**First move:** When reviewing GSC for NKST and DCKT monthly, start manually spot-checking whether top queries now trigger an AI Overview in a live search (no API for this yet, has to be eyeballed). If a high-impression query like NKST's "are there hippos in louisiana" (363 impressions, pos 9.3, only 2 clicks) is getting answered by an Overview, that's a different fix than a ranking problem — it needs a more citable, FAQ-structured answer, not just better position.

## 2. Five concrete tips for optimizing existing content for ChatGPT citation

**Source:** "5 tips for optimizing existing content for ChatGPT" (2 min read), via TLDR Marketing 2026-10-07. [Link](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7512844858777120768/?utm_source=tldrmarketing)

**The tactics:**
1. Lead with the answer before adding context, so key claims are easy to extract.
2. Use clear statements instead of hedging.
3. Mention relevant brands and competitors for context.
4. Turn headings into specific questions that mirror user queries.
5. Refresh old posts with new data, screenshots, and sections — not just a new publish date.

**Why it matters here:** This is a cheap, no-new-content-required pass over pages that already exist and already rank. It pairs directly with item #1 above — if AI answer engines are where a growing share of informational queries get resolved, these are the mechanical changes that make existing content more likely to be the source an LLM pulls from.

**First move:** Treat NKST's `/alligators-vs-crocodiles/` post (91 views / 8d, the highest-traffic blog post on the site per this run's GA4 pull) as a test case. Rewrite its subheadings as direct questions ("Are alligators more dangerous than crocodiles?" instead of a generic heading), move the direct answer to the first sentence under each heading, and add one fresh data point or image. No new copy structure needed beyond that — this is an editing pass, not a rewrite.

## 3. Google AI Overviews now appear on ~80% of branded searches

**Source:** "4 SEO strategies for AI-driven brand search" (1 min read, LinkedIn/Chris Long), via TLDR Marketing 2026-10-07. [Link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/chris-long-marketing_google-has-massively-increased-the-number-share-7512871595363749888-kuU2/?utm_source=tldrmarketing)

**The strategies:** Keep brand messaging consistent across owned channels so AI systems get clear context. Build content to fill gaps where third-party sources currently shape the AI's answer. Monitor competitor content and review sites, since negative claims can shape AI-generated brand answers. Maintain an accurate "AI instructions" page as a direct, citable source of brand information.

**Why it matters here:** This doesn't contradict anything in the vault — it reinforces the [[AEO Action Plan - All Sites]]'s existing #1 priority (real guide bios with named experts and photos, so AI systems have something concrete to cite) and its broader push for consistent, structured brand information across every site. Flagging it here because the 80% figure is a sharper number than what's cited in the current plan, useful for framing why this work matters when discussing priorities.

**First move:** None beyond what the AEO plan already calls for. This is confirmation the plan's direction is correct, not a reason to add new scope.
