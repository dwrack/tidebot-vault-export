## 1. Video length: Instagram is flat under 3 minutes, TikTok rewards 3 to 10 minutes

**Source:** "How long should a Reel be? Under 3 minutes on Instagram, longer on TikTok," virev.ai, 12 min read. https://virev.ai/blog/how-long-should-a-reel-be (via TLDR Marketing 10/16)

**The finding:**
- Instagram Reels from 10 seconds to 3 minutes all perform within 2% of each creator's own median. Past 3 minutes, Reels fall to 0.91x and recommendations to new audiences stop.
- TikTok rewards length. 3-to-10-minute videos earn 1.22x the creator's median, and beat the same creator's 10-to-19-second clips for 68% of creators.
- The comparison is within-creator (each creator's own median), so it controls for account size. It's still correlational: creators who post 3-minute TikToks may just be better at TikTok.

**Why it cleared the bar (rule 1, contradicts a written plan):**
- The morning-brief playbook itself says the winning HPB TikTok formula is 7-10 second clips with a hook, a question, a 👀, a search term, and a Seabrook/Clear Lake geo tag. That formula is built on what has worked for @houstonpedalbarge so far, not on a length test.
- The daily video factory plan (8/27/26, 4-5 videos a day per brand) and the 532 organic system both lean on short-form. Nothing in either doc argues for long TikToks.
- The Instagram half is reassuring for the factory: length doesn't matter under 3 minutes, so a 45-second cut and a 2-minute cut should reach the same people. The only hard rule is stay under 3 minutes.

**What it does not say:** that short HPB clips are wrong. The 7-10 second formula produces search-discoverable clips cheaply, and the account's converting search terms ("houston party barge", "boat lunch houston", "Houston bridal weekend", "bachelorette") work in captions regardless of length.

**First move:**
1. Re-log the Playwright browser into TikTok (QR) so the HPB pull works again.
2. Post one 3-plus-minute HPB TikTok: a full Seabrook bachelorette cruise walkthrough, boarding to sunset, with "houston party barge" and "bachelorette" in the caption and the Seabrook geo tag. Same week, post the usual short clips.
3. Compare 7-day views, profile views, and search-source share in TikTok Studio. If the long one wins or ties, add one long TikTok a week to the HPB slot mix. If it loses by a wide margin, drop it and keep the formula.
4. For every brand's Reels: cap at 3 minutes. No other length rule.

## 2. Google Postmaster Tools scores unsubscribe compliance across the whole sending domain

**Source:** "Your newsletter isn't the unsubscribe problem," audiencebridge.io, 3 min read. https://www.audiencebridge.io/p/your-newsletter-isn-t-the-unsubscribe-problem (via TLDR Marketing 10/16)

**The finding:**
- Postmaster Tools evaluates unsubscribe compliance across all email systems sending from the same domain, not per list.
- If someone unsubscribes from a newsletter but keeps getting CRM, sales, or win-back mail from the same domain, Google can flag the domain.
- Fixes: sync unsubscribe lists across platforms, use distinct From addresses and List-IDs per mailing list, make sure every system supports one-click unsubscribe and removes recipients within 48 hours. Compliance status takes 1-2 weeks to update after a fix.

**Why it cleared the bar (rule 4, cheap concrete check; also rule 2, deliverability is a booking lever):**
- The standing rule across every vault already requires a hosted one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058 URL form, no mailto) and auto-suppression from all future sends. That rule was written for the win-back and newsletter sends.
- What it doesn't cover is the cross-system case: SES win-back (us-west-2), FareHarbor's transactional mail, the review-request flow, and any Gmail outreach all send from or on behalf of the same brand domains (doorcountykayaktours.com, nolapartybarges.com, neworleanskayakswamptours.com). One unsubscribe only lands in the SES suppression list.
- A domain flag would hit FareHarbor's booking confirmations too, which is the mail that actually matters.

**First move:**
1. Open Google Postmaster Tools for doorcountykayaktours.com and nolapartybarges.com (the two domains with the most bulk history). Read the compliance status panel. 10 minutes.
2. Confirm the SES account-level suppression list is what every win-back send checks against, and that the review SMS/email flow reads the same list. If the review flow has its own opt-out store, merge it.
3. Give each list its own From address and List-ID (win-back vs newsletter vs review requests) so Google can tell them apart.
4. Re-check Postmaster in two weeks.
