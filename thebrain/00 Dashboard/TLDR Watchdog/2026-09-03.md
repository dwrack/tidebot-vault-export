## 1. YouTube's AI-citation lead just got a lot more specific — and it complicates the 30-second-video bet

**Source:** [YouTube AEO Guide: How to Dominate AI Search with YouTube](https://nogood.io/blog/youtube-aeo-guide/?utm_source=tldrmarketing) (TLDR Marketing, 7 min read, 2026-09-03)

**What it says:** YouTube now draws roughly 200x more AI citations than any other platform, and its share of social citations grew 4x in four months to overtake Reddit as the top-cited social source in Q1 2026. Automatic transcripts are the driver — every explainer video becomes a searchable text document AI models can parse directly. The catch: that citation strength concentrates almost entirely in **long-form video**, since AI models weight depth and comprehensive topic coverage that Shorts aren't built to carry.

**Why it matters here:** [[AEO Action Plan - All Sites]] already made YouTube the centerpiece of the AEO bet — Priority 1 sends 30-second guide-intro videos to YouTube first, ahead of Reels and TikTok, specifically because "AI Overviews pull from YouTube heavily." That premise just got more specific in a way that cuts against the current format: if citation strength lives in long-form, comprehensive video and Shorts "aren't built to carry" it, a 30-second intro is close to the least citable format YouTube offers. The [[YouTube — Channel Audit & AEO Fix (Aug 2026)]] doc already flagged 882 videos and 2.18M views banked with two channels missing descriptions entirely — none of that inventory is long-form explainer content either.

**First move:** Don't abandon the 30-second-intro tactic (it still earns the vertical-platform cut-downs), but add a long-form companion asset per guide/tour — a 3-5 minute "what to expect" or FAQ-style explainer, YouTube-native, with a search-written title, a full description, and captions on. Start with one per business with an active YouTube channel (DCKT, NKST, HPB, NPB, AKT, Ebb & Ember, Bike Bar are all connected this session) rather than retrofitting the whole catalog at once.

## 2. Competitor sitemaps as a free weekly SEO change log

**Source:** [Turn Sitemaps Into SEO Intel](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/warren-hance-86869b122_another-unnecessarily-weird-seo-tip-use-share-7500432802899951618-rlL0/?utm_source=tldrmarketing) (TLDR Marketing, 1 min read, 2026-09-03)

**What it says:** Save a snapshot of a competitor's XML sitemap, then diff it weekly. New pages, removals, and emerging content clusters show up before they ever show up in rankings — a Google Sheet + Apps Script template is linked in the post's comment thread.

**Why it matters here:** Free, and directly useful for the businesses actively watching a named competitor — NKST vs. Wild Louisiana (the NKST GBP audit landing in today's inbox from Gravity Trails flags Wild Louisiana passing NKST on review count, 5.0★/1,785 reviews vs. NKST's 4.9★/1,401), and DCKT/Cave Point vs. whatever's driving the review-pattern complaints about route length. Seeing what a competitor is building before it ranks is a cheap early-warning system.

**First move:** Set up the sitemap diff for Wild Louisiana's domain first, since NKST already has an active competitive-tracking need this week. Extend to other markets only if the NKST version proves useful.
