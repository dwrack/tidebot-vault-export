## 1. Content freshness is a bigger AI-citation lever than expected

**Source:** TLDR Marketing, "The Impact of Content Freshness on AI Citations" (9 min read) — [nogood.io](https://nogood.io/blog/content-freshness-ai-citations/?utm_source=tldrmarketing)

**The finding:** Content cited by AI assistants runs 25.7% fresher than content that ranks organically on Google. Roughly half of all citations trace to material updated in the last 13 weeks. That edge decays fast — median citation half-life is about 4.5 weeks across platforms. Retrieval systems re-rank the entire candidate pool on every query rather than relying on a persistent ranking, so recency becomes an early filter for weeding out sources that might be stale or wrong. Perplexity and Grok weight freshness most heavily; Google's AI Overviews stay tied to core search and actually skew slightly older.

**Why it matters here:** Every AEO bet in the vault (the Priority 1 YouTube plan flagged Sep 3, the long-form-explainer pivot, the per-channel video strategy) assumes AI citation strength is mostly about format and depth. This data says timing matters just as much — a 3-month-old page has already fallen out of half the citation window regardless of how good it is. NKST's watched "Airboat vs Kayak" comparison page is a direct test case: it's been live since early June, impressions are climbing (57 over the last week) but clicks are flat at 2, and it's never gotten a refresh pass. If AI citation strength really has a ~4.5-week half-life, a page sitting untouched for 13 weeks needs a content refresh, not just more raw traffic, to stay eligible for citation.

**First move:** Before investing further in new long-form content per the Sep 3 AEO update, audit the 5-6 highest-traffic existing pages across DCKT, NKST, and NPB for last-modified date. Any page older than ~8-10 weeks gets a light refresh (updated stats, a new photo, a re-dated intro paragraph) before writing anything new. Cheap, and it directly targets the freshness filter this data describes.

## 2. Reddit's new ad format matches a channel that's already converting organically

**Source:** TLDR Marketing, "Capture Attention That Lasts With 15-Second Engaged Video Views" (3 min read) — [business.reddit.com](https://www.business.reddit.com/blog/15-second-engaged-video-views?utm_source=tldrmarketing)

**The finding:** Reddit launched a 15-second Engaged Video Views ad goal that optimizes toward users likely to watch for at least 15 seconds or to completion, aimed at longer-form creative like product demos and testimonials. Advertisers pay only for qualifying views. Alpha testers saw average gains of 51% in 15-second view-through rate and 71% in completion rate. Now globally available in public beta, with split testing against 6-second views.

**Why it matters here:** Today's NKST GA4 pull shows reddit.com sending only 3 sessions in the last day, but those 3 sessions logged 25 "conversions" — a wildly high conversion-to-session ratio compared to every other source (Google: 199 sessions/67 conversions; direct: 58/46; Instagram: 62/0). That's too small a sample to trust on its own, but it's the second time reddit.com traffic has shown outsized value in this GA4 property, and there's no paid presence on Reddit anywhere in the portfolio today. A cheap test campaign using this new format, aimed at a real customer testimonial or a "what's it actually like" video, would tell us fast whether that signal is real.

**First move:** Set up one small test campaign (NKST or NPB, whichever has better testimonial video on hand) using the new 15-second Engaged Video Views goal, capped at a low daily budget for two weeks, and watch whether it produces the same conversion density the organic reddit.com traffic is showing.
