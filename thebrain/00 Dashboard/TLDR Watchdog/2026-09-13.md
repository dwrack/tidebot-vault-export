## 1. Google launches GeoX, a free tool to test whether ads actually increase sales

**Source:** searchenginewatch.com, 2 min read — https://searchenginewatch.com/google-launches-tool-to-check-whether-ads-actually-increase-sales/ (via TLDR Marketing, 2026-09-11)

**What it says:** Google released an open-source tool, GeoX, that tests whether an ad campaign actually caused a sale rather than just which platform claims credit for one. It works by comparing regions where a campaign keeps running against regions where it's paused or given more budget, then measuring how sales in each group differ from what historical data would have predicted anyway. It can track spend across platforms beyond Google's own and combine online and offline sales in the same test, and it plugs into Google's existing Meridian measurement software.

**Why it matters here:** HPB's Google Ads account (customer 2986009791) has posted a 0.54 ROAS for two straight weeks — $148.35 spent over the last 7 days against just $80 in tracked conversion value, 2 conversions total. Today's brief flags pausing or rebuilding the campaign (Action Item #3), but that decision has been sitting unresolved across multiple prior briefs too, which suggests the real question hasn't been settled: is this actually a bad campaign, or is conversion tracking under-crediting real bookings that come in by phone or walk-up? A geo-holdout test is exactly the tool to answer that before permanently killing spend.

**First move:** Read the GeoX methodology this week and decide whether a manual geo split (or even just a manual pause/resume test on HPB's account, since it's a single small market) is worth running before the campaign gets killed outright. If the incrementality really is zero, that's the confirmation needed to pull the plug with confidence instead of a guess.

## 2. Simple AEO tip: use "People Also Asked" to find prompts to track

**Source:** LinkedIn (Tim Soulo), 1 min read — https://www.linkedin.com/posts/timsoulo_simple-aeo-tip-instead-of-guessing-which-share-7503719107930841088-TBqY/ (via TLDR Marketing, 2026-09-11)

**What it says:** Rather than using AI to generate a guessed list of prompts to track for AI-answer visibility, pull the actual "People Also Asked" questions that show up for the top Google searches in your niche — real, already-validated search demand instead of a synthetic list.

**Why it matters here:** `AEO Action Plan - All Sites.md` already prescribes tracking AI-answer prompts per site as part of the FAQ-schema and quotable-content push. This is a cheap, concrete refinement to how that prompt list gets built — real PAA data instead of guesswork — and it costs nothing but time. NKST and DCKT are the two sites this run flagged as still having weak or cannibalized brand-term ranks (NKST's own brand name sits at #20.2; Door County Ebike is still competing with DCKT for "door county kayak tours"), which makes them the two most likely to be leaving AI-answer visibility on the table too.

**First move:** For NKST and DCKT, pull "People Also Asked" results for their top 5 category queries (e.g. "door county kayak tours," "new orleans swamp tour") and check the existing tracked-prompt list against them. Add anything missing before the next AEO review pass.
