## 1. FAQ content hidden behind a click may be invisible to search engines and LLMs

**Source:** TLDR Marketing, "Audit if your FAQ is discoverable by search engines and LLMs" (2 min read) — https://www.linkedin.com/posts/chris-long-marketing_60-second-seo-the-content-in-your-faqs-might-ugcPost-7510318926933860352-BdRQ/

**The finding:** Many FAQ components hide their answer text until a user clicks to expand it, which means the text may not be present in the page source when the page loads. Search engines and LLMs typically read what's in the source, not what appears after a click interaction. The fix is a two-minute DevTools check: load the page, view source (not inspect-after-click), and confirm the FAQ answer text is actually there.

**Why it matters here:** Every FareHarbor-themed site in the portfolio (DCKT, CPPP, NPB, HPB, NKST, Austin/Buffalo Bayou sites) uses the ACF Schema Markup field plus an on-page FAQ block for FAQPage JSON-LD, and the vault's AEO/GEO work leans hard on FAQ content being both schema-tagged and actually crawlable. If the on-page FAQ accordion hides answer text until expanded, the JSON-LD schema might be the only copy of that content search engines and AI answer engines can see — meaning the visible page content isn't reinforcing it. Given the WordPress FAQ schema notes already in this vault's CLAUDE.md, this is worth a direct check rather than an assumption.

**First move:** Open DCKT's FAQ page in a browser, view page source (Cmd+Option+U or equivalent, not DevTools inspect), and search for the text of one FAQ answer. If it's not in the raw HTML, the accordion is client-side-only and needs a server-rendered or CSS-only (not JS-toggled) approach. Repeat spot-check on one or two other sites once DCKT is confirmed either way.

## 2. ChatGPT reads a YouTube video's description, not its transcript

**Source:** TLDR Marketing, "ChatGPT reads your YouTube description, not your transcript" (1 min read) — https://www.linkedin.com/posts/davidkonitzny_chatgpt-reads-your-youtube-description-not-share-7510237770435616768-sxNC/

**The finding:** ChatGPT can retrieve a YouTube video's title, metadata, and full description, but it does not read the transcript. That makes the description the real source AI search has for understanding what a video covers. The recommendation: treat descriptions like landing pages — include relevant terms, clear context, structured sections, and timecodes so ChatGPT can tell what the video is about and where specific topics appear.

**Why it matters here:** Three automated loops in this portfolio publish to YouTube daily or on a drip schedule — DCKT YouTube Drip, HPB YouTube Drip, and Ebb & Ember's YT Scheduler/YT Mirror. If those uploads are going out with thin auto-generated descriptions (common for drip-queued content), all of that video volume is functionally invisible to ChatGPT-driven search even though the content itself might be good. This is a lever on AI search visibility specifically, not just traditional YouTube SEO.

**First move:** Pull the last 5 DCKT YouTube Drip uploads and check the descriptions against this bar — do they name the tour, location, and specific topics covered, with structure? If they're one-liners, write a description template (2-3 sentences + a short list of what's covered + timecodes if the video has distinct segments) and apply it going forward before touching the backlog.
