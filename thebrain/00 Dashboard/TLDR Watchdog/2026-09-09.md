## 1. The 11x ROAS account that lost money on every order

**Source:** Search Engine Land, 5 min read — https://searchengineland.com/roas-account-lost-money-every-order-486999 (via TLDR Marketing, 2026-09-09)

**What it says:** An ecommerce account reporting 11x ROAS was losing money on every order once returns, VAT, cost of goods, and fulfillment came out of what the ad platform called "conversion value." Most of that headline 11x came from brand-search campaigns near 18x — shoppers who'd already decided to buy — blended with a real non-brand return closer to 3x. Fixing the accounting created a second problem: bidding purely for efficiency starved a SKU of volume, leaving stock to mark down below cost at season's end. The fix was rebuilding conversion values around actual contribution margin and giving each SKU (or campaign) one deliberate job — profit, volume, or cash — rather than judging everything by blended ROAS.

**Why it matters here:** Today's Google Ads pull (Sept 9 brief) shows Gravity Trails | NOLA — the umbrella account funding NPB, NKST, Bike Bar, and Admire NOLA — reporting 4.33 ROAS yesterday and 1.86 ROAS over 7 days. Those numbers are the platform's "conversion value," which for a tour business is almost certainly gross booking value, not margin after crew, fuel, insurance, and boat maintenance. A blended 1.86-4.33 "ROAS" could be comfortably profitable or could be underwater depending on what each tour type actually costs to run. The same account is also about to hit its $6,000 monthly spend cap around Sept 16-18 (see today's brief, Action Item #2) — meaning David is about to make a "raise the cap or don't" call on a number he hasn't margin-checked.

**First move:** Before deciding whether to raise the Gravity Trails NOLA monthly cap, pull real per-tour-type margin (price minus crew/fuel/boat cost) for NPB, NKST, Bike Bar, and Admire NOLA, and compare it against what Google Ads is calling "conv_value" for each campaign. If the campaigns closest to breakeven are the ones about to lose budget when the cap hits, that's useful — don't just add money to the account because the topline ROAS number looks fine.

## 2. How I built an SEO/AEO blog engine

**Source:** Substack (Harsehaj), 10 min read — https://harsehaj.substack.com/p/how-i-built-an-seoaeo-blog-engine (via TLDR Marketing, 2026-09-09)

**What it says:** A growth engineering intern at Browserbase built an AI tool (BlogEO) that audits existing blog content, finds search opportunities, generates drafts against those gaps, and tracks AI-engine citations, with a human approval step kept in the loop. Before the audit, 53% of the site's search traffic came from just 5 of 77 posts, and only 17 of 77 posts had ever been cited by an AI engine. After 6 weeks of running the tool against the existing archive: search impressions grew 5.8x, page-one queries grew 9.8x, and average blog position improved from 10.9 to 6.6.

**Why it matters here:** This doesn't contradict `AEO Action Plan - All Sites.md` — it validates it. That plan's top findings (no FAQ schema despite FAQ content existing on 7 sites, thin 40-60-word-quotable content, no comparison content) are the exact gaps this case study says moved the needle when fixed. It's a concrete number behind a plan that's mostly been sitting as a to-do. Today's GA4 pull for NKST also hands over the starting point for free: `/tours/swamp-kayak-tours/manchac-mystic-kayak-tour/` is NKST's top page this week at 229 views, well ahead of everything else, and it's exactly the kind of concentrated-traffic page the case study says to hit first (their 53%-of-traffic-from-5-posts pattern).

**First move:** Audit NKST's `/tours/swamp-kayak-tours/manchac-mystic-kayak-tour/` page against the AEO Action Plan's Priority checklist (FAQ schema, named-guide attribution, comparison content) — it's the single highest-traffic page on the site this week and currently has none of the three.
