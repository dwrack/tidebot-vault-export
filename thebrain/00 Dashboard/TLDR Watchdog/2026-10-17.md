## 1. A 33% Sonnet price cut raised the bill 4%

- **Finding:** In a test of 191 constructed reading-assistant requests, a 33% cut in Sonnet's token prices produced a 4% higher bill with thinking off, because a changed tokenizer and longer answers increased billed tokens. Source: https://three.dev/blog/the-free-upgrade-that-raised-your-bill (14 min read).
- **Why it matters here:** Our loops (morning brief, reply batches, night shift, TideBot do-runner) run `claude -p` on Sonnet. A list price cut says nothing about the bill if tokens per job went up. One careful number beats a guess.
- **Companion item in the same edition:** "Price war in the chip aisle" (Tom Tunguz): five frontier models score within 5 points of each other, but cost per task varies 3.8x (Google's cheapest at $1.99/task), measured at max reasoning effort. Same lesson: compare cost of an acceptable answer, not list price. https://tomtunguz.com/intelligence-per-dollar-2026/
- **First move (15 min):** pick one nightly loop, pull its billed tokens for the week before and the week after the last model change, and compare. If it went up, set thinking effort explicitly on that job.

## 2. Check comments for buyers before you pay creators

- **Finding:** Cal AI paid UFC creators thousands each and saw no download spike, because the audience watching a fight isn't asking to track calories. They now read comments for people asking for what the app does, cap payment per 1,000 views at their measured revenue per 1,000 views, and prefer flat fees to keep viral upside. One creator went from about 10,000 views to 7 million. Source: https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/jake-castillo-cal-ai-sub-club-podcast-2026 (6 min).
- **Why it matters here:** The 218-creator outreach list has 32 warm IG leads. The risk is the same mismatch: a big following that is not looking to book a tour.
- **First move:** for the 5 warmest leads, scan their last 10 posts' comments for "where is this," "how much," "how do I book." No such comments, no paid deal. Where there are, quote a flat fee under that brand's measured revenue per 1,000 views.
