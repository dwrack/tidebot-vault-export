## 1. Facebook proved its own ad measurement wrong

**Source:** TLDR Marketing, "Quick Links" section, 5 minute read. Original: https://karandhir.substack.com/p/facebook-proved-its-own-ad-measurement (via TLDR link https://karandhir.substack.com/p/facebook-proved-its-own-ad-measurement?utm_source=tldrmarketing)

**Finding:** Facebook's own internal research found that standard ad attribution (the kind behind the ROAS number shown in Ads Manager) can overstate incremental conversions by 3x or more. In other words, a chunk of what Meta counts as "caused by this ad" would have happened anyway — organic purchase, direct visit, word of mouth — and gets credited to the ad regardless.

**Why it matters today:** This morning's Meta pull shows NPB's `Purchase | Retargeting` campaign (act_87863118) reporting a 19.1x `website_purchase_roas` for $22.11 spent yesterday, off 3 reported purchases worth $422.32. That's an eye-popping number, and it's exactly the kind of figure this research says to distrust at face value — retargeting campaigns are especially prone to inflated attribution because they target people who were already close to buying. If a meaningful share of those 3 purchases would have happened without the ad, the true incremental ROAS could be a fraction of 19.1x.

This isn't a one-off. It applies to every "purchase" or "value" figure Meta reports across NPB, HPB, and Buffalo Bayou's campaigns — anywhere David is using the Ads Manager ROAS number to decide whether to scale a budget.

**First move:** Before increasing budget on `NPB | Purchase | Retargeting` or any other Meta campaign reporting a purchase ROAS, cross-check the reported purchase count against FareHarbor's actual booking log for the same date window. If FareHarbor shows 3 new NPB bookings on Sep 13 that plausibly came through retargeting, the number holds up. If FareHarbor's booking count for the day doesn't support 3 incremental bookings from that specific audience, the 19.1x is mostly attribution noise, not real lift. This is a 10-minute check, not a new tool or process — just don't let the Ads Manager dashboard number drive a budget decision unverified.

## 2. The Availability Heuristic: 7 Ways Google Ads Can Steer Your Decisions

**Source:** TLDR Marketing, "Resources & Tools" section, 5 minute read. Original: https://searchengineland.com/the-availability-heuristic-7-ways-google-ads-can-steer-your-decisions-487769 (via TLDR link, same URL with utm_source=tldrmarketing)

**Finding:** The article argues Google Ads' own interface nudges advertisers toward certain decisions by what it makes visible. Default dashboards surface metrics that aren't necessarily the most useful ones for the account. Hidden account-level settings can silently override campaign-level targets (for example, an account-wide conversion goal setting overriding what a specific campaign is optimizing for). Search term reports can reveal irrelevant traffic inflating apparent performance that isn't visible from the summary view. The piece's recommendation: review account settings, conversion goal configuration, search query reports, and Google's auto-recommendations manually and regularly, rather than trusting the default dashboard view to represent what's actually happening.

**Why it matters today:** Two of the four Google Ads accounts pulled this morning are in bad shape in ways that a settings audit — not a spend increase or decrease — might actually explain:
- **DCKT (customer 8672151991)** spent $0 with 0 impressions yesterday, the second day in a row. Across the full trailing week it's spent only $27.39 total on 334 impressions. That pattern (near-total silence with an occasional trickle) is consistent with a campaign that's technically "enabled" but effectively starved — by a low daily budget, an ad group stuck in "Limited" status, or an account-level setting quietly capping it. A quick look at the dashboard alone won't show this; it requires opening the campaign and checking budget pacing and any policy/limited-by flags directly.
- **HPB (customer 2986009791)** has a 7-day ROAS of 0.53, essentially unchanged from last week's 0.54, and $21.86 spent yesterday for 0 conversions. The tile's own goal note already suspects a phantom/broken conversion tracking setup. If the account's conversion goal is misconfigured (e.g., counting a low-value micro-conversion as the primary goal, or double-counting), the ROAS Google reports could be misleading in either direction, and the standard summary view wouldn't surface that.

**First move:** For both DCKT and HPB, open the Google Ads UI directly this week (not through the API summary this brief already pulls) and check three things per account: (1) campaign/ad group status flags — anything sitting on "Limited by budget" or similar, (2) the conversion actions configured for the account and whether the primary conversion goal is actually a real booking event vs. a page-view or click proxy, (3) the Search Terms report for the last 7 days, to see if spend is going to relevant queries at all. This is a diagnostic pass, not a spend change — the goal is figuring out whether DCKT's silence and HPB's flat ROAS are budget/targeting problems or tracking problems, since the fix is different for each.
