# Outreach Scripts — NOLA Website Outreach

*What to say at the counter, on the phone, and in the follow-up. Fill in the price from [[NOLA Website Outreach — Project Hub]] before using any of these. Business-specific hooks and best visit times are in [[Prospect Cards — Round 1]].*

---

## The rule

You are not selling a website. You are showing someone a finished thing with their name on it and asking one question. The whole pitch is: phone out, site loaded before you walk in, hand it to them, shut up.

Keep the first visit under three minutes unless they extend it. The goal of visit one is a reaction and a name, not a signature.

---

## Walk-in (primary channel)

**Before you go in**
- Preview loaded on your phone, scrolled to the hero. Airplane mode off, page fully cached.
- Leave-behind card in your pocket (QR to the preview, three-line offer, your cell).
- Know the decision-maker's name if the card has one.
- Buy something. A coffee, a po'boy, a pint. You are a customer first.

**Opening (to whoever is at the counter)**

> "Hey, is [owner name / the owner] around? I'm not selling anything, I actually made something for y'all and wanted to show it to them."

If the owner is not in:

> "No problem. Can I leave this for them? It's a website I built for [Business]. It's already done, they just need to look at it. My number's on there."

Hand over the card. Ask: "When are they usually in?" Write it in the touch log. Leave.

**If the owner is there**

Hand them the phone. Say the hook from their prospect card, then:

> "I do a lot of work with Google listings for local businesses, and yours kept coming up with great reviews and no website. So I built one. Everything on it is from your Google page: your reviews, your photos, your hours. It shows live whether you're open right now. Scroll down, there's your menu."

Then stop talking. Let them scroll. The site does the work.

**When they look up**

> "It's yours if you want it. [Offer in one sentence, e.g. 'It's ninety-nine a month, no contract, that covers the domain, hosting, and any changes you ever want, you just text me.'] First month's free. I can have it live at [business].com by tomorrow."

**Then one of three things happens:**

- **Yes** → "Great. What's the best number to text you on? I'll send you the link tonight and a couple of questions about photos." Get the number, the email if they have one, and the name they want on the domain. Leave.
- **Maybe / I need to think** → "Totally. The link's on this card, show your [partner / spouse / kids]. I'll check back in a few days." Hand the card. Leave. Log it.
- **No** → "No worries at all. Keep the card, the site stays up for a month if you change your mind. Thanks for the [coffee / po'boy]." Leave. Log the objection word for word.

Do not negotiate on visit one. Do not explain SEO. Do not mention Google's algorithm.

## Phone (second channel, or for follow-up)

Call during the quiet window on the prospect card. Ask for the owner by name.

> "Hi, this is David Rack, I'm a local business owner here in New Orleans. Is [name] available? ... I built a website for [Business] and wanted to get it in front of you. It's already done, I just need thirty seconds to text you the link. What's the best number?"

If they ask what it costs before seeing it:

> "Ninety-nine a month, first month free, no contract. But look at it first, then decide. Can I text it to you?"

Text the link the moment you hang up. Follow up in two days.

---

## Text follow-up sequence

Send from your personal cell, not a business line. Three touches, then stop.

**Touch 1 (same day as the visit or call)**

> Hi [name], David from earlier. Here's the site I built for [Business]: [link]. Scroll all the way down, the menu and hours are on there. Let me know what you think.

**Touch 2 (three days later, if silent)**

> [Name], one thing I forgot to mention: the "open now" badge at the top updates itself, and if you ever change hours you just text me and it's fixed in ten minutes. Want me to put it live under [business].com?

**Touch 3 (one week after touch 2, if still silent)**

> Last one from me. I'll leave the preview up through [date, ~30 days from build]. If you want it, text me any time before then and it's yours. If not, no hard feelings, and thanks for making a great [po'boy / pint / croissant].

Then close the card as "Ghosted (3 touches)" and revisit in 90 days.

## Objections

**"How much?"** (before they've looked)
> "Ninety-nine a month, but look at it first. If you don't like it the price doesn't matter."

**"I don't need a website, everyone finds us on Google."**
> "They find the pin. Then they tap it and there's nothing to read. This is what they'd read. It also means when someone searches '[their best dish] near me' there's an actual page to land on instead of a pin."

**"My nephew / cousin can do it."**
> "He probably can. This one's already done, though, and it's ninety-nine a month including the domain and every edit forever. If he wants to take it over later I'll hand him the files, no charge."

**"We're on Facebook / Instagram."**
> "Keep doing that. This links to it. Facebook is for people who already know you. This is for the ones who don't yet."

**"I need to think about it."**
> "Do. It's on the card. Show whoever you need to show." Then leave. Do not push.

**"Where'd you get the photos?"**
> "Your Google page, they're public. Before it goes live under your name I'll ask you for your own photos, or you tell me which of these you're fine with. You have the last word on every image."

**"What if I want changes?"**
> "Text me. Hours, prices, a new photo, a closure for a holiday. Same day, no charge. That's what the monthly is for."

**"Is this a scam?"**
> "Fair question. I own [NOLA Party Barge / New Orleans Kayak Swamp Tours], look us up. I'm not asking for a card today. Look at the site, and if you want it I'll send a Square invoice after it's live."

## After a yes: onboarding checklist

- [ ] Get: preferred contact number, email (if any), the domain they want (check availability on the spot)
- [ ] Register domain (dedicated account, not personal), point it at the site
- [ ] Remove `noindex` and the `robots.txt` block for that site only; add a real `sitemap`
- [ ] Ask for 5–10 of their own photos, or written okay on the Google ones used
- [ ] Confirm hours, phone, and menu prices with them line by line (the scrape is a starting point, not gospel)
- [ ] Add the site URL to their Google Business Profile website field (they must do this, or grant you manager access)
- [ ] Add "Sites by David Rack" footer link (decide the wording once, use it everywhere)
- [ ] Send first invoice on day 31
- [ ] Log the close in the hub: date, price, what convinced them
