# Original Research Studies: Link Earning Plan (Oct 2026)

Source: BuzzStream, "PR for GEO" (TLDR Marketing, David flagged 2026-10-21). Growth play #12 (journalist bait) now runs through this doc.

## What the article actually says (the parts that matter for us)
- Journalists say the thing they most want from PR people is data and research (Cision 2026, BuzzStream State of Digital PR).
- Research only pays off when it answers a question **buyers** already search. "Related to our industry" isn't enough. BuzzStream's $461 guest-post price study gets cited in AI answers because people shopping for links search that question.
- Three formats earn links: proprietary data tied to a news moment (Sole Supplier's Squid Game Vans spike), a **recurring yearly report** on your own data (PODS Moving Trends), and emotional survey data.
- Links still correlate with Google rank, and ranking pages get pulled into AI answers. Only 23% of AI brand mentions cite the brand's own site, so the third-party mention is the prize.
- Pick the outcome before the campaign, baseline it, track the same prompts for months. Don't claim credit you can't trace.
- National outreach works best with real brand search (~20k branded searches/month). We're below that, so we pitch **local news, trade press and niche media first**, and let those links carry us.

## The studies, ranked

### 1. NPB: "New Orleans is planning its parties at the last minute" (data ready now)
- **The finding:** in 2021 the typical party boat booking came 17 days before the trip. In 2026 it's 6. Same-day or next-day bookings nearly tripled (10% → 28%). Fri to Sun is 90% of revenue, and the 2pm and 4pm slots carry half of it (from `npb-ads-data-pack.json`, July 2026).
- **Why a journalist cares:** last-minute travel and the squeezed bachelorette/wedding budget are running stories. We have six years of hard numbers from one of the busiest group-party operators in the city.
- **Blog post:** "How Far Ahead Should You Book a New Orleans Bachelorette Party? What 40,000 Bookings Tell Us." It answers a real buyer search and carries the charts. Sections: booking window by year, busiest days and slots, when Saturdays sell out by month, the "book by" cheat sheet.
- **AI prompt to own:** "how far in advance to book bachelorette activities in New Orleans."
- **Pitch list:** NOLA.com business desk, Gambit, Biz New Orleans, New Orleans CityBusiness, WWL/WDSU consumer segments, The Knot / Brides / Bustle wedding desks, Skift and Travel Weekly (trend angle), FareHarbor's own blog (they like operator data).
- **Make it yearly:** "New Orleans Party Season Report" every January. The PODS move. Each year's edition re-earns links.
- **Timing:** pitch in early November, ahead of holiday parties and the spring bachelorette planning wave.

### 2. All kayak brands: "Rain Check": what weather actually cancels outdoor plans (needs one data join, Claude can do it)
- **What's proven so far:** DCKT's 2026 cancel rate went 15.6% → 22.8%, and 91% of the jump was last-24-hour cancels on washout days.
- **The real study:** join every cancel at all 6 brands (2020 to 2026) to Open-Meteo actual rain, wind and temperature for the tour hour. Then add Open-Meteo's historical **forecast** archive to test the killer question: how many trips get cancelled because of a forecast that never happened?
- **Headlines it can produce:** "X% of rain cancellations happened on days with less than a tenth of an inch of rain." "The rainiest month to book a tour in Door County / New Orleans / Austin / Houston." "The wind speed where kayak trips start getting cancelled."
- **Why it works:** local TV weather teams love this kind of data (WBAY, WLUK, WWL, KXAN, KHOU), as do the Green Bay Press-Gazette and Peninsula Pulse. It also answers "will my tour get cancelled if it rains," which every one of our FAQ pages fields. And it feeds play #1 (credit over refund) with real evidence.
- **Blog posts:** one hub study plus a per-city "best month to paddle" post on each brand site, with each post linking the hub.
- **Timing:** build over the winter and publish in late February, when people plan summer trips and weather desks need spring content.

### 3. "Impulse paddlers": most kayak tours get booked less than a day out (data ready now)
- **The finding:** 57 to 63% of Houston and Austin kayak bookings happen within 24 hours of the trip. Door County runs the other way: its 30+ days-ahead share quadrupled (3% → 12%) and the median lead time nearly tripled.
- **Pitch angles:** "Door County visitors are planning further ahead every year" goes to the Door County Visitor Bureau, Peninsula Pulse, Milwaukee Journal Sentinel travel and WI State Journal. "Outdoor tours are now an impulse buy" goes to Arival, Skift and FareHarbor.
- **Blog posts:** "Do You Need to Book a Door County Kayak Tour in Advance? (7 Years of Booking Data)" and the same post for Austin and Houston. These are utility posts that rank for buyer questions.
- **Effort:** low. It shares the pipeline with study #1, so run them together.

### 4. NKST "Gator Index" + LSKT "Bat Tracker": start logging now, publish 2027
- No wildlife dataset exists yet. The NKST guide log already has 31 structured entries, so add three fields: gator count, water temp, notable species. LSKT guides log nightly bat emergence time and crowd size from March.
- One season of data gives us "When are alligators most active? 1,000 swamp tours of data" and "Austin's bat emergence, night by night." Both are evergreen, both are what Atlas Obscura, Thrillist, NOLA.com, the Statesman and KXAN cover, and both own huge searches ("best time to see alligators New Orleans," "what time do the Austin bats come out").
- This also unparks play #26 (the free gator/swamp-conditions tool). The log becomes that tool's data.
- Bonus from data we already have: "What tourists actually ask a swamp tour company: 12 years of calls" (NKST voice analysis). It's a light piece, good for a local feature.

### 5. E&E: "Portland Sauna Habits 2027" survey
- The Van Westendorp survey is already staged. Add 4 publishable questions: how often you sauna, coldest plunge you've done, sauna vs. bar for a night out, what you'd give up for a weekly session. Target 200+ answers from the E&E list plus run-club partners (ties to play #16).
- Headlines: "1 in X Portlanders would trade a bar night for a sauna," "the price Portland says a sauna session is worth." Outlets: Willamette Week, Portland Monthly, The Oregonian, Eater Portland, Outside.
- It times with the banya launch, so the survey does double duty as pricing research. Jonah sees results.

## Per-business data pull, round 2 (2026-10-21)
Pulled from the FH exports for 2021-2026, uncancelled bookings, and counted by guests unless noted. Tour start times in the export carry no timezone, so hours below are local slot times. Lead times run about 0.2 days high (created_at is UTC, start_at is local). "Party" means guests per booking. NPB, HPB and BBK sell per-seat tickets, so a single-guest booking does NOT mean a solo traveler.

### NPB (38.2k bookings)
- **The tiki takeover.** The two original barges (Bayou Boogie + Party Queen) carried 97% of guests in 2021 and 17% in 2026. The two tiki boats now carry 79%, and Freaky Tiki alone is 50%. That's a "what groups want now" story: a floating bar beats a work-out. Frame it around the tiki bar, never pedaling (brand rule).
- Spring books furthest ahead: April and May median 9.7 days out, December 3.9. Gives us the "book by" cheat sheet for study #1.
- Saturday = 43% of guests. Fri + Sat + Sun = 88%. Mon to Thu combined is 13%.
- 10% of bookings are 8+ guests (the big bachelorette and corporate groups).
- Repeat guests rose 5.5% (2021) → 8.8% (2026).
- **New study, "The New Orleans Bachelorette Index":** fold these into study #1 as the yearly report. Sections: busiest weekends, slot demand, how far ahead by month, big-group share, which boat groups pick.

### NKST (14.5k bookings)
- **Spring, not summer, is swamp season.** March + April = 30% of all guests, and the 8 busiest days in five years all fall between Mar 13 and Apr 23. Summer months are 5-7% each.
- **Longer trips are winning.** The Extended Manchac tour went from 1% of guests (2021) to 10% (2026). Travelers are choosing the deeper trip.
- The 9am slot is 26% of guests. Monday (14%) beats Tue to Thu, which may be cruise passengers on embark/disembark days (unverified, worth checking against the Port of NOLA cruise calendar).
- Repeat rate is only ~2%. That's normal for a once-in-a-trip experience, which is why every guest has to be won from search.
- **New study, "When to see the swamp":** tourists come in March, while gators are most visible in warmer months (to be proven by the gator log, study #4). The tension is the headline: "Most visitors see the swamp before the gators wake up." Pairs with an evergreen "best month for a New Orleans swamp tour" post.

### DCKT (16.4k bookings)
- **Shipwreck tourism nearly doubled.** The Door Bluff Shipwreck Tour went from 11% of guests (2021) to 19% (2026). That tracks the Wisconsin Shipwreck Coast National Marine Sanctuary designation (2021). **Strongest new pitch on the list:** NOAA's sanctuary office, the Wisconsin Historical Society's maritime program, WPR, the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel, Wisconsin Sea Grant. Those are .gov and .edu-grade links, and those outlets already write about the sanctuary.
- **July 3 is the busiest day on the water, not July 4.** It's the #1 day in five years by a wide margin (1,208 guests vs 728 on the 4th). That's a clean, fun local-TV hook every late June (WBAY, WLUK, Door County Pulse).
- July = 39% of the season, August 28%.
- September (27%) and October (26%) have the highest cancel rates. That's Lake Michigan wind and waves in the shoulder season, and it goes into "Rain Check" (study #2).
- Weekdays hold steady at 12% each. Door County is a weekday-friendly destination, unlike NOLA.
- Half of all bookings are couples.

### LSKT / Austin (18.1k bookings)
- **Bats took over Austin paddling.** The Congress Avenue Bat Bridge tour went from 18% of guests (2021) to 71% (2025). The 7pm sunset slot is 26% of all guests. Austin media can't get enough bats: Statesman, KXAN, Austin Chronicle, Bat Conservation International (would link a data page).
- Friday is the busiest day (22%), ahead of Saturday (17%). Unusual, and worth a line.
- Bookings happen the same day: median lead time is about zero from March to August.
- The busiest days are Memorial Day weekend, July 4 and mid-March spring break.
- Do NOT publish: reseller share swung 29-35% (2023-25) down to 11% (2026). That's channel strategy, not a story.
- **New study:** combine with the bat log (study #4) into "Austin Bat Season Report": emergence times plus when people come to watch.

### BBK (4.6k bookings)
- **Volunteer cleanups went from 0 to 16% of guests** (Monthly Clean Up, 2024-25). Start logging pounds of trash and item counts per cleanup. Then publish a yearly "What we pulled out of Buffalo Bayou" report: Houston Chronicle, Houston Public Media, Buffalo Bayou Partnership, Bayou City Waterkeeper. Easy links, good for the brand. (It also ties to the BBK "Drainage Ditch" hero and anti-marketing play #19.)
- **New Bats & Kayaks tour hit 12-15% of guests** in its first two seasons (Waugh Drive bats). Houston's bat colony gets less press than Austin's, which makes it an underdog angle.
- Sat + Sun = 66% of guests. March is the peak month (spring break); March 22 is the busiest day.
- Repeat guests went 0 → 9.5% in four years, the fastest climb of any brand.

### HPB (6.9k bookings)
- Saturday = 53% of guests. April to September is the season.
- Repeat guests jumped to 18.5% in 2026, the highest of any brand. "Houstonians come back" is a nice line for site copy, but not a press story.
- Thin for data journalism on its own. Best angle: add Houston's **heat** to "Rain Check" (bookings and cancels vs heat index: "how hot is too hot to party outside in Houston?"). NPB gets the same heat cut.
- The 981 Google reviews are better used for site proof (play #22) than press.

- Only 3 months of Période sales, so the survey route (study #5) stays the play.

## How each study ships
1. Claude computes from the local exports (aggregates only) and writes the methodology.
2. A study page goes on the brand's own site: charts as images plus an embeddable version with a credit link, a short methodology note, and FAQ schema. The blog posts link up to it.
3. 15 to 30 one-to-one pitches through `/press-release` (trend, not update; lead with the number; sign as the business, never David). Staged for approval.
4. Seed it where it's useful: Reddit threads already ranking for the question, Source of Sources / Featured.com answers citing the study.
5. Measure: referring domains to the study URL (DataForSEO backlinks, baseline before launch), the target prompts added to the `/ai-citation` loop, and GSC clicks on the blog posts. Reported separately, no credit claimed for things we can't trace.
