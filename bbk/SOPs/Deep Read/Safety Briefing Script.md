# Safety Briefing Script
## Buffalo Bayou Kayak

### How to Use This

This isn't something you read word-for-word to guests. It's the content you need to internalize so you can deliver it naturally, in your own voice, before every single trip. Hit every point, but make it feel like a conversation.

The whole briefing should take 3-5 minutes. Longer and you lose them. Shorter and you're skipping something important.

### The Briefing

**PFDs stay on the entire time.** Not around your waist, not unclipped, not "I'll put it on if I need it." On, buckled, snug. If it rides up to your ears when you raise your arms, it's too loose and we need to adjust it. Non-negotiable.

**These are sit-on-top kayaks. They don't sink.** If you fall off, the kayak will be right there floating next to you. Don't panic. Grab the side, pull yourself back on, or hold on and I'll come help. Buffalo Bayou can have some current, so if you do go in, stay with your boat rather than trying to swim to shore.

**If you capsize, don't swallow the water.** I'm not going to sugarcoat it. Bayou water quality varies, especially after rain. If you go in, keep your mouth closed, and we'll get you cleaned up when we're back on shore.

**Stay together as a group.** I'll be leading and checking back regularly. Don't paddle ahead of me and don't fall too far behind. If you need to stop, signal me and we'll pause the whole group. Nobody paddles alone, especially on the Bayou.

**Wildlife is part of the experience, but keep your distance.** Turtles, herons, egrets, the occasional alligator gar surfacing. Look, don't touch. Don't feed them.

**If you hear thunder or see lightning, we're getting off the water immediately.** I'll direct everyone to the nearest bank. Follow my instructions and paddle directly toward where I'm pointing. Lightning on open water is extremely dangerous.

**If the water starts rising or the current picks up, I'll call us in.** Houston rain can affect the Bayou fast, sometimes from storms miles away. If I say we're heading to shore, that's not a suggestion.

**Paddle technique in 30 seconds:** Hold the paddle with both hands, shoulder-width apart, knuckles facing up. Reach forward, plant the blade, pull back to your hip, lift out, repeat on the other side. Alternating sides keeps you going straight. To turn, paddle only on one side. If you need to stop, lay the paddle across your lap.

**Signals:** If I blow my whistle once, look at me for direction. If I blow it three times, that's an emergency and I need everyone to stop paddling and listen. If you need help, wave your paddle overhead.

**Housekeeping:** Your phone is welcome but put it in a dry bag or waterproof case. There are no restrooms accessible during the tour, so take care of that before we launch. Sunscreen and water are your friends, Houston heat is no joke on the water.

### Key Points Checklist

Guides should be able to hit all of these from memory:

- PFDs on, buckled, the entire time
- Sit-on-top kayaks float, you can't sink them
- Don't swallow Bayou water if you capsize
- Stay together, don't outpace the group
- Wildlife: look don't touch, don't feed
- Lightning: off the water immediately
- Rising water/current: immediate shore return
- Basic paddle technique: grip, stroke, turning
- Whistle signals: one blast = attention, three = emergency
- Paddle overhead = "I need help"
- Phone in dry bag
- No restrooms on the water
- Stay hydrated in the heat

### Delivery Tips

Make eye contact. If someone's looking at their phone during the briefing, pause and wait.

Use humor where it fits. "The Bayou has claimed more phones than I can count" works. But don't joke about the safety-critical stuff.

Read the group. Experienced paddlers need the safety points but maybe not the paddle tutorial. First-timers need everything. Adjust depth, not coverage.

The water quality mention is awkward but necessary. Don't dwell on it. A quick, matter-of-fact line like "if you go in, just keep your mouth closed and we'll get you cleaned up after" handles it without scaring people.
