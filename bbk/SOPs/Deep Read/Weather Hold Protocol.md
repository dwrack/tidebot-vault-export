# Weather Hold Protocol
## Buffalo Bayou Kayak

### Why This Exists

Houston weather is unpredictable and often extreme. Afternoon thunderstorms roll in fast, and Buffalo Bayou adds the complication of water levels that change based on rain that might have fallen miles away. This protocol removes guesswork from weather decisions.

The rule is simple: if a threshold is hit, follow the protocol. No freelancing, no "it looks like it might clear up."

### Thresholds

| Condition | Threshold | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Wind speed | >15 mph sustained | Hold or reschedule |
| Lightning | Within 10 miles | Cancel immediately, shelter |
| Visibility | <0.25 miles (fog, rain) | Hold until clear |
| Water temperature | <50°F | Require wetsuit or cancel |
| Severe weather warning | NWS warning issued for area | Cancel, do not launch |
| Bayou flooding | Rapid water rise or flood advisory | Cancel immediately |

### How to Check

**Wind:** Use a weather app with real-time local data (Weather Underground, Windy, or NOAA). The Bayou can funnel wind differently in the downtown corridor versus open stretches, so check conditions at the actual launch site.

**Lightning:** The 30-30 rule works for a rough estimate. Lightning apps (WeatherBug, MyLightning) give real-time strike proximity. In Houston, afternoon storms can pop up with very little warning. If the sky is darkening and you're second-guessing, err on the side of caution.

**Water levels:** Check the Harris County Flood Control District gauges for Buffalo Bayou. If levels are rising or there's upstream rainfall, hold. The Bayou can go from calm to dangerous current surprisingly fast.

**Visibility:** If you can't see well down the Bayou channel, hold.

**Water temp:** Relevant mainly in winter months. Check water temp via USGS gauges.

### Decision Authority

Guides can make a hold call on their own. You don't need permission to keep people off the water.

Only management can override a hold call, and they should only do so if conditions have genuinely changed.

Guests don't get a vote on weather decisions. Be polite, be empathetic, but be firm. "I know this is disappointing, but I'm not comfortable putting the group on the water in these conditions." Offer to reschedule through FareHarbor.

### If You're Already on the Water

1. Monitor conditions throughout the tour
2. If conditions deteriorate past thresholds, head to the nearest safe bank immediately
3. Keep the group together. No one paddles ahead or falls behind.
4. If lightning is observed, get off the water and away from the bank edge. Seek shelter in a vehicle or solid structure.
5. If water levels start rising noticeably, get to shore immediately and move to high ground
6. Call management and coordinate next steps

### Houston Storm Specifics

Houston thunderstorms are often fast-moving but intense. A storm that wasn't on radar 30 minutes ago can produce dangerous lightning and heavy rain. Key things to watch:

- Dark clouds building to the west or southwest
- Sudden drop in wind followed by strong gusts
- Temperature drop
- That unmistakable "Houston storm smell" (ozone and wet pavement)

Don't wait for the rain to start. By the time it's raining hard, you should already be off the water.

### Communication

When a hold or cancellation is called:

1. Text management immediately with the reason and current conditions
2. Contact affected guests as early as possible (use FareHarbor contact info)
3. Offer rescheduling first, refund if rescheduling doesn't work
4. Document the hold/cancel: date, time, conditions, who was affected

### The Bottom Line

No trip is worth a safety incident. A cancelled tour costs revenue. An accident costs everything. Make the safe call and the business will back you up every time.
