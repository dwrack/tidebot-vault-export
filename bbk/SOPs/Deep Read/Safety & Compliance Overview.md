# Safety & Compliance Overview
## Buffalo Bayou Kayak

### Regulatory Framework

Buffalo Bayou Kayak operates on Buffalo Bayou in Houston, Texas. The primary regulatory authority is the **Texas Parks & Wildlife Department (TPWD)**, which oversees vessel registration, safety equipment requirements, and commercial operation standards on Texas waterways.

Harris County and the City of Houston may also have local ordinances relevant to commercial paddling operations on the Bayou. Management monitors these and communicates any changes.

### Vessel Registration

All commercial kayaks must carry current TPWD registration. Registration stickers must be visible on each vessel. Expired or missing stickers can result in fines during an on-water inspection.

### PFD Requirements

Every person on the water must have a USCG-approved Type III PFD. That means:

- One PFD per guest, properly fitted before launch
- Guides wear PFDs at all times on the water (no exceptions)
- PFDs must be in serviceable condition: buckles working, straps intact, foam not degraded
- Children under 13 must wear a PFD at all times per Texas law (our policy is everyone wears one, so this is already covered)

A Type IV throwable device should be available at the launch site.

### Bayou-Specific Safety Considerations

Buffalo Bayou presents unique risks compared to lake paddling:

**Water levels.** The Bayou can rise quickly after rainfall, including rain that falls miles upstream. What looks calm can become dangerous current within an hour. Guides must check water levels before launch and stay aware during tours.

**Water quality.** The Bayou's water quality varies. After heavy rain, runoff increases bacteria levels. Guests who capsize and ingest water or have open wounds should be monitored and advised to clean up thoroughly post-tour.

**Debris.** Floating debris (branches, trash, construction material) can appear unexpectedly, especially after high water events. Guides should scout the route visually before launch and stay alert during the tour.

**No restroom access.** There are no restroom facilities accessible during tours on the Bayou. Guides must communicate this clearly to guests before launch.

### Sound-Producing Devices

Every guide must carry a whistle on the water. One blast for attention, three blasts signals distress.

### Incident Reporting

All incidents must be documented and reported to management within 2 hours. This includes capsizes, injuries, equipment failures, encounters with emergency services, and near-misses.

Texas requires reporting of boating accidents to TPWD if there's death, disappearance, injury requiring medical treatment beyond first aid, or property damage exceeding $2,000. Management handles the formal filing, but the guide's incident report is the foundation.

### SOP Acknowledgment

All guides must sign an SOP acknowledgment via DocuSeal before any on-water activity. This is in addition to the standard contractor agreement. The signed acknowledgment confirms the guide has read and understood all standard operating procedures.

### Waiver and Liability

Every guest must have a signed waiver before getting on the water. Waivers are managed through FareHarbor and tied to specific bookings. This applies to regular bookings and membership holders alike.

### Guide Certifications

All guides must maintain current CPR/First Aid certification. If your cert expires, you're off the schedule until it's renewed. No grace period.

### Weather Compliance

The Weather Hold Protocol sets clear thresholds:

- Wind >15 mph: hold or reschedule
- Lightning within 10 miles: cancel and shelter immediately
- Visibility <0.25 miles: hold
- Water temp <50°F: require wetsuit or cancel
- Bayou flooding or rapid water rise: cancel immediately

When in doubt, cancel. No tour is worth a safety incident.
