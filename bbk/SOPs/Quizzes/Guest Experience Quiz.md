### Question 1
A guest arrives and is visibly nervous about the Bayou water. They keep asking "is it safe?" What do you do?

A) Tell them it's perfectly clean
B) Be honest and reassuring: "We use sit-on-top kayaks so you stay above the water. PFDs keep you safe, and capsizing is rare. If it happens, we get you back on the boat quick."
C) Tell them to cancel if they're worried
D) Downplay their concern and change the subject

**Answer: B**
Don't lie about water quality or dismiss concerns. Redirect to the safety measures in place. Most nervous guests calm down once they understand the setup and feel confident in their guide.

---

### Question 2
A couple on a date paddle asks you to take photos of them from your kayak. You're managing the group and the current at the same time. What do you do?

A) Refuse, you need to focus on safety
B) Find a calm stretch, raft up safely, take a quick photo, and get moving
C) Tell them to use a selfie stick
D) Say you'll do it at the end

**Answer: B**
Photo ops matter for the guest experience. Find a safe moment, take the shot, move on. It's 30 seconds and it makes the trip feel special.

---

### Question 3
Mid-tour, a guest says they need to use the restroom. What do you do?

A) Tell them to hold it
B) Acknowledge the situation, remind them there's no restroom access on the water, and if it's urgent, discuss heading to shore early. This is why you mention it before launch.
C) Tell them to go in the Bayou
D) End the tour early for the whole group

**Answer: B**
This is why the pre-launch restroom reminder exists. If someone's in distress, figure out the closest shore option. Don't make them feel bad about it.

---

### Question 4
A group of friends is getting rowdy, splashing each other and rocking their kayaks intentionally. What do you do?

A) Join in, they're having fun
B) Let it go, they signed the waiver
C) Calmly tell them you're glad they're having fun but rocking kayaks can lead to capsizes, and ask them to keep it to splashing if they want to play
D) End the tour

**Answer: C**
Fun is fine, capsizing is not. Set a reasonable boundary without killing the vibe. If they ignore you and someone capsizes, it becomes a safety incident on your watch.

---

### Question 5
A guest with limited mobility arrives. They can sit up fine but have limited arm strength. Their friend is with them. What do you do?

A) Tell them the tour isn't accessible for them
B) Discuss options: put them in a tandem with their friend in the back doing most of the paddling. Confirm they're comfortable and can safely sit in the kayak.
C) Ask them to get a doctor's note
D) Call management and make them wait

**Answer: B**
Tandem kayaks solve a lot of accessibility challenges. If the guest can sit safely and their partner can paddle, there's usually a way to make it work. Have the conversation, assess the situation, and make it happen if it's safe.

---

### Question 6
A guest tips you in cash and says "buy yourself a beer after this." What's the right response?

A) "I'll take two"
B) "Thanks, I really appreciate that"
C) "We can't accept tips"
D) "You should tip through the app instead"

**Answer: B**
Be gracious and genuine. Tips are appreciated. Don't make it complicated.

---

### Question 7
A regular member shows up and starts telling a new guest all about the Bayou and kayaking safety, partly contradicting your briefing. What do you handle this?

A) Let them take over, they know the drill
B) Thank them for their enthusiasm and gently redirect: "I appreciate that! Let me make sure everyone hears the same safety info though" and continue your briefing
C) Tell them to stop talking
D) Skip the rest of the briefing since the member covered it

**Answer: B**
Members mean well, but the safety briefing needs to come from you, consistently and completely. Acknowledge them warmly and take back the floor.

---

### Question 8
After a tour, a guest privately tells you they're disappointed because they saw more trash in the Bayou than wildlife. What do you say?

A) "Yeah, it's pretty bad sometimes"
B) "I'm sorry that took away from your experience. The local conservancy is working on cleanup efforts and it's actually improved a lot. I'll pass your feedback along."
C) "You should have gone somewhere else then"
D) "That's not something I can control"

**Answer: B**
Validate the disappointment, offer context, and don't be defensive. Route the feedback to management, it may be worth sharing with the local conservancy partnership efforts.

---

### Question 9
A guest shows up 20 minutes late. The group already launched. What do you do?

A) Tell them they missed it, rebook online
B) If you haven't gotten far, consider whether it's safe to add them. If the group is gone, explain what happened, help them reschedule through FareHarbor, and be genuinely apologetic.
C) Send them out alone to catch up
D) Blame them for being late

**Answer: B**
Never send someone out alone on the Bayou. If the group is reachable and conditions allow, great. If not, reschedule and be kind about it. Don't send them into the Bayou solo.

---

### Question 10
A guest asks for a recommendation on whether the membership is worth it. They live in Houston and like kayaking but aren't sure they'd use it enough. What do you say?

A) "It's only $300, just go for it"
B) "If you think you'd come out even 3-4 times a year, it pays for itself. And a lot of members tell me they end up coming way more than they expected once they have it."
C) "I don't know anything about the membership"
D) "You should probably think about it"

**Answer: B**
Give them real math and real feedback. 3-4 visits covers the cost. The frequency insight from other members makes it tangible. Don't hard-sell, just help them evaluate.
