## Buffalo Bayou Kayak

### Question 1
During morning inspection, you notice the Bayou looks higher than usual and the current seems stronger. It rained heavily last night but the sky is clear now. What do you do?

A) Launch normally, the rain stopped
B) Check the Harris County Flood Control District gauges, assess current strength, and hold if levels are elevated or still rising
C) Let the guests decide if they're comfortable
D) Launch but stay close to shore

**Answer: B**
Bayou levels can remain elevated or continue rising for hours after rain, especially from upstream rainfall. Check the gauges and use your judgment. If the current is noticeably stronger than normal, hold.

### Question 2
A guest capsizes in the Bayou. They surface, they're conscious, but they definitely swallowed some water. What's your priority after getting them back on the kayak?

A) Continue the tour, they're fine
B) Get them stable, check for distress, note the water ingestion, and recommend they rinse their mouth. After the tour, advise them to monitor for illness and seek medical attention if symptoms develop.
C) Call 911 immediately
D) End the tour and refund everyone

**Answer: B**
Swallowing Bayou water isn't an automatic emergency, but it's a health concern worth noting. Get the guest stable, document it, and give them a heads-up to watch for symptoms. Note it in the incident report.

### Question 3
What document, unique to Buffalo Bayou Kayak, must every guide sign before any on-water activity?

A) A non-compete agreement
B) An SOP acknowledgment via DocuSeal
C) A TPWD permit application
D) A waiver of liability

**Answer: B**
Buffalo Bayou Kayak requires a signed SOP acknowledgment via DocuSeal from every guide. This is in addition to the standard contractor paperwork.

### Question 4
You check the weather and see sustained winds at 12 mph with gusts to 17 mph. The Bayou is in a sheltered stretch today. What's the call?

A) Launch normally, 12 mph sustained is fine and the stretch is sheltered
B) Hold the trip. Gusts exceeding 15 mph trigger the threshold regardless of sheltering.
C) Launch but avoid the open stretches
D) Ask the guests if they're experienced enough

**Answer: B**
If gusts are exceeding 15 mph, that's a hold. Sheltering can reduce effective wind, but the threshold exists for a reason. Conditions can change during a tour.

### Question 5
You're about to launch and realize you're short one PFD. You have exactly enough for all guests but none for yourself. What do you do?

A) Go without, you're a strong swimmer
B) Do not launch. Every person on the water needs a PFD, including the guide. Find a replacement or reduce the group size by one.
C) Use a Type IV throwable as your PFD
D) Borrow one from a nearby kayaker

**Answer: B**
Guides wear PFDs. Period. A Type IV throwable is not a substitute for a wearable PFD. Reduce the group or find another PFD.

---

### Question 6
During a tour, you notice the water level rising and the current increasing. You're about 20 minutes from the launch site. What do you do?

A) Paddle faster to get back before it gets worse
B) Head to the nearest safe bank immediately, get guests on shore, and call management
C) Finish the tour since you're almost done anyway
D) Let the current carry you downstream and figure it out

**Answer: B**
Rising water on the Bayou is an immediate shore-up situation. Don't try to race it. Get to the nearest safe bank, get everyone on dry ground, and coordinate with management.

### Question 7
TPWD registration stickers on two of your kayaks are faded to the point of being hard to read. The registrations are current, just the stickers look rough. What do you do?

A) No issue, the registration is current
B) Flag it to management for replacement stickers. A TPWD officer may not be able to confirm the registration if the sticker is illegible.
C) Write the registration number on the hull with a marker
D) Only use those kayaks when you know TPWD isn't patrolling

**Answer: B**
If an officer can't read the sticker, it's effectively the same as not having one. Get replacement stickers. Don't wait for an inspection to find out.

### Question 8
A guest has a visible open wound on their leg (scrape from earlier in the day). They want to paddle. What do you do?

A) Let them paddle, it's their choice
B) Cover the wound with a waterproof bandage from the first aid kit and advise them that Bayou water shouldn't contact open wounds. If the wound is significant, recommend they skip the water portion.
C) Refuse to let them on the tour
D) Ignore it

**Answer: B**
Bayou water quality makes open wound contact a legitimate health concern. Help them cover it. If the wound is too large to bandage effectively, have an honest conversation about the risk and let them decide.

### Question 9
How long do you have to submit a written incident report to management after something happens?

A) End of the week
B) 24 hours
C) 2 hours
D) Only if the guest complains

**Answer: C**
All incidents get a written report to management within 2 hours. Fresh details are accurate details.

### Question 10
A guest asks why they can't just sign a general waiver that covers all their future visits. They're a $300/year member and don't want to sign every time. What do you tell them?

A) "Good point, I'll mention that to management"
B) "Waivers are tied to specific bookings for legal protection. It only takes a minute each time."
C) "You're right, your membership covers that"
D) "I'll just skip it for you since you've signed before"

**Answer: B**
Every booking gets a waiver, every time, including members. It's a liability protection issue. Be matter-of-fact about it. Most people accept it once they understand the reason.
