### Question 1
You arrive for morning inspection and discover the Bayou has risen about 2 feet overnight from rain. The current is noticeably faster. You have tours booked starting in an hour. What do you do?

A) Launch normally, 2 feet isn't that much
B) Check the Harris County Flood Control gauges, assess current strength. If levels are still rising or the current is unsafe for guest paddlers, hold all tours and contact management.
C) Let the first group try it and see how they do
D) Wait an hour and check again without contacting anyone

**Answer: B**
Rising water is a hold condition. Check the gauges, don't just eyeball it. If levels are elevated or still climbing, hold everything and let management help coordinate rescheduling.

### Question 2
FareHarbor goes down during check-in. Guests are arriving and you can't pull up bookings or waiver status. What do you do?

A) Cancel tours until the system is back
B) Collect names, confirm bookings verbally, have guests sign paper waivers, and run the tour. Reconcile in FareHarbor when it's back online.
C) Skip waivers and run the tours
D) Send everyone home

**Answer: B**
Tech fails sometimes. Paper waivers are your backup. Collect names, run the tour, update the system later. Never skip waivers entirely.

### Question 3
A $300/year member calls and says they want to do a solo paddle without a guide. Your operation doesn't typically send people out alone. What do you tell them?

A) "Your membership covers that, go ahead"
B) "Memberships are for unlimited rentals, but we have safety requirements. Let me check with management on our solo paddle policy."
C) "No, never"
D) "Sure, sign a different waiver"

**Answer: B**
Don't make up a policy on the spot. The membership covers unlimited rentals, but safety policies around solo paddling may apply. Check with management and get back to the member with a clear answer.

### Question 4
At the end of the day, you discover one kayak has a new crack in the hull that wasn't there during morning inspection. What do you do?

A) It'll be fine for tomorrow, just put some tape on it
B) Pull the kayak from rotation, photograph the damage, report it to management immediately, and note when you think it may have happened
C) Leave it for the next guide to deal with
D) Try to repair it yourself

**Answer: B**
Damaged equipment comes out of rotation immediately. Document it with photos, report it, and note any theories on when it happened (which trip, which guest, or during transport). Management decides on repair or replacement.

### Question 5
You're managing rentals and guided tours on the same day. A walk-up customer wants a rental kayak, but all your kayaks are allocated to upcoming guided tours. What do you do?

A) Let them take one of the tour kayaks
B) Explain that all kayaks are reserved for guided tours right now, and offer to help them find a rental slot later today or book for another day
C) Send them to a competitor
D) Tell them to come back tomorrow without checking availability

**Answer: B**
Tour allocations are locked. Help the walk-up find a time that works. Never pull from a booked tour to satisfy a walk-up.

---

### Question 6
A guide who just finished a tour stored all the PFDs wet in a closed bin instead of hanging them to dry. You find them the next morning. What do you do?

A) Just hang them out now, no big deal
B) Hang them immediately, check for mildew, and mention it to management. In Houston humidity, one night in a closed bin can start mildew growth.
C) Throw them away and order new ones
D) Use them as-is

**Answer: B**
Houston humidity is gear's worst enemy. One night wet in a closed bin can mean mildew. Get them drying, inspect for damage, and make sure it doesn't happen again. This is exactly why the end-of-day sign-off exists.

### Question 7
You're mid-tour and your radio battery dies. You're leading a group of 10 and there's no other guide. What do you do?

A) Keep going, the radio was just backup
B) Keep the group tight, use voice and visual signals, and note the dead radio so it gets charged before the next trip
C) End the tour early
D) Use your phone to call management for instructions

**Answer: B**
A dead radio on a single-guide trip isn't an emergency, but you need to compensate. Keep the group closer together and rely on voice and whistle. Log it so it gets charged.

---

### Question 8
A guest leaves their sunglasses on a kayak and calls 30 minutes after the tour ends. The kayaks are already racked and locked. What do you do?

A) Tell them to come back during operating hours
B) If you're still on site, check the kayak. If you've left, coordinate with management for retrieval and let the guest know a timeline.
C) Tell them they're probably gone
D) Say you'll look but never follow up

**Answer: B**
Help them. If you can check now, check now. If not, coordinate a plan and follow through. Lost items are a small effort that builds huge goodwill.

---

### Question 9
It's 2 PM and you see dark clouds building to the southwest. Your next tour launches at 3 PM. What do you do?

A) Wait until 3 PM and see what happens
B) Monitor the storm's movement. If lightning is detected within 10 miles or a severe weather warning is issued by 2:30, start contacting guests to cancel/reschedule. Don't wait until 3 PM.
C) Hope for the best
D) Launch early to beat the storm

**Answer: B**
Houston storms move fast. If it looks threatening at 2 PM, make the call early enough to save guests a wasted trip. Don't wait until they're standing at the launch site.

### Question 10
A new guide asks you whether they really need to go through the DocuSeal SOP acknowledgment process. They say they've read everything and are ready to go. What do you tell them?

A) "You're probably fine, just get it done when you can"
B) "The signed DocuSeal acknowledgment is required before any on-water work. It's not optional, but it only takes a few minutes."
C) "Skip it, nobody checks"
D) "Ask management, I'm not sure"

**Answer: B**
It's a hard requirement. Be clear and helpful about it. The process is quick, and skipping it creates a compliance gap that could become a real problem.
