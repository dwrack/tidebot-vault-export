### Question 1
A guest asks what else there is to do in the area after the tour. You're new to Houston and don't have great recommendations yet. What do you do?

A) Say "I'm not really from around here, sorry"
B) Tell them to check Google
C) Share 2-3 nearby spots you've researched ahead of time as part of being prepared for this role
D) Make up a recommendation

**Answer: C**
Part of being a guide in Houston is knowing the area around Buffalo Bayou. You should have a short list of nearby restaurants, parks, or things to do. Guests expect local knowledge, and "I don't know" undermines the whole experience.

---

### Question 2
A guest is clearly struggling on the water. They're falling behind and getting frustrated. What do you do?

A) Keep the group moving, they'll figure it out
B) Drop back, check in quietly, offer help with paddle technique or suggest staying closer to you
C) Tell the whole group to slow down for them
D) Ignore it, some people just aren't great at kayaking

**Answer: B**
Never call out a struggling guest in front of the group. Drop back, check in privately, help with technique. Small adjustments often fix the problem.

---

### Question 3
A member ($300/year) shows up and expects preferential treatment over regular guests during check-in. What do you do?

A) Give them priority since they're a member
B) Treat all guests the same during check-in, but make the member feel valued with a personal greeting: "Good to see you back on the water"
C) Tell them membership doesn't give them special treatment
D) Skip their waiver check since they've done this before

**Answer: B**
Members are valuable, but check-in process is the same for everyone, including waivers. A warm, familiar greeting signals that you recognize them without creating a two-tier system in front of other guests.

---

### Question 4
You arrive at the launch site and there's trash scattered around the staging area that isn't yours. What do you do?

A) Leave it, it's not your mess
B) Clean it up before guests arrive
C) Take a photo and send it to management, but leave it
D) Complain about it to arriving guests

**Answer: B**
Guests don't care whose trash it is. They see the launch site as your operation. Clean it up, mention it to management if it's a recurring issue, but never let guests arrive to a messy site.

---

### Question 5
A guest mentions they're considering the $300 annual membership. When's the best time to talk about it?

A) During check-in before the tour
B) During the safety briefing
C) After the tour, when they've just had a great experience
D) Never, let them find it on the website

**Answer: C**
Post-tour is the perfect moment. They're still buzzing from the experience, and a casual "If you want to come back anytime, we have a $300 annual membership for unlimited rentals" is a natural conversation, not a sales pitch.

---

### Question 6
You're running late for your shift due to Houston traffic. You'll be about 10 minutes behind. What do you do?

A) Just show up and hope no one notices
B) Text management immediately with your ETA so they can prep if needed
C) Call in sick instead of showing up late
D) Skip the morning inspection to make up time

**Answer: B**
Communicate early. 10 minutes late with a heads-up is manageable. 10 minutes late with no warning leaves everyone scrambling. And never skip inspection to save time.

---

### Question 7
A guest makes an insensitive comment about the Bayou's water quality, loudly saying things like "this water is disgusting." How do you handle it?

A) Agree with them
B) Ignore it completely
C) Acknowledge the concern casually and redirect: "The Bayou's actually in better shape than it used to be. The park conservancy does a lot of work on it. Either way, we keep everyone in the boat and PFDs on the whole time."
D) Get defensive about the Bayou

**Answer: C**
Don't argue, don't ignore. Acknowledge, give a quick positive fact, and reinforce safety. Other guests are listening and taking cues from how you handle it.

---

### Question 8
A corporate group wants to bring snacks and drinks on the kayaks. What do you tell them?

A) "No food or drink allowed on the kayaks"
B) "Water bottles are fine in a dry bag. Snacks are tricky on the water, but we can plan a break on shore if you'd like."
C) "Go for it, just don't make a mess"
D) "You'll need to ask management"

**Answer: B**
Be practical. Water is always fine. Snacks on kayaks usually means trash in the Bayou. Offer a shore break as an alternative if the trip allows for it.

---

### Question 9
You overhear a guest saying they expected more from the experience and feel it was overpriced. What do you do?

A) Tell them to take it up with customer service
B) Acknowledge their feeling without being defensive, focus on delivering a great rest of the tour, and flag it to management afterward
C) Offer them a discount
D) Explain all the costs involved in running the business

**Answer: B**
You can't change the price mid-tour, but you can influence the value of the experience. Focus on what's left of the tour and make it great. Flag the feedback so management can evaluate pricing or listing language.

### Question 10
What do you do if you find out another guide has been skipping the DocuSeal SOP acknowledgment step with new guides they're helping onboard?

A) Mind your own business
B) Mention it to management privately. The signed SOP acknowledgment is a requirement, not optional.
C) Tell the guide to stop doing it in front of guests
D) Skip it yourself since other people are skipping it

**Answer: B**
The DocuSeal acknowledgment is a compliance requirement. If it's being skipped, management needs to know. Don't ignore it and don't normalize it.
