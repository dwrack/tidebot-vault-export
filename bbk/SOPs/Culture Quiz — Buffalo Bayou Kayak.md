# Culture Quiz — Buffalo Bayou Kayak

Read "How We Run Things" first. Then work through these five scenarios. There aren't trick answers, but there are wrong ones.

---

## 1. The Empty Slot

**Principle: Own Your Patch**

Two guests booked for a Tuesday afternoon tour. Just two. It's hot, you'd rather not go out for a pair.

**What do you do?**

You show up ready and run the tour like it's a group of twenty. Those two guests booked because they wanted to paddle Buffalo Bayou. They're getting the full experience. Small groups often leave the best reviews because they get more of your attention. The bayou is your territory regardless of headcount.

---

## 2. The Skyline Selfie

**Principle: Be Real**

You're leading a Houston Skyline Tour. One guest is filming everything, constantly asking the group to pose, repositioning people for better angles. The rest of the group is getting annoyed and missing the actual experience.

**What do you do?**

You don't call them out in front of everyone. Redirect naturally: "Hey, there's an incredible angle of the skyline from about 100 yards up. Let me get the group positioned there, it'll be way better than anything from here." Give them a great shot AND get the group moving again. If it keeps happening, a quiet one-on-one: "I want to make sure everyone gets to enjoy the tour. How about we do a group photo at the turnaround point?" Protect the group experience without making anyone feel bad.

---

## 3. The Waiver Rush

**Principle: Safety Is the Job**

Tour's about to launch. A couple walks up and says they signed their waivers online, but when you check FareHarbor their booking shows unsigned. They're getting frustrated. The rest of the group is in their kayaks ready to go.

**What do you do?**

No signed waiver, no tour. That's the rule. But you don't have to make it painful. Pull up their booking-specific waiver link on your phone, hand it to them: "Takes about 30 seconds, I just need it in the system before we go." Stay calm, stay friendly, get it done. You never use a generic QR code or skip the signature because the group is waiting. The group can wait 60 seconds. An unsigned waiver is a liability you carry forever.

---

## 4. The Quiet Problem

**Principle: Respect the Work**

You overhear another guide complaining to guests during a tour. Nothing terrible, but things like "they never fix the equipment" and "management doesn't care about the guides." The guests look uncomfortable.

**What do you do?**

You don't ignore it, and you don't confront them in front of guests. After the tour, pull them aside privately. Something like: "Hey, I heard some of what you were saying out there. I get the frustration, but that stuff lands different when guests hear it. If there's an equipment issue or a management problem, bring it to me or bring it up directly. Venting to guests just makes everyone look bad." Keep it peer-to-peer, not preachy.

---

## 5. The Better Way

**Principle: Get It Out of Your Head**

You figured out that launching beginners before experienced paddlers and staggering by 2-3 minutes makes the whole group flow better. Less bunching, less waiting, more time on the water. You've been doing it on your tours and it works great.

**What do you do?**

You share it. Text the group chat, mention it at the next meetup, tell management. Whatever works. The whole point is that your insight makes every guide's tours run smoother, not just yours. If you keep it in your head, it dies with your schedule. The operation gets better when people share what they learn.
