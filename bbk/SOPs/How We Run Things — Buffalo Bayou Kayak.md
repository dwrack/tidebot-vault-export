# How We Run Things — Buffalo Bayou Kayak

This isn't a policy manual. It's how we actually operate. Five principles, no fluff.

---

## 1. Own Your Patch

Buffalo Bayou is your territory. The downtown Houston skyline, Allen's Landing, the bat colony under the Waugh Drive Bridge, the urban wildlife corridor running through the middle of the fourth-largest city in America. You know this water. You know this city.

That means you know the current conditions before guests ask. You know where the herons nest this month. You know which sections get choppy when the wind picks up. You know what the bayou looks like after a heavy rain and whether it's safe to launch.

Nobody should know your stretch of water better than you do.

## 2. Be Real

This isn't a theme park ride with a script. Guests came to paddle through downtown Houston on a bayou most locals drive over without thinking twice. Give them something real.

Talk about the ecology. Point out the turtles, the egrets, the bats. Tell them about the history of Allen's Landing, where the city literally started. If you have a favorite spot on the route, say so and say why.

Your personality is the experience. Houston personality, bayou knowledge, genuine enthusiasm for urban wildlife. That's what gets five-star reviews.

## 3. Respect the Work

Show up on time. Check your gear before guests arrive. Clean the launch site after your shift. Rinse the kayaks and SUPs properly. Stow the paddles and PFDs where they go.

This stuff isn't glamorous. It's what separates an operation that runs well from one that doesn't. When you skip steps, the next guide or the next guest pays for it.

## 4. Get It Out of Your Head

If you notice the launch ramp is getting slippery, say something. If a guest gives you feedback about the rental process, pass it along. If you figured out a better way to stage tandem kayaks for faster turnaround, share it.

The worst thing you can do is solve a problem and keep it to yourself. What you learn on the water makes the whole operation better, but only if you actually communicate it.

## 5. Safety Is the Job

Every guest wears a life jacket. No exceptions. Every guest has a signed waiver before they touch a kayak or SUP.

**Waivers are booking-specific, not generic.** Here's how it works:

- Pull up the FareHarbor manifest for your tour or rental time slot
- Check each guest's waiver status in the booking
- If someone hasn't signed, pull up their booking-specific waiver link and have them sign on-site
- Do NOT use generic QR codes or universal waiver links. Every signature must be tied to a booking
- No signed waiver, no launch. Period.

Gear gets inspected before every shift. PFDs checked for tears and buckle function. Paddles counted. First aid kit stocked. Communication device charged.

If conditions aren't safe, you cancel. Wind, lightning, visibility, water conditions. You don't need permission to make that call. You need good judgment.

No alcohol on any kayak or SUP experience. Full stop.

---

## The Deal

You run clean tours. You take care of the equipment. You keep people safe. You show them a side of Houston they didn't know existed.

In return, you get to work on the water in the middle of a major city, set your own schedule as a contractor, and build something worth being proud of.

That's the deal.
