## Format 1: Static

### Variant 1: "Four Hours, Not Whenever We Get To It"

**Angle:** Four Hours, Not Whenever We Get To It. New in pass 42, proven, first execution.

**Evidence quote:** "Respond within 4 hours during business hours, 12 hours max." (SOP Guest Communication Templates, "Notes on Complaint Responses," verbatim, owned.)

**Objection handled:** If something goes wrong with my booking, will anyone actually get back to me, or does it disappear into a small-business inbox.

**Body framework:** Ogilvy factual-specific proof, a real SLA number stands in for the vague "we care about you" claim.

**Hook (draft):** "Four hours. That's our max response window, business hours."

**Overlay text (draft):** "You message us. You hear back within 4 hours during business hours."

**Primary text (draft):** If something comes up with your booking, you're not shouting into a void. We respond within 4 hours during business hours, 12 hours max outside of them. A real person answers, not a form letter. Houston Skyline Tour, $55/person, 2 hours, launching from Allen's Landing.

**Format spec:** Static. Text-overlay card, clock or phone-notification visual over launch-point or skyline photography; existing assets likely sufficient.

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Ogilvy, alone. A static fact-card lives or dies on one specific, checkable number, and "4 hours" is exactly the kind of factual specific Ogilvy would lead with instead of an adjective like "responsive" or "attentive."

**Framework applied:** State the number, let it carry the whole claim, close with the fact that a real person is behind it rather than a system.

**Reasoning:** Halbert's urgency-driven voice was considered, this is technically a service-standard claim, but there's no scarcity or deadline pressure here, just a plain fact worth stating cleanly. Ogilvy's simpler "the number is the headline" approach fits a one-image static card better than Halbert's more conversational hustle.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, hook tightened.

**Line-by-line:**
- Draft hook, "Four hours. That's our max response window, business hours," buries the number behind two clauses of setup. Rewritten to "Four hours. That's the longest you'll wait for a real answer," which keeps the short-first-sentence rule and gets to a concrete outcome (an answer, not a "window") instead of internal process language.
- "We Respond Within 4 Hours. Every Time." Passes all three: visualizable (a real span of time you can picture waiting through), falsifiable (a stated internal SLA, checkable against the SOP), ownable (few small tour operators publish an exact response-time number this precisely).
- Draft primary text's "you're not shouting into a void" is a strong, visual phrase, kept as-is. "A real person answers, not a form letter" was tightened slightly for rhythm, dropping "not" repetition elsewhere in the paragraph.

**Sharpened final:**

- **Hook:** "Four hours. That's the longest you'll wait for a real answer."
- **Overlay text:** "You message us. You hear back within 4 hours during business hours, 12 hours max after."
- **Primary text:** If something comes up with your booking, you're not shouting into a void. We respond within 4 hours during business hours, 12 hours max outside of them. A real person answers, not a form letter. Houston Skyline Tour, $55 per person, 2 hours, launching from Allen's Landing.
- **Headline:** "We Respond Within 4 Hours. Every Time."
- **CTA:** Book Your Tour. Sales/Purchase-objective campaign.

**The one line to keep:** "You're not shouting into a void." The single most visualizable phrase in the batch for what small-business silence actually feels like from the customer's side.
