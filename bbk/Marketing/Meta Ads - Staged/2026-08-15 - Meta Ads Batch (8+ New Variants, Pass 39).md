## Variant 1 — Reel: "The Divorce Boat"

**Angle:** The Divorce Boat. First execution.
**Evidence:** "Many people jokingly (and wrongly!) refer to tandem kayaks as 'divorce boats'... they're actually great for relationships as they require good communication and coordination." (Dynamic Paddlers, Facebook). "If you want to stay married, keep away from kayaks." (Boston Globe Magazine, headline).
**Objection handled:** Will paddling with my partner turn into an actual argument, will I look uncoordinated in front of them.
**Hook (draft):** "They call tandem kayaks 'divorce boats.' Here's why ours won't earn it."
**Body framework:** Halbert reframe. Name the joke the reader half-knows, defuse it, back it with BBK's actual water conditions.
**Shot list:**
1. Text overlay: "Tandem kayaks have a nickname online."
2. Cut: couple/pair getting into a tandem kayak, laughing.
3. Text overlay: "They call it the 'divorce boat.'"
4. Cut: calm, flat bayou water, skyline in the background.
5. Text overlay: "Ours is flat water. Three miles from downtown. Nobody's arguing."
6. Cut: pair paddling in sync, smiling.
7. End card and CTA.
**Primary text (draft):** Tandem kayaks have a real nickname online: "divorce boats." The Boston Globe wrote about it. So has half the internet's paddling community. Here's the thing, that reputation comes from whitewater and open water, where one bad stroke throws off the whole boat. Buffalo Bayou is flat, slow, three miles from downtown. You'll find the rhythm together in about five minutes. Houston Skyline Tour, $55 per person, 2 hours.
**Headline:** "Still Married After the Divorce Boat"
**CTA overlay text:** "Paddle It Together"
**CTA:** Book Your Tour. Sales/Purchase-objective campaign.
**Format spec:** Reel. Existing tandem-kayak launch and on-water footage likely covers most beats; would benefit from a couple/pair-specific clip showing coordinated paddling, may not currently exist in the asset library.
