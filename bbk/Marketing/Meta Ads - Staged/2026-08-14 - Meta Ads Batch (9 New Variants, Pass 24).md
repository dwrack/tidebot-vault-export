### Variant 1: "Fall Is Houston's Best-Kept Secret"

**Angle:** Fall/Winter: The Bayou's Best-Kept Secret. New this pass, proven, the strongest angle in the batch on evidence quality.

**Evidence:** "Fall is Houston's best-kept secret. The heat breaks in October, the crowds thin out, and the bayou has a different energy. Cooler paddling, great light. It's the best time to be on the water." (Agent Instructions, verbatim, live guide talking point.) Corroborated: paddlingmag.com recommends October-November for Houston-area kayaking. Corroborated: Martin A, TripAdvisor, Nov 2025, 3-star: "We did have a fun experience. Kids enjoyed rowing and racing." Shelia M, TripAdvisor, Oct 2025, 5-star: "It was a peaceful paddle, especially on the way back in the dark."

**Hook options (per this pass's new 2026 trend finding, text-overlay-first, sound-off-safe, the hook has to land silently in the first half-second):**
1. Text overlay, no audio yet: "Fall is Houston's best-kept secret."
2. "Everyone books this in July. That's the mistake."
3. "The bayou in October is a different place."

**Body framework:** Text-overlay-first hook (this pass's new trend finding) into a slow, real-paced reveal. No forced verbal energy, the text does the opening work silently, then real ambient audio and a guide's actual voice carry the rest, matching the calm/authentic execution style already proven in pass 23's "Bayou Was Made for the Feed" angle.

**Shot list / visual beats:**
1. Cold open, no sound, just the text overlay: "Fall is Houston's best-kept secret."
2. Cut to water, mid-paddle, golden fall light, cooler-looking air (breath faintly visible if the footage allows, otherwise long sleeves on guests).
3. Text overlay, second beat: "The heat breaks in October."
4. Cut: an empty stretch of bayou, no other boats in frame, genuinely uncrowded.
5. Guide, to camera, real audio starts here: "This time of year, you basically have the water to yourself."
6. Cut: skyline at golden hour, same shot type as prior passes' reveal angles, but noticeably cooler light and color temperature than the summer footage already in rotation.
7. Text overlay, closing beat: "Best time to be on the water. Not the busiest. The best."
8. End card and CTA.

**Voiceover/caption track:** Minimal. The two text beats above carry the open, then one real line of guide audio ("This time of year, you basically have the water to yourself"), then silence again until the CTA.

**CTA overlay text:** "Claim a quiet morning. $55"

**Primary text (caption):** Everybody books this in July and fights the heat for it. Fall is the move. The heat breaks in October, the crowds thin out, and the bayou has a different energy entirely, cooler paddling, better light, and most mornings you'll have long stretches of water to yourself. Houston Skyline Tour, $55/person, 2 hours, all ages, Allen's Landing.

**Headlines:** "Fall Is Houston's Best-Kept Secret" and "Everyone Books July. That's the Mistake."

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Schwartz's market-sophistication instinct, applied to a false assumption instead of a competitor claim: the reader isn't comparing operators, they're comparing seasons, and they've silently decided summer is the only one that counts. Ogilvy's factual-specific discipline for the payoff line, "you basically have the water to yourself" is a checkable claim, not an adjective.

**Framework applied:** Name the reader's unstated assumption (this is a summer thing) before correcting it, so the correction lands as news instead of a generic seasonal push. The proof is BBK's own guide script, already true, already said out loud to guests every day, just never paid to reach anyone before.

**Reasoning:** This angle's entire strength is that it isn't invented copy, it's a script guides already deliver verbatim. The job here isn't to write something new, it's to stop hiding something true. Copywriting-2.0's "steal your own customer's words" rule applies just as much to a company's own internal script as it does to a guest review.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, one line cut for redundancy.

**Line-by-line:**
- First draft: "Experience Houston's magical fall season on the water." Fails all three tests: not visualizable ("magical" is a rating, not a picture), not falsifiable (no actual claim), not ownable (any seasonal tourism ad on earth could paste this in). Cut entirely.
- "You basically have the water to yourself." Passes all three: visualizable (you can picture the empty stretch of bayou), falsifiable (a checkable, specific claim about crowd density), ownable (this is BBK's own guide talking point, not a stock seasonal line).

**Sharpened final:** as written above.

**The one line to keep:** "Everyone books this in July. That's the mistake." Seven words that reframe the entire booking calendar.
