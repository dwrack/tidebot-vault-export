## Format 1: Reels

### Variant 1: "What I Did Today"

**Angle:** Right Next To The Rest Of Your Day. New this pass, proven, the strongest angle in the batch on evidence quality.

**Evidence:** "Right next to the rest of your day. Paddle in the morning, eat lunch in Montrose, see a show that night. We fit into a normal Houston day, we don't take it over." (Brand Story & Values.md, named differentiator, verbatim.) Operationalized with real detail from Agent Instructions: "After your paddle, Irma's is a few blocks away for some of the best Mexican food in the city. The lemonade is famous." / "If you're coming early, grab coffee at Boomtown Coffee on Main Street." / "Tacos Tierra Caliente on Airline (it's a truck, cash only, life-changing)."

**Hook (sharpened, one kept of three drafted):** "Paddle in the morning. Tacos by dark. Still just a Tuesday."

**Body framework:** Three-beat day-in-the-life, matching the "What I Did" three-panel trend mechanic exactly to BBK's own named Brand Story differentiator: the screen splits into panels that fill in as the day happens, so the pitch is structural, not just spoken.

**Shot list / visual beats:**
1. Cold open, text overlay: "What I did today."
2. Screen splits into three stacked horizontal panels, each fills in as the video plays.
3. Panel 1: guest paddling, downtown skyline behind, morning light. Tag: "Morning: paddled through downtown."
4. Panel 2: a hand holding coffee outside Boomtown Coffee on Main Street, a short walk from the dock. Tag: "Before: coffee a few blocks from the water."
5. Panel 3: a plate of tacos at a food-truck window, cash in hand. Tag: "After: tacos on Airline. Cash only. Worth it."
6. All three panels hold on screen together for a beat, the whole day visible at once.
7. Guide or guest, to camera, real audio: "Paddle in the morning, eat where the locals eat, still make it to whatever's next. Right next to the rest of your day."
8. End card and CTA.

**Voiceover/caption track:** Minimal. The panel tags carry the open, one spoken line near the end.

**CTA overlay text (sharpened):** "Still Just a Tuesday. $55"

**Primary text (caption):** You don't need a free Saturday for this. Paddle downtown in the morning, grab food or coffee a few blocks from the dock, and still make it to whatever you had planned tonight. Buffalo Bayou Kayak is built to fit next to the rest of your day, not replace it. Houston Skyline Tour, $55/person, 2 hours, all ages, Allen's Landing.

**Headlines:** "Right Next To The Rest Of Your Day" and "Paddle in the Morning. Still Have Your Whole Day."

**Objection handled:** I don't have time to build a whole outing around this, what do I even do before or after.

**Format spec:** Reel. Needs new B-roll pickups at Boomtown Coffee and a taco-truck shot (Tacos Tierra Caliente on Airline) if not already in the asset library; panel 1 can reuse existing skyline-paddle footage.

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Sugarman's slippery-slide instinct, applied structurally rather than just verbally: the three-panel build is the slide, each panel earns the next. Ogilvy's factual-specific discipline for the payoff, naming Boomtown Coffee and Airline Drive instead of "great food nearby."

**Framework applied:** Let the video's own structure make the argument (look how much fits into one day) instead of a voiceover asserting convenience. Then anchor every beat to a real, named place so the claim isn't decoration.

**Reasoning:** This is a named Brand Story line that never got specific supporting detail until this pass's research pulled the actual local recs out of the guide script. The job is to make the abstract promise ("fits into your day") concrete by naming exactly what fits: a coffee shop on Main Street, a taco truck on Airline. Ogilvy's rule that the factual specific beats the adjective is the whole mechanism here.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, two of three hooks cut, one CTA line rewritten.

**Line-by-line:**
- First draft hook: "What I did today. All of it." Fails ownable: any lifestyle influencer on any platform could caption any video with this. Cut entirely.
- First draft hook: "You don't need to clear your calendar for this." Fails ownable: "clear your calendar" is stock convenience-copy language, the exact phrase a meal-kit or subscription-box ad would use. Cut.
- "Paddle in the morning. Tacos by dark. Still just a Tuesday." Passes all three: visualizable (three concrete images in sequence), falsifiable (a specific, checkable claim about how the day actually goes), ownable (the flat "still just a Tuesday" understatement matches BBK's established voice, and no competitor can borrow the specific local details underneath it).
- First draft CTA: "Fit It Into Your Day. $55." Fails ownable: "fit it into your day" is the generic version of the exact claim the sharpened hook already makes better. Rewritten to "Still Just a Tuesday. $55," which repeats the ownable phrase instead of restating the promise abstractly.

**Sharpened final:** as written above (single hook, rewritten CTA).

**The one line to keep:** "Paddle in the morning. Tacos by dark. Still just a Tuesday." Three concrete beats and a flat, unimpressed closer, doing the entire angle's job in nine words.

### Variant 2: "The One Who Has to Pick"

**Angle:** Don't Be The One Who Picked The Bad Activity. New this pass, emerging.

**Evidence:** "You're probably worried about being the person who picked the activity nobody wanted to do. That's the worst feeling. Here's why that doesn't happen with this one..." (Agent Instructions, pre-emptive accusation audit, verbatim.)

**Hook options:**
1. "You're the one who has to pick the activity this time."
2. "Nobody says it, but everyone's thinking it: please don't pick something lame."
3. "The fear isn't the activity. It's being the one who chose it."

**Body framework:** Pre-emptive accusation audit, borrowed directly from BBK's own sales framework: name the unspoken fear before the viewer has to say it, then resolve it visually instead of with more talking.

**Shot list / visual beats:**
1. Open: one person, phone in hand, scrolling group-activity options, visibly stuck. Text overlay: "When you're the one who has to pick."
2. Text overlay, second beat: "Everyone's thinking the same thing. Please don't pick something lame."
3. Guide or narrator, to camera, real audio: "You're probably worried about being the person who picked the activity nobody wanted to do. That's the worst feeling."
4. Cut: the same person now on the water with their group, paddling, visibly relaxed, skyline behind them.
5. Text overlay: "Here's why that doesn't happen with this one."
6. Cut: group laughing together mid-paddle, genuine, unposed.
7. Guide/narrator (sharpened): "Worst case, you still get the skyline photos."
8. End card and CTA.

**Voiceover/caption track:** Two spoken lines pulled directly from BBK's own accusation-audit script, closing with the sharpened worst-case line above.

**CTA overlay text:** "Make the Safe Pick. Reserve Now."

**Primary text (caption, sharpened):** You know the feeling. You're the one who has to pick the activity, and if it flops, that's on you for the next six months. Kayaking through downtown Houston is different enough to be memorable, easy enough that nobody's out of their depth, and worst case, you still get skyline photos out of it. Houston Skyline Tour, $55/person, 2 hours, all ages.

**Headlines (sharpened):** "You're the One Who Has to Pick" and "Worst Case, You Still Get the Skyline Photos"

**Objection handled:** what if my group hates what I picked and I hear about it for years.

**Format spec:** Reel. Needs one new staged-but-real cold open (a person alone, deciding); everything after it can reuse existing group-paddle B-roll from prior birthday/bachelorette Reels.

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Schwartz's exact-internal-monologue instinct: name the reader's unstated fear (being the one blamed for a bad pick) before offering relief, this is a Problem Aware reader who knows the anxiety but doesn't yet believe a low-risk option exists. Halbert's A-pile conversational hook, "please don't pick something lame" is how the reader actually talks to themselves, not brand copy.

**Framework applied:** Open inside the reader's real internal monologue verbatim, which is BBK's own pre-emptive accusation audit doing Schwartz's "identify the problem" move for us, already field-tested in live sales chats.

**Reasoning:** This angle only works if the fear gets named plainly before the pitch shows up. The accusation audit script already does that job; the copy-mentors work here is packaging it into a visual scene (the stuck-scrolling cold open) rather than inventing a new argument.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, headline and closing line rewritten for a claim-risk problem, not a style problem.

**Line-by-line:**
- First draft: "Nobody's ever mad they went kayaking through downtown Houston." Fails falsifiability in a way that matters here specifically: this vault has four standing, dated operational flags (a no-show/cancellation pattern, a kayak-took-on-water safety complaint, a service-consistency complaint, a disputed lost-belongings complaint). An absolute "nobody's ever mad" claim is one screenshot of a bad review away from being publicly and embarrassingly disproven. Cut, not for tone, for risk.
- "Worst case, you still get the skyline photos." Passes all three: visualizable (you can picture the photo even on a mediocre day), falsifiable in a way that's actually defensible (a modest, specific claim instead of an absolute one), ownable (ties to BBK's real, checkable payoff instead of a blanket reassurance any operator could print).

**Sharpened final:** as written above (headline and closing VO/body line both swapped).

**The one line to keep:** "You're the one who has to pick the activity, and if it flops, that's on you for the next six months." Visualizable, falsifiable as a relatable truth, and the "six months" detail is what makes it stick instead of reading as generic anxiety copy.

#### Copy-mentors pass
