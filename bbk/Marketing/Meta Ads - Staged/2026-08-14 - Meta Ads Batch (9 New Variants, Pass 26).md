## Format 1: Reels

### Variant 4: "It's Not For Everyone"

**Angle:** It's Not For Everyone. New in pass 26, emerging.

**Evidence:** "Honestly, if you're not sure this is your kind of thing, that's okay. It's not for everyone. But the guests who do it almost always say it was the most surprising thing they've done in Houston." (Agent Instructions, Priority 6 sales-close bank, "THE TAKEAWAY," verbatim.)

**Hook options:**
1. "This isn't for everyone. Genuinely."
2. "If you're not sure this is your thing, that's fine. We mean it."
3. Text overlay only: "We're not going to try to convince you."

**Body framework:** The Takeaway (reverse-psychology honesty close). No hard sell in the open, the pattern-interrupt is the ad taking the guest's side of the doubt before making its case.

**Shot list / visual beats:**
1. Guide or narrator, direct to camera, calm and unhurried, no forced energy: "Honestly, if you're not sure this is your kind of thing, that's okay. It's not for everyone."
2. Beat of silence. Cut to real paddle footage, skyline sliding past, real pace, no music swell yet.
3. Text overlay: "But almost everyone who tries it says the same thing."
4. Cut: a guest's genuine, unposed reaction, mid-paddle, looking up at the skyline or spotting something in the water.
5. Text overlay, closing beat: "Most surprising thing I've done in Houston."
6. End card and CTA.

**Full body script (caption/primary text):** We're not going to oversell this one. If you're not sure a kayak tour through downtown Houston is your kind of thing, that's genuinely okay, it's not for everyone. But almost every guest who tries it says some version of the same thing: they didn't expect Houston to look like this. Houston Skyline Tour, $55/person, 2 hours, all ages.

**Objection handled:** Is this really for someone like me, or am I not the target audience for this.

**CTA:** Reserve Your Spot. Sales/Purchase-objective campaign.

**Format spec:** Reel. Needs one new direct-to-camera guide/narrator clip delivering the open line calmly (not performative); everything after can reuse existing skyline-paddle B-roll.

### Variant 1: "450 lbs. The Actual Number."

**Angle:** No Awkward Conversations Needed. New in pass 26, proven. Primary execution, the standout angle this pass.

**Evidence:** "'I'm worried about weight/size': Acknowledge: 'Appreciate you asking.' Reframe: 'Our kayaks hold up to 450 lbs combined.' **Hook options:**
1. "450 lbs combined. That's the actual number, not a guess."
2. "Some questions are hard to ask out loud. Here's the answer first."
3. "If you're over the limit, we just use two kayaks. That's it."

**Body framework:** Quiet objection-first (a modified PAS with no agitation stage, by design, per the guardrail). Name the fact before the fear gets a chance to form, keep it logistics-first, close with a plain next step instead of a sales push.

**Full body script (static primary text):** Some questions are hard to ask out loud, so here's the answer before you have to ask it. Our kayaks hold up to 450 lbs combined per boat. If your group is over that, we split you into two kayaks and keep going, no big deal. Give us a rough sense of your group when you call or text, and we'll have the right setup staged before you get here. We handle this all the time. Houston Skyline Tour, $55/person, 2 hours, all ages, Allen's Landing.

**Image concept:** A tandem kayak sitting steady and level in calm water at the dock, no guests in frame yet, or two guests already settled in and paddling normally. Nothing that draws attention to anyone's body. Plain, practical, unremarkable.

**Objection handled:** I'm worried I'm too big or heavy for this, and I don't want an awkward conversation about it at the dock in front of my group.

**CTA:** Contact Us. Sales/Purchase-objective campaign.

**Format spec:** Static hero. Needs a plain, neutral dock or on-water tandem-kayak shot with no dramatic framing.
