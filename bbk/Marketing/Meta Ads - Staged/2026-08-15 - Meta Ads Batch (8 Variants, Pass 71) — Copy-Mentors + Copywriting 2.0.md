### Variant 1: "Houston's Official Bird Doesn't Live at the Zoo, It Lives on This Bayou"

**Angle:** Houston's Official Bird Doesn't Live at the Zoo, It Lives on This Bayou. Proven this pass, first execution.

**Evidence quote:** "I was blown away just observing the Great Blue Herons, Egrets, and the city's official Bird, the Yellow-crowned Night Heron." (TripAdvisor guest review, first surfaced pass 10, verbatim per this vault's phrase bank.) Corroborated by Houston Audubon's 2019 press release: "the Yellow-crowned Night-Heron as the official Bird of Houston," voted via public bracket rounds from "over 60 bird species originally nominated," named 2019-09-24 (direct WebFetch, pass 71).

**Objection handled:** Will I actually see anything interesting, or is "wildlife" just marketing talk every operator says.

**Body framework:** Ogilvy factual-specific, a real, named, voted-on civic fact replaces a vague "see wildlife" claim.

**Hook (draft):** "Houston has an official bird. You can paddle right up to where it lives."

**Overlay text (draft):** "Houston's official bird isn't at the zoo. It's on the bayou."

**Primary text (draft):** Houston has an official bird: the Yellow-crowned Night-Heron, voted in by Houstonians in 2019 out of more than 60 nominated species, per Houston Audubon. One guest reviewer said observing it firsthand, alongside Great Blue Herons and egrets, was what stuck with them most. Sightings are common, not guaranteed, same as any wildlife. Sunset Skyline Tour and Houston Skyline Tour, $55/person, 2 hours, all ages.

**Format spec:** Static. Text-overlay card, a Yellow-crowned Night-Heron along the bayou if existing wildlife photography has one, otherwise a heron or egret shot with downtown skyline in frame; existing tour wildlife photography likely sufficient.

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Ogilvy, alone. A specific, named, institutionally-voted-on fact is exactly Ogilvy's territory: "you'll see wildlife" is a claim, "you'll see the bird Houston itself voted as its official bird" is a fact someone can check against Houston Audubon's own record.

**Framework applied:** Lead with the surprising civic fact (Houston has an official bird), attach the specific species and the vote behind it, close with the practical booking facts.

**Reasoning:** Sugarman's curiosity-gap structure was considered for the hook, since "Houston has an official bird" naturally invites a "wait, what is it" reaction, but the underlying sell is a verifiable, checkable fact rather than a mystery meant to be dragged out, so Ogilvy's plain factual approach fits the static card better than a build-the-curiosity structure. That curiosity mechanic gets used instead in Variant 2's Reel, where it has room to unfold.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, hook and headline tightened.

**Line-by-line:**
- Draft hook, "Houston has an official bird. You can paddle right up to where it lives," is two ideas competing for the same beat. Rewritten to "Houston voted on an official bird in 2019. It's on this bayou," which folds the civic-fact hook and the location payoff into one sentence and passes the falsifiable test immediately, the year and the vote are both checkable.
- "Houston's official bird isn't at the zoo. It's on the bayou." Passes all three: visualizable (a specific bird, a specific place), falsifiable (Houston Audubon's own 2019 record), ownable (no competitor in this market can claim this specific civic-vote proof point).
- Primary text's closing detail, "sightings are common, not guaranteed, same as any wildlife," matches this vault's standing honesty rule from `Brand Story & Values.md` and shouldn't be cut for punch, kept as-is.

**Sharpened final:**

- **Hook:** "Houston voted on an official bird in 2019. It's on this bayou."
- **Overlay text:** "Houston's official bird isn't at the zoo. It's on the bayou."
- **Primary text:** Houston has an official bird: the Yellow-crowned Night-Heron, voted in by Houstonians in 2019 out of more than 60 nominated species, per Houston Audubon. One guest reviewer said observing it firsthand, alongside Great Blue Herons and egrets, was what stuck with them most. Sightings are common, not guaranteed, same as any wildlife. Sunset Skyline Tour and Houston Skyline Tour, $55 per person, 2 hours, all ages.
- **Headline:** "Meet Houston's Official Bird, From a Kayak"
- **CTA:** Book Your Tour. Sales/Purchase-objective campaign.

**The one line to keep:** "Houston voted on an official bird in 2019." Eight words that turn a routine wildlife claim into a civic fact nobody expects an outdoor-tour ad to make, and one no competitor can borrow.
