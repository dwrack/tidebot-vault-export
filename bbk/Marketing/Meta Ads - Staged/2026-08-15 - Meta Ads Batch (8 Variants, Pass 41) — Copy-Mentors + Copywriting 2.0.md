### Variant 1: "You'll See Us Coming From Two Miles Out"

**Angle:** You'll See Us Coming From Two Miles Out. New in pass 41, proven, first execution. See pre-note 1 above.

**Evidence quote:** "Navigation lights are critical for us... Every vessel on the water after sunset must have a white light. This is federal law, not optional." / "White light visible 360 degrees, visible at 2 miles." (SOP Coast Guard & Compliance, verbatim, owned.)

**Objection handled:** Is a sunset or night tour actually safe and visible to other boat traffic.

**Shot list / visual beats:**
1. Text overlay: "Full Moon Tour. After dark, on the bayou."
2. Cut: kayak silhouette against the lit downtown skyline at dusk.
3. Text overlay landing the exact spec.
4. Cut: close-up of the navigation light glowing against dark water.
5. Text overlay: the federal-requirement line.
6. Cut: full group paddling under moonlight, skyline reflected on the water.
7. End card and CTA.

**Body framework:** Ogilvy factual-specific proof, let the exact number do the reassuring instead of an adjective.

**Hook (sharpened):** "Two miles. That's how far off you can see our light."

**Primary text (sharpened):** Paddling the bayou after dark sounds riskier than it is. Every kayak on our night tours carries a navigation light visible from two miles out, it's federal law and we don't skip it. Full Moon Tour, $65 per person, 2 hours, under the skyline.

**Headline:** "Visible From Two Miles Out, Every Night Tour"

**CTA overlay text:** "See the Bayou After Dark"

**CTA:** Book Your Tour. Sales/Purchase-objective campaign.

**Format spec:** Reel. Needs a low-light shot of the navigation light itself, likely a new capture; skyline-at-dusk footage may already exist in the asset library.

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Ogilvy primary, Bencivenga secondary. Ogilvy because this is his exact case, a real, specific spec ("visible at 2 miles") replaces any adjective about safety. Bencivenga for the closing framing, "it's federal law and we don't skip it" is a proof-tier statement, not a marketing claim, and it needs to read like one.

**Framework applied:** Lead with the specific number, use it as the entire proof, close with the fact that this is a requirement being met, not a promise being made.

**Reasoning:** Schwartz's belief-correction voice was considered (the reader might assume "dark water" automatically means "riskier"), but the correction here doesn't need a mental reframe, it needs one hard number. Ogilvy's rule that the factual specific beats the adjective is the more precise fit for a claim this checkable.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, hook tightened.

**Line-by-line:**
- Draft hook, "It's dark on the water. Here's why that's not a problem," tells the reader there's a solution without showing it, which is weak on the visualize test. Rewritten to "Two miles. That's how far off you can see our light," which opens with the actual number (Sugarman's short-first-sentence instinct, borrowed here even though the primary master is Ogilvy) and gives the eye something concrete before the sentence is even finished.
- "Visible From Two Miles Out, Every Night Tour." Passes all three: visualizable (a distance you can picture), falsifiable (a real spec, checkable against the SOP), ownable (specific enough that a generic "we're safe" competitor ad couldn't lift it without the same underlying requirement being highlighted).
- Primary text draft's opener, "Paddling the bayou after dark sounds like it should feel riskier. It doesn't have to," was slightly hedgy ("doesn't have to" implies effort on the reader's part). Tightened to "Paddling the bayou after dark sounds riskier than it is," a flatter, more confident statement.

**Sharpened final:** as written above (hook and primary-text opener rewritten).

**The one line to keep:** "Two miles. That's how far off you can see our light." Two short sentences, one hard number, no adjective doing any of the work.
