## Format 1: Fact-Overlay

### Variant 1: "Yes, There Are Alligators in Buffalo Bayou"

**Angle:** Yes, There Are Alligators, and No One's Been Hurt. New in pass 29, proven, first execution. See pre-note 2 above.

**Evidence quote:** "Yes, American alligators do live in Buffalo Bayou, though sightings are not guaranteed. They are most commonly spotted resting on the banks in the early morning. Alligators in urban bayous generally avoid humans, and no guest has ever had an unsafe encounter on one of our tours." (Marketing/Schema/faq-page.json, live FAQPage schema, verbatim, Read directly.)

**Objection handled:** Are there actually alligators in there? Am I going to get attacked?

**Overlay quote text (verbatim, owned schema):** "Alligators in urban bayous generally avoid humans, and no guest has ever had an unsafe encounter on one of our tours."

**Attribution line:** Buffalo Bayou Kayak, official site FAQ.

**Body framework:** Hierarchy of Proof, the owned FAQ answer stands in as the receipt, kept to a narrow claim.

**Hook (sharpened, one kept of two):** "Yes, there are alligators in Buffalo Bayou. Here's what that actually means for you."

**Primary text (sharpened):** We're not going to pretend they're not there. Alligators do live in Buffalo Bayou, most often spotted resting on the banks in early morning. Sightings aren't guaranteed, and they generally avoid humans. No guest has ever had an unsafe encounter on one of our tours. Houston Skyline Tour, $55 per person, 2 hours.

**CTA:** Book Your Tour. Sales/Purchase-objective campaign.

**Format spec:** Fact-overlay. Existing calm-water and bank photography works, no new asset required. Do not use footage that appears to bait, approach, or feature an alligator up close.

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Bencivenga, alone. Same mechanism this vault has used for every fear-adjacent fact claim to date: state the checkable truth once, narrowly, and stop. The reader isn't asking for reassurance in the abstract, they're asking a specific yes-or-no question, and Bencivenga's proof-over-assertion register is the only one careful enough to answer it without either alarming the reader or overselling safety the brand can't guarantee.

**Framework applied:** State the checkable, narrow fact (presence, low sighting frequency, avoidance behavior, track record) and stop there. Never expand into a blanket safety claim.

**Reasoning:** Any master with more showmanship (Halbert, Sugarman) would risk making a real wildlife fact read like a stunt. This claim needs to be believed, not sold.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Needs rework. Hook tightened.

**Line-by-line:**
- Draft hook, "Yes, there are alligators in Buffalo Bayou. Here's the honest version," fails the visualize test on its second half: "the honest version" is an abstract label, not an image. Rewritten to "Here's what that actually means for you," which points forward to a specific, concrete answer instead of describing the answer as a category.
- "We're not going to pretend they're not there." Passes all three: visualizable (a direct admission), falsifiable (a real, checkable fact), ownable (most tourism ads avoid this topic entirely, which is exactly the gap this line names).
- "Sightings aren't guaranteed, and they generally avoid humans. No guest has ever had an unsafe encounter on one of our tours." Checked specifically against the guardrail: all three qualifiers from the source are present and none is dropped or upgraded. Passes all three tests: visualizable (a specific claim about behavior and history), falsifiable (a checkable, sourced statement), ownable (a level of candor a competitor's generic "safe and fun" copy wouldn't attempt).

**Sharpened final:** as written above (hook tightened, body held).

**The one line to keep:** "We're not going to pretend they're not there." Nine words that do more trust-building than any reassurance could.
