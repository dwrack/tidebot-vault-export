## Format 1: Reels

### Variant 1: "The Bacteria Advisory Is Real. Here's What It Actually Says."

**Angle:** You're On It, Not In It. New this pass, proven strength, the strongest new angle: the first in the whole research run built on an official government source rather than a review or blog.

**Evidence:** "Buffalo and Whiteoak Bayous, and several tributaries, sometimes have bacteria levels that are higher than state standards for contact recreation." / "Swimming and wading are considered contact recreation." (Houston-Galveston Area Council, Buffalo and White Oak Bayous Bacteria Implementation Group.)

**Hook options (first 2 seconds, under 12 spoken words per this pass's 2026 hook-performance data):**
1. "Buffalo Bayou has a real bacteria advisory. Here's the catch."
2. "I've heard the water rumors for years. Here's what's true."
3. "The state has data on this water. We're not hiding it."

**Body framework:** Contrarian-statement hook into voluntary-vulnerability myth-bust, the same admit-first pattern BBK's own brand script already uses for water questions ("You're in a sit-on-top kayak, so you're above the water," Agent Instructions doc), extended here with an actual citation instead of a general reassurance.

**Shot list / visual beats:**
1. Calm water close-up. Text card: "Buffalo Bayou has a real bacteria advisory."
2. Guide or narrator to camera: "It's real. Here's what it actually says."
3. Text overlay, the H-GAC line verbatim: "bacteria levels higher than state standards for contact recreation."
4. Text overlay: "Contact recreation means swimming and wading."
5. Cut to guests in sit-on-top kayaks, clearly above the waterline, paddling normally.
6. Narrator: "You're paddling on top of it in a boat. Not swimming in it. Not wading in it."
7. Cutaway: a heron or turtle sighting.
8. End card and CTA.

**Voiceover/caption track:** "Buffalo Bayou has a real, official bacteria advisory. We're not going to pretend it doesn't exist." Then: "It's specifically about swimming and wading. You're in a sit-on-top kayak the whole tour, on top of the water, not in it."

**CTA overlay text:** "The Honest Version of the Water Question. $55"

**Primary text (caption):** Buffalo Bayou has a real bacteria advisory. The Houston-Galveston Area Council's own data says the bayou sometimes runs higher than state standards, and that finding is specifically about contact recreation: swimming and wading. A guided kayak tour is neither. You're in a sit-on-top kayak the entire time, paddling on top of the water, not swimming or wading in it. Houston Skyline Tour, $55/person, 2 hours, all ages, Allen's Landing.

**Headlines:** "The Bacteria Advisory Is Real. Here's What It Actually Says." and "We Read the Water Report So You Don't Have To"

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Claude Hopkins/Ogilvy's reason-why voluntary-weakness technique, the same admit-the-hard-thing-first move BBK's own brand script already uses for the water question, upgraded here with an actual government citation instead of a general reassurance line. Schwartz for naming the Unaware-stage objection ("is this water contaminated") directly instead of waiting for it in the comments.

**Framework applied:** State the fact a skeptic could otherwise use against the brand, first and unprompted, sourced to something checkable, then draw the narrow, honest line that actually protects the guest's decision.

**Reasoning:** This is the same first-party honesty pattern passes 15 and 16 already proved works (The Ick Factor, Say the Quiet Part About the Trash), now run against a much stronger, third-party-verifiable source. The risk is overclaiming, so the script never says the water is clean and never says the finding doesn't apply. It says exactly what's true: the advisory is about contact recreation, and a boat tour isn't contact recreation.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, one line tightened for precision against the guardrail.

**Line-by-line:**
- First draft: "Don't worry, the bacteria thing doesn't apply to us." Fails the guardrail outright (implies blanket dismissal) and fails can-nobody-else-say-it (every operator with a water problem says a version of "don't worry"). Cut entirely.
- "You're paddling on top of it in a boat. Not swimming in it. Not wading in it." Passes all three tests: visualizable (you can picture exactly where the guest's body is relative to the water), falsifiable (a specific, checkable physical claim, not a vibe), ownable (only a brand willing to cite the actual advisory would open with it).

**Sharpened final:** as written above.

**The one line to keep:** "Not swimming in it. Not wading in it." Six words that do the entire guardrail's job without a single hedge word.

### Variant 2: "You Can Watch the Bats Free. Here's What You Don't Get."

**Angle:** The View From the Water, Not the Sidewalk. New this pass, proven strength, the first angle to answer the real "why pay" objection instead of just selling the moment in isolation.

**Evidence:** "You can watch the emergence for free, nightly, at the corner of Waugh Drive and Allen Parkway. Viewing is free from the platform, sidewalk or bridge. No reservations are needed." (downtownhouston.org / Buffalo Bayou Partnership.)

**Hook options:**
1. "You can watch the bats free from the sidewalk. Here's what you miss."
2. "Waugh Bridge is free every night. We're not pretending otherwise."
3. "Everyone on the bridge is looking down. We're the ones underneath them."

**Body framework:** Product/Outcome Showcase opening (this pass's 2026 trend finding: open on the payoff already in frame rather than building to a reveal), into a direct concession-and-contrast structure: acknowledge the free option fully and fairly, then name specifically what the boat adds that the sidewalk can't.

**Shot list / visual beats:**
1. Open at second 0 on bats already streaming directly overhead, kayak POV, no build-up.
2. Cut wide: the sidewalk crowd at Waugh Drive, looking down and across at the bridge.
3. Text overlay: "Free from the sidewalk. No reservation needed. That part's true."
4. Cut back to kayak POV, guide narrating, bats streaming overhead.
5. Text overlay: "From the water, you're under them. Not across from them."
6. Guide pointing something out, guests looking straight up, skyline at dusk behind them.
7. Wide shot, kayaks silhouetted against the last light.
8. End card and CTA.

**Voiceover/caption track:** "You can watch the bats for free, every night, from Waugh Drive. We're not going to tell you that's not true." Then: "From the water, you're not looking across at them. You're underneath them, and your guide's telling you what you're actually looking at."

**CTA overlay text:** "The View You Can't Get From the Bridge. $60"

**Primary text (caption):** You can watch the bat emergence for free, every night, from the Waugh Drive sidewalk. No reservation needed. That's true, and we're not going to pretend it isn't. What the sidewalk doesn't give you: 300,000 bats streaming directly overhead instead of across the water, a guide who knows the colony and narrates the whole thing, and the rest of the paddle, downtown skyline at sunset, wildlife along the route, before the bats even start. Sunset Skyline Bat Bridge Kayak Tour, $60/person, 2 to 3 hours, ages 7+.

**Headlines:** "You Can Watch the Bats Free. Here's What You Don't Get." and "The View From Underneath, Not Across the Street"

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Halbert's direct concession, name the competing offer honestly before the reader can raise it themselves, plus Sugarman's slippery-slide structure applied to the open: start the ad already inside the best moment, then explain how they get there.

**Framework applied:** Full, fair acknowledgment of the free alternative first, immediately followed by the specific, non-interchangeable benefit (proximity, narration, the rest of the paddle) that the free option structurally cannot offer, no matter how the sidewalk crowd frames it.

**Reasoning:** Brand voice never bashes competitors, and the free sidewalk isn't a competitor, it's a fair, well-known objection. The move is honesty plus contrast, not denial. This also gives the Bat Bridge Reveal angle (pass 9) a genuinely different companion instead of a repeat: that one sells the moment, this one sells the moment specifically against the free version most locals already know about.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, one clause cut for redundancy.

**Line-by-line:**
- First draft: "Why pay when you can experience the magic for free? We think it's worth it." Fails can-nobody-else-say-it (generic upsell logic any operator could paste in) and fails falsifiability (what does "magic" even mean). Cut.
- "You're underneath them. Not across from them." Passes all three tests: visualizable (a precise spatial claim about where the bats are relative to the guest), falsifiable (true or false based on where each vantage point actually is), ownable (only the boat can make this specific claim, the sidewalk physically cannot).

**Sharpened final:** as written above.

**The one line to keep:** "You're underneath them. Not across from them." The entire objection-answer in five words, no adjectives required.
