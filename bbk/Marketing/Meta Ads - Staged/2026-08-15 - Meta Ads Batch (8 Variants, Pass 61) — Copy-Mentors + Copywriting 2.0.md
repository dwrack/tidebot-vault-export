### Variant 1: "Not a First-Date Kayak"

**Angle:** Not a First-Date Kayak. New in pass 61, emerging, first execution.

**Evidence quote:** "It can be, with one caveat: it works best for a second or third date, not a first meeting with a stranger, since a two-hour guided tour is a bigger time commitment than coffee." (houston-date-ideas-on-the-water.md, FAQ A2, verbatim, owned.)

**Objection handled:** Will spending two hours alone with someone I barely know feel too intense or awkward for a first date.

**Body framework:** Halbert plain-directness, matches the brand's own documented "vulnerability and honesty" sales pattern, an unprompted small downside stated flatly makes everything else read as more credible.

**Hook (draft):** "This isn't a first-date activity. We'll tell you that upfront."

**Overlay text (draft):** "Not a first date. A second or third one."

**Primary text (draft):** We'll be straight with you: this isn't the move for a first meeting with someone you just matched with. Two hours in a kayak is a bigger ask than coffee. But once you've already met a few times, it's one of the better date ideas in Houston, doing something together instead of talking across a table cuts through the awkwardness fast. Sunset Skyline Tour, $55/person, 2 hours, launching from Allen's Landing.

**Format spec:** Static. Text-overlay card, couple in a kayak at golden hour, downtown skyline behind; existing sunset-tour photography likely sufficient.

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Halbert, alone. This is Halbert's exact register, a plain, conversational admission that answers a real, specific worry before the reader even has to ask it, no proof-stacking needed, just an honest statement of when this product is and isn't the right call.

**Framework applied:** Name the exact hesitation (too much, too soon), tell the reader flatly when it works instead, close with the payoff for the right timing.

**Reasoning:** Ogilvy's factual-specific style was considered, since there's a real number here (two hours vs. coffee), but the emotional core of this angle is permission-giving and self-aware honesty, not a specific proof point standing in for a vague claim. That's Halbert's territory more than Ogilvy's.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, hook tightened.

**Line-by-line:**
- Draft hook, "This isn't a first-date activity. We'll tell you that upfront," is two clauses doing the work of one. Rewritten to "Not a first-date activity. We'll say so upfront," which is shorter and reads more like something a person would actually text.
- "We'll Tell You When Not to Book This." Passes all three: visualizable (a real decision point), falsifiable (a real, sourced piece of dating-stage guidance), ownable (no competitor publishes explicit sequencing advice like this).
- Primary text's "doing something together instead of talking across a table cuts through the awkwardness fast" is a strong, concrete line, kept as-is.

**Sharpened final:**

- **Hook:** "Not a first-date activity. We'll say so upfront."
- **Overlay text:** "Not a first date. A second or third one."
- **Primary text:** We'll be straight with you: this isn't the move for a first meeting with someone you just matched with. Two hours in a kayak is a bigger ask than coffee. But once you've already met a few times, it's one of the better date ideas in Houston, doing something together instead of talking across a table cuts through the awkwardness fast. Sunset Skyline Tour, $55 per person, 2 hours, launching from Allen's Landing.
- **Headline:** "We'll Tell You When Not to Book This"
- **CTA:** Book Your Tour. Sales/Purchase-objective campaign.

**The one line to keep:** "We'll be straight with you." Three words that do the same job the brand's own "vulnerability and honesty" playbook is built around, said before the reader has asked for it.
