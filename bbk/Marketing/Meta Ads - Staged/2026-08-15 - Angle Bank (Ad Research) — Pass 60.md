One angle can't fill 8 variants across 4 required formats. So this pass built a full inventory of every real, evidence-backed angle name ever logged across `Ad Research/ad-research-pass*.json` (28 through 60) and cross-referenced it against every angle actually scripted into a finished ad in `Marketing/Meta Ads - Staged/*Meta Ads Batch*.md`. That surfaced 5 previously-researched angles (all from pass 30, one from pass 28's dropped-candidates list correctly excluded) that were real and evidence-backed but had simply never been turned into a script. Combined with pass 60's 1 new angle, that's the 4 angles this batch uses.

## Detail and sourcing

- **They End Up Coming Back More Than They Expected** — Guest Experience Quiz's answer key for the membership-recommendation scenario: *"If you think you'd come out even 3-4 times a year, it pays for itself. And a lot of members tell me they end up coming way more than they expected once they have it."* Distinct from "Break Even in Six Trips" (pass 41, the math) and "Pays for Itself by Month Three" (pass 25, the sales script's rounding). Both of those sell the breakeven math; this one sells a behavioral surprise. Rated emerging: "a lot of members tell me" is a guide's anecdotal training-material line, not a counted statistic. **Deliberately not dressed up as a guest testimonial** in the review-overlay execution, per pass 60's own explicit instruction.

- **The Thing That Looks Like an Alligator (It's a Fish)** — a documented Safety Briefing Script line (*"Turtles, herons, egrets, the occasional alligator gar surfacing. Look, don't touch. Don't feed them."*) corroborated by two independent third-party sources (savebuffalobayou.org confirming alligator gar are a known bayou sighting, garfish-texas.com confirming the 20-40 lb size). The strongest unscripted angle in the backlog on evidence quality. Distinct from the already-scripted "Yes, There Are Alligators, and No One's Been Hurt" (pass 29), which is a safety-reassurance angle about real, if rare, alligators. This is a different animal (a large native fish, not a reptile) and a different emotional register (surprise/discovery, matching the Brand Story's "surprise is the secret" principle).

- **Ten Minutes to "I've Got This"** — an unpublished blog draft's observation (*"Most people are steering fine within five minutes. It's closer to riding a bike slowly than anything athletic."*) corroborated independently by a general (non-BBK) Fodor's Travel Talk Forums post about the same clumsy-then-fine learning curve. Distinct from "Standing By Minute 15" (paddleboard-specific, about balance); this is kayak-specific, about steering/coordination.

- **We'll Point You Somewhere Else If We're Not the Right Fit** — an unpublished blog draft's language about redirecting a guest to a different stretch of the bayou or a different launch point when it genuinely fits their trip better, plus a real anecdote about a guest confused by conflicting "best of" lists. The rarest trust device in the whole backlog: an operator that will actively point a lead elsewhere.

## Guardrails carried into scripting

- **They End Up Coming Back More Than They Expected**, review-overlay execution (Variant 2): this is an internal guide/staff quote, not a guest review. No star rating, no reviewer name, no "Google review" framing anywhere on the asset. Labeled "From our guides, Guest Experience training notes." Same treatment pass 41 gave "The Only Thing We've Lost Out Here Is Phones" in its own review-overlay slot. This deliberately deviates from the vault's default review-overlay pattern (a real, named, attributed guest review) because no unused, appropriately-attributable real guest review existed for this angle: the vault's one strong recent guest review (Nicole Blanchard, Birdeye) has already been used twice for two different sentences/claims across passes 10/38 and 42, and the three freshest reviews all credit a guide ("Chad") who pass 60's own research explicitly flagged as too risky to build public creative around since he's still missing from `Operations/Guides & Rental Agents.md`.
- **The Thing That Looks Like an Alligator (It's a Fish):** never call the alligator gar an alligator or imply danger. Frame as prehistoric-looking, harmless, older than the city. Runs alongside, not in place of, the existing real-alligator safety angle; they answer different curiosities.
- **We'll Point You Somewhere Else If We're Not the Right Fit:** no specific competitor business named in the ad copy, even though pass 30's research note judged naming real alternatives (it listed examples) as brand-appropriate. None of the underlying verbatim evidence quotes name a business, and naming a real third-party operator in paid ad copy carries an accuracy/liability risk this batch chose not to take on without David's sign-off. The honesty/redirect spirit is preserved without a specific name.
- **Ten Minutes to "I've Got This" and We'll Point You Somewhere Else If We're Not the Right Fit** both source their BBK-specific evidence from unpublished, unapproved blog drafts sitting in `Marketing/Blog Drafts - Staged/`. First-party, real, owned language, but not yet fact-checked or live. Rated emerging accordingly, not proven.
- **No fabricated testimonials anywhere in this batch.** Every claim traces to a real owned-document fact, a real unpublished-but-owned blog draft (flagged as such), or a real corroborating third-party source.
- **No competitor named anywhere**, per standing brand voice (`Brand Story & Values.md`: "We never bash competitors by name, and we don't need to").
- Objective is always Purchase/Sales, never Traffic, per this vault's standing convention.
- Brand voice throughout: guests, paddle, the bayou, downtown, urban nature, wildlife (never oversold, "common" not "guaranteed"). No "customers," no "extreme," no "expedition."
