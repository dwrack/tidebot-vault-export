**Read this before the scripts:**

1. **The FAQ/"You're On It, Not In It" tension, resolved.** Pass 28's research flagged that BBK's FAQ language on swimming ("bring a small towel... if you want to go for a swim, sometimes you get wet from paddling") sits alongside the already-scripted "You're On It, Not In It" angle (pass 17), built on the claim that a guided kayak tour is not contact recreation under Houston-Galveston Area Council bacteria-advisory standards. Read against the actual pass 17 script (`2026-08-14 - Meta Ads Batch (8 New Variants, Pass 17).md`, Variants 1, 3, and 6), that angle's real claim is narrow: the tour activity itself, paddling on top of the water in a sit-on-top kayak, is not swimming or wading, so the state's contact-recreation bacteria advisory doesn't apply to it. It never claims a guest can't get wet or can't choose to swim; it says the tour isn't swimming. Read that way, it's compatible with the FAQ's casual "if you want to go for a swim, sometimes you get wet" line, which is about an optional, incidental choice, not the tour's core activity. Per the task instructions, that script was left untouched. To keep it that way, nothing in this batch (especially Variants 2 and 3, You Can't Sink This Thing) states or implies guests never get wet or can never swim; both scripts stay scoped to what happens if you flip, not to whether you can voluntarily get in the water.
2. **Variants 2 and 3 (You Can't Sink This Thing) carry this pass's heaviest guardrail, heavier than any single angle in pass 27.** The research angle's working title and its cited evidence ("sit-on-tops won't sink," from the internal Incident Report Steps SOP) sit in direct, uncomfortable proximity to a specific, still-open operational flag: Karen M's TripAdvisor review (Aug 2025, "Nice people and scary situation") describes a kayak taking on water mid-tour to the point of nearly sinking, alongside a trainee-guide handling question. That's not a vague safety worry, it's the same territory this angle's working title would otherwise assert an absolute claim over. Both scripts below were deliberately rewritten away from the angle's literal working title ("You Can't Sink This Thing") to a narrower, defensible claim: if you flip, you float, because every guest wears a life vest and sit-on-top kayaks are designed not to submerge, and a guide is on the water to help. Neither script claims a flawless equipment record, an unblemished safety history, or that capsizing can't happen. **Recommend Connor read these two specifically before launch**, and confirm current pre-launch equipment checks (the same recommendation first raised in pass 16's research) are happening as documented, since Variant 5 below describes exactly that checklist.
3. **Variant 5 (Everything Gets Checked Before You Ever See It) sits adjacent to the same flag from a different angle.** Karen M's review is also the source of the standing objection "is the equipment actually checked before I get in." This script answers that question honestly, with the real internal Morning Inspection Checklist, but describes the documented routine, not a claim that nothing has ever gone wrong or could go wrong. The checklist's own bluntest line ("A PFD with a broken buckle is not a PFD, pull it from rotation immediately") was deliberately left out of the guest-facing copy, per pass 28 research's own note that it reads too internal for a first execution; it's logged as a strong line for a future pass instead.
4. **Variant 6 (Your Guide Reads Water You Can't See) makes a local-knowledge claim, not a blanket guide-quality guarantee.** Karen M's review also raised a trainee-guide question. This script stays scoped to what the internal Role Guide document and the real guest review both say: guides track changing water conditions. It does not claim every guide is always fully experienced or that the trainee-guide question is resolved.
5. **Variant 7 (No One Gets Left Behind) is built on BBK's own sales-script language, not the competitor-review signal the research report also surfaced.** The research pass found an unverified (WebSearch-summarized, not raw-text-confirmed) TripAdvisor pattern about a competitor's guides moving ahead of slower guests. That signal is not used anywhere in this script, no competitor is named or alluded to, per brand voice. The claim here is scoped entirely to BBK's own documented practice: "your guide adjusts the pace to the group," pulled verbatim from the Agent Instructions sales script's own seniors/less-active-guest guidance.
6. **Style rule:** no em dashes, no double dashes, anywhere in any headline, hook, body line, description, or CTA. Confirmed by direct grep of this file's creative fields after the sharpen pass (see Style rule confirmation section at the end).
7. **Fact-check note:** every FAQ, SOP, and sales-script line used below was read directly this pass (FAQ quotes verified against `Ad Research/ad-research-pass28.json`'s direct-fetch record; SOP quotes verified by reading `SOPs/Quick Ref/Morning Inspection Checklist.md`, `SOPs/Quick Ref/Guest Check-In Steps.md`, `SOPs/Quick Ref/Incident Report Steps.md`, and `SOPs/Deep Read/Role Guide — Kayak Guide.md` directly), not carried forward from memory or an earlier pass's table.

### Variant 2: "If You Flip, You Float"

**Angle:** You Can't Sink This Thing (float/capsize reassurance). New in pass 28, proven per research, first execution. Standout, highest-care script this pass. See pre-note 2 above before reading.

**Evidence quote:** "All participants wear life vests, so you will float. Guides are on the water with every tour and will assist anyone who falls in." (buffalobayoukayak.com/faq/, direct-fetched verbatim.) "Guide the guest to hold onto the kayak (sit-on-tops won't sink)." (SOPs/Quick Ref/Incident Report Steps.md, Capsizing section, verbatim, Read directly.)

**Objection handled:** What actually happens if I flip the kayak?

**Shot list / visual beats:**
1. Calm water, an empty sit-on-top kayak floating stably, a guide nearby.
2. Text overlay: "Every guest wears a life vest. The whole tour. No exceptions."
3. Cut: guide's hand resting on the kayak, showing how far it sits above the waterline.
4. Text overlay: "If you go in, your guide is already on the water with you."
5. End card and CTA.

**Body framework:** Proof-first reassurance, scoped narrowly to floatation and guide response, not a blanket safety guarantee.

**Hook (sharpened, one kept of two):** "What actually happens if you flip? You float. Here's why."

**Primary text (sharpened):** If you flip a sit-on-top kayak, you don't go under, you float. Every guest wears a life vest the entire tour, and a guide is on the water with you the whole time to help if you go in. Houston Skyline Tour, $55 per person, 2 hours.

**Headline:** "If You Flip, You Float"

**CTA overlay text:** "Book Your Tour"

**CTA:** Book Your Tour. Sales/Purchase-objective campaign.

**Format spec:** Reel. Needs a real, controlled demo shot: a guide showing a sit-on-top kayak's stability at the dock or in shallow water, not a dramatized capsize. Do not restage or reference any actual guest incident.

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Bencivenga, alone. Same believability-gap mechanism pass 27 used for its federal-law variant, applied here to a physical-safety fear that sits directly next to an open, unresolved complaint. The reader isn't just skeptical of a generic claim, a specific bad outcome is documented in this exact category. Bencivenga's proof-over-assertion register is the only one careful enough to state the narrow, true fact without drifting toward either fear-mongering or an overpromise the brand can't back up.

**Framework applied:** State the checkable, narrow fact (floatation, life vest, guide presence) and stop there. Never expand into a record claim.

**Reasoning:** Halbert's showmanship or Sugarman's curiosity-loop energy would both risk making a genuine physical-safety fact sound like a sales pitch, exactly wrong for the territory this angle sits in. Bencivenga's flat, specific register is the only one built for a claim this literal, right next to a claim this sensitive.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Needs rework. Headline changed from the angle's working title, one hook option cut.

**Line-by-line:**
- "You Can't Sink This Thing" (the research angle's own working title, considered as a headline option) fails the guardrail on inspection: it's an absolute claim in the exact territory of a specific, documented complaint about a kayak nearly sinking. Rewritten to "If You Flip, You Float," a narrower, true, still-visualizable claim about what happens to the guest, not a claim about the equipment's failure rate.
- "What actually happens if you flip? You float. Here's why." Passes all three tests: visualizable (a specific physical moment, a body in water next to a floating kayak), falsifiable (a checkable claim about sit-on-top design and life-vest policy, not a vague reassurance), ownable (most competitors in this category don't volunteer this level of specificity about what happens if something goes wrong).
- Second drafted hook, "Flip a sit-on-top kayak and here's what happens next," was a weaker, less direct restatement of the surviving hook. Cut.
- "Every guest wears a life vest the entire tour, and a guide is on the water with you the whole time to help if you go in." On inspection, passes all three cleanly: visualizable (a vest, on a body, the whole time), falsifiable (a specific, checkable operational fact, not a superlative), ownable (states presence and policy plainly rather than claiming a guarantee).

**Sharpened final:** as written above (headline rewritten, one hook option cut, body held).

**The one line to keep:** "If you flip a sit-on-top kayak, you don't go under, you float." A physical fact stated plainly, with nothing claimed beyond it.
