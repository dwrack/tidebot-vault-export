## Angles scripted this pass

1. **The Guide Has a Name (and a Personality):** proven, the strongest new angle this pass. Seven different named guides (Blake, Josh, Sebastian, Kaleb, Katrina, Chad, David) show up individually across roughly three years of reviews, not as generic "staff." Distinct from the already-scripted "Real Reviews: Blake Made Me Feel Safe" (a single-guide, safety-specific execution). This is the broader roster pattern.
2. **Solo Travelers Welcome:** emerging, one clean source (Mona H, TripAdvisor, Jun 2025, solo-tagged trip). Thin evidence, real audience gap the vault has never addressed directly.
3. **The Ick Factor (Honest Water Talk):** emerging. Research (Houston Press, a piece about a *different* operator's self-guided canoe trip) surfaced a real, visceral pre-trip fear pattern about the water itself, not general safety or ability fear. Rather than misattribute that outside quote to a BBK guest, both scripts built on this angle pull from BBK's own **pre-approved, first-party** vulnerability script in `Buffalo Bayou Kayak — Agent Instructions (Copy-Paste).md`: "I'll be straight with you: the bayou isn't crystal blue water..." That line has never run as paid ad copy before.
4. **Say the Quiet Part About the Trash:** emerging, one source (Martin A, TripAdvisor, Nov 2025, describing debris in the water). Deliberately kept separate from the already-scripted "We Clean What We Paddle" (angle 12). That one is stewardship pride; this one is radical-candor trust-building using an unflattering real quote as proof of honesty.

## Not scripted this pass: operational flags, not ad angles

Research surfaced three things that are not ad copy and are flagged directly to David/Connor in the research doc instead:
- A TripAdvisor review (Karen M, Aug 2025) describes a kayak taking on significant water mid-tour, close to sinking, plus untrained/trainee guides and inconsistent waiver checks. Safety-relevant, needs a direct operational look before any "safe, worry-free" copy runs.
- The no-show/last-minute-cancellation pattern first flagged in pass 15 now has a third dated instance (Amy W, May 2024), extending it to a roughly two-year tail. Reinforces the standing recommendation to hold any reliability/trust-guarantee angle.
- Reddit access is confirmed structurally down across two independent tools this pass (this research's own WebSearch/WebFetch, and David's own `reddit-finder` scraper hitting the identical failure on a live, on-topic thread). Worth a look before pass 17, not a content gap, an access problem.
