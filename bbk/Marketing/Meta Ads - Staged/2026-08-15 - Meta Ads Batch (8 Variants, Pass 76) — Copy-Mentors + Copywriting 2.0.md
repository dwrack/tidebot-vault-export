## Format 1: Static

### Variant 1: "There's a Second BBK Tour, and It's Not on the Bayou at All"

**Angle:** There's a Second BBK Tour, and It's Not on the Bayou at All. First execution.

**Evidence quote:** "Join us on a kayak (a tandem or single) with a local, well informed guide on a two hour unforgettable experience. Learn how the town of Kemah was founded, the impact of hurricanes, and the importance of Clear Lake." (buffalobayoukayak.com/all-activities/, direct fetch, pass 76.)

**Objection handled:** I already know Buffalo Bayou Kayak, I've seen the downtown tour, is there anything else.

**Body framework:** Ogilvy factual-specific. Name the actual second product instead of teasing "more experiences."

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Ogilvy, alone. This is a headline-does-80%-of-the-work problem: the entire job is getting a guest who thinks they already know BBK to stop and register that a second, entirely different product exists. Ogilvy's move is a factual specific, not a tease.

**Framework applied:** State the surprising fact plainly, in the brand's own name, then let the specifics (location, price, age minimum) do the proving. No adjectives doing the selling.

**Critique of the draft:** The draft hook, "You know the downtown tour. You don't know about this one," fails Ogilvy's own standard before it fails anything else, it's vague on both halves. "This one" isn't a product name, it's a placeholder. Ogilvy would never publish a headline that could apply to any unnamed second thing. Rewritten to name the actual fact: the company, the count, the location.

**Reasoning:** Halbert's direct-address was considered for the hook (naming the assumption "you know everything we offer" and correcting it) but that's exactly what Variant 4's review-overlay does with this same angle. Keeping Variant 1 in Ogilvy's plain-fact register, rather than Halbert's confrontational one, gives the batch two genuinely different entry points into the same angle instead of two variants that sound alike.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Draft hook and headline don't survive the 3-test filter as written. Rewritten below; primary text mostly held up with one overclaim caught and cut.

**Line-by-line:**
- Draft hook, "You know the downtown tour. You don't know about this one," fails the falsifiable test: "this one" isn't a checkable claim, it's a placeholder for one. Rewritten to **"Buffalo Bayou Kayak runs a second tour. It's not on the bayou."** Visualizable (a second, specific tour), falsifiable (true or false, checkable against the site), ownable (only BBK can say this about BBK).
- Draft headline, "The Tour Most People Don't Know We Run," passes visualize and falsify but is soft on ownable, a version of this sentence could describe almost any under-marketed product at almost any company. Rewritten to **"A Second Buffalo Bayou Kayak Tour, on Clear Lake"** which names the specific, checkable, ownable fact (Clear Lake) instead of the vague "most people don't know" framing.
- Overlay text, "Not on the bayou. Not downtown. A whole different trip," was checked and mostly passes, but "a whole different trip" is a soft abstraction closing out two strong concrete lines. Rewritten to end on the specific instead: **"Not on the bayou. Not downtown. Kemah, on Clear Lake."**
- Primary text originally opened "Most people who know Buffalo Bayou Kayak know the downtown tour, the skyline, the bats, the bayou," which is a claim about what "most people" know, unfalsifiable and unownable, anyone could say it about any company's flagship product. Cut the "most people" framing entirely and opened directly on the fact instead.

**Sharpened final:**

- **Hook:** "Buffalo Bayou Kayak runs a second tour. It's not on the bayou."
- **Overlay text:** "Not on the bayou. Not downtown. Kemah, on Clear Lake."
- **Primary text:** There's a Buffalo Bayou Kayak tour that isn't on Buffalo Bayou. The Kemah Kayak Historical and Ecological Tour runs on Clear Lake, with a guide who covers how Kemah was founded, what the hurricanes did to it, and why Clear Lake matters to the area. $50 per person, 2 hours, ages 8 and up.
- **Headline:** "A Second Buffalo Bayou Kayak Tour, on Clear Lake"
- **CTA:** Book the Kemah Tour. Sales/Purchase-objective campaign.
- **Format spec:** Static. Text-overlay card. Clear Lake/Kemah waterfront photography if it exists in the asset library; if not, a clean text/graphic card rather than stock water photography that could be mistaken for the downtown bayou.

**The one line to keep:** "It's not on the bayou." Six words that do the entire job of a company named "Buffalo Bayou Kayak" telling you its own product isn't on the bayou, a sentence no other kayak operator in Houston could truthfully say about their own business.
