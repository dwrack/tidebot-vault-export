## Format 1: Static

### Variant 1: "Solo Travelers Welcome"

**Angle:** Solo Travelers Welcome. Proven this pass, first execution.

**Evidence quote:** "Once you get your kayaking skills down, it's easy to maneuver... our guide still made it a worthwhile experience." (Mona H, TripAdvisor, Jun 2025) and "Did a 3 hour solo trip all the way to Shepherd Dr. and back. Pretty great experience" (Christiana Reckling, reviews.birdeye.com), cited together as two independent accounts.

**Objection handled:** I don't have anyone to go with, is this going to be awkward or unwelcome solo?

**Body framework:** Ogilvy factual-specific. Two real, separately-sourced guests replace a vague "solo travelers welcome" claim with two checkable facts.

**Hook (draft):** "Nobody to bring? Two different guests already found out solo works fine out here."

**Overlay text (draft):** "Two guests. Two solo trips. Two good ones."

**Primary text (draft):** Solo doesn't mean awkward out here. One guest called her guided trip "a worthwhile experience." Another paddled a solo rental trip all the way to Shepherd Dr. and back and called it "pretty great." Both were on their own. Both had a good time. Skyline Tour, $55/person, 2 hours, all ages. Rentals also available, starting at $20/hour if you'd rather go at your own pace.

**Format spec:** Static. Text-overlay card, single kayak on calm water, no other paddlers in frame, downtown skyline visible.

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Ogilvy, alone. Two named, dated, real guest accounts is Ogilvy's proof-through-fact philosophy applied directly to the "will I be the only one, and will it be weird" hesitation: not a brand claim that solo works, but two separate people who already found out.

**Framework applied:** Name the worry implicitly by stating "solo doesn't mean awkward," resolve it with both accounts side by side, close with the practical facts for both the guided and self-guided path.

**Reasoning:** Halbert's direct-address style was considered for the hook, since this is fundamentally a reassurance play, but stacking two specific, quotable facts side by side favors Ogilvy's plain factual register for a static card. Halbert's directness is used instead in Variant 4's Reel, where a faster, more conversational reassurance beat fits the format.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, hook tightened, primary text trimmed for card length.

**Line-by-line:**
- Draft hook, "Nobody to bring? Two different guests already found out solo works fine out here," passes all three: visualizable (a specific person going alone), falsifiable (two real, dated accounts), ownable (no competitor has these two guests' actual words). Kept as-is.
- Overlay text, "Two guests. Two solo trips. Two good ones," was checked against overclaiming. "Two" keeps the scope exactly as honest as the evidence: two, not "so many," not "most." Kept as-is.
- Primary text originally ran two full quotes plus both product paths in one block, too long for a static card. Trimmed to keep both quotes short and specific, cut a redundant transition sentence, kept both product paths since Variant 1 is meant to be the general entry point before Variants 2 and 3 go deeper on each guest individually.

**Sharpened final:**

- **Hook:** "Nobody to bring? Two different guests already found out solo works fine out here."
- **Overlay text:** "Two guests. Two solo trips. Two good ones."
- **Primary text:** Solo doesn't mean awkward out here. One guest called her guided trip "a worthwhile experience." Another paddled a solo rental trip and called it "pretty great." Both went alone. Both had a good time. Skyline Tour, $55 per person, 2 hours, all ages. Rentals also available, starting at $20 an hour if you'd rather go at your own pace.
- **Headline:** "Solo Works Fine Out Here"
- **CTA:** Book Your Tour. Sales/Purchase-objective campaign.

**The one line to keep:** "Both went alone. Both had a good time." Seven words that turn two separate reviews into one clean, checkable permission slip.
