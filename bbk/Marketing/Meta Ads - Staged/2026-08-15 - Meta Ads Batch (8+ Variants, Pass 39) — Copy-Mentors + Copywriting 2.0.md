## Format 1: Reel

### Variant 1: "The Divorce Boat"

**Angle:** The Divorce Boat. New in pass 39, emerging, first execution. See pre-note 2 above.

**Evidence quote:** "Many people jokingly (and wrongly!) refer to tandem kayaks as 'divorce boats'... they're actually great for relationships as they require good communication and coordination." (Dynamic Paddlers, Facebook, verbatim.) "If you want to stay married, keep away from kayaks." (Boston Globe Magazine, headline, verbatim.)

**Objection handled:** Will paddling with my partner turn into an actual argument, will I look uncoordinated in front of them.

**Shot list / visual beats:**
1. Text overlay: "Tandem kayaks have a nickname online."
2. Cut: a pair getting into a tandem kayak, laughing.
3. Text overlay naming the nickname.
4. Cut: calm, flat bayou water, skyline in the background.
5. Text overlay landing the reassurance.
6. Cut: pair paddling in sync, smiling.
7. End card and CTA.

**Body framework:** Halbert's reframe. Name the fear the reader already half-knows (the joke), defuse it fast, and back the defusal with a checkable fact rather than a vibe.

**Hook (sharpened, one kept of three):** "The internet has a nickname for tandem kayaks. It's not a compliment. Ours doesn't earn it."

**Primary text (sharpened):** Tandem kayaks have a real nickname online: "divorce boat." The Boston Globe wrote about it. So has half the internet's paddling community. Here's the part that matters: that reputation comes from whitewater and open water, where one bad stroke throws off the whole boat. Buffalo Bayou is flat, slow, three miles from downtown. Most pairs find the rhythm in about five minutes. Houston Skyline Tour, $55 per person, 2 hours.

**Headline:** "Still Married After the Divorce Boat"

**CTA overlay text:** "Paddle It Together"

**CTA:** Book Your Tour. Sales/Purchase-objective campaign.

**Format spec:** Reel. Existing tandem-kayak launch and on-water footage likely covers most beats; would benefit from a couple/pair-specific clip showing coordinated paddling, may not currently exist in the asset library, flagged for a dedicated shoot.

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Halbert primary, Bencivenga secondary. Halbert because this is exactly his A-pile problem: the reader already knows the joke, so the hook has to sound like it's in on it, not explaining it to them. Bencivenga for the closing fact, a claim this close to "this could go wrong" needs a real, checkable detail (flat water, three miles) right next to it, not just tone, to clear the "yeah, right" test.

**Framework applied:** Name the fear conversationally, defuse it with humor, close the loop with a specific, provable fact.

**Reasoning:** Sugarman's slippery-slide structure was considered, since the joke-then-reveal shape fits his pattern too, but Halbert's conspiratorial, talking-to-one-person voice fits a shared cultural joke better than Sugarman's narrative build, which works best when the reader doesn't yet know where the story is going. Here, they already do.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, hook and headline both needed tightening.

**Line-by-line:**
- Draft hook, "They call tandem kayaks 'divorce boats.' Here's why ours won't earn it," front-loads the explanation before the punchline, which slows the slide. Rewritten to "The internet has a nickname for tandem kayaks. It's not a compliment. Ours doesn't earn it," which delays the reveal one beat longer (curiosity) and keeps the payoff in the same breath as the tension.
- Draft headline, "Still Married After the Divorce Boat," passes all three: visualizable (you can picture a couple stepping off the boat, still fine), falsifiable in the loose observational sense (a real, checkable claim about outcome), ownable (built on this specific, sourced nickname, not a generic "fun for couples" line). Kept as written.
- "Buffalo Bayou is flat, slow, three miles from downtown." Passes all three: visualizable, falsifiable (a real, checkable distance and water condition), ownable (this exact three-mile framing is the vault's own repeated, evidence-backed number).
- Cut "Here's the thing" from the draft body's transition, a filler phrase that does no work the next sentence doesn't already do alone.

**Sharpened final:** as written above (hook rewritten, one filler phrase cut).

**The one line to keep:** "The internet has a nickname for tandem kayaks. It's not a compliment. Ours doesn't earn it." Three short sentences, no adjectives, and the whole ad's argument in one breath.
