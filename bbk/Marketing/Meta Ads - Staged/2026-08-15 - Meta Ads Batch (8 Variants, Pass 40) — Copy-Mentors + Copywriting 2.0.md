## Format 1: Reel

### Variant 1: "The Version You're Picturing Isn't the Real One"

**Angle:** Not the Advanced Course. New in pass 40, emerging, first execution. See pre-note 2 above.

**Evidence quote:** "No special fitness level is required. Kayaking on flat water is low-impact and accessible to most adults and older children." (buffalobayoukayak.com/faq/, verbatim, owned.)

**Objection handled:** Is Buffalo Bayou the advanced or riskier kayaking option compared to a calmer alternative, should I just play it safe and pick that instead.

**Shot list / visual beats:**
1. Text overlay: "When people hear 'Buffalo Bayou,' some picture rapids."
2. Cut: flat, glassy water, skyline in the background.
3. Text overlay: "This is what it actually looks like."
4. Cut: first-timer stepping into a kayak, guide steadying it.
5. Text overlay landing the FAQ line.
6. Cut: relaxed paddling on calm water.
7. End card and CTA.

**Body framework:** Schwartz belief-correction. Meet the reader at the wrong mental picture they already carry, then replace it with a checkable one.

**Hook (sharpened):** "You're picturing rapids. Wrong picture."

**Primary text (sharpened):** Some people hear "Buffalo Bayou" and assume it's the advanced option. It's not. No special fitness level is required, kayaking here is low-impact and accessible to most adults and older children. Flat water, guided, three miles from downtown. Houston Skyline Tour, $55 per person, 2 hours.

**Headline:** "Not the Advanced Option. Just the One With a Skyline."

**CTA overlay text:** "See What It Actually Looks Like"

**CTA:** Book Your Tour. Sales/Purchase-objective campaign.

**Format spec:** Reel. Needs a clean flat-water establishing shot up top; likely covered by existing footage.

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Schwartz primary, Bencivenga secondary. Schwartz because this is a textbook belief-correction problem, the reader isn't in on a joke, they're carrying an actual wrong assumption that has to be met and replaced, which is exactly his awareness-stage method. Bencivenga for the closing fact, correcting a belief this specific needs a real, checkable detail (the FAQ's own language) right next to it, not just reassurance in tone.

**Framework applied:** State the wrong picture the reader already has, name it plainly, then hand over the real one with a checkable fact attached.

**Reasoning:** Halbert's conspiratorial "we're both in on this" voice, used for pass 39's Divorce Boat, was considered but doesn't fit here, that angle worked because the reader already half-knew the joke. This angle is different: the reader doesn't know they have the wrong picture, so the ad has to inform, not wink.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, hook and primary text opener tightened.

**Line-by-line:**
- Draft hook, "You're picturing whitewater. This is not that," is accurate but "this is not that" points at something the viewer hasn't seen yet, weakening the visual. Rewritten to "You're picturing rapids. Wrong picture," which is shorter, still names the exact wrong image, and lands as a flat statement rather than a vague denial.
- "Not the Advanced Option. Just the One With a Skyline." Passes all three: visualizable (the skyline is BBK's own repeated visual signature), falsifiable (a real, checkable positioning claim), ownable (no competitor uses this exact skyline-as-proof framing).
- Draft primary text opener, "Some people hear 'Buffalo Bayou' and picture something more advanced than a first kayak trip should be," was wordy. Rewritten to "Some people hear 'Buffalo Bayou' and assume it's the advanced option," which is tighter and echoes the sharpened hook's language without repeating it verbatim.
- Checked against pre-note 2's guardrail: no named competitor anywhere in this script. Confirmed.

**Sharpened final:** as written above (hook and primary-text opener rewritten).

**The one line to keep:** "No special fitness level is required. Kayaking here is low-impact and accessible to most adults and older children." It's owned, verbatim, and does more reassurance work than any adjective the ad could add.
