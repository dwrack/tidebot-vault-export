## Format 1: Reels

### Variant 1 — "Where Houston Started"

**Angle:** Founding-of-Houston History, promoted from pass 9's reserve list. You launch from the exact spot the Allen brothers landed in 1836 and decided to build a city.

**Evidence:** "The spot we launched from was the original 'Port of Houston.'" — Dave H, TripAdvisor, Jun 2026.

**Hook options (first 2 seconds):**
1. "This is the spot Houston started. You're about to paddle away from it."
2. "1836. Two brothers stepped off a boat right here. You're about to do the same, backwards."
3. "Every Houston street map starts at this dot. Most people who live here have never stood on it."

**Body framework:** Then-and-now bridge. Plant one real historical fact (the date, the two names), then cut hard to the present-tense paddle-out, so the history isn't a lecture, it's a setup for something the viewer is about to physically do.

**Shot list / visual beats:**
1. Text card over a still shot of the Sunset Coffee Building / Allen's Landing marker: "1836. Allen's Landing." Held for a beat, no motion.
2. Cut to guests walking down to the launch, kayaks lined up, gear going on.
3. Water-level shot pushing off from the dock, wake trailing out behind the kayak.
4. Guide's voice, brief and unpolished: "This is where the city started. You're paddling away from it right now."
5. Wide shot, skyline rising ahead as the group paddles out, water flat and calm.
6. End card + CTA.

**Voiceover/caption track:** "In 1836, two brothers got off a boat right here and decided to build a city. You're launching from the same spot." / "Most people who've lived in Houston their whole life have never actually stood at Allen's Landing."

**CTA overlay text:** "Paddle From Where Houston Began — $55"

**Primary text (caption):** In 1836, the Allen brothers stepped off a boat at this exact spot and decided to build a city here. Almost 200 years later, you launch from the same landing. One guest put it simply: "the spot we launched from was the original Port of Houston." Sunset Skyline Tour. $55/person, 2 hours, all ages, Allen's Landing.

**Headlines:** "Paddle From Where Houston Started" · "You're Standing Where the City Began"

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Ogilvy (the fact, well told, over the adjective) + Schwartz (channel existing civic curiosity, don't manufacture it).

**Framework applied:** Ogilvy's rule that a real, specific fact outsells a vague claim of significance: "1836" and "the Allen brothers" do more work than "historic landing." Schwartz's awareness model treats the reader as Unaware they'd care about this fact at all, so the job is to plant it plainly and let the present-tense paddle-out do the persuading, not to argue a benefit.

**Reasoning:** The real risk with a history angle is sliding into museum-brochure tone, which the brand voice explicitly rejects ("we don't lecture"). Kept the historical fact to one sentence and let the second half of the ad be pure action.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, one line cut for reading like a textbook.

**Line-by-line:**
- First draft: "Allen's Landing holds deep historical significance as the founding site of Houston." → FAILS all three tests: not visualizable (nothing to picture), barely falsifiable in any interesting way, and could be pasted onto any historical-marker plaque in America. Cut. Replaced with "In 1836, the Allen brothers stepped off a boat at this exact spot and decided to build a city here." → PASSES: visualizable (two people stepping off a boat), falsifiable (a real date, real people, checkable), ownable (true only at this literal launch point).
- "Almost 200 years later, you launch from the same landing." → PASSES: a concrete time span that ties the historical fact directly to something the reader is about to do, not just read about.

**Sharpened final:** as written above.

**The one line to keep:** "You're launching from the same spot." Four words that turn a history fact into something the reader is about to physically do, not something they're being told to admire.

**Hook options:**

2. "Two hours where nobody in your family checked a phone."

**Shot list / visual beats:**
1. Kids climbing into a tandem kayak, a parent buckling a life vest, real excitement, not posed.
2. Water-level shot: a mother duck and ducklings crossing ahead, kids pointing and laughing.
3. Kid mid-paddle-stroke, racing energy, parent laughing behind them.
4. A turtle sunning on a log, a kid leaning in to look.
5. Family group shot, skyline behind them, unposed smiles.
6. Text overlay over the group shot: "Kids enjoyed rowing and racing with the parents." (real guest quote, attributed on screen).
7. End card + CTA.

**Voiceover/caption track:** "Somewhere around the second duckling sighting, the phones go into the dry bag and stay there." / "Kids 6 and up get their own seat. The rest of the family just tries to keep up."

**CTA overlay text:** "Get the Whole Family on the Water — $55"

**Primary text (caption):** Somewhere around the second duckling sighting, the phones go into the dry bag and stay there. One recent guest summed it up: "Kids enjoyed rowing and racing with the parents." Kids 6 and up get their own seat in a tandem kayak with an adult. Houston Skyline Tour. $55/person, 2 hours, all ages, all equipment included.

**Headlines:** "Kids vs. Ducklings, Kids Lose" · "Two Hours, Zero Phones"

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Sugarman (open on a specific, curious moment, not a claim) + Blair Warren's "encourage their dreams" (the dream of a phone-free family afternoon that actually works, instead of the usual failed attempt).

**Framework applied:** Sugarman's slippery-slide rule: the duckling-race image is specific and slightly funny, which earns the second sentence, where "family fun for all ages" would have lost the reader immediately.

**Reasoning:** Parents don't respond to the word "family-friendly." They respond to a specific image of their actual kid doing an actual funny thing. The real review quote is used as proof, not as the whole pitch.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, one line cut for being interchangeable with any activity brand's copy.

**Line-by-line:**
- First draft: "It's the perfect bonding activity for the whole family." → FAILS all three tests, especially can-nobody-else-say-it: this exact sentence runs on a thousand family-activity websites. Cut, replaced with the duckling-race hook, which only this bayou, with this specific and seasonally common wildlife, can produce.
- "Kids 6 and up get their own seat in a tandem kayak with an adult." → PASSES: visualizable, falsifiable (it's BBK's actual policy), and specific enough it quietly answers a real logistics question ("can my 8-year-old paddle") inside what reads like a lifestyle line.

**Sharpened final:** as written above.

**The one line to keep:** "Somewhere around the second duckling sighting, the phones go into the dry bag and stay there." Nobody else can time an ad to this specific bayou's specific, common wildlife pattern.

### Variant 4 — "Same Tour. Same Guide. Same Bayou. $14 Less."

**Angle:** Book Direct & Skip the Markup. Brand new this pass, never scripted before. Built on a real, checkable price gap: the identical tour lists for $69/adult on a travel booking site, and $55/person direct.

**Evidence:** Expedia lists the "2 Hour Houston Skyline Tour" (Buffalo Bayou Kayak Tours, 1019 Commerce St, kayaks/paddles/life jackets included) at $69.00/adult. buffalobayoukayak.com direct price for the same tour: $55/person, verified against the company's own zero-tolerance pricing sheet.

**Image concept:** A clean, simple price-comparison graphic over a tour photo: "$69 elsewhere" struck through, "$55 direct" in its place. No browser mockups or platform logos, keeping the point about direct-vs-third-party rather than any one named site.

**Headline:** "Same Tour. Same Guide. Same Bayou. $14 Less."

**Primary text:** Some travel booking sites list this exact tour, same launch point, same guide, same gear, for $69 a person. Book it straight through us and it's $55. No markup, no service fee stacked on top, just the direct price. Sunset Skyline Tour, $55/person, 2 hours, all ages, Allen's Landing.

**Description line:** Book direct at buffalobayoukayak.com. Same tour, lower price.

**CTA button:** Book It Direct

**Headline A/B option:** "You Might Be About to Overpay for This Exact Tour"

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Halbert (isolate the unspoken objection before it's stated: "you might already be looking at this tour somewhere else right now") + Bencivenga (a checkable number beats any adjective).

**Framework applied:** Halbert's rule that the best ad finds the reader mid-thought and finishes the sentence for them. A meaningful share of this audience is actively comparison-shopping on travel sites in the moment they see this ad; it meets them there instead of pretending they haven't already been looking around.

**Reasoning:** The obvious temptation is to bash third-party booking sites by name. The brand's own rule against bashing competitors by name doesn't technically cover travel platforms, but the same caution applies for a second reason: a named platform's price is a moving target, and naming one risks the ad going stale or looking petty. Kept it generic ("some travel booking sites") on purpose.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works. One line killed for tone.

**Line-by-line:**
- First draft: "Don't get ripped off booking through third-party sites." → FAILS on brand-fit (accusatory, and not provably a "rip-off," just a fee difference the ad can't fully explain) and reads like every other direct-booking campaign in every industry. Rewritten to the neutral, factual "no markup, no service fee stacked on top," which makes the same point without a claim the ad can't back up.
- "Same Tour. Same Guide. Same Bayou. $14 Less." → PASSES all three tests: visualizable (a real number), falsifiable (checkable against both listings the same day), ownable (a live price fact specific to this business today).

**Sharpened final:** as written above.

**The one line to keep:** "Same Tour. Same Guide. Same Bayou. $14 Less." Four short, factual beats landing on the number, no adjectives doing any of the work.
