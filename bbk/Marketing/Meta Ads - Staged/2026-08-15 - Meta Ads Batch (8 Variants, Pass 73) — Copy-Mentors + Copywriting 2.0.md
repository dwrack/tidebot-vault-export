## Format 1: Static

### Variant 1: "You Book It For The Bats, You Leave Talking About The Skyline"

**Angle:** You Book It For The Bats, You Leave Talking About The Skyline. Emerging this pass, first execution.

**Evidence quote:** "The boys were more excited about the houston skyline (viewed at night in that bayou) than the bats." (Nicole Blanchard, reviews.birdeye.com, direct fetch, pass 73.)

**Objection handled:** What if my kids don't actually care about bats, will a night bat tour still be worth it for them.

**Body framework:** Ogilvy factual-specific. A real guest's account replaces a vague "the whole family will love it" promise with a checkable, specific outcome.

**Hook (draft):** "Book it for the bats. Don't be shocked if the skyline steals the show."

**Overlay text (draft):** "One family booked it for the bats. Their kids talked about the skyline all the way home."

**Primary text (draft):** A guest told us her boys were more excited about the downtown skyline at night than the 300,000 bats the tour is built around. Bat sightings are common at dusk, not guaranteed, same as any wildlife. But the skyline reflected on dark water is part of every trip, no luck required. Sunset Skyline Bat Bridge Kayak Tour, $60/person, 2-3 hours, ages 7+.

**Format spec:** Static. Text-overlay card, night skyline reflected on the water with kayaks in frame if available; existing sunset or night-tour photography likely sufficient.

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Ogilvy, alone. A specific, named, verbatim guest account of what actually happened is Ogilvy's proof-through-fact philosophy applied to a parental hedge-your-bet worry: "the kids will probably love it" is a claim, "one mom's kids ended up more excited about the skyline" is a fact a skeptical parent can weigh.

**Framework applied:** State the worry implicitly by naming the trade-off (bats vs. skyline), resolve it with the real account, close with the honest hedge and the practical booking facts.

**Reasoning:** Halbert's direct-address conversational style was considered for the hook, since this is fundamentally a reassurance play, but the underlying proof is a specific, quotable fact rather than an emotional appeal alone, which keeps this in Ogilvy's register for the static card. Halbert's directness gets used instead in Variant 2's Reel, where a faster, punchier reassurance beat fits the format.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, hook tightened, primary text's hedge clause checked against brand voice.

**Line-by-line:**
- Draft hook, "Book it for the bats. Don't be shocked if the skyline steals the show," passes all three: visualizable (a specific trade-off a parent can picture), falsifiable (it's a real guest's stated experience, not an invented claim), ownable (no competitor's bat tour has this specific guest account). Kept as-is.
- Overlay text, "One family booked it for the bats. Their kids talked about the skyline all the way home," was checked for overclaiming: "one family" keeps the scope honest rather than implying a pattern. Kept as-is.
- Primary text's "Bat sightings are common at dusk, not guaranteed, same as any wildlife" is required by `Brand Story & Values.md`'s standing honesty rule and should never be cut for punch. Kept as-is, moved earlier in the sentence for better flow: "not guaranteed" now lands right after "common at dusk" instead of trailing the sentence.

**Sharpened final:**

- **Hook:** "Book it for the bats. Don't be shocked if the skyline steals the show."
- **Overlay text:** "One family booked it for the bats. Their kids talked about the skyline all the way home."
- **Primary text:** A guest told us her boys were more excited about the downtown skyline at night than the 300,000 bats the tour is built around. Bat sightings are common at dusk, not guaranteed, same as any wildlife, but the skyline reflected on dark water is part of every trip, no luck required. Sunset Skyline Bat Bridge Kayak Tour, $60 per person, 2 to 3 hours, ages 7 and up.
- **Headline:** "Booked For Bats, Remembered For the Skyline"
- **CTA:** Book Your Bat Bridge Tour. Sales/Purchase-objective campaign.

**The one line to keep:** "No luck required." Three words that turn an emerging, single-source guest quote into a real reassurance: even if the bats are a coin flip, the skyline isn't.
