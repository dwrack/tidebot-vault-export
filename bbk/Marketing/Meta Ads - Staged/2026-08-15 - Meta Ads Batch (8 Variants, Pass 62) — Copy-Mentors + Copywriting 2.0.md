## Format 1: Static

### Variant 1: "Two Hours Gets You to an Actual Cemetery, Not Just a Loop"

**Angle:** Two Hours Gets You to an Actual Cemetery, Not Just a Loop. New in pass 62, emerging, first execution.

**Evidence quote:** "We had a two-hour rental, which was reasonably priced, and we were able to paddle all the way to Glenwood Cemetery (just past the first (eastern) Memorial Drive bridge)." (TripAdvisor guest review, verbatim per WebSearch, web.)

**Objection handled:** Is a 2-hour rental with no guide just paddling in circles near the dock with nothing to actually see or do.

**Body framework:** Ogilvy factual-specific, a real, named, checkable place replaces a vague "explore the bayou" claim.

**Hook (draft):** "A 2-hour rental gets you further than you'd think."

**Overlay text (draft):** "Two hours. One real destination: Glenwood Cemetery."

**Primary text (draft):** A 2-hour rental isn't just floating near the dock. One guest reviewer put it well: they paddled all the way to Glenwood Cemetery, just past the first Memorial Drive bridge, on a rental they called "reasonably priced." No guide, no schedule, just you, a kayak, and a real destination downstream. Single kayak or paddleboard, 2 hours, $40. Tandem, $65.

**Format spec:** Static. Text-overlay card, single or tandem kayak on open water with downtown skyline receding behind, a sense of distance/progress in the shot; existing rental photography likely sufficient.

#### Copy-mentors pass

**Channeling:** Ogilvy, alone. A specific, named, googleable landmark is exactly Ogilvy's territory, the kind of concrete fact that does more selling than an adjective ever could. "Explore the bayou" is a claim; "you can paddle to Glenwood Cemetery" is a fact someone can check.

**Framework applied:** Lead with the number people already assume (2 hours = short, close to the dock), then overturn it with the specific place, close with the practical booking facts.

**Reasoning:** Sugarman's slippery-slide curiosity gap was considered for the hook, since there's a reveal structure here, but the core sell is a verifiable fact, not a mystery, so Ogilvy's plain factual approach fits better than a build-the-curiosity structure.

#### Copywriting-2.0 pass

**Verdict:** Works, hook and headline tightened.

**Line-by-line:**
- Draft hook, "A 2-hour rental gets you further than you'd think," is a claim about a claim, one layer removed from the actual fact. Rewritten to name the place directly: "A 2-hour rental gets you to Glenwood Cemetery," which passes the falsifiable test immediately instead of making the reader wait for the primary text.
- "Two hours. One real destination: Glenwood Cemetery." Passes all three: visualizable (a real place with a name), falsifiable (checkable on a map), ownable (no rental competitor in this market has this specific proof point in their ads).
- Primary text's "just you, a kayak, and a real destination downstream" is a strong, concrete closing image, kept as-is.

**Sharpened final:**

- **Hook:** "A 2-hour rental gets you to Glenwood Cemetery."
- **Overlay text:** "Two hours. One real destination: Glenwood Cemetery."
- **Primary text:** A 2-hour rental isn't just floating near the dock. One guest reviewer put it well: they paddled all the way to Glenwood Cemetery, just past the first Memorial Drive bridge, on a rental they called reasonably priced. No guide, no schedule, just you, a kayak, and a real destination downstream. Single kayak or paddleboard, 2 hours, $40. Tandem, $65.
