## FAQ SCHEMA
_(For Google rich snippets and AI search citation. 5 Q&As.)_

**Q1:**
```
What should I wear kayaking for the first time?
```
**A1:**
```
Wear quick-dry clothes, not cotton, since cotton stays wet and gets uncomfortable if you get splashed. Athletic shorts or leggings, a t-shirt or rash guard, and shoes that can get wet, like water shoes or old sneakers, work best. Skip flip-flops, they come off in the water. A hat and sunglasses with a strap help in Houston's sun, and you'll be handed a life vest to wear the entire trip.
```

**Q2:**
```
Is kayaking hard for beginners?
```
**A2:**
```
No, not on flat water like Buffalo Bayou. Most first-timers get comfortable within five minutes of basic instruction: how to hold the paddle, how to steer, how to stay balanced. There's no current to fight and no rapids, so it's more about finding a rhythm than any real physical difficulty. Reasonable fitness is enough; you don't need to be an athlete.
```

**Q3:**
```
What do I need to bring on a first kayak tour?
```
**A3:**
```
Bring sunscreen, a water bottle, sunglasses with a strap, and a dry bag or ziplock for your phone if you want it with you. Skip anything you'd be upset to lose in the water: expensive watches, loose jewelry, anything that isn't secured. The tour provides the kayak, paddle, and life vest, so gear-wise that's the short list.
```

**Q4:**
```
What happens if I flip the kayak?
```
**A4:**
```
On flat water like Buffalo Bayou it's uncommon, but if it happens, your guide is trained to help you back in quickly, and you're wearing a life vest the entire time, so staying afloat isn't the issue. Most first-time flips happen from leaning too hard to one side rather than the water itself being rough. Your guide gives a quick briefing on balance before launch specifically to prevent this.
```

**Q5:**
```
How fit do you need to be to kayak?
```
**A5:**
```
You don't need to be athletic. Paddling flat water uses arms, shoulders, and core in an easy, repetitive motion, closer to a walk than a workout. Most guests in reasonable general health, including many older adults and kids around age seven and up, do the full trip without trouble. If you can sit comfortably for two hours and move your arms, you can do this tour.
```

## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
"I've never kayaked before, is that okay?" We get some version of that email or call almost every day. Yes. It's more than okay, it's most of our guests.

Nobody's born knowing what to wear on a kayak or what actually happens once you show up. So here's the plain, no-guessing version: what to wear, what to bring, and what the first ten minutes on the water actually look like.

## The nervous first-timer email we get every week

Here's the thing nobody tells you before you book: **the paddle is the easy part.** The nerves are almost always about the unknowns, not the actual kayaking. What do I wear. What if I flip. Am I going to look like an idiot. Real questions, and worth real answers instead of a brochure line.

We built this tour around first-timers on purpose. Flat water, no current, a guide who runs a short briefing before anyone touches a paddle. If you've made it this far into the article still worried, that's exactly who this is for.

## What to actually wear

Quick-dry over cotton, every time. Cotton soaks up water, stays wet, and turns a warm Houston afternoon into a clammy one. Athletic gear does the job fine.

- **Top:** A t-shirt or rash guard. Nothing you're precious about, you might get splashed.
- **Bottom:** Athletic shorts or leggings, quick-dry if you have them.
- **Shoes:** Water shoes or old sneakers you don't mind getting wet. **Not flip-flops,** they come off the second you step in mud or shallow water.
- **Sun protection:** A hat, and sunglasses with a strap so they don't end up in the bayou.
- **Life vest:** We provide it. You wear it the entire trip, no exceptions, guide included.

One guest showed up in dress sandals and finished the tour barefoot in the kayak. Fine outcome, avoidable choice. Water shoes cost less than lunch.

## What to bring, and what to leave in the car

Short list on purpose:

- Sunscreen, applied before you launch, not after.
- A refillable water bottle. Houston heat adds up fast, even on the water.
- Sunglasses with a retention strap.
- A dry bag or a ziplock bag if you want your phone with you for photos.
- Cash or a card if you're tipping your guide afterward, entirely optional.

Leave in the car: expensive watches, loose jewelry, anything electronic that isn't in a dry bag, and anything you'd genuinely be upset to lose to the bayou. We can't fish your AirPods off the bottom, and we've tried.

## What happens when you show up

No mystery here, this is the actual sequence:

1. **Check in** at Allen's Landing, 1019 Commerce Street.
2. **Get fitted for a life vest.** Takes about two minutes.
3. **Short safety briefing.** How to hold the paddle, how to steer, what to do if you flip. Five minutes, not a lecture.
4. **Launch with the group.** Your guide sets an easy pace and stays with everyone.
5. **The actual paddle.** Wildlife, skyline, history, whatever the tour's built around.
6. **Return to the landing.** Same easy pace back.

Most people are steering fine within five minutes. It's closer to riding a bike slowly than anything athletic.

## The "what if I flip" question, answered honestly

It happens. Not often, but we're not going to pretend it never does. Usually it's someone leaning too hard trying to reach for something, not the water being rough, because the water isn't rough.

If it happens, your guide gets you back in quickly, and you're wearing a life vest the entire time, so the actual risk is low and the discomfort is mostly "well, that's embarrassing," not dangerous. We've had guests laugh about it five minutes later. Nobody's ever asked for a refund over it.

## You'll be fine, here's why we mean it

We're not saying that to close the sale. We're saying it because we've watched hundreds of first-timers walk down to Allen's Landing nervous and walk back up talking over each other about the heron they saw.

The bayou is flat. The pace is easy. The gear list is short. If you were putting off booking because you didn't know what to wear or what would happen, that gap is closed now. → [internal link: book the Houston Skyline Tour → /houston-skyline-kayak-tour]

First trip's the hardest part to start. Grab a spot at buffalobayoukayak.com and let your guide handle the rest.
```
