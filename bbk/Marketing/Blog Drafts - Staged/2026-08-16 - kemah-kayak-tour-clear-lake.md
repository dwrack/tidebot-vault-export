## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
Most people who know Buffalo Bayou Kayak know the bayou. What they don't know is that we also run a tour 30 miles away, on a completely different body of water, in a town most Houstonians only think about when they're headed to the boardwalk for dinner.

It's called the Kemah Kayak Historical and Ecological Tour, and it's been quietly sitting on our activities page without so much as a proper writeup. That changes today.

## Wait, Buffalo Bayou Kayak has a tour in Kemah?

Yes. We get this question a lot once people find out, usually right after "wait, you guys do more than the downtown thing?" We do. Buffalo Bayou Kayak's main tours launch out of the Sunset Coffee Building at Allen's Landing, 1019 Commerce St in downtown Houston, on Buffalo Bayou itself. The Kemah tour is a separate product, on Clear Lake, in the town of Kemah, roughly 30 miles southeast of downtown, where the lake opens out toward Galveston Bay.

Different water. Different town. Different story to tell. That's the whole point.

We staff the Kemah tour the same way we staff the bayou: guides who've actually paddled the water they're taking you out on, not someone reciting a script off a laminated card. It's the same standard, applied to a second location most guests don't know exists yet.

We're not going to pretend Kemah is "just like the bayou but further away." It isn't. The bayou tour is about the surprise of finding nature tucked inside a big city. The Kemah tour is about a small coastal town with a real history and a lake that shaped it, and a guide who'll walk you through both from a kayak instead of a boardwalk bench.

## What the Kemah Kayak Historical and Ecological Tour covers

Here's the tour, no padding: **$50 per person, 2 hours, ages 8 and up.** Guests paddle Clear Lake with a local guide who covers how the town of Kemah got its start, the hurricanes that have shaped this stretch of the Texas coast, and why Clear Lake matters ecologically, not just scenically.

That last part is worth sitting with for a second. A lot of people who've been to Kemah have eaten at the boardwalk, ridden the Ferris wheel, watched the boats come in. Almost nobody's heard the actual story of the place from someone who knows it, and even fewer have heard it from the water, which is honestly where a coastal town's history makes the most sense anyway.

Your guide doesn't stop at history and ecology, either. Somewhere in those two hours, you'll get real, local recommendations for where to eat and what to do with the rest of your day in Kemah. Not a printed list. A guide who's actually eaten there telling you where to go.

## Tandem or single, your call

Guests choose a tandem or a single kayak depending on who they're paddling with and how they want to spend the two hours. Couples and families tend to go tandem. Solo paddlers who want to move at their own pace go single. Either way, you're guided, so nobody's handed a paddle and pointed at the horizon.

We built the downtown bayou tours around the same idea: no experience required, a guide who actually knows the water, and equipment that doesn't need explaining. The Kemah tour runs on that same logic. → [internal link: our downtown Houston Skyline Tour → /kayak-tours/houston-kayak-tour/]

## Booking the Kemah tour, and the one thing worth knowing about it

One honest note before you book. The Kemah tour doesn't have its own page on our website yet, it currently lives on our activities listing alongside everything else we run. → [internal link: see the Kemah tour and book → /all-activities/] That's a gap on our end, not a red flag on the tour itself, and it's one we're fixing.

A few things we're not going to guess at here, because a stale detail on a page like this does real damage to trust: the exact meeting point in Kemah, current departure times, and whether the standard weather and cancellation terms match the downtown tours exactly. All of that gets confirmed the moment you book. If any of it matters to your plans, check the live calendar or ask us directly before you lock in a date.

What we can tell you straight: it's $50, it's 2 hours, it's ages 8 and up, and it's guided the same way our downtown tours are. Everything else, get it fresh from the booking page, not from this post.

## Bayou tour or Kemah tour: how to pick

A rough way to sort it:

- **Want the "there's nature in the middle of Houston" surprise, close to downtown, on a short window?** Book the bayou tour.
- **Already headed to Kemah for the day, or want a coastal town's actual history instead of the boardwalk version?** Book the Kemah tour.
- **Planning a full day in the Clear Lake area?** Kayak first, eat where your guide tells you to, and you've built a better afternoon than most visitors put together on their own.
- **Not sure which fits your group?** Both tours run guided and beginner-friendly, so the real question is which town and which water you'd rather spend two hours on, not which one's "easier."

Neither tour is the backup plan for the other. They're two different reasons to get on the water.
```
