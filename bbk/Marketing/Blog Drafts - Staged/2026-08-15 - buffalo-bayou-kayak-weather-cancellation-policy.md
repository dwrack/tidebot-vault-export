## FAQ SCHEMA

**Q1:**
```
Will my kayak tour be cancelled if it rains?
```
**A1:**
```
Not necessarily. Light rain does not cancel a Buffalo Bayou Kayak tour, paddling in a drizzle is a normal part of the trip. Tours are cancelled or held for lightning, severe weather, or water levels that rise significantly after heavy rainfall. Guests get a full refund or a free reschedule if that happens.
```

**Q2:**
```
What is Buffalo Bayou Kayak's cancellation policy?
```
**A2:**
```
Guest-initiated cancellations made at least 24 hours before the scheduled tour time get a full refund. Cancellations within 24 hours are not eligible for a refund. Weather-related cancellations made by Buffalo Bayou Kayak are separate and always come with a full refund or a free reschedule.
```

**Q3:**
```
How do you decide whether to cancel for weather?
```
**A3:**
```
Guides check the forecast and radar the morning of any tour with weather in play and make the call based on lightning risk, wind, and bayou water level, not just whether it's cloudy. A rising bayou after heavy rain moves the current faster than normal, which is the actual safety line, not the rain itself.
```

**Q4:**
```
Does Houston hurricane season affect kayak tours?
```
**A4:**
```
Yes, June through November is Houston's hurricane season, and tropical systems can bring heavy rain and elevated water levels to Buffalo Bayou even from storms that stay well offshore. Tours scheduled during an active tropical system are evaluated day by day, with guests notified as early as possible if a hold or cancellation is likely.
```

**Q5:**
```
What if I get partway through a tour and it starts storming?
```
**A5:**
```
Guides monitor conditions throughout the tour, not just before launch, and will bring the group in early if lightning develops or conditions change. This happens rarely, but when it does, guests are not charged extra and are offered a reschedule for the portion of the tour they missed.
```

## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
"Is my tour still happening?" is the single most-texted question we get on a gray Houston morning. Here's the actual answer, not the vague one, so you're not refreshing the radar app at 6 a.m. wondering.

## The short version

Rain alone doesn't cancel your tour. Lightning, severe weather, or a bayou running high after heavy rainfall does. If we cancel on you, it's always a full refund or a free reschedule, your call. If you cancel on us with less than 24 hours' notice, that's on you and it's not refundable. Those are the only two rules that matter. Everything below is the how and why.

## Rain doesn't cancel a tour, this does

A light Houston drizzle changes nothing. Guests paddle in it, guides run the tour in it, and honestly, a soft rain on the bayou with nobody else out there is one of the better versions of this trip. **We're not going to pretend a little water falling from the sky is a safety issue when the whole activity involves sitting in water.**

What actually cancels a tour:

- Lightning anywhere in the area, full stop, no exceptions
- Severe weather warnings from the National Weather Service
- Sustained high wind that makes the kayaks hard to control
- A bayou water level that's risen noticeably after heavy upstream rain, which speeds up the current past what's safe for a flatwater tour

That last one surprises people. It's not really about the rain falling on you, it's about what rain upstream does to the water you're paddling on hours or a day later. Buffalo Bayou can rise fast after a big storm even when the sky over downtown is clear.

## How the call actually gets made

Guides check the forecast and radar the morning of any tour with weather in the picture, not the night before, since Houston weather changes fast enough to make an evening forecast useless by sunrise. If there's lightning risk or the bayou's running high, the call gets made and guests get notified as early as possible, not at check-in time.

We'd rather cancel two hours early and give someone their morning back than have them show up to a locked gate. That's happened at other outfits around here, guests showing up to find out on-site that the tour's off with no warning. It's a bad way to find out and an easy thing to avoid with one text.

## Cancellation policy, plain numbers

Two different situations, two different rules, and they get mixed up constantly:

1. **You cancel on us, 24+ hours out:** full refund, no questions.
2. **You cancel on us, under 24 hours:** no refund. That window exists because a last-minute cancellation is a spot we usually can't resell.
3. **We cancel on you for weather:** always a full refund or a free reschedule, your choice, regardless of how much notice we had.

Notice the difference: your notice window matters, ours doesn't. That's on purpose. Weather isn't something either of us controls, and you shouldn't eat the cost of a call that wasn't yours to make.

## Hurricane season is its own animal

June through November is Houston hurricane season, and it changes how we watch the forecast even on days that look fine downtown. A tropical system doesn't have to make direct landfall here to raise the bayou. Storms that stay well offshore or make landfall along the coast can still dump enough rain upstream to push water levels up for days afterward.

If there's an active named storm anywhere near the Gulf during your booking window, expect day-by-day evaluation instead of a single upfront answer. We're not going to promise a tour will run five days out when a storm's still forming. We will promise to tell you as early as we know, not the morning of.

## What actually happens if we cancel on you

You get a text or a call, not a vague email buried in a promo newsletter. You choose: full refund back to your original payment method, or a free reschedule to another date, no fee either way. Groups booked for a bachelorette party, a birthday, or a corporate outing get the same options, plus we'll work with you directly on rebooking a date that fits the whole group instead of leaving that on you to coordinate.

**Nobody on our end wants to cancel a tour.** It costs us a paddling day too. But we'd rather eat that cost than put a group on the water when the bayou's not safe to be on.

## The one thing worse than a cancelled tour

A tour that should have been cancelled and wasn't. If you ever feel like conditions look worse than what your guide is telling you, say something. We'd rather hear it and confirm than have a guest quietly white-knuckle a trip they weren't comfortable on.
```
