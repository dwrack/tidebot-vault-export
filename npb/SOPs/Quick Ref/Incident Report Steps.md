# Incident Report Steps

Safety first, then document everything. Same-day, no exceptions.

---

1. Ensure the immediate safety of all guests and crew
2. If anyone is injured, administer first aid
3. Call 911 if the situation requires emergency medical response
4. Contact Jeffrey immediately (call, not text)
5. If on the water, secure the vessel and return to dock

> [!warning] If someone is in the water, all other actions stop. Recovery first. Everything else second.

6. Once safe, begin documentation:
   - What happened (specific, factual description)
   - When it happened (time, point in the trip)
   - Where it happened (on vessel, at dock, on water, specific location)
   - Who was involved (guests, crew, bystanders)
   - What actions were taken in response
   - Names and contact info of any witnesses

7. Photograph the conditions: the scene, any damage, any injuries (with permission), weather/water conditions
8. Preserve any physical evidence (don't clean up the scene until documented)
9. File the written incident report the same day

> [!warning] USCG reporting is required within 24 hours for: death, injury requiring more than first aid, significant vessel damage, grounding, fire, pollution/hazardous discharge. If in doubt, report it. Under-reporting is worse than over-reporting.

10. Determine if USCG 24-hour reporting applies and file if so
11. Determine if LDWF (Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries) reporting applies
12. Send completed report to Jeffrey and to the Davey Jones' Locker incident file
13. Follow up with any injured parties within 24 hours

> [!tip] Write the report like someone who wasn't there needs to understand exactly what happened. No assumptions, no editorializing. Just the facts and the timeline.
