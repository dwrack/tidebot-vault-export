# Contractor Offboarding Checklist

Manager-facing. Clean separation, no loose ends, no lingering access.

---

## Confirm the Exit

1. Confirm the contractor's ending (resignation or termination)
2. If termination: document the reason, any prior warnings, and the final conversation
3. Set the final shift date
4. Notify the contractor in writing (text or email) with the last scheduled date

## Collect Property (Before or On Final Day)

5. Collect all keys (vessel, storage, site)
6. Collect radios
7. Collect any issued PFDs or branded gear
8. Collect any company-owned equipment in their possession

> [!warning] Do not let the final shift pass without collecting keys and radios. If they don't return on the last day, follow up within 24 hours.

- [ ] Keys collected
- [ ] Radios collected
- [ ] PFDs / branded gear collected
- [ ] Other equipment collected

## Within 24 Hours of Final Shift

9. Remove from FareHarbor (staff access)
10. Remove from Slack workspace
11. Remove from Google Drive / shared folders
12. Remove from DocuSeal
13. Change any door codes or lock combinations they had access to

- [ ] FareHarbor access removed
- [ ] Slack access removed
- [ ] Google Drive access removed
- [ ] DocuSeal access removed
- [ ] Codes/locks changed

## Within 7 Days

14. Update any permits that list the contractor by name
15. Update the Crew Roster
16. Update TWIC records
17. Update the Hurricane Plan if they were listed
18. Recover any duplicate keys made

- [ ] Permits updated
- [ ] Crew Roster updated
- [ ] TWIC records updated
- [ ] Hurricane Plan updated

## Within 30 Days

19. Process final pay
20. Flag for 1099 filing if total payments exceeded $600 for the year
21. Update the vault with offboarding date and reason
22. Log the offboarding in the offboarding record

> [!tip] If the departure was on good terms, note that in the log. Good captains sometimes come back, and you want to know who left clean.

- [ ] Final pay processed
- [ ] 1099 flagged if >$600
- [ ] Vault updated
- [ ] Offboarding log completed
