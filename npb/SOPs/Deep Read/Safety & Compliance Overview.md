# Safety & Compliance Overview

## Why This Matters

NOLA Party Barge operates under USCG Subchapter T, which governs Small Passenger Vessels. This isn't a suggestion or a best practice. It's federal law. The Coast Guard can and does board vessels for inspection, and violations can mean fines, vessel seizure, or criminal charges.

Beyond the legal reality: we're putting people on water. People who trust us to bring them back safe. Every piece of equipment, every inspection, every protocol exists because at some point, somewhere, someone got hurt or killed when it was skipped.

This document covers what you need to know. The Quick Ref checklists cover what you need to do daily.

## Vessel Classification

Our four vessels (Freaky Tiki, Twerkin Tiki, Bayou Boogie, Party Queen) are classified as Small Passenger Vessels under 46 CFR Subchapter T. This applies to vessels under 100 gross tons carrying more than 6 passengers for hire.

Key implications:

- Each vessel must hold a valid Certificate of Inspection (COI) issued by the USCG
- The COI specifies the maximum number of passengers, the operating area, and the minimum crew required
- Operating outside the COI parameters is a federal violation, period

## Required Equipment

### Life-Saving Equipment

Every vessel must carry PFDs (Personal Flotation Devices) sufficient for the maximum passenger capacity listed on the COI, plus crew.

- **Type I PFDs:** Offshore life jackets. Best for open, rough water. Designed to turn an unconscious person face-up.
- **Type II PFDs:** Near-shore vests. For calmer inland water. Will turn some unconscious wearers face-up.
- **Type III PFDs:** Flotation aids. Comfortable for extended wear but the wearer may need to position themselves face-up.
- **Type IV PFD:** Throwable device. Must be immediately accessible on deck at all times. This is what you throw to someone in the water. Not stored in a compartment. On deck, ready to grab.

The specific types required depend on the vessel's COI. Know what your vessel requires and count them every morning.

Children under 13 must wear a PFD for the entire trip. This is both policy and law in Louisiana.

### Fire Safety

- Fire extinguishers: must be charged, accessible, and have current inspection tags. Check the gauge during morning inspection. If it's in the yellow or red, the vessel doesn't go out.
- Flares: must be on board and within their expiration date. Expired flares are the same as no flares during an inspection.

### Navigation and Communication

- Navigation lights: required for all operations. Bow light, stern light, masthead light. Test every morning.
- Horn: required signaling device. Must be functional. Test every morning.
- VHF Marine Radio: must be on and monitoring Channel 16 (the international distress and calling frequency) at all times while underway. This is not optional. Ch 16 is how the Coast Guard reaches you, and how you reach them.

### Documentation

Two documents must be physically on the vessel at all times:

- **Vessel Documentation:** The federal registration document for the vessel.
- **Certificate of Inspection (COI):** Must be posted visibly where passengers can see it. Not in a drawer. Not in the captain's bag. Posted.

The captain must also carry their USCG license on their person during all operations.

## COI Requirements

The Certificate of Inspection is issued after a USCG inspection and is valid for a set period (typically 5 years with annual re-inspection). It specifies:

- Maximum passengers
- Minimum crew
- Authorized operating area (route)
- Required safety equipment
- Any special conditions

Operating with an expired COI is operating illegally. The annual inspection is scheduled in advance, and the vessel must be presented in full compliance.

### What Happens During a USCG Inspection

Inspectors will:

- Check all life-saving equipment (count PFDs, verify types, check condition)
- Inspect fire extinguishers and flares
- Test navigation lights, horn, and radio
- Review vessel documentation and COI
- Inspect the hull, engine, and electrical systems
- Verify manning requirements (enough licensed crew)
- Check that the COI is posted visibly
- May interview crew on safety procedures
- May conduct a man-overboard drill

The inspection is thorough. If you're doing morning inspections correctly every day, you're already ready. The daily checklist exists so the annual inspection is never a surprise.

## Manning Requirements

Each vessel's COI specifies the minimum crew required. This always includes at least one USCG-licensed captain. Never operate below minimum manning. If a crew member doesn't show, the trip doesn't go until you have sufficient crew.

## Incident Reporting

### USCG Reporting (24-Hour Requirement)

You must report to the USCG within 24 hours if any of the following occur:

- Death
- Injury that requires medical treatment beyond first aid
- Significant damage to the vessel (affecting seaworthiness)
- Grounding
- Fire
- Pollution or hazardous material discharge
- Loss of main propulsion or steering

Under-reporting is worse than over-reporting. If you're not sure whether it qualifies, report it. The Coast Guard would rather get a report that turns out to be minor than find out later you hid something.

### Louisiana LDWF Reporting

The Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries has its own reporting requirements for boating accidents. Any accident resulting in death, disappearance, injury requiring medical treatment, or property damage exceeding the state threshold must be reported. Timelines vary by severity, death and disappearance require immediate notification.

### Our Internal Reporting

Every incident, no matter how minor, gets an internal incident report filed the same day. See the Incident Report Steps Quick Ref for the process. This protects the company, protects you, and creates a record that's critical if anything escalates later.

## The Bottom Line

Compliance isn't paperwork for its own sake. Every rule here exists because the water is unforgiving and our guests are trusting us with their safety. Do the checks. Keep the records. When in doubt, over-report and over-prepare. Nobody ever got in trouble for being too safe.
