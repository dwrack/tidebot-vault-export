## How Your Pay Works

- You're paid for work you actually do, not for a block of time.
- **Core rate** = working a trip.
- Everything outside working the trip is **its own line item**, its own rate.
- Nothing is bundled. Nothing is assumed.
- Some things pay **per trip**. A few pay **per day**, at the end of the day.
- Work three trips, get paid three times. Open and close the boat, that's two more lines.

## What Each Line Item Requires

**1. Trip worked (core)** — per tour
- On the boat from boarding through landing
- Guests taken care of the whole way
- Boat reset before you walk

**2. Opening boat prep** — per day, first trip only
- Cooler stocked, ice loaded
- PFD count verified
- Boat set: seating, sound, trash bags, supplies
- Anything broken or missing reported before guests board

**3. Closing and end-of-day** — per day, last trip only
- Boat shut down and secured
- Trash out, marina swept
- Gear stowed and locked up

**4. Turnaround between trips** — per tour
- Trash out, ice topped, cooler restocked
- Deck wiped, boat ready for next boarding

**5. Deep clean** — per occurrence
- Full interior and exterior, beyond a normal turnaround
- Must be assigned in writing in advance

**6. Boat move or relocation assist** — per occurrence
- Between slips, between marinas, or to and from service
- Must be assigned in writing in advance

**7. Fuel, ice or supply run** — per occurrence
- Must be assigned in writing in advance

**8. Weekend coverage** — per weekend
- Named for weekend coverage
- First call for gaps, walk-ups, day-of problems

**9. Reel published** — per clip used
- Paid once per clip the Company actually publishes
- Not paid per clip submitted
- See `SOPs/Deckhand — Social Capture Playbook.md`

**10. Upsell commission** — % of sale
- Drink and cooler upsells
- Photo packages
- Ice and branded merch
- Must be recorded at point of sale
- See `SOPs/Deckhand — On-Boat Revenue & Tips Playbook.md`

## Tips

- Pooled per trip, split **70% captain / 30% deckhand**
- The Company takes **nothing** off the tip pool. Ever.
- Full policy: `SOPs/Tip Split Policy.md`

**How it runs:**
- Captain counts the pool at end of trip, cash and digital
- Split happens same day. No IOUs.
- If the count is ever in question, you and the captain count it together
- Cash handed directly to you by a guest still goes in the pool. The system only works if it's consistent.

**Want to earn more on tips?** The gap between a $50 day and a $200 day is almost never service quality. It's whether the close got asked well and made easy.

## What Is Not Paid

- Onboarding paperwork and document collection
- Training shifts and shadow trips
- Ride-along coaching trips
- The Monday weekly check-in (optional, and it's for you)
- Self-study, SOP reading, the Culture Quiz
- Clips you submit that don't get published

**Why:** this is an investment in yourself. The coaching and the on-water experience go with you whether you work here or somewhere else.

## How to Claim an Add-On

**Pre-authorized only** (items 5, 6, 7):
- Jeff or JT assigns it in writing before you do it. Slack DM is fine.
- Not assigned in writing = doesn't pay.
- This isn't distrust. It keeps the paperwork clean on both sides and means you never do work wondering if it counts.

**Automatic** (items 2, 3, 4):
- Paid off the trip schedule and your end-of-day report. You don't claim them.

> `JEFF:` If pre-authorization is too rigid for how the marina runs, the alternative is crew log it at end of day and you approve at the pay run. Pick one, don't run both.

## When the Day Goes Sideways

Full table is Section 7 of your ICA. Short version:

- **Weather cancel 4+ hours out** → nothing owed, nothing worked
- **Weather cancel inside 4 hours** → 50% of core trip rate, plus any per-day items already earned
- **Mechanical fail before departure** → 50% of core trip rate, plus any per-day items already earned
- **Anything that goes wrong after you've committed** → full core rate plus every add-on you actually performed

**The principle:** once you've shown up and done the work, the work gets paid.
