## 1. The Deal

This agreement is between **NOLA Party Barge LLC** ("the Company") and **[DECKHAND NAME]** ("you," "the Deckhand"). You are an independent contractor, not an employee. You're responsible for your own taxes and your own time outside the shifts you accept.

What you bring: a strong work ethic, a great attitude with guests, and the willingness to be the host of someone's best day.

What we bring: the boats, the bookings, the SOPs, the gear, the training, and a coaching system designed to make you better at the job, not just check on you.

The job, simply: prep the boats, take care of the guests, keep the marina sharp, and walk off shift knowing you crushed it.

## 3. Onboarding & Training Path

The training path is structured. You aren't expected to know it all on day one. The goal is for you to feel set up, not thrown in.

### Phase 1: Paperwork (Before Anything Else)
- This ICA signed via DocuSeal
- W-9 on file
- Photo ID copy on file
- Background check cleared
- Emergency contact and payment method submitted
- Crew Intake Form complete

### Phase 2: Orientation (½ day, paid as a shift)
- Walk through the **Crew & Captain Performance Guide** with Jeffrey or a senior crew member
- Tour the marina: where every supply, tool, and piece of equipment lives
- Read **How We Run Things** and complete the **Culture Quiz**
- Review the SOPs that touch your day:
  - Deckhand Daily Standard & 3-Strikes
  - Deckhand Downtime Playbook
  - Deckhand On-Boat Revenue & Tips Playbook
  - Deckhand Social Capture Playbook
  - Quick Reference SOPs

### Phase 3: Shadow Shift (1 shift, paid)
You shadow an experienced deckhand through a full day. You watch arrival prep, boat prep, departure handling, between-trips reset, and end-of-day. Debrief with them after.

### Phase 4: Assisted Shifts (2-3 shifts, paid at full rate)
You run the day, the senior deckhand or captain is there to steer you if needed. Each shift, a quick debrief at the end. What worked, what to adjust.

### Phase 5: Solo Sign-Off
Manager signs off when:
- The 5 daily check-ins are completed honestly and on time without reminders
- You can prep a boat fully without supervision
- Guest interactions are warm and confident
- The marina is left in better shape than you found it

Sign-off is logged in the **Crew Roster & Credentials Tracker**. You're now in the regular rotation.

## 4. The Coaching System

We're not interested in catching mistakes. We're interested in helping you get better.

### 4.1 Tidebot (Daily)
Tidebot is the AI assistant in the crew Slack. He nudges you on the morning Arrival Checklist, answers questions about supplies and the manifest, and reminds you of end-of-day items before you walk off. He's not a snitch. He's the friend who pokes you to wrap a thing up before you leave.

### 4.2 The Form Is the Trust
You log your work via the 5 phone forms (Arrival, Boat Prep, Pre-Departure, Between Trips, End of Day). Each takes 1-2 minutes. Done honestly, the form is the entire trust system. No supervisor follows you around. The form is the trade.

If you completed the work, the form takes seconds. If the form takes you forever, you didn't do the work.

### 4.3 Real-Time Coaching
Captains and managers are allowed and encouraged to give in-the-moment coaching: "next time try this," "watch how I close the trip on this group," "here's a faster way to flip the boat." You're allowed and encouraged to ask back. This is the coaching loop. Use it.

### 4.4 Weekly Check-In (Optional)
Every Monday, Jeffrey is available for a 15-minute 1:1 if you want one. Bring something you want to get better at, or a frustration, or a recurring problem. No agenda from us.

### 4.5 Performance Guide Updates
The **Crew & Captain Performance Guide** is a living document. If something doesn't match reality, tell us. We'd rather change the SOP than have you grumbling about it.

## 5. What We Need From You Per Shift

Per-shift duties are detailed in:
- `SOPs/Deckhand — Daily Standard & 3-Strikes.md`
- `SOPs/Deckhand — Downtime Playbook.md`
- `SOPs/NOLA Party Barge — Crew & Captain Performance Guide.md`

Summary:

### 5.1 Arrival (Before Boat Prep)
Each boat has its own assigned arrival duty. Non-negotiable, checked daily:
- **FREAKY TIKI:** Outdoor customer area, restrooms inspected
- **TWERKIN TIKI:** Marina sweep + trash from roadside to property
- **BAYOU BOOGIE:** Docks swept and scrubbed
- **PARTY QUEEN:** Plants watered, landscaping evened out

If not all boats are going out: all hands on the customer area and restrooms.

### 5.2 Boat Prep (Before Guests)
Speakers loaded and tested, playlist queued, inverter on, surfaces wiped, restroom clean and stocked, supplies staged, cooler clean.

### 5.3 During Trips
- Help guests on board with food and drinks
- Run the upsell on coolers, ice, photos per the **On-Boat Revenue Playbook**
- Capture social content per the **Social Capture Playbook**
- Read the captain, anticipate, don't make them ask twice

### 5.4 Between Trips
Trash to dumpster, restroom reset, surfaces wiped, cupholders cleared, music reset, manifest checked.

### 5.5 Site Check (One Crew Member Per Day)
Customer areas clear of all tools, cords, gas cans 30 minutes before first check-in. Photo + Slack post in #marina-daily. End of day, same walk, same photo.

### 5.6 End of Day
- All trash bagged and out
- Restroom clean
- Surfaces wiped, floor swept
- Seats stowed in restroom
- Cooler drained and wiped
- Speakers to charging
- Inverter stowed
- Motors trimmed up, batteries off
- Dock lines double-checked
- **End of Day Sign-Off form submitted before you leave**

The 5 phone forms get logged. That's the day.

## 7. When Things Go Wrong

We don't run a punishment system. We run a coaching system. Strikes only enter the picture when coaching has already happened and the same issue is repeating.

### 7.1 What Counts as a Strike (Per the Existing Daily Standard SOP)
A strike is one of these, and only these:

1. **Skipping a check-in entirely** when you were on shift and should have done it
2. **Lying on a check-in.** Marking something complete when it wasn't (Jeff and JT do random spot-checks)
3. **Walking off end-of-day without the End of Day Sign-Off submitted**

Three of those in a rolling 90-day window means a different conversation. Outcomes range from extra coaching to contract termination, depending on severity.

### 7.2 What Does NOT Count as a Strike
- Flagging a problem ("restroom needs attention," "low on TP," "weird thing with the inverter"). That's the job.
- Asking for help in the crew Slack
- A bumpy day where something didn't go to plan but you logged it honestly
- Telling Tidebot or Jeff you couldn't get to something and why
- Being new and asking a hundred questions in your first month

If you're honest, we'll figure it out together. The only thing that breaks trust is silence or a fake checkbox.

### 7.3 Immediate Termination
Some things end the contract on the first occurrence, no coaching, no strikes:

- Theft from the Company, crew, or guests
- Showing up to a shift impaired (alcohol or drugs)
- Harassment or discrimination toward guests or crew
- Falsifying an incident report or covering up a guest injury
- Endangering guest safety on purpose or through extreme negligence

These aren't coaching items. They're hard lines.

## 10. Confidentiality, IP, Non-Solicit

### 10.1 Confidentiality
Don't share customer info, SOPs, internal Slack content, or financials outside the Company.

### 10.2 IP
- Photos, videos, and social content you create during a paid shift, on Company vessels, or with Company branding, are owned by the Company.
- The Company grants you a limited license to use that content for your own portfolio with credit.
- Form responses, incident reports, and check-ins are Company property.

### 10.3 Non-Solicit (Customers Only, Not Employment)
For 6 months after this agreement ends, you won't solicit Company customers for competing tour services in the New Orleans market. You're free to take any other deckhand work, including for competitors. We don't restrict your career.
