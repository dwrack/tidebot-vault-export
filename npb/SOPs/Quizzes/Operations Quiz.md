# Operations Quiz

**Passing score: 80% (8 out of 10 correct minimum)**

The day-to-day stuff that keeps everything running. These scenarios test whether you know the processes and can prioritize when things get messy.

---

## Question 1

You're assigned to Bayou Boogie today. What's your boat-specific arrival duty before you start the vessel inspection?

A) Set up the customer area and clean restrooms
B) Sweep the vessel and empty trash
C) Clean the dock and boarding area
D) Landscaping and grounds maintenance

**Best answer: C**

Bayou Boogie = dock cleaning. Freaky Tiki handles customer area and restrooms. Twerkin Tiki does sweeping and trash. Party Queen does landscaping. These duties follow the vessel, not the person. If you're on Bayou Boogie, you clean the dock.

## Question 2

During your between-trips reset, you notice the cooler is almost out of ice and the next group is a 20-person bachelorette party arriving in 25 minutes. What's your priority?

A) Finish cleaning the boat first, then deal with ice
B) Get ice immediately, it's the most time-sensitive item that directly impacts the next group's experience
C) Text Jeffrey and ask him to bring ice
D) Skip ice and offer apologies to the next group

**Best answer: B**

Ice for a 20-person party group is non-negotiable. Cleaning matters, but a slightly unfinished wipe-down is less noticeable than a warm cooler. Handle the time-sensitive, high-impact item first. If you can delegate or do both in parallel, even better. Texting Jeffrey for ice (C) when you can solve it yourself is exactly the kind of thing not to bother him with.

## Question 3

You pull up the next manifest in FareHarbor and it shows 14 guests, but only 12 waivers are signed. The group is already arriving at the dock. What do you do?

A) Board everyone and have the two unsigned guests sign later
B) Greet the group, identify the two guests without signed waivers, pull up the booking-specific waiver link on a device, and have them sign before boarding
C) Use a generic QR code to speed things up
D) Ask the group leader to vouch for the unsigned guests

**Best answer: B**

No boarding without a signed waiver. No exceptions. But you handle it smoothly, not like a bouncer. Greet everyone warmly, then pull aside the two who need to sign. Use the booking-specific waiver link, not a generic QR code (C). "We just need to get these two signed real quick and we're good to go." Vouching (D) isn't a legal substitute for a signature.

## Question 4

It's morning inspection and you discover that your flares are present but one of three has expired. You have 30 minutes before the first trip. What's the right sequence?

A) Run the trip with two good flares and replace the third later
B) Check if another vessel has a spare in-date flare you can use (without dropping their count below requirement), replace it, then proceed with inspection
C) Cancel the first trip
D) Order new flares online for next-day delivery

**Best answer: B**

All required flares must be in-date. But canceling the trip (C) is premature if you can solve the problem in 30 minutes. Check the spare inventory on site or see if another vessel has extras beyond their requirement. If you can't source an in-date flare, then yes, the vessel doesn't go out. Online ordering (D) doesn't help you in 30 minutes.

## Question 5

You're running back-to-back trips. The first trip ran 10 minutes long because the group was having such a great time. Now you have 20 minutes instead of 30 for your between-trips reset, and the next group is already at the dock. What do you prioritize?

A) Rush through every reset step as fast as possible
B) Hit the essentials: trash cleared, next manifest checked, PFDs counted, cooler situation assessed. Skip the deep clean and make up for it after the next trip.
C) Tell the next group you need 10 more minutes
D) Skip the reset and board the next group immediately

**Best answer: B**

You don't skip the reset (D), that's how the next group gets a dirty boat and a missed safety check. But you also don't need to do a deep clean when you're squeezed on time. Hit the items that affect safety (PFDs, manifest) and guest experience (trash, cooler) first. The detailed wipe-down can happen after the next trip. Making guests wait (C) is a last resort, not a first move.

## Question 6

A crew member scheduled for today's second shift texts you at 7 AM saying they can't make it. You're the only one on site. The second shift has three trips booked. What do you do?

A) Cover the shifts yourself
B) Contact Jeffrey immediately with the problem AND any solution you can suggest (other available crew, schedule adjustments)
C) Cancel all three trips and process refunds
D) Post in the group chat asking if anyone can pick up

**Best answer: B**

This is a Jeffrey call. Staffing emergencies that affect operations go to the manager. But don't just dump the problem on him. "Hey, [name] is out today. I can cover the first trip but not all three. Is [other captain] available?" That kind of proactive problem-solving is what separates good crew from great crew. Posting in the group chat (D) might help too, but Jeffrey needs to know regardless since he coordinates the schedule.

## Question 7

During a between-trips reset, you find a guest's phone left behind on a seat. What do you do?

A) Put it in lost and found
B) Check the previous manifest, try to identify the owner, and contact them or their booking holder through FareHarbor. Secure the phone in the meantime.
C) Leave it where it is in case they come back
D) Turn it in to Jeffrey at the end of the day

**Best answer: B**

A lost phone is urgent to the guest. They're probably already panicking. If you can identify them from the manifest and reach out quickly (through FareHarbor contact info or calling the number if you can access the lock screen), you might get it back to them before they leave the area. Leaving it (C) risks it getting knocked into the water. End of day (D) is too late.

## Question 8

You get a weather alert: winds are currently at 17 mph with gusts to 22, and the forecast shows it increasing over the next two hours. You have a trip departing in 45 minutes. What's the process?

A) Cancel immediately, winds are above 20 in gusts
B) Contact Jeffrey with the specific conditions, discuss whether to run, hold, or cancel. If you run, monitor continuously and be ready to cut short.
C) Wait and re-check in 30 minutes
D) Run it but stay close to the dock

**Best answer: B**

This is the gray area. Sustained winds at 17 are below the 20 mph threshold, but gusts at 22 are above it and the forecast is trending worse. This is exactly the kind of borderline call where captain and manager discuss it together. You might run a shortened trip staying in protected water, you might hold for 30 minutes and reassess, or you might cancel. The point is: you communicate, you decide together, and you don't just hope for the best.

## Question 9

End of day: you're doing sign-off and realize that one of the handheld radios is missing. Nobody on today's crew knows where it is. What do you do?

A) Note it and deal with it tomorrow
B) Report it to Jeffrey tonight via Slack, check all vessels and storage areas, and document that it's missing in the end-of-day log
C) Assume it was left on a boat and it'll turn up
D) Buy a replacement out of pocket

**Best answer: B**

Missing equipment gets reported the same day, not tomorrow. Check the obvious places first (all four vessels, storage compartments, dock area). If it doesn't turn up, log it and tell Jeffrey. Equipment accountability is part of end-of-day sign-off. Assuming it'll "turn up" (C) is how things stay missing permanently.

## Question 10

You're running the first trip of the day and discover mid-trip that the vessel documentation folder is missing from the boat. Everything else checked out during morning inspection. What happened, and what do you do now?

A) Continue the trip, the documentation is probably at the office
B) Complete the current trip safely, then do not run another trip on this vessel until the documentation is on board. Report it immediately.
C) Radio the office to have someone bring it to the next dock stop
D) It doesn't matter as long as you have your captain's license

**Best answer: B**

Vessel documentation must be physically on the vessel at all times during operation. You missed it during morning inspection, that's a process failure to learn from. Don't abandon guests mid-trip, but the vessel is grounded after this trip until the documents are back on board. If a USCG inspector had boarded you mid-trip, this would be a violation. Your captain's license (D) is separate, both are required.

## Question 11

It's a Saturday in peak season. You have back-to-back-to-back trips. Between trip 2 and trip 3, the music system stops working. Trip 3 boards in 15 minutes. What do you do?

A) Run the trip without music and apologize to the group
B) Troubleshoot quickly (check connections, bluetooth, power). If you can't fix it in 10 minutes, grab a portable speaker as backup. Brief the group that you're going old-school and lean harder into your hosting and storytelling.
C) Cancel the trip
D) Plug your phone into the vessel's aux and use that

**Best answer: B**

Music matters, but a broken speaker isn't a safety issue and doesn't justify a cancellation. Troubleshoot first. If it's a quick fix (bluetooth disconnected, power cable loose), great. If not, adapt. A portable backup speaker, your phone on aux (D is actually part of B), whatever gets sound going. And if you truly have zero music options, you better be the most engaging host those guests have ever met. The trip can still be great without perfect sound if the captain brings the energy.

## Question 12

You check FareHarbor in the morning and notice that the 2:00 PM slot appears to be double-booked: two separate groups, 16 people total, but the vessel only holds 14 per the COI. What do you do?

A) Figure it'll work out, people often no-show
B) Contact Jeffrey immediately with the specific booking details so the conflict can be resolved before either group arrives
C) Contact both groups yourself and ask one to reschedule
D) Squeeze everyone on, two extra people won't matter

**Best answer: B**

Never exceed COI capacity. Ever. And don't wait to see if it resolves itself (A). Booking conflicts are a management issue, Jeffrey needs to know now so he can contact one of the groups and reschedule or move them to another vessel. You shouldn't contact guests directly about scheduling changes (C) because that's a management decision about which group to move and what to offer them. And exceeding capacity (D) is a federal violation.
