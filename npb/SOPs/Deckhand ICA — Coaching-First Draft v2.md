> **Major flags surfaced so far:**
> 1. Deckhands are closer to the employee classification line than captains. The IRS economic-realities test treats heavy operational direction (per-boat duty mapping, required forms, mandated capture content) as a strong employee signal.
> 2. LHWCA exposure: deckhands working on or near vessels in navigable waters may fall under the Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act for shore-side injuries. Jones Act may also apply for on-vessel work.
> 3. Per-shift fee framing is closer to wages than per-deliverable. This draft keeps shift framing for now since deckhand work is genuinely shift-based, but the bonus structure has been moved to deliverable triggers (drinks sold, photo packages sold) to strengthen the 1099 posture.
> 4. Hold-harmless, IP, indemnification: lawyer's job. Placeholders below.

## 1. The Deal

This agreement is between **NOLA Party Barge LLC** ("the Company") and **[DECKHAND NAME]** ("you," "the Deckhand"). You are an independent contractor, not an employee. You're responsible for your own taxes and your own time outside the shifts you accept.

**What you bring:** a strong work ethic, a great attitude with guests, the willingness to be the host of someone's best day, and an eye for the small things that make a shift run clean.

**What we bring:** the boats, the bookings, the SOPs, the gear, the training, and a coaching system designed to make you better at the job, not just check on you.

**Your job, simply:** prep the boats, take care of the guests, keep the marina sharp, and walk off shift knowing you crushed it.

**Your right to refuse:** every shift offered to you is an offer, not an assignment. You can decline any shift for any reason, and declining shifts is not grounds for any consequence under this agreement. The Company will continue to offer you future shifts at its own discretion based on operational fit, but no clause in this agreement penalizes refusal.

## 4. The Coaching System

We're not interested in catching mistakes. We're interested in helping you get better.

### 4.1 Tidebot (Daily)
Tidebot is the AI assistant in the crew Slack. He nudges you on the morning Arrival Checklist, answers questions about supplies and the manifest, and reminds you of end-of-day items before you walk off. He's not a snitch. He's the friend who pokes you to wrap a thing up before you leave.

> `JEFF:` Confirm Tidebot is live in the crew Slack today.

### 4.2 The Form Is the Trust
You log your work via the 5 phone forms (Arrival, Boat Prep, Pre-Departure, Between Trips, End of Day). Each takes 1-2 minutes. Done honestly, the form is the entire trust system. No supervisor follows you around. The form is the trade.

If you completed the work, the form takes seconds. If the form takes you forever, you didn't do the work.

### 4.3 End-of-Day Audio Log (New)
After your last trip, in addition to the End of Day Sign-Off form, file a short voice memo in the crew Slack covering:
- How the day went, anything notable
- Maintenance issues observed
- Active todos for tomorrow in your area
- Anything Jeffrey or JT should know

This pairs with the form. The form is your checkbox layer; the audio log is your context layer.

### 4.4 Real-Time Coaching
Captains and managers are allowed and encouraged to give in-the-moment coaching: "next time try this," "watch how I close the trip on this group," "here's a faster way to flip the boat." You're allowed and encouraged to ask back. This is the coaching loop. Use it.

### 4.5 Weekly Check-In (Optional)
Every Monday, Jeffrey is available for a 15-minute 1:1 if you want one. Bring something you want to get better at, or a frustration, or a recurring problem. No agenda from us.

### 4.6 Performance Guide Updates
The **Crew & Captain Performance Guide** is a living document. If something doesn't match reality, tell us. We'd rather change the SOP than have you grumbling about it.

## 7. Edge Cases & Pay Framework

Shifts don't always go to plan. This section is the full pay table for the most common edge cases. Read it as part of Section 2.

| Scenario | Deckhand pay |
|---|---|
| Weather cancel ≥ 4 hrs before scheduled shift start | $0 (no work, no pay) |
| Weather cancel < 4 hrs before (you're prepping or en route) | 50% of shift fee (commit fee) |
| Weather cancel after you arrive at marina | Full shift fee |
| Weather hold mid-shift (had to turn back) | Full shift fee |
| Mechanical fail before departure | 50% of shift fee + reschedule priority |
| Mechanical fail mid-shift | Full shift fee |
| Trip cancelled by Company at the dock for non-weather reason | Full shift fee |
| Deckhand offered a shift, declines | $0 (no consequence) |
| Deckhand accepts shift, no-shows | $0 + strike per Section 8 |
| Shift ends early at guest request | Full shift fee |

**Shift swaps:** deckhands may swap an accepted shift directly with another qualified deckhand. Notify Jeffrey in writing (Slack DM is fine) before the swap takes effect.

**Late callouts:** if you accepted a shift and need to call out, the standard is at least 4 hours of notice unless an emergency makes that impossible. Less than 4 hours of notice without a real emergency counts as a strike per Section 8.

## 8. When Things Go Wrong

We don't run a punishment system. We run a coaching system. Strikes only enter the picture when coaching has already happened and the same issue is repeating.

### 8.1 What Counts as a Strike
A strike is one of these, and only these:

1. **Skipping a check-in entirely** when you were on shift and should have done it
2. **Lying on a check-in.** Marking something complete when it wasn't
3. **Walking off end-of-day without the End of Day Sign-Off submitted or the end-of-day audio log filed**
4. **Late callout** with less than 4 hours of notice and no real emergency
5. **No-call no-show** for an accepted shift

**Spot-check rhythm:**
- **Phase 1 (training mode, first 60-90 days):** site checks happen alongside crew. Visible, training-mode, paired with coaching. Strikes are not called for honest mistakes.
- **Phase 2 (after habit forms):** spot-checks become random. Strike rules apply.

**Strike ladder:**
- **1st strike:** coaching note in the log + 30-day watch period
- **2nd strike:** mandatory ride-along + written warning
- **3rd strike:** contract review with termination on the table

Strike escalation routes through the shared David + Jeffrey manager DM channel. Any contract review involves both.

### 8.2 What Does NOT Count as a Strike
- Flagging a problem ("restroom needs attention," "low on TP," "weird thing with the inverter"). That's the job.
- Asking for help in the crew Slack
- A bumpy day where something didn't go to plan but you logged it honestly
- Telling Tidebot or Jeff you couldn't get to something and why
- Being new and asking a hundred questions in your first month
- Refusing a shift offer

If you're honest, we'll figure it out together. The only thing that breaks trust is silence or a fake checkbox.

### 8.3 Immediate Termination
Some things end the contract on the first occurrence, no coaching, no strikes:

- Theft from the Company, crew, or guests
- Showing up to a shift impaired (alcohol or drugs)
- Harassment or discrimination toward guests or crew
- Falsifying an incident report or covering up a guest injury
- Endangering guest safety on purpose or through extreme negligence
- **Departing on a trip without all FareHarbor waivers signed and verified**
- Leaving the dock with insufficient PFDs onboard

These aren't coaching items. They're hard lines.
