## 1. The Deal

This agreement is between **NOLA Party Barge LLC** ("the Company") and **[DECKHAND NAME]** ("you," "the Deckhand"). You are an independent contractor, not an employee. You're responsible for your own taxes and your own time outside the shifts you accept.

**What you bring:** a strong work ethic, a great attitude with guests, the willingness to be the host of someone's best day, and an eye for the small things that make a shift run clean.

**What we bring:** the boats, the bookings, the SOPs, the gear, the training, and a coaching system designed to make you better at the job, not just check on you.

**Your job, simply:** prep the boats, take care of the guests, keep the marina sharp, and walk off shift knowing you crushed it.

**Your right to refuse:** every shift offered to you is an offer, not an assignment. You can decline any shift for any reason, and declining shifts is not grounds for any consequence under this agreement. The Company will continue to offer you future shifts at its own discretion based on operational fit, but no clause in this agreement penalizes refusal.

## 2. Compensation

All rates live in **Exhibit A — Deckhand Pay Schedule**, attached to and part of this agreement. Exhibit A is re-issued when rates change; a new exhibit requires your acknowledgment, not a re-signing of this full agreement.

### 2.1 How Your Pay Is Built
- You're paid for the work you actually do, not for a block of time.
- **Core rate** = working a trip, boarding through landing, boat reset before you walk.
- Everything outside working the trip is **its own line item** with its own rate.
- Most items pay **per trip**. A few pay **per day**, at the end of the day.
- Nothing is bundled. Nothing is assumed.

**The line items, in short:**

| Line item | Unit |
|---|---|
| Trip worked (core) | per tour |
| Opening boat prep | per day |
| Closing and end-of-day | per day |
| Turnaround between trips | per tour |
| Deep clean | per occurrence |
| Boat move or relocation assist | per occurrence |
| Fuel, ice or supply run | per occurrence |
| Weekend coverage | per weekend |
| Reel published | per clip used |
| On-boat upsell commission | % of sale |
| Tips | per trip, 30% of pool |

Dollar amounts and the full trigger for each item are in Exhibit A.

### 2.2 Add-On Work Is Pre-Authorized
- Deep cleans, boat moves, and fuel, ice or supply runs pay only when Jeff or JT assigned the work **in writing** before you did it. Slack DM is fine.
- Not assigned in writing = doesn't pay.
- Opening, closing, and turnaround pay automatically off the trip schedule and your end-of-day report. You don't claim them.

> `JEFF:` If pre-authorization is too rigid for how the marina runs, the alternative is crew log it at end of day and you approve at the pay run. Pick one, don't run both.

### 2.3 Edge-Case Pay
Days don't always go to plan. Section 7 is the full pay table for weather cancellations, mechanical failures, and schedule changes. Read it as part of this section.

### 2.4 Tips
- Tips are pooled per trip and split **70% captain / 30% deckhand**.
- The Company takes **nothing** off the tip pool.
- Full policy: `SOPs/Tip Split Policy.md`.

**How it runs:**
- The captain counts the pool at end of trip, cash and digital
- Split happens same day, no IOUs
- If the count is ever in question, you and the captain count it together
- Cash handed directly to you by a guest still goes in the pool

### 2.5 Sales Commission (Tied to What You Actually Sell)
Commission isn't abstract and it isn't review-based. It's tied to revenue you generate on the boat. Sell things, earn things.

- Drink and cooler upsells
- Photo packages
- Ice and branded merch
- Published reels, paid per clip the Company actually uses

Percentages and per-clip amounts are in Exhibit A. See `SOPs/Deckhand — On-Boat Revenue & Tips Playbook.md` for how to run the sell without being weird about it.

> Note: review-based bonuses are intentionally not included. Google has begun flagging reviews that name staff, and the focus is on running great trips, not gaming reviews.

### 2.6 What Is Not Paid
- Onboarding paperwork and document collection
- Training shifts and shadow trips
- Ride-along coaching trips
- The Monday weekly check-in (it's optional, and it's for you)
- Self-study, SOP reading, the Culture Quiz
- Clips you submit that don't get published

These are an investment in yourself. The coaching and the on-water experience go with you whether you work here or somewhere else.

The training path is structured. You aren't expected to know it all on day one. The goal is for you to feel set up, not thrown in.

### Phase 1: Paperwork (Before Anything Else)
- This ICA signed via DocuSeal
- W-9 on file
- Photo ID copy on file
- Background check cleared (standard for all new hires going forward)
- Emergency contact and payment method submitted
- Crew Intake Form complete

> `JEFF:` Lock in a background check provider so this is real (Checkr, GoodHire, etc.).

### Phase 2: Orientation (½ day, paid as a shift)
- Walk through the **Crew & Captain Performance Guide** with Jeffrey or a senior crew member
- Tour the marina: where every supply, tool, and piece of equipment lives
- Read **How We Run Things** and complete the **Culture Quiz**
- Review the SOPs that touch your day:
  - Deckhand Daily Standard & 3-Strikes
  - Deckhand Downtime Playbook
  - Deckhand On-Boat Revenue & Tips Playbook
  - Deckhand Social Capture Playbook
  - Photography Upsell SOP (when live)
  - Quick Reference SOPs

> `JEFF:` Confirm the Culture Quiz exists and is filled out.

### Phase 3: Shadow Day (1 day, unpaid)
You shadow an experienced deckhand through a full day. You watch arrival prep, boat prep, departure handling, between-trips reset, and end-of-day. Debrief with them after. You're observing, not working the boat, so this one is on you. It's the last unpaid step.

### Phase 4: Assisted Days (2-3 days, paid at full Exhibit A rates)
You run the day yourself. A senior deckhand or captain is on the marina to step in if you need them. Each day ends with a quick debrief: what worked, what to adjust.

You're doing the actual work, so you're paid the actual rates. Every Exhibit A line item you perform pays in full. No probationary rate, no training discount.

### Phase 5: Solo Sign-Off
Sign-off happens through the crew Slack channel with Tidebot capturing the workflow and Jeffrey approving when you're ready. Criteria:
- The 5 daily check-ins are completed honestly and on time without reminders
- You can prep a boat fully without supervision
- Guest interactions are warm and confident
- The marina is left in better shape than you found it

Sign-off is logged in the **Crew Roster & Credentials Tracker**. You're now in the regular rotation.

## 4. The Coaching System

We're not interested in catching mistakes. We're interested in helping you get better.

### 4.1 Tidebot (Daily)
Tidebot is the AI assistant in the crew Slack. He nudges you on the morning Arrival Checklist, answers questions about supplies and the manifest, and reminds you of end-of-day items before you walk off. He's not a snitch. He's the friend who pokes you to wrap a thing up before you leave.

> `JEFF:` Confirm Tidebot is live in the crew Slack today.

### 4.2 The Form Is the Trust
You log your work via the 5 phone forms (Arrival, Boat Prep, Pre-Departure, Between Trips, End of Day). Each takes 1-2 minutes. Done honestly, the form is the entire trust system. No supervisor follows you around. The form is the trade.

If you completed the work, the form takes seconds. If the form takes you forever, you didn't do the work.

### 4.3 End-of-Day Audio Log (New)
After your last trip, in addition to the End of Day Sign-Off form, file a short voice memo in the crew Slack covering:
- How the day went, anything notable
- Maintenance issues observed
- Active todos for tomorrow in your area
- Anything Jeffrey or JT should know

This pairs with the form. The form is your checkbox layer; the audio log is your context layer.

### 4.4 Real-Time Coaching
Captains and managers are allowed and encouraged to give in-the-moment coaching: "next time try this," "watch how I close the trip on this group," "here's a faster way to flip the boat." You're allowed and encouraged to ask back. This is the coaching loop. Use it.

### 4.5 Weekly Check-In (Optional)
Every Monday, Jeffrey is available for a 15-minute 1:1 if you want one. Bring something you want to get better at, or a frustration, or a recurring problem. No agenda from us.

### 4.6 Performance Guide Updates
The **Crew & Captain Performance Guide** is a living document. If something doesn't match reality, tell us. We'd rather change the SOP than have you grumbling about it.

## 7. Edge Cases & Pay Framework

Days don't always go to plan. This section is the full pay table for the most common edge cases. Read it as part of Section 2.

**Two terms used below:**
- **Core rate** = the per-trip rate in Exhibit A, line 1.
- **Add-ons earned** = every other Exhibit A line item you actually performed that day (opening prep, closing, turnarounds, pre-authorized runs).

**The principle:** once you've shown up and done the work, the work gets paid.

| Scenario | Deckhand pay |
|---|---|
| Weather cancel ≥ 4 hrs before scheduled start | $0 (no work, no pay) |
| Weather cancel < 4 hrs before (you're prepping or en route) | 50% of core rate (commit fee) + add-ons earned |
| Weather cancel after you arrive at marina | Full core rate + add-ons earned |
| Weather hold mid-trip (had to turn back) | Full core rate + add-ons earned |
| Mechanical fail before departure | 50% of core rate (commit fee) + add-ons earned + reschedule priority |
| Mechanical fail mid-trip | Full core rate + add-ons earned |
| Trip cancelled by Company at the dock for non-weather reason | Full core rate + add-ons earned |
| Deckhand offered a shift, declines | $0 (no consequence) |
| Deckhand accepts shift, no-shows | $0 + strike per Section 8 |
| Trip ends early at guest request | Full core rate + add-ons earned |

**Note on opening prep:** if you completed the opening boat prep and the day then gets cancelled, opening prep pays in full regardless of which row above applies. You did the work.

**Shift swaps:** deckhands may swap an accepted shift directly with another qualified deckhand. Notify Jeffrey in writing (Slack DM is fine) before the swap takes effect.

**Late callouts:** if you accepted a shift and need to call out, the standard is at least 4 hours of notice unless an emergency makes that impossible. Less than 4 hours of notice without a real emergency counts as a strike per Section 8.

## 8. When Things Go Wrong

We don't run a punishment system. We run a coaching system. Strikes only enter the picture when coaching has already happened and the same issue is repeating.

### 8.1 What Counts as a Strike
A strike is one of these, and only these:

1. **Skipping a check-in entirely** when you were on shift and should have done it
2. **Lying on a check-in.** Marking something complete when it wasn't
3. **Walking off end-of-day without the End of Day Sign-Off submitted or the end-of-day audio log filed**
4. **Late callout** with less than 4 hours of notice and no real emergency
5. **No-call no-show** for an accepted shift

**Spot-check rhythm:**
- **Phase 1 (training mode, first 60-90 days):** site checks happen alongside crew. Visible, training-mode, paired with coaching. Strikes are not called for honest mistakes.
- **Phase 2 (after habit forms):** spot-checks become random. Strike rules apply.

**Strike ladder:**
- **1st strike:** coaching note in the log + 30-day watch period
- **2nd strike:** mandatory ride-along + written warning
- **3rd strike:** contract review with termination on the table

Strike escalation routes through the shared David + Jeffrey manager DM channel. Any contract review involves both.

### 8.2 What Does NOT Count as a Strike
- Flagging a problem ("restroom needs attention," "low on TP," "weird thing with the inverter"). That's the job.
- Asking for help in the crew Slack
- A bumpy day where something didn't go to plan but you logged it honestly
- Telling Tidebot or Jeff you couldn't get to something and why
- Being new and asking a hundred questions in your first month
- Refusing a shift offer

If you're honest, we'll figure it out together. The only thing that breaks trust is silence or a fake checkbox.

### 8.3 Immediate Termination
Some things end the contract on the first occurrence, no coaching, no strikes:

- Theft from the Company, crew, or guests
- Showing up to a shift impaired (alcohol or drugs)
- Harassment or discrimination toward guests or crew
- Falsifying an incident report or covering up a guest injury
- Endangering guest safety on purpose or through extreme negligence
- **Departing on a trip without all FareHarbor waivers signed and verified**
- Leaving the dock with insufficient PFDs onboard

These aren't coaching items. They're hard lines.
