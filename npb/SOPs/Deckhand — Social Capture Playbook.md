# Deckhand — Social Capture Playbook
*The 6 shots every trip should have. Plus how to get them without being weird about it.*

---

## Why You

You're the camera that's on the boat every day. We can pay a videographer once a month, but you're there for 50 trips. Even half-decent footage from real groups beats glossy ad footage every time. Real wins on social.

The clips you send become the ads we run on Meta and the reels we post on Instagram and TikTok. The crew that consistently feeds the content channel gets first pick of shifts and bonus pay (ask Jeff for current rate, usually $5-$10 per usable clip, $25 for a clip that gets used in a paid ad).

---

## The Camera

Your phone, vertical (9:16), in 4K if your phone can do it. Hold it horizontal **only** for wide drone-feel shots that don't have people in them.

Lock focus and exposure on the subject before recording (tap and hold on the screen). The most common mistake is letting the phone auto-adjust mid-clip, which makes everything look amateur.

Clean your lens. Wipe it on your shirt before you start filming. Salt spray and fingerprints kill clips fast.

## The 6 Shots (Per Trip)

You don't need all 6 every trip, but aim for 3-4. Anything you grab is more than we have.

### 1. The Boarding Shot
**What:** A slow vertical pan as the group boards. Get smiles, the welcome, the music starting. 8-15 seconds.
**Why:** This is the single most useful clip we have. It shows the energy and the size of the group. Use it as the opener of every reel.
**When:** Right after they walk down the dock.
**Pro tip:** Stand a bit lower than the boat entry and pan up as they board. It makes the boat look bigger.

### 2. The Group Reaction Shot
**What:** The first time the group hits the open water and reacts. Hands up, drinks raised, laughing, dancing.
**Why:** This is the emotion shot. Sells the experience.
**When:** Right after you leave the slip and the music kicks in.
**Pro tip:** Don't say "everybody look at the camera." Just film. Candid is the win.

### 3. The Hero Boat Shot
**What:** A wide shot of your boat from a distance, ideally from a passing angle (when another boat passes you, or if you can grab a quick clip from the dock as you come back in).
**Why:** Shows the product. The boat itself sells.
**When:** Whenever the boat is clean, the people are visible, and the light is good.
**Pro tip:** Golden hour (1 hour before sunset) is your friend. Anything you film then looks 10x better.

### 4. The Detail Shot
**What:** A close-up. A drink being poured, the speakers thumping, the cooler opening, the captain's hand on the wheel, ice in a cup with the boat moving in the background.
**Why:** Detail shots are what makes a 15-second reel feel like a movie. Without them, everything reads flat.
**When:** Mid-trip, in calm moments. 3-5 seconds is enough.
**Pro tip:** Shoot 4 of these per trip. We can never have too many.

### 5. The Captain or Crew Moment
**What:** You or the captain doing the job. Telling a story, pointing something out, dancing, helping a guest.
**Why:** People follow people. They book because they like the crew, not the boat.
**When:** Anytime you're being yourself and not staged.
**Pro tip:** Have another crew member film you, then return the favor.

### 6. The Closing Shot
**What:** The walk-off at the end. Group hugs, the dock approach, sunset on the water, somebody saying "best day ever."
**Why:** Endings sell. They make the viewer feel what the guest felt.
**When:** Last 5 minutes of the trip.

## Bonus Shots (Always Welcome)

- **Wildlife:** Birds, dolphins, the occasional gator. Jeff loses his mind every time.
- **Weather drama:** Storm clouds rolling in (only film if it's safe), perfect blue sky reflected on water.
- **Funny moment:** Someone's hat blowing off, a perfectly synced dance moment, a kid's first time on a boat.
- **Behind the scenes:** Cleaning the boat, prepping the cooler, the crew laughing at something. People love this stuff.
- **Drone-style:** Hold your phone way out over the water (carefully) and pan slow. Looks more cinematic than you'd think.

## The YouTube Pass (New, June 2026)

One per trip: hold the phone **horizontal** (16:9) and film a slow 20-30 second sweep of the trip at its best moment. Boat moving, group vibing, scenery rolling by. This is the only required horizontal shot, and it feeds the YouTube channel we're building. Same delivery: drop it in #crew-content with the rest.

## Consent (Important)

You don't need a signed release for guests on the boat — they signed a waiver that includes media usage when they booked. **But:**

- If a guest asks not to be filmed, immediately stop and respect it. Don't argue.
- Don't film bachelorette / private group moments that obviously feel like they should stay private (private toasts, breakdowns, etc.).
- If a guest is in a swimsuit and looks uncomfortable, don't push that angle.
- Kids: keep clips general (group shot from behind, etc.). Avoid close-ups of children's faces in content unless a parent has explicitly said it's fine.

When in doubt, ask: "Y'all good if I grab a few clips for our Instagram?" 99% of groups light up at this.

## How to Send

Drop everything to the **#crew-content** Slack channel **same day**. Tomorrow doesn't work — we lose context, you forget what's what, and we miss the post window.

Format:
> Boat: [your boat]
> Trip time: [start time]
> Group type: [bachelorette / corporate / family / etc.]
> [Drop the videos]

That's it. No need to caption or describe each clip. Jeff or our editor will figure it out.

If a clip is exceptional (the kind of thing you'd post on your own page), flag it: "🔥 this one's special."

---

## What NOT to Do

- Don't film while you're supposed to be hosting. Hosting always wins. Quick clips between actions only.
- Don't post anything to your personal accounts before sending it to us. We get first use. If you want to post after, totally cool, just tag @nolapartybarge.
- Don't film with audio that's playing music we don't have rights to (well-known top 40 songs). Use ambient audio. We'll add music ourselves.
- Don't film vertical for landscape framing or vice versa. If unsure, vertical.
- Don't apply filters. We process the footage.

---

## The 30-Second Daily Habit

If you do nothing else, do this every shift:
- 1 vertical clip of your boat looking great (no people)
- 1 vertical clip of guests reacting at the moment of departure
- 1 vertical clip of golden-hour sunset at landing

3 clips, 30 seconds total. Drop them to Slack at end of day. That alone keeps the engine fed.

We post a reel a day. We run ads weekly. The footage in those ads should be your shifts. The crew with their face and their boat in the most ads gets the strongest reputation, which gets the best shifts, which gets the best tips. The flywheel is real.
