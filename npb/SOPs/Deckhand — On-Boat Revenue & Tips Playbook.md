# Deckhand — On-Boat Revenue & Tips Playbook
*How to make more money for yourself and the business without being weird about it.*

---

## The Mindset

People tip and rebook for one reason: they felt taken care of. They didn't feel like they were a transaction. Everything in this playbook is in service of that feeling, not against it. If you ever feel like you're "selling," you're doing it wrong.

The best deckhands at this marina walk off the boat with $80-$150 in tips on a good trip. They do four things differently: they remember names, they ask better questions, they create a peak moment, and they know exactly when to ask for the close.

---

## The 4 Money Levers (Per Trip)

Each trip has four small chances to bring in revenue or income, in this order:

1. **Cooler upsell at boarding** (existing revenue, often left on the table)
2. **The peak moment** (drives tips and reviews)
3. **The review ask, in the right spot** (drives future bookings)
4. **The rebook ask, only at landing** (drives lifetime value)

Tips happen when 2, 3, and 4 land cleanly.

---

## 1. Cooler Upsell at Boarding

Most groups underbuy water and snacks because they assume "we'll just buy it on the boat." Half the time they don't get around to ordering. You can prevent that.

**As they board, while you're being friendly:**

> "Hey, real quick before we shove off, the cooler is stocked with [list 2-3 things, e.g., 'cold waters, a few seltzers, ice if you need a top-off']. Now's the easy time to grab whatever you want for the trip. After we get going I'll be running around so it's harder."

That's it. Don't push. Don't list everything. Just make the moment easy.

**Why this works:** People want permission to grab something without feeling cheap. You gave it to them. The "harder later" line creates a small urgency without being pushy.

---

## 2. The Peak Moment

Every trip needs one moment that gets remembered. That memory is what drives the tip and the review. If the trip is a flat 90 minutes of music and pretty water, people will say "it was nice" and tip 10%. If there's a peak, people will say "you HAVE to do this" and tip 20%+.

**Examples of a peak moment:**
- A perfectly timed song drop right when the group hits the open water (Big Freedia or a NOLA classic always works)
- A story you tell at exactly the right spot (e.g., a cool fact about a landmark you're passing — see Performance Guide)
- A toast you lead at sunset or at the turnaround
- A photo moment you actively set up for the group ("Everybody on this side, I'm taking the shot")
- A surprise: a small lagniappe (NOLA word for "a little something extra") like extra ice, a quick second pass by a landmark, etc.

**You should know what your peak moment is going to be before the trip starts.** Pick one and execute it. Don't wing it.

---

## 3. The Review Ask

The single most underused move on this boat. Most crew either don't ask, or they ask wrong (at the end, in a rushed line, after the moment has passed).

**The right time:** Right after the peak moment, while the group is still in the energy. Not at the dock. Not while they're packing up.

**The right script:**

> "Hey y'all, real quick — if you're having a good time out here, the single biggest thing that helps us keep doing this is a Google review. Takes you 30 seconds. I'll send the QR code around right now if anybody's down, and I genuinely appreciate it. If something's been off, also tell me right now so I can fix it before we land."

That last line is gold. It invites feedback before they leave a bad review. They almost never have a real complaint, and asking shows you actually care.

**Have a QR code printed and laminated, accessible mid-trip.** Ask Jeff if your boat doesn't have one.

**What "30 seconds" gets you:**
- A Google review at the moment of peak emotion (way better than 2 days later)
- A reminder of your name in their head when they tip
- A reason for them to mention you by name in the review (which boosts your reputation directly)

---

## 4. The Rebook & Referral Ask

Save this for landing. After the peak, after the review ask, when the energy is easing and they're collecting stuff.

**Two things to drop, casually, not at the same time:**

**(a) The rebook:**

> "Y'all coming back to NOLA at all? If you do, just text the number on the receipt and I'll personally help you set something up — and we'll take care of you with a discount. You don't have to use FareHarbor or whatever, just message us direct."

This makes them feel like an insider. It also gets them in our SMS list, which is more valuable than another OTA booking.

**(b) The friend referral:**

> "If you've got friends coming to NOLA, send them our way — we have a referral thing where you both get a discount. I'll tell you the code at the dock if you want it."

Don't push the code on them. Make them ask for it.

**Ask Jeff or JT for the current referral code. It changes seasonally.**

## Tip Mechanics

You should have a tip jar visible by the entry/exit when guests are boarding off, and a tip QR code (Venmo, Cash App, Zelle) on a small laminated card on the boat. Don't be weird about it — visibility alone does most of the work.

**At landing:**

> "Y'all were a great group. Tips are super appreciated — there's a jar right here or a QR code on the rail if you'd rather phone it in. Thank you for spending your day with us."

Then back off. Don't hover. Tip-pressure is the fastest way to get a 10% tip instead of a 25% tip.

## Future Group Bookings (The Big One)

This is where the real money is. Bachelorette parties book once. Corporate groups book yearly. Frats and sororities book again. Wedding parties refer their friends.

**Listen for:**
- "We're getting married next year"
- "Our company is doing a retreat"
- "My sister is getting married in the fall"
- "We're a [team / club / group] and we do this every year"

**When you hear it:**

> "Stop, we have to talk before you leave. Group bookings, weddings, corporate, that's actually our favorite stuff to do — we'll customize the boat, the food, all of it. Take my number / Jeff's number / give me your contact and we'll get you a quote that beats anything else you'll find."

Then text the lead to Jeff that night. He'll follow up. If a group books because of your intro, **you get a referral commission** (ask Jeff for the current rate).

---

## What NOT to Do

- **Don't ask for the tip mid-trip.** It poisons the energy. Tip is at landing.
- **Don't push the cooler if the group is BYOB-ing heavily.** Read the room.
- **Don't read the review ask off a card.** Memorize it.
- **Don't ask "did you have fun?" at the end.** Ask "what was your favorite part?" — it's a totally different conversation.
- **Don't pitch all four levers in one breathless run.** They're spaced through the trip on purpose.

---

## The Tip Math (Why This Matters)

A 12-person trip:
- Bad version: $0 cooler upsell, no peak moment, no review, no tip = $0 to you
- Average version: $40 cooler, soft peak, light tip = ~$30 to you
- Dialed version: $80 cooler upsell, real peak moment, review locked in, $150 in tips, 1 group lead = ~$150 to you that day, plus a possible referral commission later

Same trip, same boat. Different deckhand experience. The difference is execution.
