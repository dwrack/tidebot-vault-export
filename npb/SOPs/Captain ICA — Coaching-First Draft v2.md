## 1. The Deal

This agreement is between **NOLA Party Barge LLC** ("the Company") and **[CAPTAIN NAME]** ("you," "the Captain"). You are an independent contractor, not an employee. You're responsible for your own taxes, your own health insurance, and your own time outside of the trips you accept.

**What you bring to the table:** your USCG license, your skill on the water, and the tour itself. The narration, the stories, the music calls, the energy you build, the way you read a group and adjust. Each trip you run is a deliverable that's distinctly yours, not a script you read off a card.

**What we bring:** the boats, the bookings, the marina, the SOPs, the back-office support, and a coaching system designed to make you better at this job over time, not just keep you in line.

**Your deliverable, simply:** run safe, legal, memorable trips that guests rave about and rebook.

**Your right to refuse:** every shift offered to you is an offer, not an assignment. You can decline any shift for any reason, and declining shifts is not grounds for any consequence under this agreement. The Company will continue to offer you future shifts at its own discretion based on operational fit, but no clause in this agreement penalizes refusal.

## 3. Onboarding & Training Path

We don't drop you on a boat alone. The path is structured and the goal is for you to feel confident before you ever sign off.

### Phase 1: Paperwork (Before Anything Else)
- This ICA signed via DocuSeal
- W-9 on file
- Photo ID, USCG license, TWIC, CPR/First Aid, Maritime Consortium enrollment, drug test result, all on file
- Background check cleared
- Emergency contact and payment method submitted
- Crew Intake Form complete

### Phase 2: Orientation (½ day, paid as a shift)
- Walk through the **Crew & Captain Performance Guide** with Jeffrey or a senior captain
- Equipment tour, safety equipment locations, emergency procedures
- Read the culture doc **How We Run Things**, complete the **Culture Quiz**
- Review the relevant SOPs: Pre-Tour Preparation, The First 15 Minutes, Welcome Script, Wow Moment design, Tip Close, Coast Guard & Compliance

> `JEFF:` Confirm the Culture Quiz exists and is filled out. If not, please draft one this week.

### Phase 3: Shadow Shift (1-2 trips, paid)
You ride along with an experienced captain. Observation only. You watch the full arc: arrival, build, peak, land, send-off. Debrief with the captain after each trip. Shadow shifts are paid at the standard per-tour rate.

### Phase 4: Assisted Shifts (2-4 trips, paid at full per-tour rate)
You run the trip, the experienced captain is on board to step in if anything goes sideways. After each, a 10-minute debrief: what worked, what to adjust, what to try next time.

### Phase 5: Solo Sign-Off
Sign-off happens through the crew Slack channel with Tidebot capturing the workflow and Jeffrey approving when you're ready. Criteria:
- Guest handling is solid
- Safety procedures are automatic
- Gear, motor, and FareHarbor competency confirmed
- You can run the emotional arc without a script

Sign-off is documented in the **Crew Roster & Credentials Tracker**. You're now in the regular rotation.

## 4. The Coaching System

This is the part that matters. We're not interested in catching you doing something wrong. We're interested in making you a better captain every month than you were last month.

### 4.1 Tidebot (Daily)
Tidebot is the AI assistant in the crew Slack. He nudges you on prep, answers logistics questions, and reminds you to log pre-trip and post-trip. Use him. He's not a snitch, he's the friend who pokes you so you don't walk off the dock with something unfinished.

> `JEFF:` Confirm Tidebot is live in the crew Slack today, or flag if it's still in build.

### 4.2 Captain Audio Log (Pre-Trip and Post-Trip — Every Trip)
For every trip you run, you record two short voice memos:
- **Pre-trip (before departure):** vessel name, scheduled trip time, passenger count, weather, pre-departure safety check status, any flags you're carrying into the trip.
- **Post-trip (after dock):** how the trip went, any incidents or near-misses, equipment issues, guest issues, anything notable for the operation.

Drop both in the **Captain Audio Log** Slack channel. We process these per the **Captain Audio Log Processing SOP** and use them to improve the operation. The pre-trip log is also your USCG-compliant logbook entry, this is non-negotiable from a compliance standpoint.

If multiple trips run back-to-back, a single combined post-trip log covering all trips is acceptable as long as each trip is identified.

**Your access to your own logs:** you can request copies of your own audio logs at any time. They're useful for your own records, building captain status (USCG sea time documentation), or future employment elsewhere. The Company has rights to use them; you have rights to access them.

### 4.3 Weekly Check-In (15 min, optional but offered)
Every Monday, Jeffrey is available for a short 1:1 if you want one. Bring something you want to get better at, or a problem you keep running into, or just a vent. No agenda from us, you drive it.

### 4.4 Ride-Along Coaching (Quarterly, Per Captain)
Once per quarter, every captain gets one ride-along coaching trip. This is either a secret-shopper observation or Jeffrey on board, with a written debrief afterward: three things you did well, one thing to work on. Development tool, not performance review.

### 4.5 Performance Guide Updates
The **Crew & Captain Performance Guide** is a living document. If something in there doesn't match reality on the water, tell us, we'll change it. The standard you're held to is the one that's actually written down, not folklore.

## 7. Edge Cases & Pay Framework

Trips don't always go to plan. This section is the full pay table for the most common edge cases. Read it as part of Section 2.

| Scenario | Captain pay |
|---|---|
| Weather cancel ≥ 4 hrs before scheduled departure | $0 (no work, no pay) |
| Weather cancel < 4 hrs before (you're prepping or en route) | 50% of per-tour rate (commit fee) |
| Weather cancel after captain arrives at marina | Full per-tour rate |
| Weather hold mid-trip (had to turn back) | Full per-tour rate |
| Mechanical fail before departure | 50% of per-tour rate (commit fee) + reschedule priority |
| Mechanical fail mid-trip | Full per-tour rate |
| Guest no-show or under-booked group cancels after captain commits | Full per-tour rate |
| Captain calls trip aborted for safety (impaired guests, USCG, etc.) | Full per-tour rate |
| Trip cancelled by Company at the dock for non-weather reason | Full per-tour rate |
| Captain offered a shift, declines | $0 (no consequence) |
| Captain accepts shift, no-shows | $0 + strike per Section 8 |
| Trip ends early at guest request | Full per-tour rate |

**Shift swaps:** captains may swap an accepted shift directly with another qualified captain. Notify Jeffrey in writing (Slack DM is fine) before the swap takes effect so the manifest and FH assignment are updated.

**Late callouts:** if you accepted a shift and need to call out, the standard is at least 4 hours of notice unless an emergency makes that impossible. Less than 4 hours of notice without a real emergency counts as a strike per Section 8.

## 8. When Things Go Wrong

The coaching system is designed to fix small things before they become problems. We expect you to use it. We expect Jeffrey to use it. If a coaching conversation has happened and the same issue keeps repeating, then we're in a different conversation.

### 8.1 Strike System (Documented Coaching Failure)
Strikes apply only after a documented coaching conversation has happened on the same issue and the issue continues. They are tracked in the coaching log.

A strike is one of:
1. **Repeating an issue** that's already been coached and acknowledged in writing
2. **Skipping or falsifying** a required check-in, log, or report (including pre-trip or post-trip audio logs)
3. **Walking off-shift** without filing your end-of-day audio log
4. **Late callout** with less than 4 hours of notice and no real emergency
5. **No-call no-show** for an accepted shift

**Strike ladder:**
- **1st strike:** coaching note in the log + 30-day watch period
- **2nd strike:** mandatory ride-along + written warning
- **3rd strike:** contract review with termination on the table

Strike escalation routes through the shared David + Jeffrey manager DM channel. Any contract review involves both.

**What does not count as a strike:**
- Asking for help
- Flagging a problem honestly
- A bumpy day where something didn't go to plan but you logged it
- A first-time mistake on something we hadn't coached yet
- Refusing a shift offer

If you're honest, we'll figure it out together.

### 8.2 Immediate Termination (No Coaching, No Strikes)
The following are firing offenses with no progression. One time, contract ends:

- Operating a vessel under the influence of alcohol or drugs
- Allowing guests to operate the vessel
- Skipping the safety briefing
- Departing with insufficient PFDs
- Departing without all FareHarbor waivers signed and verified
- Falsifying an incident report or failing to report an incident
- Harassment or discrimination toward guests or crew
- Ignoring a weather hold or USCG advisory
- Theft from the Company, crew, or guests

## 9. Conduct & Brand Representation

When you're on shift, on Company property, or wearing Company gear, you represent NOLA Party Barge. Off shift on your personal time, you're your own person. Two narrow exceptions:

- **No public statements that materially harm the brand.** Posting harassment, discrimination, or threats against guests, crew, or the Company on any platform under your real name or while identifiable as NPB crew is grounds for immediate termination per Section 8.2.
- **No misrepresentation.** Don't represent yourself as a Company spokesperson or speak on the Company's behalf publicly without authorization.

Outside of those, what you say, post, and do off the boat is your business.
