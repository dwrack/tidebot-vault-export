```
Can you bring your own alcohol on a boat in New Orleans?
```
**A1:**
```
On a private BYOB party boat, yes. You bring whatever you want to drink, with no drink minimum and no marked-up bar. Ticketed cruises and steamboats are the opposite, you buy from their bar at their prices. If controlling your own drink budget matters, book a private BYOB boat instead of a per-seat cruise.
```

## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
The fastest way to wreck a boat party budget in New Orleans is to let somebody else run the bar. I've watched groups book a "party cruise," get on board excited, and then spend the next two hours doing mental math on $14 drinks while a stranger's bachelorette party blasts their own playlist three feet away. That's not a party. That's a tab with a view. A real BYOB boat party fixes all of it, and planning one is simpler than people think. Here's how.

## What BYOB actually buys you

BYOB sounds like a small detail. It isn't. It changes the whole shape of the day.

When you bring your own everything, **you control three things that the cruise companies usually control for you:** the drinks, the budget, and the vibe. You decide if it's champagne and seltzers or a cooler of beer. You decide the playlist instead of getting whatever the operator picked. And you decide how much to spend, because there's no marked-up bar deciding it for you.

The other half of it is privacy. On a private boat, it's your group only. Nobody else gets on. No drink minimum, no upsell, no "would you like to add bottle service." Just your people and the bayou.

## The real cost math

People assume private is automatically the expensive option. It usually isn't, once you actually run it.

Here's the math that surprises everyone:

- You pay **one flat private rate** for the whole boat
- You **split it across the group**, so per-person drops fast as the group grows
- You buy your **own drinks at grocery prices**, not bar prices
- There's no tab, no minimum, no surprise charge at the end

For a group of 15 to 20, that combination tends to land close to what the same crew would've spent on a normal night of overpriced cocktails in the Quarter. Except instead of standing in a crowd, you've got an entire boat and the open water.

## Pick your boat, pick your time

The boat mostly comes down to headcount. They each have their own feel:

- **Freaky Tiki** → 25 cap, the big-group workhorse
- **Twerkin Tiki** → 25 cap, the rowdy one
- **Party Queen** → 22 cap, smoother energy
- **Bayou Boogie** → 18 cap, right for a tighter crew

Each trip runs about **one hour and 45 minutes** on Bayou Bienvenue. That's not a quick 30-minute loop. It's enough time to settle in, get a few drinks deep, watch for gators and egrets, and actually have a moment. [internal link: the Freaky Tiki → /the-freaky-tiki/]

## The sunset slot is the one to grab

If you can pick your time, take the late-afternoon slot. I'll die on this hill.

The light over the bayou in the last two hours before sunset is the whole thing. Cypress trees go gold, the water flattens out, the sky does something ridiculous, and every photo your group takes looks like you hired someone. From late spring through fall, that golden hour is reliably gorgeous out here.

It's also the slot that books first, so don't sit on it. If you want to turn the sunset into the main event, there's a sunset cocktail cruise with a seafood boil that leans all the way into it. [internal link: sunset cocktail cruise with a seafood boil → /sunset-cocktail-cruise-seafood-boil/]

## Your BYOB packing list

Keep it lean. The crew handles the boat. You handle the party:

1. **Drinks** and a cooler with plenty of ice (more ice than you think, it's the South)
2. A **downloaded playlist** so it doesn't cut out when the signal drops on the water
3. **Sunscreen and sunglasses**, non-negotiable from June through September
4. A **phone strap or waterproof case**, because someone always nearly loses one overboard
5. **Snacks** if your trip's a long one, plus any decorations for the occasion
6. **Cash or a card** for the captain and deckhand tip, they earn it

Notice what's not on the list: a bartender, a DJ, or a tour script. You don't need them. That's the point.

## Three rules that keep it smooth

A few things I tell every group before they book:

- **Mind the distance.** Our marina is about seven miles from the French Quarter, a 15 to 20 minute Uber. A lot of boats are an hour out in Slidell or Lafitte. Don't lose half your day to a van ride. Check the launch spot first.
- **Cans and plastic over glass.** It's a boat. Glass and bare feet don't mix. Keep it easy on yourselves.
- **Let the crew do their job.** Every captain's licensed and every boat's USCG certified. They drive, they keep it safe, you relax. We're the stage crew, not the performers.

Want a little wildlife with your party? The bayou's got gators, turtles, and egrets, and they don't work for us, they just live here. [internal link: swamp tour with alligator sightings → /swamp-tour-with-alligator-sightings/]

## Book the boat, bring the rest

That's the whole playbook. Pick the boat that fits your group, grab the sunset slot before someone else does, hit the grocery store, and show up. No bar tab, no strangers, no hour-long drive. Just your people, your drinks, and the bayou doing its thing.

Bourbon Street will still be there when you get back. [internal link: see the full fleet → /boat-rental/]
```
