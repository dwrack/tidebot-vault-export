**Q1:**
```
How much does a bachelorette party boat in New Orleans cost?
```
**A1:**
```
You book the whole boat for a flat private rate, then split it across the group, so the per-person cost drops fast with more people. Because it is BYOB, you also skip the bar markups that wreck a budget on a bar crawl. A group of 15 to 20 usually finds it lands close to what they would have spent on overpriced Bourbon Street drinks anyway, except they get the whole boat to themselves.
```

**Q2:**
```
What is the best boat for a New Orleans bachelorette party?
```
**A2:**
```
It comes down to group size. Freaky Tiki and Twerkin Tiki both hold 25 and are the go-to for bigger bachelorette crews. Party Queen holds 22. Bayou Boogie holds 18 and fits a tighter group. All of them are private, so it is just your party, no strangers, your music and your drinks the whole time.
```

**Q3:**

```
Can you drink on a bachelorette boat in New Orleans?
```
**A3:**
```
Yes. The private party boats are BYOB, which means you bring whatever you want to drink. There is no drink minimum and no marked-up bar. Bring champagne, seltzers, a cooler, whatever the bride likes. The captain drives and the deckhand helps so the group can relax.
```

**Q4:**

```
How far is the bachelorette boat from the French Quarter?
```
**A4:**
```
About seven miles, roughly a 15 to 20 minute Uber. That matters for a bachelorette weekend that is already packed. Most swamp and party boats sit 45 minutes to an hour out of town, which burns a big chunk of the day in a van. Staying close to the Quarter keeps the schedule loose and the group happy.
```

```markdown
Every bachelorette weekend in New Orleans hits the same fork in the road. Somebody in the group chat suggests a bar crawl, somebody else sends a link to a "party cruise" that turns out to be four strangers' groups jammed on one boat, and the bride quietly hopes for something better. I run the boats, so I'm biased, but I've watched enough of these weekends to know exactly where they go sideways. Here's how to plan one that the bride actually remembers, instead of one she politely thanks you for.

## Why the bar crawl is the wrong move

A Bourbon Street bar crawl is the safe default, and that's the problem. Everybody's done it. You stand in a crowd, pay $80 across the night for watered-down hurricanes in plastic cups, and you go home with a hangover and zero photos worth keeping.

The bride flew people in from four different cities for this. A crowd of strangers and an $18 daiquiri isn't the story you want her telling at the rehearsal dinner.

Put the same group on a private boat instead. Your people, the bayou opening up on both sides, your music, your drinks, and that first moment when the boat pulls away from the dock and the whole group realizes the weekend just got good. That reaction is the entire point. A bar crawl can't manufacture it.

## Pick the boat by your group size

Don't overthink this part. The boat is mostly a function of how many people RSVP'd, and they each have their own personality:

- **Freaky Tiki** → 25 cap, the workhorse for a big bachelorette crew
- **Twerkin Tiki** → 25 cap, the one with attitude (the name does the talking)
- **Party Queen** → 22 cap, smooth and a little more grown-up
- **Bayou Boogie** → 18 cap, perfect for a tighter group that doesn't need 25 seats

Every one of them is **private**. That's the part that matters. Book it and it's your group only. No couple from Ohio making small talk while you're trying to do champagne toasts. [internal link: the Freaky Tiki → /the-freaky-tiki/]

## The BYOB math that wins the group chat

Here's the line that ends the bar-crawl debate in your group chat: **it's BYOB.**

You bring whatever you want to drink. No drink minimum. No $14 cocktails. No surprise bar tab at the end that somebody has to Venmo-chase for a week. A group hits the grocery store, grabs champagne and seltzers and a cooler of ice, and the drink budget is whatever you decide it is.

Run the numbers and it usually shocks people. A flat private boat rate split across 18 to 20 women, plus BYOB drinks at grocery prices, often lands close to what the same group would've burned on Bourbon Street anyway. Except now you've got the whole boat and a story.

## Build the day around the boat, not the other way around

The boat trip runs about **one hour and 45 minutes** on the water. Build the rest of the day around it instead of cramming it between two other things.

A schedule that actually works:

1. **Late morning** → brunch in the Quarter, the slow kind, no rush
2. **Early afternoon** → grocery run for the cooler, then a 15 minute Uber to the marina
3. **Afternoon** → the boat, the bayou, the photos, the bride losing it when the music drops
4. **Evening** → back in the Quarter by happy hour, riding the high, dinner reservation locked
5. **Night** → now you can do Bourbon Street if you want, on your terms, not as the whole plan

The boat anchors the day. Everything else flexes around it.

## What to actually bring

Keep the packing list short. The crew handles the boat. You handle the vibe:

- Drinks (champagne, seltzers, whatever the bride loves) and a cooler with ice
- A real playlist, downloaded so it doesn't cut out on the water
- Sunscreen and sunglasses, the bayou sun is no joke from June through September
- A sash, a banner, a champagne gun, whatever sells the moment
- A waterproof phone case or a strap, because someone always almost drops a phone overboard

That's it. Don't overpack. The boat does the heavy lifting.

## The mistake that ruins bachelorette weekends

I'll tell you the one I see wreck weekends more than weather: **the drive.**

A group books a swamp tour or a party boat that's an hour out in Slidell or Lafitte, not realizing it's an hour each way. Now the bride is sitting in a van on I-10 for two hours of her own bachelorette. The energy dies in traffic. People get cranky. The whole afternoon's gone.

Our marina sits about **seven miles from the French Quarter.** That's a 15 to 20 minute Uber, not a road trip. When you compare boats, look at the launch location before you look at anything else. A great boat an hour away is worse than a good boat 15 minutes away, every single time on a packed weekend.
```
