# Bayou Weatherman — Episode 1: The Party Forecast
*Weekly host segment. Ties to weather/seasonal search AND drives this-weekend bookings. Same crew member every week = the face people subscribe to. 30-60 sec, vertical. Film Thursday, post Thursday night for the weekend.*

## The character
"Stormin'" [pick a crew member, give him a name] is the most dramatic weatherman on the Gulf Coast. Suit jacket over a tank top, maybe a clip-on tie, sunglasses. Full local-news energy, big gestures, points at the sky like there's a map there. He delivers the real weekend weather wrapped in a "party forecast." Total commitment to the bit is the whole gag.

## What to shoot (crew, every Thursday)
- One take, crew member on the dock, boats behind him, phone vertical and steady (prop it on a cooler).
- Have him actually point at the sky/water like a weather map. Re-do it til the energy's high.
- B-roll to cut in: a boat leaving the dock, a cooler of drinks, a sunset. 3-4 sec each.

## Script template (swap the real forecast each week)

> (big news-anchor voice)
> "GOOD evening New Orleans, I'm Stormin' [Name] with your BAYOU PARTY FORECAST.
> [points at sky]
> Saturday: sunny, 84 degrees. A 100% chance of champagne showers.
> Scattered twerking after 3pm. [cut to boat clip]
> A HIGH pressure system of pure vibes movin' in off the Gulf.
> Sunday, mild, perfect for a swamp cruise, gator activity LIGHT to MODERATE.
> [leans in]
> Bottom line, New Orleans: it is a beautiful weekend to be on the water.
> Boats are bookin' up. Don't get left on dry land.
> Back to you, uh... me."

## Why this works
"New Orleans weather" is a real search term (it's in the GSC data). Weather is the #1 thing that decides whether someone books a boat this weekend, so this segment literally converts a weather check into a booking. Weekly cadence = the subscribe + posting rhythm handled by one recurring bit. And it's the cheapest possible shoot: one person, one phone, one minute, on the dock.
