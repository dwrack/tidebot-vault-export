# LinkedIn Corporate Outreach: Playbook

Started 2026-10-12. Sender: Michael's LinkedIn, run by Michael's Claude on his Mac (changed 2026-10-22; David's name stays off it). No always-on job yet: each weekday Michael's Claude finds, drafts and tracks, and Michael sends. Start with `Michael Handoff (2026-10-22).md`.

This file is the brain. The daily job reads it before it writes anything, so edit here to change how the drafts sound.

## The one rule

Every message has to be useful to the reader even if they never book. If a draft only makes sense as a sales pitch, it doesn't go out.

## How it runs

1. **Find:** 5 new people a weekday, rotating through the segments below. Duplicates and out-of-market people get dropped.
2. **Draft:** Claude reads each profile and picks a segment and a "give" sized to their company. It writes a connection note (200 characters max, no pitch, no link) and a follow-up for after they accept.
3. **Show:** Michael's Claude lists the 5 in the session with every draft ready to copy.
4. **Send:** Michael sends from his phone and tells his Claude, so it can track:
   - ✅ = connection request sent
   - 🤝 = they accepted (the follow-up draft shows up 2 days later)
   - 💬 = they replied (the engine stops nudging, the conversation is yours now)
   - ❌ = skip this person forever
5. **Report:** every Friday, Michael's Claude gives David a one-line scorecard (sent, accepted, replied, booked) and logs it in `Tracker.csv`.

Nothing ever sends automatically. LinkedIn restricts accounts that automate sending, and a bot voice is exactly what we're avoiding.

## Hard limits

- 5 new requests a day, 25 a week. LinkedIn starts flagging around 100 a week.
- Maximum 2 messages without a reply, then stop. No "just bumping this."
- No links in the connection note. In the follow-up, send one link at most, and only to something useful.
- Never name past guests or say who booked. "A few folks from your team have been out with us" is fine. Names are not.
- Business name is always "NOLA Party Barge" in full.
- Never mention pedaling.

## Segments and what we give each one

The size of the company decides the give. A 12-person law firm and a 3,000-person convention want completely different things.

### 1. Connectors: DMCs, hotel group sales, conference services, concierges
They book other people's groups, all year long. One good relationship is worth 20 cold leads.
- **Give:** make their job easier. Quotes in under 2 hours, holds on convention weeks before the client commits, and a one-page partner sheet they can drop straight into proposals.
- **Bigger give:** a free sunset fam trip for their sales team (David approves each one).
- **Angle:** "When a planner asks you for something off the Bourbon Street circuit, we're an easy yes."

### 2. Small local teams, 2-25 people (fits one boat)
Office managers, EAs, founders, HR at local firms: law, real estate, agencies, construction, medical practices.
- **Give:** the math. A private boat for 25 people works out to about $48 a head. That's less than a bar tab, nobody has to drive between bars, and the whole team is in one place.
- **Ideas to offer:** a weekday 4pm team wind-down, a client-appreciation ride (big with law and real estate), a new-hire welcome, closing a deal or hitting a quarter.
- **Timing hook:** right now (Oct-Dec) the hook is the holiday party that isn't another restaurant back room.

### 3. Mid-size local companies, 25-100 people (2-4 boats)
- **Give:** a full-team outing that doesn't feel corporate. Boats run together, with a friendly department-vs-department gator count.
- **Ideas:** a holiday party flotilla, a spring kickoff, a summer Friday. Add the photo package so HR gets content for recruiting posts.
- **Angle:** one booking, one invoice, and nobody gets lost in the Quarter.

### 4. Out-of-town conventions and incentive groups, 100+
Corporate meeting planners whose groups are coming to New Orleans. Most of the time we reach them through Segment 1.
- **Give:** a convention-week heads-up. What tends to sell out the week they're in town, how to stagger departures so 100+ people can rotate through, and the transport from the CBD.
- **Angle:** the dine-around alternative. Two hours on the bayou is the night people talk about on the flight home.

### 5. Nonprofits, schools, healthcare
A lot of past guests come from here (schools, Ochsner, Teach For America).
- **Give:** a staff or volunteer appreciation ride, a board thank-you, or a donated ride for a fundraiser auction (David approves donations).
- **Angle:** their people work hard and get thanked with pizza. This is better.

### 6. Energy, industrial, safety-first cultures (Shell, Entergy, PBF and similar)
- **Lead with safety:** licensed captains, Coast Guard limits, a safety briefing on every departure. Their HR and safety people care about this before anything else.

## Personalization signals, best first

1. **Past-guest company.** Their company shows up in our past-guest list ("a few folks from your team have been out with us").
2. **Something they actually posted** in the last 30 days.
3. **New role** in the last 90 days ("congrats on the move to X").
4. **Company news**: a convention coming to town, an expansion, an award.
5. **Their headline or about section.** Use one specific detail and never flatter.

If there's no real hook, the note stays short and plain. A fake compliment is worse than none.

## Voice

The drafts should sound like Michael texting someone he respects, in his own voice and not a template:
- Short, with contractions.
- No "I hope this finds you well," no "I'd love to pick your brain," no "synergy," no em dashes.
- One specific detail about them and one useful thing from us. Asks stay small: "worth a quick chat?" or "want the sheet?"

## Open decisions (David)

- Do DMCs and hotels get a commission or net rate, and how much? Drafts say "partner rates" until this is set.
- Fam trips: how many a month, and which boat or slot?
- Fundraiser donations: yes or no, and how many a year?
