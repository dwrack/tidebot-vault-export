## Track A. Tales of the Cocktail 2027 sponsorship

**Dates.** Tales 2026 ran July 19 to 24. 2027 isn't announced yet; assume the third or fourth week of July 2027. Tales 2026 had 300+ brand events (tasting rooms, happy hours, guest shifts, headline evening events). Every one of those needed a venue. That's the actual product we're selling.

**Reframe the ask.** "Sponsor our marina" is a hard sell. "Host your Tales 2027 brand event on the only venue in New Orleans that's on the water" is an easy one. Brands already have a Tales activation budget line. We're competing with the Ritz ballroom and a rented Warehouse District loft, not asking for new money.

**Unique positioning statement (pick one):**

1. *The only Tales venue that floats.* 300 brand events in a week and every one of them is in a hotel or a bar. Yours is on a bayou with alligators, a sunset and a band on the sand.
2. *Take the industry out of the Quarter for one night.* 15 minutes from the Ritz. A marina, covered boats, live music, your bottles behind every bar.
3. *Where the bartenders go after their shift.* Not another tasting room. A place they'd come to on their own.

Recommendation: #1 for the deck headline, #3 for the pitch to brand ambassadors (they're the ones who have to convince HQ).

**Package.**

| | Title ($50k, one brand) | Presenting ($20k, one brand, different category) |
|---|---|---|
| Exclusivity | Category exclusive across the whole week (only whiskey, or only rum, etc.) | Category exclusive in its category |
| Naming | "[Brand] presents NPB at Tales" on all boats, marina, socials, crew shirts | Named on one signature night + all boats |
| Product | Supplies product; we sell it, we keep the margin (needs the ATC/TTB licensing track to be live by July 2027, see Alcohol Licensing project) | Same, their category only |
| Events | Two nights: one industry-only (bartenders, media), one ticketed public | One night, co-hosted |
| Talent | Sponsor money funds the band/act + promotion; we handle production | Contributes to the act |
| Content | Walkthrough, boat cam, recap reel, crew posts all week | Recap reel + boat cam |
| Guest shifts | Their bartender/ambassador runs the boat bar (Tales "guest shift" format they already know) | One guest shift |

Where the money goes: roughly 40% act + production, 30% promotion (Meta, local press, bartender invite list), 30% margin. Say that out loud in the deck. Brands trust a budget they can see.

**Target brands.** Filter was: bartender-approved, craft identity, real budget, ideally thematic (rum/tequila/pirate/tiki). Not Jack Daniels. Note Brown-Forman owns Old Forester AND Jack; the Old Forester contact is the right door into Brown-Forman, and Brown-Forman is already a Tales Spirited Awards sponsor.

**How to work it.** One connector per event, contacted 4 to 6 months before the date. The ask is always the same shape: "We have a waterfront marina and boats. If you organized, promoted and hosted a night here for [event], you'd keep [door split / bar split / a fee]. We supply the venue, boats, crew and content. If it works, it's yours every year." That's a partnership offer, not a solicitation, and it's why it lands where cold email doesn't.

**Bench.** Keep a short list of "plug" people (DJs, bartenders, band leaders, krewe people) who can also crew. Tag them in the Staff Roster with what scene they bring. Charles is the prototype, minus the punctuality.

## Track D. Show the space (this week, feeds everything above)

JT's read is right: the Instagram doesn't give a feel for the marina. Wharf-era walkthroughs were some of the best-performing videos. Fixes, cheapest first:

1. **One 60-second walkthrough, phone, no edit.** Dock to sand to indoor room to fire pit to boat. Post it. Re-shoot monthly as the grass and mural come in.
2. **"Get ready with me" from the crew.** Hand the phone to Sophia or a deckhand: "walk me through the space like you're showing a friend." Nobody has to be good on camera. Three of these a week.
3. **Progress posts.** New mural, grass seed taking, drainage fixed. "What we're working on today." This is the content that makes people feel ownership before they've been.
4. **The crew 1-on-1s as content.** The Fri 9/11 round is already scripted and recorded (`Operations/Crew 1-on-1 Script`). Ask each person on camera at the end: "show me your favorite spot here." 20 seconds, that's a post. Note: the 1-on-1 recordings themselves are internal and have a "keep/coach/cut" note attached. Public clips need the crew member's ok and should be shot separately, not cut from the review recording.
5. **Heat is real.** If the space looks rough midday, shoot at 7am or golden hour only until October.

Everything from #1 and #2 becomes the sizzle reel for Track A and the LinkedIn posts for Track B. That's why this is "this week."
