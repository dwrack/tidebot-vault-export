CONSENT / PRODUCTION FLAG — READ BEFORE ANY FOOTAGE IS PLANNED:
This piece scripts a private, personal toast. `SOPs/Deckhand — Social Capture Playbook.md`'s Consent section explicitly names this exact category of moment as one that should stay private: "Don't film bachelorette / private group moments that obviously feel like they should stay private (private toasts, breakdowns, etc.)." That rule applies here without exception.

This means:
- Do NOT point a camera at an actual guest during a real private toast to capture their face or their words.
- Do NOT record the audio of what they actually say.
- Every shot in the list above is written to be non-identifying (hands, backs, silhouettes, water, the guide's hand on the throttle) specifically so this piece can be built without violating that rule.
- If a real moment like this happens on an actual tour, the right move is for the guide to check in with the guest afterward and ask if they'd be comfortable with even non-identifying footage being used — not to film in the moment and ask forgiveness later.
- The safer default, and the one this note recommends: shoot this piece as a recreation, with a crew member standing in for the "guest" in the non-identifying shots (hand on a flask, back turned, etc.). That removes any real consent risk entirely while keeping the piece exactly as scripted. Flag this choice for David in the main slate doc's Next Steps rather than defaulting to "capture it if it happens."
