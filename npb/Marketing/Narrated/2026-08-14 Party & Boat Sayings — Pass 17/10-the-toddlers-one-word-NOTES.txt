PIECE 10 — Funny / heartfelt lane — "The Toddler's One Word"
Voice: EL-Jessica (ElevenLabs)   Music: Jon Cleary-style solo piano, warm (clearance unconfirmed)   Length: 20.5s
Product: Naturalist eco swamp tour

SCRIPT (timed to narration):
 0.0s  She's two. Has exactly one word for everything on this tour.     [toddler in a lap on the boat, wide-eyed, pointing]
 3.0s  Gator.                                                           [toddler's line, "gator," on-screen text overlay]
 4.6s  That's a heron.                                                  [close-up: heron standing nearby, toddler still pointing and saying "gator"]
 6.6s  Gator.                                                           [toddler repeats, undeterred]
 8.0s  That's a cypress knee.                                           [close-up: cypress knee in the water]
 9.8s  Gator.                                                           [toddler again, guide chuckling]
11.2s  Actual gator finally shows up. She's ready for it.               [real alligator surfacing nearby, toddler pointing excitedly]
14.2s  "Gator." Same tone as the heron. Same tone as the tree root.      [toddler's calm, satisfied delivery, parents laughing]
18.0s  She wasn't wrong about the world. Just early.                    [closing wide shot, family laughing, golden light]

On-screen text: quote captions on "Gator" each time it's said, small and consistent — becomes a visual gag itself.
Shot list: toddler wide-eyed, pointing → "gator" caption on heron → heron close-up → "gator" again → cypress knee close-up → "gator" again, guide chuckling → real gator surfacing → toddler's calm delivery, parents laughing → closing wide family shot.
Casting/production note: needs a real toddler guest and a patient, consenting parent — this only works candid, a scripted kid reads false immediately. Flag in #crew-content for a genuine version to get caught rather than staged, same standard as Pass 15's "Question That Stumped the Guide." Per the Social Capture Playbook's consent section, keep clips general/candid rather than tight close-ups on a child's face unless a parent has explicitly said it's fine.
