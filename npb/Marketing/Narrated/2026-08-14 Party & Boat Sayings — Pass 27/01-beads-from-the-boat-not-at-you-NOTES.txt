COPY PASSES: Drafted, then routed through /copy-mentors (channeling Sugarman's slippery slide for the curiosity-loaded opener, "one extra thing," resolved two lines later; Ogilvy's specific-over-adjective instinct on "batture," the real Louisiana term for the land between the levee and the water, which does more work than a generic word like "riverbank" could). Then /copywriting-2.0 (3-test filter): the original line 3 claimed the float lasts "two hours," which is wrong (the real cruise runs 1 hour 45 minutes per `Brand Story & Values.md`) and therefore fails the falsify test for the wrong reason, a specific claim that's specifically incorrect; cut and rewritten to "for the length of the cruise," which is accurate and still concrete.

PRODUCTION FLAG: This is the highest-risk concept in this pass, and it needs more than a content sign-off before it moves forward. Throwing objects from a moving boat toward people on the riverbank is a different risk category than anything used in any of this slate's prior 120 pieces: line-of-sight to bystanders, projectile risk, other river traffic, and possible permitting or insurance questions. Do not treat this as shoot-ready until ops and insurance have reviewed whether and how it could run safely (reduced speed, controlled distance, soft throws only, or possibly restricted to a specific stretch of the route). If that review comes back negative, this piece should be shelved rather than reworked, since the premise itself (beads thrown from the boat) is the whole idea.
