# NPB — Title + Meta Rewrites for the 17 High-Impression Pages

**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Target:** 182,987 impressions currently converting at 0.47%. At 2.5% these pages produce ~4,570 clicks/quarter instead of 854.
**Source:** `SEO-AEO-GEO Deep Dive — nolapartybarges.com (Aug 2026).md` §1

---

## The Real Problem (found while drafting these)

**Eight of the seventeen pages have no written meta description at all.** The field is being auto-filled with the first ~200 characters of body copy, so it trails off mid-sentence in the SERP. Examples of what Google is currently showing:

> "When December rolls around, the lively city of New Orleans undergoes a charming transformation. The air is crisp, and the festive spirit is palpable. But as you plan your"

> "Mardi Gras season is one of the wildest and fun times of the year in New Orleans as well as the United States, and 2023 is looking to be even more exciting than usual. Th"

> "The Intersection of Style and Culture New Orleans, a city pulsating with life and vibrant energy, is renowned for its unique cultural tapestry. Among the many aspects tha"

Another **three** use the site tagline as their meta — *"New Orleans Pedal Party Boat Booze Cruise & Tours"* — duplicated across pages and off-brand.

That is the CTR problem in one sentence. It isn't rankings. It's that eleven of your seventeen biggest pages have no sales pitch in the search result.

Also worth flagging: **`/krewe-of-endymion/` still says "2023"** in both its title and H1. Three Mardi Gras seasons stale on the page pulling 14,440 impressions.

---

## How These Are Written

- Titles under 60 characters so Google doesn't truncate them
- Metas 130-155 characters
- Plain ASCII only. No em dashes, no curly quotes, no ampersand entities
- Brand suffix dropped from most titles to save characters. The Site Title change handles brand attribution
- For the informational pages, the hook is something the AI Overview cannot give: a judgment, a specific number, a local opinion. Ranking above an AI answer is not enough anymore. The title has to promise what the summary won't contain
