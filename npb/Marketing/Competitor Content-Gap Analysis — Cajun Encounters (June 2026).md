# Competitor Content-Gap Analysis — Cajun Encounters (June 2026)

Ran the content-gap technique against Cajun Encounters (David's chosen competitor for this pass). Pedaling deliberately left out as a feature (no-pedal rule).

## The competitor: Cajun Encounters (cajunencounters.com)

Big, established New Orleans operator: Honey Island swamp tours, plantation tours, city bus tours, ghost bus tours, distillery + combo packages. Claims "#1 rated" with 61,000+ Google reviews. They cover wildlife/ecosystem well (alligators, egrets, cypress, the "Whiskey Tree"), captain credibility, tour times/durations, French Quarter hotel pickup, family-friendliness, and heavy social proof. They have a group-tours page naming bachelor/bachelorette parties and a Cajun Pavilion seafood boil.

## The gap

Three structural openings NPB can own:

1. **Alcohol.** Their own policy: "Alcohol is NOT permitted on the boats or public transport buses." To host a bachelorette you book a private boat and negotiate "ground rules," and staff can revoke drinking privileges. NPB's whole identity is BYOB on a private barge. Biggest wedge: Cajun Encounters structurally cannot market "bring your own drinks and party."
2. **Built for tourists, not celebrations.** They herd 22 strangers onto a flat-bottom boat (or 10-12 "VIP," ages 13+) and drive everyone 45+ minutes to Slidell. No content about planning YOUR group's day, intimate small-boat vibe, your own playlist, a celebration that's actually yours. Their group page names no pricing, no duration, no party specifics.
3. **Their FAQ is a ghost town.** The FAQ lists the exact questions people search (what to wear, mosquitoes, kids, best time for gators, food/drink policy, restrooms, private tours) and serves no answers on the page. Every one is an unanswered-question SEO layup.

## 10 content ideas to own

1. **BYOB Bachelorette Party Boats in New Orleans: The Complete Planning Guide** — "New Orleans bachelorette boat / BYOB party boat NOLA." They ban alcohol on standard boats and treat parties as a negotiated exception.
2. **Can You Drink on a New Orleans Swamp Tour? (What Every Operator's Alcohol Policy Actually Means)** — "can you bring beer on a swamp tour." Their honest answer is "no," so they'd talk themselves out of the sale; NPB answers "yes, on ours."
3. **Private Party Barge vs Big Swamp Tour Boat: Which New Orleans Outing Fits Your Group?** — "best New Orleans tour for a group of 12." An honest comparison spotlights their 22-stranger shared boats and the long drive, exactly what NPB beats.
4. **Best Months to See Alligators in the New Orleans Bayou (and When to Skip the Swamp)** — "alligator season Louisiana." Their FAQ lists the question, publishes no answer.
5. **What to Bring on a New Orleans Party Barge or Swamp Outing: The Real Packing List** — "what to bring/wear swamp tour New Orleans." FAQ poses these with no answers; easy to outrank.
6. **Birthday Party on the Bayou: Planning a Private Boat Celebration in New Orleans** — "birthday boat rental New Orleans." Their group page names bachelorette/bachelor but says nothing about birthdays.
7. **Sober and Family-Friendly Bayou Outings in New Orleans: A Party Without the Pressure** — "family swamp tour / non-drinking group activity." NPB owns it by showing the same private boat flexes BOTH ways (BYOB or dry, family or adults), which a fixed-policy operator can't message.
8. **How Far Is the Swamp from the French Quarter? Drive Times, Docks, and Pickup, Explained** — "how far is Honey Island swamp from New Orleans." Their tours launch from Slidell (55+ min) and they bury it; NPB wins on closer dock logistics.
9. **The Party + Swamp Combo: How to Do a Celebration AND See Gators in One New Orleans Day** — "combine party and swamp tour." Their combos are swamp + plantation bus, the opposite of a private party-plus-eco outing only NPB's hybrid delivers.
10. **Small-Boat vs Big-Operator Swamp Tours in New Orleans: Why Group Size Changes Everything** — "best small group swamp tour New Orleans." Their volume model (22-passenger boats, hotel batching, 4 daily departures) is the thing this critiques.

## Build first

The whole set rests on the alcohol / private-celebration wedge, defensible because it's tied to NPB's actual product, not just copy. Lead with #1, #2, #3: highest commercial intent and the cleanest "competitor literally cannot say this" moat. #4 and #5 are quick-win SEO plays against their empty FAQ; ship those fast and cheap.
