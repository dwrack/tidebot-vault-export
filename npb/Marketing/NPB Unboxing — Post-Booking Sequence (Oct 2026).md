# NPB Unboxing: the sequence between "paid" and "boarded"

*Written 2026-10-13 (David). Companion to [[NPB Lead Capture + Drip Plan (Oct 2026)]] (that one is pre-booking, this one starts at the FareHarbor confirmation). Add-on prices come from [[NPB Add-On Menu]]. Status: concept + copy brief. Nothing built, nothing sending.*

## The one-line version

Right now a guest pays, gets a FareHarbor receipt, and hears nothing until the dock. The unboxing turns that dead window into 5-6 touches that (a) make the trip feel bigger than a boat ride, (b) ask 4-6 tap-to-answer questions so we know who is coming and why, and (c) sell them the rest of their weekend: our add-ons, our swamp tour, the bike bar, costumes and gear through an Amazon page, and partner spots. Every touch is keyed to the answers.

## Why it works for us specifically

- They have already said yes. Post-purchase is the highest-trust moment we get. Add-on attach rates in tour booking land 15-30% when the pitch lands after the sale, not in the cart.
- Our booking window is short (most bookings are now in-week, see [[Booking Lead Time Shift]]). So the sequence has to collapse to 2-3 touches when the trip is under 7 days out. Design for that first, long-lead is the bonus.
- Tiki seats are 74% of revenue and the group is almost always an occasion (bach, birthday, cousins, work trip). Occasion is the single most valuable fact we can collect, and FareHarbor doesn't ask.
- Costumed groups = Jeff's social content (already the logic behind add-on #51). The unboxing is where we get them to commit to a theme before they arrive.

## The questions (tap to answer, never a form)

Each question is a row of 3-5 buttons in the email or a "reply 1-4" in SMS. One question per touch, max two. Every answer writes to the lead record and routes the next offer.

| # | Question | Buttons | What it unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | What's the occasion? | Bachelor/ette · Birthday · Cousin/family trip · Work · Just because | Occasion kit (sash/veil kit, captain shoutout, cake to the boat), content angle for crew |
| Q2 | First time in New Orleans? | First time · Been once or twice · Basically a local | First-timers get the full "rest of your weekend" push; locals get the swamp tour + partner spots only |
| Q3 | Where are you staying? | French Quarter · CBD/Warehouse · Marigny/Bywater · Uptown · Airbnb somewhere else · Not booked yet | Shuttle seat add-on ($15 pp) if FQ/CBD, parking note if driving, hotel partner referral if not booked yet |
| Q4 | How long are you in town? | Just the day · Weekend · 3-4 days · A week+ | 2+ days = swamp tour + bike bar cross-sell; day-only = add-ons only |
| Q5 | What's the vibe? | Chill sunset · Full send · Theme party · Mix | Theme = costume bin + Amazon costume page; full send = playlist takeover + glow pack + hangover kit |
| Q6 | Who's the guest of honor and what do they love? (free text, optional) | text box | Captain shoutout script, chalkboard name, crew knows who to hype |

Keep Q1 and Q4 in the first email. They drive the most money. The rest ride on later touches.

Timing is relative to trip date (T). If the booking lands inside the window, skip the earlier touches and send the next one that still fits, at least 4 hours apart.

## The Amazon piece (costumes, etc.)

Yes, do it, but not the way most people would.

- **Amazon Associates bans affiliate links in email and SMS.** Links are only allowed on a site or app we control. So the drip never links to Amazon directly. It links to a page on nolapartybarges.com: `/pack` (the packing list) and `/costumes` (the costume shop). Those pages hold the Associates links and the required disclosure line.
- Commission is 3-4% on costumes and apparel. On a $40 pirate kit that's about $1.50. The money is not the point. The point is (1) they show up in costume, which is our content, (2) a reason to open touch 3, and (3) once the page exists it ranks for "what to wear on a New Orleans boat party," which is lead-gen.
- Build it as Amazon Idea Lists under one NPB storefront so a page is one link per theme: Pirate, Tiki, Mardi Gras, 70s Disco, Cowboy (matches the costume bins, so rent-vs-buy is the same five themes). Add non-costume gear: waterproof phone pouch, polarized shades, bluetooth speaker (no, we have the sound system, cut it), sunscreen stick, foldable cooler, bachelorette sash sets, LED glow packs, cheap ponchos.
- Pair every theme with the rent option: "Don't want to pack it? Rent the Pirate bin at the dock, $45 for the whole group." Rental money beats commission every time, so the bin is always the first button.
- Disclosure line on the page, once: "Some links on this page are Amazon affiliate links. We make a small cut, you pay the same."

## Making it an actual unboxing, not a drip

The drip is the mechanism. These are what make it feel like opening a box:

- **The Boarding Pass.** Touch 1 attaches a one-page PDF or image: group name (from Q6 or the booking name), boat, date, slot, captain's first name, a QR to the trip page. People screenshot it and post it. That is the unboxing visual.
- **A captain intro clip.** 15 seconds, phone-shot, "I'm Max, I've got you Saturday at 4." Rotate 3-4 of them. Jeff can shoot all of them in one afternoon.
- **The trip page.** One mobile page per booking (`/trip/<code>`, same pattern as the review link) that holds: countdown, boarding pass, logistics, the add-on buttons, the packing list, the playlist request box, the swamp tour button. Every email links to it. It is also where a guest who lost the emails ends up.
- **Group-share nudge.** "Forward this to the crew" with a share link to the trip page. One booker, 10 guests, and right now we only have the booker's email. The trip page captures the other nine (name + phone for day-of texts). That is the list-building play hiding inside the unboxing.
- **Day-of hype text** the morning of: weather, "the gators are out," captain's name. Short, human, from 6046.
- **Optional physical piece** for private charters only: a koozie or sticker pack mailed to the booker if the trip is 10+ days out. Cheap, memorable, skip for public seats.

## Numbers to beat (rough, so we know if it worked)

Per 100 public-seat bookings (avg ~4.5 pax, $59):
- Add-on attach 20% at ~$25 avg group = $500
- Costume bin 8% at $45 = $360
- Swamp tour cross-sell 5% of groups, 4 pax at ~$65 = $1,300 (biggest line, and it's a sister brand)
- Amazon commission: ~$30, ignore it
- Guest emails captured via trip page share: ~150 new contacts

Call it ~$2,100 per 100 bookings plus the list. Track three things only: answer rate on Q1, add-on attach rate, swamp tour code redemptions.
