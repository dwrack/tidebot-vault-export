12 new narrated short-form pieces (14-17.5 seconds each) built around party sayings, boat sayings, and the things people actually say out loud on the water: toasts, captain lines, running jokes, and group banter, split across NPB's two on-water products, per the brief. Format is modeled exactly on the Admire NOLA narrated series (`Admire NOLA/Content/Narrated/01-poetic-NOTES.txt` and `Admire NOLA/Content/Script & Caption Bank.md`): timestamped VO lines with a bracketed visual on every line, a named voice sample, a music bed, on-screen text treatment, and a shot list.

Split across NPB's two on-water products, consistent with every prior pass's two-product framing (the vault's third product, the per-seat social boat, has never been part of this slate series and isn't introduced here either):
- **Private BYOB party fleet** — pieces 01-06
- **Naturalist eco swamp tour** — pieces 07-12

```
 0.0s  A corporate group boards like corporate groups always do. A little stiff, half of them still on their phones.   [wide shot: group boarding in business-casual, several checking phones]
 3.0s  Then somebody quietly points out who actually planned the whole thing.   [close-up: guests glancing toward one person, an admin or manager]
 5.5s  The captain doesn't make a speech. Just leans in and says it plain: "Your team showed up for you. That's not nothing."   [close-up: captain speaking to a manager, no fanfare]
10.0s  Nobody claps. The boss just looks around the boat a second too long.   [wide shot: boss glancing at the team, quiet]
13.0s  Ten minutes later, phones are away and somebody's already dancing.   [closing wide shot: group loosened up, dancing, phones down]
```

**On-screen text:** none. The captain's line is the whole piece; text on top of it would undercut the delivery.
**Shot list:** wide shot, group boarding, phones out → close-up, guests glancing toward the organizer → close-up, captain speaking to the manager → wide shot, boss looking around the boat → closing wide shot, group dancing, phones away.
**Copy passes:** channeling Halbert's plain, unadorned honesty (the line does its own work, no need to explain it) with Ogilvy's specificity on what surrounds it, phones out at boarding, phones away by the close, makes the shift visualizable instead of asserted. Copywriting-2.0 cut an early draft's line 5, "and just like that, the boat did its job," flagged as a self-congratulatory brand statement rather than an observed beat, and replaced it with the concrete "ten minutes later, phones are away and somebody's already dancing."
