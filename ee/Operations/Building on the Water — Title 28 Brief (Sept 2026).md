# Building on the Water: Portland Title 28 Brief (Sept 2026)

Built 2026-09-30 from the Oslo offsite follow-up. Source: Portland City Code **Title 28, Floating Structures** (chapters 28.01-28.09, read on portland.gov today) plus zoning **Chapter 33.236**. Related: `The Rose/Research/research_structure.md` (Rose-specific float, stability and engineer shortlist).

> Internal. Holman items are off the record.

---

## The short version

1. **Title 28 covers every floating thing we want to build.** Our marina (173 NE Bridgeton Rd) is inside Portland city limits. The V2 privates, the Airstream on a float, slip cabanas, the marina Rose, practitioner rooms on a float and a food float all count as a "floating structure": a structure on a float, held by piling or moorage (28.02.020.AA).
2. **Every engineered float needs an Oregon-registered Engineer of Record** who stamps the drawings plus the flotation and stability calcs (28.06.040.A). This is the main cost and lead time for each new unit.
3. **Separation distance is the rule that shapes the site plan.** Commercial floating structures need **20 ft wall to wall** (10 ft with 1-hour fire-rated walls, 6 ft if fully sprinklered with no openings) (28.06.050). It decides how many cabanas fit, where the Airstream can go, and how the marina Rose sits next to the boat.
4. **Zoning decides whether a use is allowed at all.** Any use on a float has to be allowed on the **upland lot it's tied to** (33.236.020). **The marina is CE (Commercial Employment), so every use we listed is allowed** (see below). The environmental overlay ("c") is the one to watch for new floats.
5. **The big one for Holman:** Holman is on the **Willamette**. The Greenway rules **prohibit floating structures that aren't river-dependent or river-related**, and they name restaurants and bars as examples, unless we get a Greenway goal exception (33.236.015). A sauna is arguable. A food bar is not. The **marina is on the Columbia side, so this rule doesn't apply there.** That's another reason to build the Rose at the marina first.

## The marina's zone (looked up 2026-09-30, PortlandMaps)

**173 NE Bridgeton Rd = `CEchx(MU-N)`**: **CE, Commercial Employment**, comp plan Mixed Use-Neighborhood, inside Portland city limits.

**What CE allows (Table 130-1), and since floats take the upland lot's uses (33.236.020), the same list applies on the water:**

| Our idea | Use category | CE |
|---|---|---|
| Saunas, V2, Airstream, marina Rose | Retail Sales And Service (or Commercial Outdoor Recreation) | **Allowed** |
| Food: oyster/ceviche, juice, focaccia, soup | Retail Sales And Service | **Allowed**, no size cap in CE |
| Massage, therapists, practitioners | Retail Sales And Service / Office | **Allowed** |
| IV hydration | Retail Sales And Service / Office (Medical Centers is also Y) | **Allowed** |
| Slip cabanas (rented) | Retail Sales And Service / Commercial Outdoor Recreation | **Allowed** |
| Big events (festival-scale) | Major Event Entertainment | Conditional use (review needed) |
| Houseboats / overnight living | Household Living | Allowed in CE, but the noise overlay adds rules |

**The 3 overlays:**
- **c = Environmental Conservation (33.430).** Applies to new *development*, grading and vegetation removal in the mapped resource area. Rebuilding existing structures on the same footprint is exempt (33.430.080.D.4), so the **social sauna rebuild is fine**. **New floats, piling, gangways and shoreline work** (Airstream float, cabanas, marina Rose) likely need to meet the environmental standards or go through Environmental Review. Ask about this at the BDS early-assistance meeting.
- **h = Aircraft Landing.** Height limits near PDX. Only matters for tall pieces, like the Rose crown/DJ booth.
- **x = PDX Airport Noise Impact (33.470).** Only regulates **residential** uses (insulation, noise easements). Doesn't touch commercial.

**Bottom line:** everything on the list is an allowed use at the marina. The real work is Title 28 (engineer, separation) plus the environmental overlay for anything new in the water.

## Key numbers (what the engineer and Grant design to)

| Rule | Requirement | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Freeboard, occupied floor | **1 ft 8 in** minimum, waterline to finished floor | 28.06.040.E.2 |
| Freeboard, walkways | **1 ft** minimum | 28.06.040.E.2 |
| Max list (tilt) | **4.0°**, or clearance at max list ≥ 1/3 of normal, whichever is stricter. Resisting moment ≥ applied moment | 28.06.040.E.4 |
| Off-center load for the stability check | 100 lb/lineal ft (first floor). Floors over 20 ft wide: 5 lb × width per ft | 28.06.040.E.4 |
| Float live load | Commercial = building-code loads for the occupancy (a sauna is assembly, so heavier than the 40 psf floating-home number) | 28.06.040.E.3 |
| Design current | **1.5 knots** minimum, plus max wave/wake at the site, impact and earthquake | 28.06.040.B |
| Piling height | Top at least **2 ft above the connection at the 100-year flood** | 28.06.040.D |
| Wood near water | Anything within 18 in of water is decay-resistant or pressure-treated, with hot-dip galvanized connectors | 28.06.020 |
| Log floats (no engineer) | Only for conventional log-raft construction when roof height ≤ 75% of float width. **Not our path** | 28.06.030 |
| Separation, commercial floats | **20 ft walls / 16 ft roofs.** 10/8 with 1-hr walls (windows ≤ 25%). 6/4 fully sprinklered + 1-hr walls, no openings | 28.06.050 |
| Walks around a commercial float | **36 in wide on at least 2 opposite sides**, direct to a finger or main walkway | 28.06.060 |
| Main / marginal walkway | **6 ft** wide. Fingers 3 ft | 28.06.060 |
| Gangway | **5 ft** wide (4 ft if there's more than one), max slope 1:2.5, non-slip | 28.06.060 |
| Lighting | Commercial: **1 foot-candle** at the walking surface | 28.06.060 |
| Two exits (where the building code needs them) | Two main-walkway points far apart, or fingers on opposite sides, or a walk around 3 sides | 28.06.055 |
| Fire standpipe | Needed if any structure is **> 250 ft** from where the fire truck sets up. 250 GPM @ 100 PSI, annual NFPA 25 test | 28.06.050 |
| Second gangway | If the marginal walkway is > 250 ft, or any point is > 250 ft from a gangway | 28.06.050 |
| Electrical | Oregon Electrical Specialty Code + Title 26. Anything within 2 ft of water = wet location. Overhead drops 14 ft up. Transformers 8 ft from combustibles | 28.07 |
| Plumbing | RPBA backflow at the city connection. Marine/NSF flex lines. Sewer hose flexible to -4°F. Running water is OK as freeze protection | 28.08 |
| Mechanical / gas | Oregon Mechanical Specialty Code + Title 27 + Title 28. Gas piping and stoves need permits | 28.09 |
| Signage | Site map at the gangway head, every structure and slip numbered | 28.06.070 |

**Permits (28.03.020):** separate permits for each new floating structure, deck/walkway additions, alterations, **relocations**, electrical, plumbing, mechanical and gangways. Small deck extensions (≤ 5%, or 8 in) are exempt. A final inspection plus a **Certificate of Compliance** comes before anyone uses the space. The Harbor Master does moorage inspections. Appeals and "alternate methods" go to the Portland Permitting & Development Administrative Appeal Board.

**Existing stuff (28.05):** our current structures are grandfathered as nonconforming. **Relocating** a structure into the marina triggers the new-construction separation rules. An addition over 25% of floor area also triggers them.

## Not in Title 28 but still in play (check with the engineer / BDS)

- **Environmental overlay review** (the "c" overlay) for new floats, piling and shoreline work.
- **Oregon DSL** waterway lease for any new footprint over state-owned submerged land.
- **Army Corps** (Sec. 10 / 404) and the **in-water work window** for any new piling.
- **Oregon Mechanical / Fuel Gas code** for the gas stoves on new floats.
- **Insurance:** insurers will ask for the Certificate of Compliance on every new float.
