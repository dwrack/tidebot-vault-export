## 1. The Morning After

**Internal name:** Hangover Killer. **Public name:** The Morning After. We don't promise to cure anything (brand rule: describe what happens, don't promise feelings), and "hangover cure" claims are the kind of thing that gets screenshotted.

**Booking-page copy:**

> **The Morning After**
> Saturday and Sunday, 9am. 1 hour 45 minutes. Up to 8 guests.
>
> Rough night? Come sit by the river.
>
> We start you with water and fresh mint tea. Then three rounds of heat, kept a little gentler than usual. The middle one's a mint aufguss: peppermint on hot stones, a slow towel overhead, nothing to do but breathe. Between rounds there's the river if you want it, and the deck if you don't. We finish with ten minutes of slow breathing and our house drink, Low Tide: coconut water, lime, ginger, honey, a little salt, a lot of mint.
>
> We won't tell you how you'll feel after. Most people leave quieter than they came.
>
> **Good to know:** If you've had a drink in the last 8 hours, you'll do the session but skip the cold plunge. That's a hard rule, not a judgment. Hydrate before you come.
>
> **Add an IV drip** from our licensed nursing partner, booked and paid with them directly. Their nurse handles everything, start to finish.

**IV add-on line (for booking confirmation email):**
> Want an IV? [Partner name] runs drips on the float during The Morning After. Book with them at [link] at least 24 hours ahead. They'll do a quick health screen with you first. Drips start after your last cold round, so you'll do two rounds of heat instead of three.

Notes:
- The IV is the partner's service, sold by the partner. We don't take payment for it or a cut of it. See [[IV Hydration — In-House Business Plan]] for why.
- Good private fit for bachelorette and wedding groups the morning after. Calm, not a party, which keeps it on-brand.

## 4. Bring a Buddy (the safety offer)

### Important: the offer can't be women-only

The idea in the note was "women: add a friend for 50% off." The intent is right and we should keep it. **But Oregon's public accommodations law (ORS 659A.403) bars businesses from pricing differently based on sex.** A Portland bar got called out in 2012 for "$3 for guys, $5 for dolls" pricing, and ladies-night discounts are treated as illegal here ([Portland Mercury](https://www.portlandmercury.com/news/2012/03/22/5809569/gender-bender)). Confirm with counsel before launch. A women-only discount is a complaint waiting to happen.

**Fix:** make the *offer* open to everyone, and keep the *reason* front and center. The safety message can absolutely speak to women; the price just can't check gender.

### The offer
- Book a social session, add one buddy for **50% off** their spot.
- One buddy per booking. Social sessions only (not privates, not rituals at launch).
- Periode: set up as a "+1 Buddy" ticket type at half price, or a promo code.

### Booking-page / social copy

> **Bring a Buddy. Half off.**
>
> Nobody should walk into a new place feeling alone, or feel unsafe in a space that's supposed to be calm. Same goes for the river: the safest way into cold water is with someone watching. Our rule on the float is no one plunges alone.
>
> So bring someone. A friend, a sister, a coworker, the person who'll actually come with you. Their spot is 50% off.

Shorter version for IG:
> Bring a buddy, their spot's half off. Nobody plunges alone here, and nobody should have to show up alone either.

Why it works beyond safety: it pairs with the "no alone swimming" rule we already have, it fills weekday seats, and every buddy is a new guest who's now seen the float.
