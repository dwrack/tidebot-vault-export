# Private Saunas (V2): Water Line Plan for Alex

Draft 2026-11-09, revised same day. Built from the V2 Coordination Set (Revs L to AD) and the Electrical Design package (Rev AJ). Anything marked MEASURE has not been measured yet. No prices yet. No permit for this extension.

## The job in one line
Run cold water from the dock hookup onto the private sauna float, under the front deck, to a spigot in each sauna and to the on-demand water heater at the showers on the center wall. Cold water only; the heater makes the hot water right at the showers. Then protect the whole run from freezing.

## Route
1. **Source.** The dock water hookup nearest the float's SE corner. The "west dock water" was still off as of the last ET list, so confirm it's on.
2. **Dock to float.** The float and the dock move against each other. Use the same braided-hose quick-disconnect style already used at the dock section joints, just a longer one, with a little slack and a strain-relief clamp at each end. No rigid pipe across the gap. Closest match found online: GoBluTech Universal stainless braided hose, NSF 61 drinking-water liner, stainless garden-hose quick connects on both ends, 5 ft ($69.99) or 25 ft ($84.99). MEASURE the gap plus slack for the float's movement before buying. Put a backflow preventer (hose-bib vacuum breaker or dual check) at the dock end.
3. **Entry.** Main shutoff ball valve plus a drain valve right where the water comes onto the float. This sits near the power inlet and JB-0 at the SE corner.
4. **Trunk.** 3/4" PEX runs west under the front deck in the shared utility lane: power on the north edge, water in the middle, gas on the south edge, a few inches apart. Keep a gentle slope toward the entry so the whole run drains back from one valve.
5. **Branches.**
   - East sauna: 1/2" tee off the trunk to its spigot.
   - Center wall: one 3/4" cold line up to the on-demand heater at the showers. No separate hot line runs under the deck; the heater feeds both shower heads right there on the wall.
   - West sauna: 1/2" tee to its spigot.
   - Each branch gets its own ball valve under a lift-out hatch, so one sauna can be shut off without killing the others.

## Inside the sauna
PEX is rated to 180°F and the room runs hotter, so **no PEX inside the hot room.** Keep the PEX under the deck and outside the wall, then come through the wall low with a brass nipple to the spigot.

## Showers
- Two outdoor showers back to back on the center wall. Open deck, water runs off the deck.
- Each shower gets its own valve at the head.
- Add isolation valves with drain ports at the heater so it can be flushed and drained.

## Shower fixtures: options (matte black, outdoor-rated)
River water is fresh, not salt, so 304 stainless or solid brass under a black finish is fine; 316 is better but not required. Black finishes fade and chip in sun, so pick powder coat over stainless or brass, never over zinc or plastic.

Plumbing note: only hot water from the heater reaches the showers, so the heater's set temperature IS the shower temperature (set it around 105°F). A plain on/off valve works. If we want guests to be able to cool it down, tee a cold line into a mixing valve at each shower.

## Freeze protection (before the first hard freeze)
- No heat tape. Anti-freeze drip valves instead: one at the far end of each water line so water keeps moving through the whole run when it gets cold.
  - End of the east sauna spigot branch
  - End of the west sauna spigot branch
  - End of the cold line at the heater (just before the heater inlet)
  - Mount each one so it drips onto the deck or into the river, not into a wall.
  - Thermostatic freeze-protection valves are the clean way: they open on their own around 35°F and close when it warms up, so they don't waste water all summer. A cracked hose-bib drip works too but has to be opened and closed by hand.
- Closed-cell foam pipe insulation over the cable, UV-rated or wrapped wherever sun hits it.
- The Rinnai's built-in freeze protection only works with power on. Never unplug it in winter.
- Hard-freeze backup: shut the main, open the drain valve, both spigots and both showers, and the line drains back.
