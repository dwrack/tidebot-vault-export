# Private Saunas (V2): Water Line Plan for Alex

Draft 2026-11-09. Built from the V2 Coordination Set (Revs L to AD) and the Electrical Design package (Rev AJ). Anything marked MEASURE has not been measured yet. No prices yet.

## The job in one line
Run cold water from the dock hookup onto the private sauna float, under the front deck, to a spigot in each sauna and to the shared tankless heater that feeds the two showers on the center wall. Then protect the whole run from freezing.

## Who does what
| Part | Who |

Mario's gas work is permitted, and the Title 28 brief says plumbing on the float needs one too. Ask Mario whether the water side rides on his permit before Alex closes up the deck.

## Route
1. **Source.** The dock water hookup nearest the float's SE corner. MEASURE: where it is, its size, and the pressure (screw a gauge onto the hose bib, $10 to $15). The "west dock water" was still off as of the last ET list, so confirm it's on.
2. **Dock to float.** The float and the dock move against each other. Use a short flexible potable hose (NSF-61, drinking-water rated) with a little slack and a strain-relief clamp at each end. No rigid pipe across the gap.
3. **Entry.** Main shutoff ball valve plus a drain valve right where the water comes onto the float. This sits near the power inlet and JB-0 at the SE corner. Heat-tape outlet E13 is planned there.
4. **Trunk.** 3/4" PEX runs west under the front deck in the shared utility lane: power on the north edge, water in the middle, gas on the south edge, a few inches apart. Keep the slope gentle toward the entry so the whole run drains back from one valve.
5. **Branches.**
   - East sauna: 1/2" tee off the trunk to the spigot on its side wall.
   - Center wall: 3/4" up the riser to the tankless heater in the cedar box. Hot and cold 1/2" stubs to both showers on that wall.
   - West sauna: 1/2" tee to its spigot.
   - Each branch gets its own ball valve under a lift-out hatch, so one sauna can be shut off without killing the others.

## Inside the sauna (the part to get right)
The spigot is on the side wall of a 190 to 210°F room. PEX is rated to 180°F, so **no PEX inside the hot room.** Keep the PEX under the deck and outside the wall. Come through the wall low with a brass nipple and use an all-brass wall-mount faucet with a metal handle, mounted low on the side wall. Cold water only at the spigot. Add a hose-thread vacuum breaker if the faucet has hose threads. Grant wants no pooling on the spigot side, so check the floor slopes to the grate before the faucet goes in.

## Showers
- Two showers back to back on the center wall, outdoors, river side screens.
- Hot water from one shared outdoor natural-gas tankless heater (electric won't work on our single 20 A circuit).
- Each shower gets a pressure-balance or thermostatic valve so nobody gets scalded when the other shower turns on. Mario's call on the model.
- Ask for the heater's freeze-protection and service valve kit (isolation valves with drain ports), so the heater can be flushed and drained.
- Open question, not Alex's: shower and floor-drain water goes straight to the river. The DEQ and marina question on that is still open in the coordination set.

## Freeze protection (do this before the first hard freeze)
- Self-regulating heat cable on every exposed run: the dock hose, the entry, the trunk where it isn't buried in the deck, the riser, and the branches up to the wall. Plug into the C2 outlets (E13). The electrical package budgets about 250 W for heat tape.
- Closed-cell foam pipe insulation over the cable, UV-rated or wrapped wherever sun hits it.
- The tankless has about 140 W of built-in freeze protection that only works with power on. Never unplug it in winter.
- Hard-freeze backup: shut the main, open the drain valve and both spigots, and the line drains back.
