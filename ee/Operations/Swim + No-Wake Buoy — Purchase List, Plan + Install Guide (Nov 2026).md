## 3. Rules that decide the design (checked 2026-11-01)

**Oregon State Marine Board (OSMB)**
- Any private waterway marker placed after July 1, 2015 needs an OSMB permit. No exceptions for floating businesses. Source: [OAR 250-010-0206](https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_250-010-0206) and the [Waterway Markers page](https://www.oregon.gov/osmb/boater-info/Pages/Waterway-Markers.aspx).
- Regulatory markers can only mark a rule that already exists. The good news: [OAR 250-010-0025](https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_250-010-0025) already makes slow-no-wake the law within 200 ft of a marina with 6+ moored vessels or a floating home moorage with 6+ contiguous structures. Our float sits inside a moorage like that, so a "SLOW NO WAKE" buoy marks existing law. That is the buoy to apply for.
- A "swim area" marker (orange diamond with cross, "boats keep out") only counts as a legal swim area when a public body has established it by rule or ordinance ([OAR 250-010-0270](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/oregon/Or-Admin-Code-SS-250-010-0270)). A private business cannot create one on its own. So: do not label the buoy "SWIM AREA." The swim line along the float does the physical job; ask OSMB on the call whether a "boats keep out" diamond is permittable for a DSL-permitted structure. Take their answer, not mine.
- Buoy spec is fixed by rule: white body, two international-orange bands, symbol and message in the white between the bands ([OAR 250-010-0245](https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_250-010-0245)). Any "regulatory buoy" from a marine supplier meets this.
- Application: [Waterway Marker Application (fillable PDF)](https://www.oregon.gov/osmb/forms-library/Documents/Boating%20Safety/WaterwayMarkerApplicationFillable.pdf). Needs marker type, size, message, and a scale drawing or map with the exact spot. Decision within 30 days. Permit holder maintains the markers.

**US Coast Guard, Northwest District (private aid to navigation, PATON)**
- The Columbia is federal navigable water. A lit buoy is a private aid to navigation and needs a Coast Guard permit under 33 CFR 66, form [CG-2554](https://media.defense.gov/2017/Oct/16/2001827524/-1/-1/0/CG_2554.PDF). The District also has a [fillable PATON questionnaire](https://www.pacificarea.uscg.mil/Portals/8/District_13/dpw/docs/Fillable_PATON_Application_Questionnaire.pdf) and a [PATON guide](https://www.pacificarea.uscg.mil/Portals/8/District_13/dpw/docs/patonguide.pdf). Submit by email to D13-SMB-D13-PATON@uscg.mil. Average wait about 3 months per the [WA permit handbook entry](https://www.oria.wa.gov/permithandbook/permitdetail/98).
- Light rule: regulatory marks that are lit show a WHITE light, any rhythm except quick flashing, group flashing (2), or Morse "A." Spec the lantern as flashing white, 1 flash every 4 seconds (Fl W 4s). The PATON manager may assign a different rhythm; use whatever the permit says.
- Coast Guard can inspect it any time. Keep it lit, upright, and clean or they can order it removed.
- Permit is for the aid only. It does not grant any exclusive use of the water.

**Riverbed and the anchor**
- The riverbed is state land managed by Oregon DSL. The marina's DSL lease almost certainly covers the moorage footprint; an anchor inside that footprint is usually fine with the lessee's OK. Confirm with the marina owner in writing (email is fine).
- Army Corps of Engineers (Portland District) technically has jurisdiction over anything placed in navigable water. Most single buoys fall under a nationwide permit with no paperwork beyond the PATON, but ask the Portland District regulatory office when you file the PATON. One email.
- Multnomah County Sheriff River Patrol enforces no-wake. Worth a courtesy heads-up once the buoy is in; they are also the people who will tell boaters it is real.

### Option A: braced arm off the sauna corner

```
                 river flow  ---->

            (sign + light on tip post)
                  o
                   \  arm, 10-12 ft, angled ~30 deg downstream
                    \
                     \___ brace
   ===================\==\============================  sauna, outside edge
   ||   mount 1 ^      ^ mount 2 (6-8 ft down the edge)
   ||  o----o----o----o----o----o----o   swim line clips to the arm tip
```

**Design**
- Main arm: 3" schedule 40 pipe, 12 ft. Galvanized steel is about 91 lb, 6061 aluminum about 31 lb. Aluminum if Grant can weld it, it is easier on the mounts and will not rust-streak the sauna.
- Brace: 2" schedule 40 pipe, about 9 ft, from a second mount 6 to 8 ft down the outside edge to a point two thirds of the way out the arm. Arm plus brace plus the sauna edge make a triangle, so no single bolt carries a bending load.
- Height: arm at deck level, well clear of the water. Logs and debris pass under it. Nothing but the swim line touches the river.
- Angle: about 30 degrees downstream from square, so a boat or tall debris that does reach it slides off instead of hooking.
- Root: pinned at mount 1 with a hinge, and a shear bolt where the brace meets the arm. A hard hit breaks one cheap bolt and the arm folds back along the sauna instead of tearing the frame.
- Tip: a 2" vertical post, 4 ft tall. Two sign panels in a V (one faces upstream, one faces out to the channel), "SLOW NO WAKE" with the orange circle, size per the OSMB permit [size from OSMB]. Solar lantern on top of the post. Pad eye under the tip for the swim line.
- Visibility: SOLAS reflective tape the full length of the arm, orange and white, so the arm itself reads at night and to a paddler at eye level.

**Loads (estimate)**
- Nothing in the water means no buoy drag. The load is the swim line bowing in the current, call it 50 to 150 lb at the tip, plus wind on the sign.
- Worst case about 1,800 ft-lb at the root if the brace were missing. With the brace, the mounts see a few hundred pounds each in push and pull. Through-bolted plates with backing plates handle that.

**Install (Grant, about half a day)**
1. Confirm what the outside edge of the frame is made of and where a through-bolt with a backing plate can land. Two mounts, 6 to 8 ft apart.
2. Fabricate the arm, brace and tip post on shore. Dry-fit the hinge and shear bolt.
3. Bolt mount plates. Hang the arm on the hinge, pin the brace, torque, then fit the shear bolt last.
4. Mount signs and lantern, set the flash rhythm to the permit value, tape the arm.
5. Clip the swim line to the tip pad eye. Photograph it for both permit files.

**What can go wrong**
- OSMB or the Coast Guard may want a buoy in the water or the marker farther out. Ask before building. If they insist on a buoy, hang the 9" regulatory buoy from the tip on 2 ft of chain; it floats under the arm and still cannot wander.
- Head height. The arm sits at the edge of the plunge area. Tape it, and keep the swim line inside it so swimmers stay clear.
- Boat lane. Confirm 12 ft of projection does not crowd the marina's boat lane or a neighbor's slip [marina owner].
- Wind and ice load on the sign panels in a winter storm. Keep panels modest and through-bolted.

### Option C: two-anchor (bridle) buoy

A single anchor cannot hold a buoy still: on 2x scope it slides 55 to 80 ft downstream of its anchor as the current changes, and heavier chain only trims that by about 3 ft. Two anchors can.

```
   river flow  ---->

                       (BUOY, lit)
                        /       \
              leg, 75 ft         leg, 75 ft
                /                   \
   [upstream anchor]             [downstream anchor]
        <---- 60 ft ---->|<---- 60 ft ---->
```

**Design**
- Two anchors in line with the current, 120 ft apart, buoy over the midpoint. Each leg runs from its anchor straight up to a ring just under the buoy.
- Leg length 75 ft, set so both legs come just taut at 45 ft high water. At 35 ft low water there is slack, and the buoy drifts about 6 ft downstream until the upstream leg takes up. That is the whole wander.
- Legs are rope with chain at both ends: 15 ft of 3/8" galvanized chain at the anchor (abrasion on the bottom), about 55 ft of 3/4" polyester 3-strand in the middle, 5 ft of 3/8" chain at the top (prop and log wear). All-chain legs would hang about 180 lb on the buoy and need a much bigger buoy; rope-and-chain hangs about 50 lb.
- Buoy: step up from the 9" to an 18" class regulatory buoy. Hanging weight plus the downward pull of the upstream leg in freshet comes to roughly 175 lb, and the buoy wants about twice that in net buoyancy to stay upright and readable. Buoyancy varies by maker, so read "net buoyancy" on the spec sheet before ordering. [buoy model and net buoyancy]
- Anchors: the 200 to 300 lb blocks in the old plan are too light for this. A taut leg pulls up as well as sideways, and concrete loses 40% of its weight under water. Use 1,500 lb or heavier concrete blocks set by Rick's crane, or two helix screw anchors set by a diver. [Rick's call]
- Swivel between the ring and the buoy. Every shackle pin seized with stainless wire.

**Parts**

| Item | Spec | Rough price |
|---|---|---|
| Regulatory buoy | 18" class, "SLOW NO WAKE", net buoyancy 350 lb or more | $450-800 |
| Solar lantern | Same as section 5B | $180-550 |
| Anchors | 2 concrete blocks, 1,500 lb+ each, cast-in 1" galvanized eye | $200-350 each DIY |
| Anchor placement | Rick's crane barge, both blocks, positioned by GPS | [Rick quote] |
| Chain | 3/8" galvanized proof coil, 40 ft total (2 x 15 ft bottom, 2 x 5 ft top) | $160-240 |
| Rope | 3/4" polyester 3-strand, 2 x 55 ft, spliced eyes with galvanized thimbles | $180-300 |
| Ring, swivel, shackles | 5/8" galvanized ring, 1/2" swivel, 8 shackles | $150 |
| Reflective tape, seizing wire | | $30 |

Rough total $1,700 to $3,200 plus Rick's time.

**Install**
1. Rig both legs, the ring, swivel and buoy on the dock. Seize every pin. Photograph it.
2. Rick sets the upstream block at its GPS mark with the leg attached and the top end buoyed off with a temporary float.
3. He sets the downstream block 120 ft down-current the same way.
4. From a work boat, bring both leg tops to the ring, shackle on the swivel and buoy, release.
5. Record both anchor positions and the buoy position. Watch it for 15 minutes at the dock, then check again after the first strong ebb.

**What can go wrong**
- Logs. Two legs are two things to snag. Inspect after every high-water event.
- Stage above the design high water pulls the buoy under. If the gauge is forecast past 45 ft of water at the site, pull the buoy for the event.
- Rope chafe at the thimbles. Inspect yearly, replace legs every 5 years or so.
- This is the option where my numbers matter most and are least proven. Have Rick or a mooring supplier size the anchors, legs and buoy before buying anything.

## 7. Install guide

Install steps for each mounting option are in section 4. The swim line is the same for both.

**Swim line**
1. Install stainless pad eyes on the outside corners of the sauna float, through-bolted with a backing plate, not screwed into the deck.
2. Thread the floats onto the rope every 5 to 6 ft, with a stopper knot on each side of each float so they stay spaced.
3. Snap hook on the upstream end to the arm tip (A) or the upstream pad eye (C). Run the line out and back along the plunge area. Snap hook at the downstream end goes to the weak link, which goes to the downstream pad eye.
4. Leave slack. In current the line will bow downstream; a taut line pulls floats under and loads the pad eyes.

## 8. Maintenance and what will go wrong

- **Logs.** The Columbia carries whole trees after every big rain and all through freshet. The buoy will get hit. A hit either drags the anchor a few feet or wraps the chain. Inspect after every high-water event and at least monthly. Chain inspection = pull the top chain up to the swivel and look at the swivel and shackle pins.
- **Stage change (option C only).** The buoy drifts about 6 ft downstream at low stage and sits dead over the midpoint at high stage. If the gauge is forecast above flood stage, consider pulling the buoy for the event; a buried or dragged buoy is a navigation hazard you are responsible for.
- **Light.** Oregon November through February has 2 to 3 hours of useful sun. A lantern rated for 5+ days of autonomy will usually make it; a cheap one will go dark in January. Check it from the dock at dusk once a week. A dark PATON is a reportable violation.
- **Birds and algae.** Wipe the solar panel and the message band monthly. Cormorant mess kills the panel output faster than cloud cover.
- **Winter decision.** Many Columbia moorages pull private buoys December to February. If you want to pull ours, say so on both permits as "seasonal" with the dates, so the Coast Guard does not log a missing aid. Year-round is fine too, it just means winter inspections in the cold.
- **Swim line.** Drop it to the dock in winter (no one is plunging in a freshet) so it does not collect debris. Replace rope every 2 to 3 seasons; poly chalks in UV.
- **Hardware life.** Galvanized chain in fresh water lasts 5 to 8 years. Swivel and shackles are the wear points; budget to replace them every 2 to 3 years, cheap insurance.

## 9. Other things to know

- **A buoy does not stop anyone.** It gives River Patrol something to enforce and gives you a defensible position with insurance. The swim line is what actually keeps hulls out of the plunge area. Both matter; the line matters more for guest safety.
- **Insurance.** Tell the broker about the marked plunge area and the lit aid before the 11/6 renewal. It cuts both ways: a marked area shows diligence, and an unmarked swim area next to boat traffic is the kind of thing an adjuster asks about after a claim.
- **Signage on the float.** Add a plain sign at the plunge ladder: cold water, no lifeguard, members and guests only, stay inside the float line. Keep it in the Ebb & Ember voice, not legalese, but say those four things.
- **Do not overclaim.** No "designated swim area" language on the site, socials, or the float until OSMB says so. "Plunge area along the float" is accurate.
- **Permit renewal.** OSMB permits may carry a term; the PATON is open-ended but any change (move, new light, removal) requires notifying the District. Put a yearly calendar reminder on November 1 to check both.
- **Marina Master Plan.** Fold the buoy and anchor position into the marina layout drawings once installed so the future pilings and slips are planned around it, not through it.
