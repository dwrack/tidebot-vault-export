# Swim Zone: Mooring & Line Plan (Oct 2026)

Drafted 2026-10-12 for the L-shaped swim enclosure at the marina (173 NE Bridgeton Rd, North Portland Harbor). Project page: https://claude.ai/artifact/74s6Fg7rvL5FXtG9ZhuvQE

**Status: planning numbers, not an engineered design.** Current speeds for North Portland Harbor itself aren't published anywhere I could find. The loads below come from the Columbia main stem gauge at Vancouver and are conservative on purpose. Two things are still unknown and change the chain length: **water depth at the corner buoy(s)** and **exact enclosure size**. Measure the depth with a weighted line before buying chain.

---

## 1. The big call: seasonal, not year-round

Put it in after the spring freshet (usually July) and pull it in October.

- Spring freshet is May to July. Flows hit 400K to 600K+ cfs and the river carries logs. A rope line left out through freshet is basically a log catcher, and no reasonable anchor holds a 30 ft log pinned on it.
- In summer the current is low. The Vancouver gauge read **1.3 ft/s at 101,000 cfs** on 10/12 (main channel). North Portland Harbor is a side channel, so it's likely slower than that.
- Seasonal install cuts every load below by about 4x, so the hardware gets cheaper and lighter.

Install trigger: Vancouver gauge (NWS, I-5 bridge) steady **under ~10 ft** with no freshet forecast. It read 3.7 ft on 10/12. Flood stage is 16 ft.

## 2. Layout: let the docks do most of the work

The L means two sides are dock and only the rope sides sit in open water. That's the cheapest setup there is:

- **Rope ends tie to the floating docks.** Floating docks already ride the river up and down, so those ends never need slack for water level. No anchors there.
- **Only the outer corner (or corners) needs an anchor.** For a simple L that's **1 anchored corner buoy**, maybe 2 if the long side is over ~80 ft and sags.
- **Every tie-off gets a weak link.** Use a short length of lighter line or a breakaway clip between the rope and the dock cleat. If debris snags the line, the weak link parts, not the cleat or the dock.

## 3. Loads (the math behind the chain size)

Drag on the underwater part of the line and buoys, F = 0.5 × water density × Cd × area × v².

| Case | Current | Clean 80 ft line + 1 buoy | With algae/debris (×2.5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summer swim season (design case) | 2 ft/s | ~35 lb | ~90 lb |
| Worst case if left in during freshet | 4 ft/s | ~125 lb | ~310 lb |

Assumes dark 5" floats every ~3 ft, 3/4" rope, one Sur-Mark-size buoy (~1 sq ft underwater). Rope sag adds a little tension; with ~10% sag the corner sees roughly 140 lb at 4 ft/s clean.

**Design load for the corner anchor: 500 lb working, 1,000 lb holding.** That covers summer with a big margin and survives an early or late high-water week. It does not cover a log strike, which is the weak link's job.

## 4. Chain

| Spot | Size | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Anchor to buoy (main riser) | **3/8" hot-dip galvanized, Grade 30 or 43** | Working load ~2,650 lb (G30). Strength is 5x more than needed; the extra thickness is for wear where it drags on the bottom. Lasts several seasons. |
| Shackles | **7/16"** galvanized (one size up from the chain) | Shackles fail before chain. Seize every pin with stainless or monel wire. |
| Swivel | 3/8" galvanized swivel just under the buoy | Stops the chain from twisting up as the current turns with the tide. |

Don't go thinner than 3/8". Thinner chain is cheaper by a few dollars per foot and wears through at the bottom in a season or two.

## 5. Chain length

**Rule: 1.5 × (deepest water during swim season).** It needs to be long enough that the buoy stays upright at high water, but short enough that the corner doesn't wander 20 ft and let the L go slack.

Water depth = depth at low water + the seasonal swing. Summer swing here is tide (1 to 3 ft daily) plus river level (a few feet over the season). Plan on **+5 ft** over the depth you measure at low water in summer.

| Measured low-water depth | Design high-water depth | Chain length |
|---|---|---|
| 8 ft | 13 ft | **20 ft** |
| 12 ft | 17 ft | **25 ft** |
| 15 ft | 20 ft | **30 ft** |
| 20 ft | 25 ft | **38 ft** |

Buy 10 ft extra and cut to fit on install day.

If the depth is over ~20 ft or the corner wanders too much, upgrade to an **elastic mooring rode** (Hazelett-type). It stretches with the water level so the buoy stays tight on its spot. It costs more, but it's the clean fix for big level swings.

## 6. Anchor

Bottom in North Portland Harbor is most likely silt or sand. Confirm by dropping a probe or asking whoever set the marina's piles.

| Option | Holding | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Helical screw anchor (diver-installed)** | 1,000 to 3,000+ lb | Small footprint, highest holding per dollar, stays put for years, easy to leave in over winter | Needs a diver with a hydraulic driver |
| Mushroom anchor, 200 lb | ~400 lb when first set, much more once it silts in | Can self-install off a boat | Needs a season to bury; drags if hit hard before it sets |
| Concrete block | Poor | Cheap | Loses ~40% of its weight underwater and slides in current. Skip it. |

**Pick: one helical screw anchor per corner.** It's the best holding for the money, and the anchor can stay in over winter with just a small pickup line, so the yearly install is clip on chain, clip on buoy, done.

## 7. Line and buoys (from the 10/9 research)

- Rope: 3/4" polyester or nylon core. Polyester stretches less and handles UV better. Polypropylene floats on its own, but it goes brittle in sun after 2 to 3 seasons.
- Floats: **dark** (navy, charcoal or dark green) smooth HDPE so algae doesn't show. Keep a second set to swap monthly and pressure-wash.
- Corner buoy: Taylor Made Sur-Mark with "Swim Area" or "Caution Swimmers" label ($256 to $430). It has to stay white with orange bands.

## 9. Approvals

- **Oregon State Marine Board** waterway marker permit, for the buoy as an informational "swim area / caution swimmers" marker. They decide within 30 days.
- **US Coast Guard** private aid to navigation (PATON) application. The Columbia system is federally navigable, so buoys there typically need one. Confirm with Sector Columbia River.
- **Oregon DSL**: check whether the anchor spot sits inside the marina's existing submerged-land lease. If it does, likely nothing new to file.
- **Max (insurance)**: already a task for Jonah on the project page.
