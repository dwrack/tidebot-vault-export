# Holman Inspiration + Eco Board (Oct 2026)

_2026-10-17. Ideas for the Rose, the bluff and bank, and the gravel lot under the ramps (bathrooms, changing, hang-out, landscaping). Internal, off the record like the rest of Holman._

Related: [[Holman Land-Side Site Plan v1 (Oct 2026)]], [[The Rose - Master Plan (Sept 2026)]], [[The Rose - Materials Plan (Sept 2026)]]

**How this was built:** architizer.com/projects/q/ blocks automated fetches (403), so I used Architizer's own bathhouse roundup plus searches that surface Architizer and sister-site projects. Anything marked (unverified) came from a single secondary source. Output figures for solar art are designers' projections, not metered.

**Site constraints that shape every idea (from the land-side plan, verified 2026-10-15):** bank is FEMA floodway (no-rise rule), whole flat lot is inside the 50 ft river setback, soils are fill with very high liquefaction, ODOT owns the lot. So: light, removable, raised or landform structures. Nothing heavy on the bank.

## 1. Bathhouse and sauna precedents (Architizer bathhouse roundup)

| Project | Steal this |
|---|---|
| **Löyly, Helsinki** (Avanto) | Low black box wrapped in a slatted heat-treated wood "cloak." Slats give privacy from outside, keep the view from inside, shade the glass. Cool-down terraces sit between the warm box and the cloak. Stepped cloak doubles as stairs to rooftop lookouts. Deck runs to the water with stairs into the sea. FSC cladding and decking. **Closest match to the Rose + shore.** |
| **Lung Mei Beach Bathhouse, Hong Kong** | One ramp from road level down through changing rooms and outdoor showers to the beach. Landform roof echoes the ridge. **Maps almost exactly onto our bluff: lot at 34 to 38 ft down to the bank.** |
| **Casa de Baño, Mexico** (Hutchison) | Circular plan: hot bath, sauna, steam, washroom around a sky-open cold plunge. Off-grid, water-autonomous. Petal/rose geometry fits the Rose. |
| **Lake Sammamish bathhouse, WA** (Patano) | Recycled-content concrete, reclaimed cedar siding, green roof, rain gardens, wetland restoration. Pacific NW public-agency precedent, useful when talking to PP&R. |
| **Badehaus Kaiserstrand, Austria** | Pavilion lifted on 30 columns. Raised floor answers floodway and no-rise. |
| **Kogohi Bath House, Japan** (Kuma) | Long linear plan stepping down a slope, light translucent roof that floats. |
| **Lake Segrino changing pavilion, Italy** | Timber, built mostly dry and prefabricated, wooden walkway to the water. Changing rooms one side, refreshment other. Dry assembly = removable. |
| **Lahti changing pavilion, Finland** (HK Architekten) | Timber pillar walls, energy from solar panels. Summer-use public changing room. |
| **Magnum Architectes dune pavilion, France** | Minimum public kit (change, shelter, restroom), dry-assembled, local materials. |

## 2. The underpass / lot: bathrooms, changing, hang-out

- **Underpass Park, Toronto** (PFS Studio). Overpass is the roof, so program runs day and night in rain. Plant the voids between decks. **Portland rain angle fits the Downpour idea.**
- **Infra-Space 1, Boston** (Landing Studio). Landscape treats highway runoff as stormwater infrastructure. Inspection-access zones double as public program. Ask ODOT early what must stay clear for inspection.
- **China Basin Park restroom, SF** (Min Design). Restrooms tucked under an elevated landscape, wood clad, gender-neutral. **Idea: bathrooms and changing rooms built into the bluff face, planted roof is the hang-out lawn above.** Also solves the 1:3 slope.
- **Sideyard + Burnside Skatepark (Portland, Central Eastside).** Local proof that leftover space under the bridgehead gets leased for food carts, skate, retail. Multnomah County also lit the Burnside, Morrison and Hawthorne viaducts in 2023 ($232K request). Worth asking whether Hawthorne-side lighting reaches Holman. (unverified for our exact spot)
- **A8ernA, Netherlands.** Under-motorway skate park, plaza, supermarket. Proof of concept for long-term underpass use.

**Pulled together, a layout to test:**
1. Arrive under the ramps: lit, warm, wood-lined canopy with benches and a tea/cold-drink counter.
2. Landform changing block with a Lung Mei-style ramp down the bluff. Outdoor showers on the way.
3. Planted green roof lawn above the changing block (hang-out, view, no extra footprint).
4. Gangway to the dock and the Rose.
5. Cool-down terraces Löyly-style, stepped, between building and water.

## 3. Bank and landscaping (salmon-friendly, floodway-friendly)

- **South Waterfront Greenway, Portland.** Regraded steep bank to gentler slope, native planting, soil bioengineering, terraced bank with concrete planting vaults. The local template the City already knows and likes.
- **Portland Harbor sites (PGE Harborton, Alder Creek, Linnton Mill).** Native riparian planting, laid-back banks, in-water wood and rock for juvenile salmon. NOAA-cited finding: juvenile salmonids use shoreline only where there is woody bank vegetation.
- **Portland Downtown Riverfront Habitat report** (Ross Island to Steel Bridge). Simple, low-cost, incremental habitat upgrades. Our reach is right inside that stretch.
- **Huaqiao Wusong Riverfront, Architizer.** Water passes through treatment wetlands before the inner bay. Idea: **sauna and shower greywater polished through a planted bio-swale before it reaches the river** (check Title 28 and DEQ first).
- **Riverfront Park, Architizer.** Bank in three stepped layers, boulders then irises, reeds, cattails.

**Takeaway:** regrade and plant the bank as habitat, because it earns goodwill with the City, helps the River Environmental overlay, and looks lush. Plan on the design needing a no-rise analysis either way.

## 4. Solar art (to offset CO2 and be the thing people photograph)

| Reference | What it is | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| **Greetings to the Sun, Zadar, Croatia** | 300 glass plates with solar cells in a circle on the waterfront, lights up after dark from daytime sun | ~46,500 kWh/yr (estimate) |
| **Rio Iluminado, Willimantic CT** | Waterfront arch, solar array on top, reflective steel below; won a Land Art Generator competition. Not confirmed built. | ~25.5 MWh/yr (projection) |
| **Arch of Time, Houston** | Sundial sculpture, proposed, $20M | 400,000 kWh/yr (projection, unbuilt) |
| **Lunar Cubit (LAGI)** | Pyramids that collect by day, glow by moon phase at night | design only |
| **Solar murals (LAGI, e.g. La Monarca)** | Actually built. Mural printed on PV. | no output published |

**Ideas that fit us, small to big:**
1. **Petal canopy.** The Rose's own petals or the underpass canopy carry thin-film or glass-glass PV. Art and roof in one. Lowest cost per "wow."
2. **Sun-clock / sundial deck** on the lot, PV tiles in the ground-plane pattern, lights up at night like Zadar. Doubles as the arrival moment.
3. **Solar mural on the ramp-side pier face or the changing block wall.** Avenue of Murals is already a Central Eastside thing, so a PV mural reads as local.
4. **Solar path lights and a lantern grove** for the cool-down terraces. Cheap, visible, low risk.
5. **Community-funded.** Name panels or lantern tiles in the funding plan. Ties to the waitlist playbook.

**Honest note:** a sculpture-scale array offsets a small slice of a sauna's footprint. Treat it as brand and public art plus a real chunk of the facility's electricity, not as the whole offset. Get an installer quote and PGE net-metering rules before promising a number. Also floodway and ODOT land: panels on a raised canopy, not on the bank.

## 5. Making it eco without losing the feel

Quality-first order of operations: cut load, then recover heat, then offset.

| Move | Feel cost | Eco payoff | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| FSC or reclaimed cedar and heat-treated cladding (Löyly, Sammamish) | none, it looks better | low-carbon materials | Löyly's FSC cert covers only cladding, deck, one wall, so claim narrowly |
| Drain-water heat recovery to preheat boiler or stove water | none | large cut in water-heating fuel (a Quebec 145-unit case reported 68% CO2 cut vs baseline, so treat as an upper bound) | Best single mechanical bet |
| Sauna waste heat to warm water, floors, changing rooms (Fortum Culture Sauna) | adds warm floors, a feel win | recovers heat otherwise vented | Fortum used pellets; ours is gas, so verify |
| Solar thermal for shower water | none | cuts gas | Mount on the canopy or changing block roof |
| Green roof over changing block | adds lawn and view | stormwater, cooling, habitat | China Basin / Sammamish model |
| Rain capture for showers, toilets, irrigation | none | potable water saved | Check code for non-potable reuse |
| Composting or low-flush toilets where code allows | depends on finish, can be premium | water | Easy to get wrong; test on the pilot |
| Native riparian planting on the bank | none | habitat, City goodwill | Section 3 |
| Prefab, dry-assembled, demountable pavilions (Segrino, Magnum) | none | low waste, fits floodway | Also matches a pilot that may have to move |
| LED, warm-dim, solar lanterns | none, warmer | low | |
| Real carbon accounting, then buy removal for the leftover | none | honest offset | Audit gas use first, then offset what remains. Prefer verified removal over cheap credits. |

**Where I would not compromise on feel:** the stove, the heat, the cold plunge, the wood. Cut the eco corners elsewhere (lighting, water, heat recovery, materials) so the session stays the session.
