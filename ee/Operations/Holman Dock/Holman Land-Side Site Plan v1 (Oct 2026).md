## 6. Design principles (from the Oct 15 call)

1. Frame the skyline, block the freeway. Every seat faces west or along the river; backs to I-5. The hill is the first sound wall.
2. Noise is the number one downside, so it gets two tools: attenuation (hill, upward overhangs, dense planting, solid-backed cabanas) and masking (falls, river, stove). Sound engineer in the architect brief. Decibel readings on both sides are the first walk item.
3. Own the industrial story: Central Eastside Industrial District, under the ramps, next to a fireboat and a rowing club. Steel, concrete, gravel, gas-fired heat. Oslo waterfront saunas as the reference.
4. Community third space, not wellness. No health claims anywhere.
5. Two moods: the activating rose and the quiet one. On land, beach and lot carry the energy; the hill carries the hush.
6. The Rose sets the language: petal roofs, rose-part names, carved seating. The land side echoes the water half (Jess).
7. Hill cutouts, never raising the hill (Hannah). Keeps the floodway and e-overlay reviews simple.
8. Land side is fully electric, solar first (Davey, 2026-10-16). Panels on the sound wall, the restroom canopy and later the cabana roofs. No gas on the ground. The sauna's fuel waits on Dylan's written answer.

## 6b. How high can we go (verified 2026-10-17, PortlandMaps + Title 33)

Higher than the gravel lot suggests, but not on the gravel lot. The real height is on the State of Oregon lots along Water Ave.

| Land | Code ceiling | What really limits it | Realistic use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bank and bluff (OS, d e g*; scenic s overlay on the west edge of lot 1600) | Open Space; inside the 50 ft setback only river-dependent or river-related uses | Floodway, River Environmental review, scenic overlay | Low: carved seating, hill cabanas, decks |
| Gravel lot, ODOT tax lot 1600 (~16,000 sq ft) | 65 ft and 3:1 FAR (EX base zone; Central City map says base zone applies) | Appears inside the 50 ft setback; ODOT owns it; next to the freeway | 1 to 2 stories: restroom and changing block, pavilion, sound wall |
| State lots 1401 (0.79 ac) and east part of 1601 (1.93 ac), 1515 SE Water | 100 ft, up to 250 ft with Central City bonuses; 2:1 FAR on the CC map (bonus FAR to confirm) | State of Oregon owns them; a 4-story 1952 office (~150,800 sq ft) and the Boathouse are there; part sits under the ramps | The one place a real building could go: a mid-rise bathhouse, hotel or club with a skyline rooftop |

- At 2:1, lot 1401 allows roughly 69,000 sq ft and all of 1601 at most roughly 168,000 sq ft (est., lot area x FAR, before bonuses; west part of 1601 is base-zone).
- Roof solar may rise 5 ft above a flat roof without counting toward height (33.140.210).
- Scale: Hawthorne lift towers 165 ft, Tilikum towers 180 ft, OMSI District zoned to 250 ft.
- **Read:** keep the riverbank low so the Rose stays the hero. To use the land to its fullest, the play is a ground lease or partnership with the State on the Water Ave frontage: a separate, bigger conversation than the PP&R pilot. First ask: Dylan, who at the State do we talk to?

Palette: weathering steel, heavy timber, board-formed concrete, old brick, Hawthorne green. Deep rose red belongs to the Rose only.

**The two moves:**
1. Build the land side from the yard's own materials (weathering steel, heavy timber, board-formed concrete), so cabanas, the restroom block and the sound wall read as one working yard. No polish, no spa white.
2. Borrow the bridges' straight truss lines and the warehouses' sawtooth roofs for everything on land; keep curves and red for the Rose, so it reads as the single sculpture from every bridge.

View corridors (plan layer): skyline west across 300 m of river; north to the Hawthorne towers; south to the Marquam and Tilikum. Freeway edge east: block, don't frame.
