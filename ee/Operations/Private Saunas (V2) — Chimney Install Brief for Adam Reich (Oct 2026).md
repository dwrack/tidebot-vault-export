## The job in one paragraph

Two identical small saunas, 8' × 10' outside, side by side, 4' apart, on a float. Each gets a Torch TS-80F natural gas sauna stove (6" flue collar) in the front corner. We need a 6" vertical chimney per sauna: double-wall connector from the stove collar to a cathedral ceiling support box, then insulated Class A through a 1:12 shed roof, 3 ft above the roof with a cap. Roof is sheathed now; metal goes on after the pipe, so the roofer can lay panels around the penetration. We want you to install both flues (or set the boxes and inspect Grant's work, your call) and sign off so the gas inspector and insurer are happy.

## Stove and venting rules (Torch manual, attached)

- Class A chimney rated ≥800°F, vertical termination. Cap ≥3 ft above the roof at the exit, ≥2 ft above anything within 10 ft. Min 6 ft from stove top to cap. Horizontal runs only in double-wall, ≤50% of vertical, each 90° costs 5 ft. We run straight up, no offsets.
- Stove clearances: 4" to the wall behind it (intake side), 12" on the other three sides, measured from the finished wall surface.
- Torch wants the flue and controls installed by a licensed pro. That's you.

Why double-wall inside: the pipe centerline is 10.58" from the finished wall. DVL (7¼" OD) at 6" clearance works; single-wall at 18" does not.

## Sequence

1. Grant tapes the collar height and the roof build-up, headers the rafter, frames the 12¼" opening, drills the pilot up through the roof deck at the centerline.
2. You (or Grant with you checking) set the cathedral box, run the Class A through, flash it, cap it. Connector pipe can wait for the stoves to be set on the finished floor.
3. Cameron lays the metal roof: **start the panel layout at the pipe and work away from it**, so no standing seam lands under the boot if it can be helped. Boot flashing over the Class A.
4. Stoves set, connector pipe, gas, startup. Torch control box lives outside the hot room on the front wall.
