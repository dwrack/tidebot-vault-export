## The parts (C1-C10)

### C1 Lodge hot room
Round room, stove in the middle like a fire in a den, two rings of bench around it. Hot and dry, built for Aufguss.

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Inside size | Ø 4.6 m (15 ft) round. Ceiling 2.2 m at the bench line (1.2 m over the top bench), 2.9 m at the crown |
| Volume | ~40 m3 (1,427 cu ft). V2 Sunrise is ~650 cu ft, so this is about 2.2 of them |
| Benches | Lower ring EL +0.45 (7.8 m), upper ring EL +1.00 (11.6 m). At 0.70 m a person: 16 up + 11 down |
| Capacity | **15 comfortable**, ~25 packed. Code counts bench at 18 in a person (~42 here), so ask BDS which number governs |
| Heat (est.) | Install 28-40 kW (97-138K BTU/hr). Steady loss ~8 kW, most of it fresh air at 6 ACH |
| Stove | EOS custom gas core in a low centre stone altar, 250-400 kg of stone. Fallback: 2 x Torch TS-80F back to back (160K BTU, ~47 kW) inside one altar |
| Air | Intake low beside the stove, exhaust low on the far side, purge vent at the crown (the lodge vent hole) |
| Doors | Two. Glass door to the burrow (everyday) and a deck door. Out-swing, roller catch, no latch (Rose doors rule). 15 people is under the 49 that forces two exits, but two is better |
| Lining | Thermo-aspen/alder or the on-site cedar the V2 benches use. **No reclaimed dock wood inside the heat** |
| Floor | Tile on a slope to one drain at the burrow side (V2 tile lesson: pick the drain first) |
| Light | Warm LED under the upper bench. A 0.3 m triple-glazed oculus at the crown reads as the lodge vent from inside |

### C2 Stick shell (the lodge itself)
A real lodge is sticks and mud with a loose vent at the top. Ours is a rainscreen of pulled dock cedar laid like sticks over a rib frame, standing off the insulated hot room.

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Form | Half dome, base Ø 7.3 m (24 ft), 4.6 m (15 ft) tall. Real lodges run 1.5-3 m tall and up to 12 m across, so this is a big but believable lodge |
| Surface | ~99 m2 |
| Cedar needed | ~5.9 m3 solid at 60 mm effective = **~2,500 board ft**, about 2,500 lf of 2x6 dock board, ~2.8 t damp |
| Frame | 12 glulam or galvanized steel ribs on a ring beam, purlins at 400 mm, sticks screwed from the inside |
| Laying pattern | Random lengths 0.6-2.4 m, ends cut on a slant, crossing 15-25°. Tight at the base, 30-50% open near the top so it reads as the vent |
| Mud line | Bottom 0.9 m in charred (shou sugi ban) boards or corten, the dark "mud" band real lodges have |
| The face (Direction B) | Head carved over the deck door. Two big orange teeth are the door leaves: real beaver teeth are orange from iron in the enamel |
| Cavity | 0.4-1.3 m between shell and hot room. It hides the slide stair, services and the flue chase. Fire-block at the ring beam; mesh at the base for birds, rats and real beavers (they chew) |
| Reclaim process | Denail + metal-detect, pressure wash, sort A (sound, over 1.2 m) / B (shorts) / C (rot, out). Stockpile dry on stickers as the docks get replaced |
| Code | Cedar heartwood counts as decay-resistant within 18 in of water (Title 28.06.020). Exterior fire-retardant coating; keep sticks 18 in clear of the flue inside a steel vent collar |

### C3 Tail slide
The money shot. A flat, scaled beaver tail that leaves the lodge rump and curls into the pond. Same group-launch and curled-exit logic as the Rose slide petal.

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Plan shape | Paddle 9.8 m long, 3.0 m wide at the lanes, 3.5 m at the widest. Real tails run ~2.3:1; ours is ~3:1 for the ride |
| Launch | Rump deck at EL +3.60 (~4.0 m over the water), 1.2 m flat lip, grab bar |
| Ride | ~9.9 m, average 24°, steepest ~35° mid-ride, then a 2.3 m runout at 5° and a tip that curls 0.5 m under |
| Speed (est.) | ~8.1 m/s (18 mph) at the exit with water lube. That is fast; the slide maker sets the final launch height (3.2 m drops it ~10%) |
| Lanes | 3 lanes of 0.8-0.9 m. Groups of 2-3 launch together; a lifeguard at the lip calls each launch |
| Throughput | ~20 s per group, so a full round of 15 is in the water in ~2-3 min |
| Water feed | ~180 GPM (10,800 GPH) of pumped river water: two 8,000 GPH class pumps through an ODFW screen (3/32 in mesh, 0.2 ft/s). Runs only in slide hours |
| Build | GFRP shell, cedar-grey gel coat, cross-hatch scales molded into the rims only (the channel stays smooth). Galvanized steel frame. Water slide standard ASTM F2376 review by the maker |
| Depth | **2.4 m (8 ft) minimum at LOW water** under the tip zone, plus ~6 m clear of boats. Needs soundings |
| Climb | One-way stair inside the shell cavity, deck to rump, ~42° with treads for wet bare feet (not a ship ladder) |
| Year one | Custom inflatable tail (Rose slide study idea), ~$10-30K, stored Oct-Jun. Proves demand to the insurer |

### C4 Swim-in burrow
Real beavers enter their lodge underwater and surface in a dry den. Ours does the same through a moon pool cut in the float, with a dry way in too.

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Way 1: duck under | A 1.5 m wide gap in the skirt wall on the pond side. The skirt lip sits 0.45 m under the water, so you duck for about a second and come up inside |
| Moon pool | 1.5 x 2.4 m opening through the float, open to the river. Safety grate 1.8 m down so nobody goes under the hull |
| Way 2: ladder door | Stainless ladder at the float edge to a deck-level door. For anyone who will not duck, and the only way in out of season |
| Out of the water | 4 steps with 2 rails up to the burrow floor at EL 0.00 |
| The burrow | ~2.4 x 3.0 m warm wet room (~20-25°C, radiant panel). Hooks and towels. It is also the hot room vestibule, so the hot door never opens to cold air |
| Safety | Duck-under, never a tunnel: passage no longer than the skirt (~0.3 m). 12V LED strip on the lip. Grate openings under 25 mm. No pump intakes within 15 m. Lifeguard sightline from the hot-room door |
| Season | Swim-through open July-Sept with a lifeguard on. Out of season a stainless drop gate closes the gap |
| Winter mode | Gate shut, grate raised to 1.2 m on its hinges: the moon pool becomes an indoor river plunge in the burrow. Ice-hole energy, no swimming |
| Current + water | Gap on the downstream (lee) side. Rain rule: no contact 24-48 h after heavy rain until our own testing says otherwise |

### C6 Spillway + the pond
The dam makes the pond, the pond is the plunge, and the lodge sits in it. Same as nature.

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Spillway | Weir trough on the dam crest at EL +3.25, 0.6 m (2 ft) straight lip, ~2,000 GPH sheet, ~2.4 m drop. Raise the crest for a 7-8 ft drop if we want the full Foss |
| Control | Push button, 180 s cycle, same relay and pump class (Pond Boss PW8000 + a spare) as the waterfall plunge. Build the waterfall first and copy what works |
| Pond | Water held between dam, lodge and dock. Net sides + net floor at ~3 m (swim-zone plan, Oct 2026). Minimum 6.1 m (20 ft) across because of the Title 28 separation rule |
| Plunge steps | 4 log steps from the dam deck into the pond with a rail, plus a sitting ledge 0.6 m under water |
| August | Columbia surface water runs ~70°F late summer. The plunge feels cool, not cold. One chilled bucket line is the cheap fix |

### C7 Floats + mooring (Title 28)
The rules that shape every pathway. From the Title 28 brief.

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Float type | EPS foam pods in a timber/steel frame (~110 kg/m2, est.). Concrete like the Rose (~205 kg/m2) only if wakes demand the mass |
| Engineer | Oregon Engineer of Record stamps flotation + stability for every float (28.06.040.A). Use the same one as the marina Rose and V2 |
| Freeboard | 20 in minimum to an occupied floor. Every pathway lands at ~32-36 in (est.) |
| List | 4° max. Every pathway is under ~1.1° with 15 people crowded to one side (est.) |
| Separation | **20 ft wall to wall** between commercial floats (10 ft with 1-hr walls, 6 ft sprinklered). Two floats means a 20 ft gap: the pond |
| Walks | 36 in on two opposite sides of each float. Main walk 6 ft. Gangway 5 ft, 1:2.5 max |
| Mooring | Existing piles where they line up, otherwise diver-set helical anchors (swim-zone plan). No new piles: that drags in Army Corps Sec. 10 and the in-water window |
| Overlay | The "c" environmental overlay likely covers new floats. Put this on the BDS Early Assistance list |

### C8 Heat + gas
One gas run, two rooms.

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|

## Five pathways

### P1 The Pond: Two floats, the pond between them
**All in, est.:** $804K-$1,742K

**Idea.** The dam makes a pond and the lodge sits in it, like the real thing. Two separate floats, 20 ft apart, because Title 28 wants 20 ft between commercial floats. That gap is the pond. The tail slide drops into the pond, you swim to the lodge, duck under and come up in the burrow.

**Why.** The strongest story and the best loop. The separation rule turns into the feature instead of a cost.

**Give up.** Two floats means two engineering packages, two moorings and a longer gas run. Most money of the five except the Long Dam.

**Capacity.** 30 in heat at once (15 + 15), ~50 on site per session with people cooling

**Loop.** Gangway to the lodge deck, rinse, heat in the Lodge, climb the rump, slide into the pond, swim, duck under into the burrow (or swim over to the dam steps), heat in the Dam behind the falls, sit on the log steps, repeat.

### P5 Big Beaver: The sauna is inside a giant beaver
**All in, est.:** $772K-$1,647K

**Idea.** Skip the lodge. A 40 ft beaver lies on the water: you walk in through the orange teeth, the 15-person sauna is in the body, round eyes are the V2-style round windows, and the tail is the slide. The dam becomes a smaller steam room and the plunge.

**Why.** The single most shareable object on the Columbia. Oregon is the Beaver State; the photo sells itself.

**Give up.** Hardest to build (CNC ribs, a sculpted head), hardest to keep looking premium next to the Rose, and the joke can age. A lodge can be beautiful; a cartoon has to stay funny.

**Capacity.** 15 in the body + 8-10 in the dam steam room

**Loop.** In through the teeth, heat in the body, climb the rump, ride the tail, swim, ladder or burrow under the belly, steam in the dam, plunge, repeat.
