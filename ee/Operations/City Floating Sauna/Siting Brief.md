# Floating Sauna Siting — City Discussion Brief

_Last updated: 2026-06-10_

## Status
Ebb and Ember is in an active discussion with the City of Portland about potential **floating saunas** on the river. The city pointed Davey to its 2016 swimming-beach study as the analytical frame for in-water recreation in the Central City. **Open question: which river the next sauna actually goes on (Willamette Central City vs the Columbia, where the current operation sits).** The agency stack shifts between the two rivers.

## Source document
`Central City Potential Swimming Beach Sites Study (Oct 2016).pdf` (this folder). Commissioned by BPS + Parks + PDC, prepared by Mayer/Reed. It ranks 5 potential **public swimming beaches** on the **Willamette Central City** (not floating structures): Zidell bargeway, "Poetry at the Beach" (under Marquam Bridge), Hawthorne Bowl (RiverPlace), Eastbank Crescent, McCormick Pier.

## Talking points it hands us
- City's own words: the Big Pipe CSO project "lowered the contamination levels making the river safe for swimming and other recreational uses during most of the year."
- 87% of surveyed residents support public river beaches.
- City's stated priorities (= our design brief): low current velocity, accessibility into the river, shallow gradients, visibility for policing.

## Key in-water constraints (apply to a moored float)
- **Velocity:** beaches prohibited above 3 ft/sec. Summer near-shore 0–1 ft/sec; spikes to 5–7 ft/sec in floods and near bridge footings/sea-walls/structures.
- **Stage swing ~16 ft** seasonal + tidal range up to 3 ft (can reverse flow upstream). Mooring/gangway must ride it. Spring sits near ordinary high water, recedes after early July.
- **Swim window:** after early July to end of September (snowmelt-cold before). Cold + current = the "inherently dangerous" combo the study flags; this is our plunge-safety story.
- **Contamination caps — do not disturb:** DEQ Cleanup is lead. Zidell capped; Poetry's beach sand is itself a cap; **Eastbank Crescent under an active DEQ Record of Decision**. Anchors/spuds must not penetrate a cap.
- **Fish habitat / ESA:** all shallow nooks are "prime fish habitat." A floating sauna is an overwater shade structure → NMFS scrutiny (juvenile salmonid shading), in-water work window, overwater-coverage limits. Likely the hardest environmental gate.
- **Outfalls:** stormwater still discharges fall/winter/spring; no contact within 24h of rain. Don't moor at/just downstream of an outfall.
- **No-wake zones:** 200 ft near marinas/docks — a feature for us.

## Approval stack a float triggers that a beach doesn't
1. **Oregon Dept of State Lands (DSL)** — riverbed below ordinary low water is DSL property; a moored structure needs a DSL proprietary authorization/lease.
2. **US Army Corps, Section 10** (structure in navigable water); Section 404 if any fill.
3. **City Greenway land-use review, Title 33.440 Greenway Overlay Zone** (public trail both banks required).
4. **Coast Guard / navigation** clearance (stay clear of federal channel).
5. **DEQ + NMFS/ODFW** (cap + fish habitat).

## Siting recommendation (for a commercial moored sauna, if Willamette)
- **Next boat → Hawthorne Bowl / RiverPlace, moored to marina infrastructure (not the public beach).** Calmest pocket (breakwater + 200 ft no-wake), deep stable water, the only site clean of active environmental action, best downtown access/transit/parking, existing dock to tie into. Catch: it's inside Tom McCall Waterfront Park (Parks turf, boat conflict) — route through the marina, not the park beach.
- **Flagship someday → Zidell bargeway.** Best physical water (protected slipway, low current); South Waterfront Greenway Master Plan already names the slipway as a potential swim spot; Zidell Yards is master-planning now (development partner). But the barge-operation close timeline is "unknown" — too slow for the next boat given the V2 slip after Dustin's exit. Start the relationship now, aim for boat 3–4.
- **Avoid near-term:** Eastbank (active DEQ RoD), Poetry (on-beach stormwater discharge + nearby CSO outfall = weak plunge water).
