# The Rose: Community Funding Plan

_2026-09-27. How we fund the Rose with the community from day one: what we pre-sell, how we protect ourselves if the build slips, how sponsors buy into pieces of the design and the build itself, and how the whole thing sits under the Elevated Tides brand. Companion to the Design Brief (sections 11 and 12) and the Master Plan (section 6). Confidential while Holman is off the record._

**Raise target (Davey, 2026-09-29): $2.5-3.5M across every strategy.** Stack in the Parts Catalog doc, section 4.

**The one idea:** sell time and belonging, never dates. Every founding product is priced today and starts its clock the day the Rose opens. Money is refundable until the build is real, and if the Rose never opens, every holder gets made whole or better at the saunas we already run. That is how you keep the pressure without setting a trap.

---

## 1. Brand and structure: Elevated Tides leads

Davey's call: **Elevated Tides is the parent brand for the Rose and every project after it.** Ebb & Ember stays the name of the current boat (and of the experience inside the Rose if we want it), but the thing people fund, follow and sponsor is Elevated Tides.

What that means in practice:
- Everything outward-facing about the Rose says "an Elevated Tides project." Ebb & Ember gets a line in the credit ("the sauna ritual by Ebb & Ember"), not the masthead.
- The founding products are Elevated Tides products, good at every Elevated Tides site now and later. That is the reason the brand shift makes the funding easier: a member is buying into a garden, not a single flower.
- The site-naming system in section 6 uses parts of the rose, so each location reads as one plant.

**Three things that have to be settled before a dollar is taken under this name (attorney and Jonah, not marketing):**
1. **Who owns the mark.** "Elevated Tides" is the name of the marina LLC (Ohana 40 / Seaworthy 35 / Davey 25, Jonah sole Manager) and of ETE (three equal Members, Dustin exiting). The consumer brand needs a clear owner and a license to whichever entity sells the Rose products. Do not let the Rose brand end up owned by an entity Dustin still has a veto in.
2. **Which entity sells.** Deposits and memberships are liabilities of whoever takes the money. Pick the entity, open a separate bank account for founding money, and keep it out of operating cash until the Groundbreaking Gate (section 3).
3. **What we are allowed to sell.** Prepaid memberships, session credit and event rights are ordinary prepaid services. Anything that pays people back from revenue or gives them a share is a security and belongs in a Reg CF community round (Wefunder or similar) with its own paperwork. Keep the two piles separate in the language and in the bank.

## 2. What we pre-sell (the founding products)

Every product below has the same four rules:
- **Priced today, clock starts at opening.** Memberships and passes begin on the Rose's opening day, not the day you buy.
- **Good now at the current site.** Anything you buy for the Rose already works at the Columbia River boat at the same value. Nobody holds a coupon for something imaginary.
- **Refundable until the Groundbreaking Gate.** Full refund, no questions, until permits and financing close.
- **Made whole if it never opens.** After the Gate, the contingency in section 3 applies.

| Product | What it is | Price (draft) | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seed** (subscription) | $25-50/month, sliding scale, starts now. Every dollar banks 100% toward any founding product or toward sessions at the current site. Includes the build diary, votes on named decisions (scent, petal names, the opening ritual), first pick of dates. | $25 / $35 / $50 (pick your rung) | Cash flow and a live count we can publish. This is the "little pressure" product: a thousand people paying monthly is a number the City, the bank and the press all understand. |
| **Petal** (founding membership) | Locked-for-life 20-30% off every session at every Elevated Tides site. Clock starts at opening. Sliding scale inside one product. | $600 / $750 / $900 sliding | The workhorse. 500 units. The brief already drafts this; the sliding scale replaces "pick a tier" with "pay what fits." |
| **Bloom Card** (session pack) | 10 sessions at pre-opening prices, usable at the Rose or the current boat, no expiry until 24 months after the Rose opens. | ~$400-450 (about 25% off today's $59 peak) | For people who won't commit to a membership. Also the gift product. |
| **First Bloom** (private events) | The right to one of the first 52 Saturday evenings, or one of 100 weekday buyouts, in the opening year. Rough ceiling if roughly half the ladder sells (matches the brief's estimate): **$350-500K** in founding money, plus Seed subscriptions running at whatever the count reaches (1,000 Seeds at $35 average is ~$420K/yr of banked credit, most of which converts into Petals and Bloom Cards).

**What we do not sell:** anything with an opening date, month or year in it. The public promise is a sequence of gates, not a calendar. "We open the season after the last permit clears" is the whole timeline until it isn't.

## 3. The contingency: what happens if we're late or never build

This is the part that lets us take money early without lying to anyone. Write it into every product page in plain language.

**Gates, not dates.** Publish the gates and a live status: (1) team go, (2) site letter of intent signed, (3) naval architect study passed, (4) permits in hand, (5) financing closed = **Groundbreaking Gate**, (6) float launched, (7) opening. Founders see which gate we're at. Pressure comes from the count and the diary, not from a date we'll miss.

**Before the Groundbreaking Gate:** every dollar refundable on request, no fee. Seed subscriptions cancel any time and keep their banked credit.

**After the Groundbreaking Gate:** money is committed to the build, so refunds stop, but a backstop starts. If the Rose is not open **36 months after the Gate**, every holder chooses one of:
1. Full refund, or
2. Convert to credit at the current Elevated Tides sites at **120%** of what they paid, or
3. Roll into the next Elevated Tides site (the Bud, or whichever is next) with the same founding terms.

**Fine print that protects us:** the credit is only usable at an Elevated Tides site we operate; the 36-month clock resets if a permit is denied and we relocate (holders can refund at that moment instead); founding discounts apply to the standard session menu, not to buyouts or events; transfers are allowed once, with notice.

**The pressure valve (Davey's "a little pressure is good"):** the public count is a promise we make to ourselves. Publish Seeds, Petals and First Blooms sold, weekly, in the build diary. When the number is big enough, it becomes the argument to the City and the bank. It also becomes embarrassing not to build. That is the point.

## 5. The climbing wall bid: how to run it

Davey's idea: put the wall out to competitive bid among Portland's climbing gyms, let them fight for it, then quietly hand the second Rose's wall to the runner-up as a surprise.

**Roster to verify before outreach** (confirm each is still open and who runs partnerships): Movement Portland (the former Planet Granite), Portland Rock Gym, The Circuit Bouldering Gym (several Portland locations), Stoneworks (Beaverton), Source Climbing Center (Vancouver, WA). Gear brands can bid too (Metolius is an Oregon company; a hold maker or shoe brand could pair with a gym).

**Format: a scored bid, not a pure auction.** Cash alone rewards the deepest pocket. Score on:
- Cash (floor set after engineering; say the wall's hard cost plus a margin)
- In-kind: holds, wall panels, route setting for the first two seasons
- Operations: certified supervisors during climb hours, staff training, an insurance rider or shared program
- Story: a members' cross-benefit (their members get a Rose intro; ours get a gym day), a first-ascent event, a youth program

**Process:**
1. One-page invitation to every gym on the roster at the same hour, with the same packet (renders, the element brief, the scoring, a deadline 4-6 weeks out).
2. Q&A window, then sealed bids. Tell them there will be one wall on this Rose and one sponsor. True.
3. Announce the winner publicly with the wall's name ("the ___ wall").
4. **After** the announcement, call the runner-up privately: the next Elevated Tides site gets a wall, and it's theirs at their bid if they still want it. Do this only once the second site is real enough to describe. Never say it during the bid, and never run a bid for a wall we don't intend to build.

**Guardrails:** everything about the wall depends on the insurer and the engineer (the Master Plan flags both). Run the bid after the broker call and the naval architect's read on the jump petal, or bid it as "subject to engineering" and say so.

## 6. Naming the garden: parts of the rose

Davey's direction: drop North / South / East / West. Elevated Tides is the brand, each location is named for a part of the plant, and each location leans into what that part means.

The rose already gives us the vocabulary, and the Design Brief already uses some of these words for elements inside the Rose (the Thorn was the small dark sauna, the Bud the across-river site, the pistil the stove). If the parts become site names, the elements inside the Rose take descriptive names instead ("the small sauna," "the stove," "the slide"). One word, one meaning.

| Site name | What the part is | What that location leans into | Candidate site |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Elevated Tides: The Rose** | The flower itself | The flagship. The full bloom: the big hot room, the jumps, the slide, weddings | Holman (1.0) or the bigger site (2.0) |
| **The Bud** | The flower before it opens | The compact one. Closed, intimate, small groups, the "before" | Across the river from the Rose, or Rose 1.0 if the full Rose goes elsewhere |
| **The Thorn** | The defense | The hot one. Small, dark, brutal heat, cold plunge outside the door. Members and locals | A pocket site, or the current Columbia River dock |
| **The Pistil** | The heart, where the seed forms | The gathering one. Built around the stove and the ritual, Aufguss, the sound rounds | Wherever the second big room goes |
| **The Hip** (rosehip) | The fruit after the bloom | The cold one. Plunges, the river swim, the waterfall plunge, recovery | A swim-forward site |
| **The Stem** | What carries the flower | The moving one. Slides, boom walks, paddle-in, the water route between sites | Attached to the Rose, or a river-run concept |
| **The Leaf** | Where the plant breathes | The rest one. Floats, sun, quiet, no heat at all | Swim floats and lounges |
| **The Root** | Underground, unseen | Land base: workshop, changing, the office, the build yard | The gravel lot or a shop |

Other words in the bank if we need more: Sepal, Calyx, Cane, Graft, Bloom, Petal. Keep Rosarium or The Garden as the collection name for the whole set.

Recommendation for the first three: **The Rose** (flagship), **The Thorn** (rename the current boat at the marina, since it is already the small hot one with cold water outside the door), and **The Bud** (the second site). Attorney clears all of it before anything public, same as the brief says for the Rose name itself.

## 7. Helping build it: community hands

Sponsors buy pieces. Members help make them. Both are content and both are loyalty.

- **Build Days.** Members and Seeds sign up for finish work only: oiling thermo-wood, sanding, installing holds under supervision, planting the sepals, painting the petal skins with the muralist. Never structural, never in the hot room's load path. Waivers, a site lead, and the insurer's OK first.
- **Founders' wall.** Every Seed, Petal and above gets a name on it (or a rose variety if they'd rather stay private). Build it early so people can see their name go up before the Rose is done.
- **Commissions** (brief section 18) stay as designed: artists on the fire roses, the stone cage, the skins, the leaves, the lighting, the scent, the sound. Announce each as a build-diary chapter.
- **Trades as sponsors.** Timber, rubber, electrical, marine. In-kind credit against the element bands above, plus the credit line.
- **The build diary** is the engine for all of it. Grant's V2 diary already proved the format works for us. Weekly, from day one, including the boring parts.
