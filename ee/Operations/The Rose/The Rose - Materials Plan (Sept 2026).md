# The Rose: Materials Plan v0.1

_2026-09-27. Working list, no quotes yet. Every material here has to survive four things at once: a 90C hot room or Portland weather, bare wet feet, the river, and a fire marshal. Prices are rough estimates, not bids._

## The rubber Davey asked about

Playground surfacing is **poured-in-place rubber** (PIP): a base layer of shredded tire rubber (SBR) under a 3/8 to 1/2 in wear layer of colored EPDM granules, mixed with polyurethane binder and troweled on site. Its cousins are EPDM rubber tiles and pool-deck rubber.

**Where it works for us:**
- Amphitheater steps and jump-petal treads, the deck around the fire nooks, the slide's stair and landing, the rooftop lounge, the leaf floats. Soft on bare feet, high grip wet, drains through, comes in any color (rose pink and calyx green are stock EPDM colors), and it's a seamless coat, so it follows curved petal shapes.
- It can be troweled over a plywood or composite substrate on the petal shells, which is exactly the curved geometry we have.

**Where it doesn't:**
- Anywhere over ~70C. It softens and the binder off-gasses. **Never inside the hot room.**
- On the slide channel itself. Too much friction; sliding needs a hard, smooth surface.
- Under the fire rings. Keep a 1 m stone or steel apron around every fire.
- Direct sun in July: dark EPDM gets hot underfoot. Use light colors on sun-facing decks, or the aliphatic (UV-stable) binder so pink stays pink.

**Numbers:** roughly $10-18 per sq ft installed for 1.5-2 in PIP, less for tiles. Life 8-12 years before a recoat. Local installers exist (Portland has several playground surfacing contractors). Spec: ASTM F1292 impact rating, ASTM F1951 accessibility, aliphatic binder for color hold.

**Alternatives worth a sample:** cork rubber (warmer look, less grip when wet), Marine-grade EVA foam decking (SeaDek-type, what boats use, cheaper, glued sheets, less durable), and thermally modified ash decking with grooves (wood feel, less grip, needs oiling).

## Three things to decide before quotes
1. **Petal skin:** charred cedar (warm, Oregon, needs upkeep), GFRP (smooth, colored, the same trade as the slide, so one fabricator does both), or printed ASA panels (the story, unproven outdoors at this size). My pick: GFRP for the slide, jump petal and sepals; charred cedar or GFRP for the amphitheaters, chosen after a sample day.
2. **Structure of the outer petals:** steel (a marine fabricator, easier curves) or glulam (timber story, needs a good CNC shop). My pick: steel ribs with timber where hands touch.
3. **Rubber color:** one green for calyx and leaves, one pink for petal treads, and sample both in sun before committing.
