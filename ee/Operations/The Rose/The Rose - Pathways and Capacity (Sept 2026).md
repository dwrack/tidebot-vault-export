# The Rose: Pathways, Ingress, Egress and Capacity

2026-09-30 · circulation pass on the full Rose. Measured from the model code at v0.40/v0.41 (live file on the always-on Mac). Every headcount is *est.* until a code consultant and the naval architect see it. Code numbers are a rough IBC lens (Oregon's code is IBC-based); a floating assembly building may also pull in marine rules, so treat them as a first read, not a ruling.

Related: [[The Rose - Crown and DJ Booth Spec (Sept 2026)]] (crown cap, dance floor, service stair), [[The Rose - Design Brief (Sept 2026)]] (decisions 43, 47, 55, 71), [[The Rose - Master Plan (Sept 2026)]].

---

## 0. The short version

- **The float deck has no walkway around the flower.** Each fire rose sits about 0.5 m off the hot-room wall, so there is no lane past a petal. The only flat deck is five separate wedges between the petals (the clefts), roughly 25-30 m2 in total. To get from one wedge to the next you walk through an amphitheater, past its fire.
- **So the gangway can't reach the stem.** You land at door D2 (south). The new stem root at G2 (northwest) is two petals away, through P5 and P4.
- **The crown works at a cap of 40,** as the crown spec says, but T1 (about 1 m wide) is its only stair. That's legal only while the crown stays under 50 people, and it's still a poor rescue route.
- **There's one way to land:** the 1.2 m gangway to the public dock. It's wide enough, but it's the only way off.
- **Door D3 is a pile-up.** The hot-room door, the kids' sepal slide and the foot of the jump-petal ramp all land in the same cleft.
- **The leaves are swim-only.** The stalks are 0.3 m tubes, not walkways. So a boat tied at leaf L2 can't walk anyone to the stem.
- **Fix 1 (recommended): an inner lane.** A 1.1 m flat ring between the hot room and the fires, all the way round. It sits inside the flower, so the silhouette doesn't change.

## 1. Getting on and off

| Route | Width | Serves | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 gangway, Rose to the dock tip | 1.2 m (47 in) | Everyone, every day | Lands at D2's cleft (south). The only way to land |
| Public dock to the Esplanade | 3.2 m, ~60 m long | Everyone | Shared with the public |
| Stem walkway | 1.5 m, ~120 m | Walk out to the leaves | **Starts under P3, unreachable** (fix: root at G2). Ends at the last leaf, so it's no route to land |
| Boat pull-up at leaf L2 + stem cleats | n/a | Boaters | Cleats on the walkway work. The L2 leaflets don't connect to anything walkable |
| Swimmers: kids' sepal G6, slide landing, leaflet ladders | n/a | Cove swimmers | G6 is the only climb-out on the flower itself, and it's in the D3 pile-up |

**Egress read (est.):** the gangway's 47 in clears roughly 235 people by width (0.2 in each). Width isn't the problem. The problem is that there's **one path**: the far side of the flower (P3's rim) is about 15 m from the gangway head, and then comes the ~70 m walk to shore along one dock. On land, anything over 49 people needs two ways out. Worth asking the code consultant early whether a floating venue can count the stem, a rescue boat or the fireboat pier as a second way off.

**Option worth weighing:** run the stem walkway the full ~150 m to the Station 21 fireboat pier as a **gated emergency and fire-access route**. That gives a second way to land and gives the fire bureau a way in. It reverses decision 55 (walkway ends at the last leaf), and it needs the fire bureau's OK.

## 2. Moving around the float

**What's there (model):**
- The float is 15 m across (R 7.5 m = 49 ft; the brief targets 55 ft).
- The hot-room wall is lobed: 3.9 m out at the clefts, 4.8 m out behind each petal.
- The fire roses sit at ~5.4 m, centered on each petal. The amphitheater steps start 0.8 m from the fire and run out past the float edge.
- So behind every petal the hot-room wall is the fire's back wall. **There's no lane.**

**The five flat pockets (clefts):**

| Cleft | Faces | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| G2, NW | River, Hawthorne | New stem root (proposed) |
| D1, W | River | Hot-room door, screened by P6 |
| D2, S | Dock | **Gangway lands here.** Hot-room door, screened by P7 |
| D3 / G6, E-SE | Cove | Hot-room door, kids' sepal slide, foot of the P1 ramp (v0.41). **Pile-up** |
| G5, NE | Cove | Between the bridge petal and the jump petal. Jump landing water nearby |

Each pocket is ~1.3 m wide at the hot-room wall, opening to ~2 m at the float edge, about 4-6 m2. That's enough for a door and a few people, not a lobby.

**Where does the next round wait?** Nowhere yet. With 40-48 people per round, the next round and the one just out of the heat have no holding space except the amphitheaters themselves.

### Fix 1 (recommended): an inner lane

- A **1.1 m (44 in) flat ring** between the hot room and the fire roses, all the way around.
- Get it by flattening the hot-room lobes (0.45 m → ~0.15 m) and moving each fire rose out ~0.8 m. The benches are circular and sit inside the lobes, so **no hot-room seats are lost**.
- To keep the petals' outer edge where it is, shorten each amphitheater's run. That costs roughly **5-8 seats per amphitheater (est.)**.
- What you get: the gangway reaches every door, the stem root and the kids' slide without passing a fire. Staff can loop the flower. The lane doubles as the waiting line, and a rescue can carry someone from any door to the gangway.
- Everything changes inside the flower. From the water and the bridge it still reads as the same rose (form over code).

### Fix 2: split D3

- Move D3 one cleft over to G5 (NE), or move the kids' slide to G5 and leave D3 alone.
- Either way, don't land the P1 ramp foot, a door and a kids' slide on the same 5 m2.

## 3. Inside the hot room

| Item | Model | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Seats | ~57 drawn (brief target 40-48) | Three tiers at radii 2.0-2.6, 2.6-3.2, 3.2-3.75 m. About 54 m of bench in total |
| Doors | 3, each 0.9 m, all at clefts | Over 49 people needs 2 exits. 3 is good. Out-swinging, no latches |
| Pit floor | Ring from the stove (r 0.95) to the first tier (r 2.0), 0.6 m below the deck | ~1 m wide, where the Aufguss host works |
| Getting to a seat | From each door, step down through the tiers to the pit | **Stepped aisles aren't drawn yet.** Three aisles cost ~6-9 seats, so a realistic count is ~48-51 |

**The capacity math:** at the code's 18 in per person, 54 m of bench counts as ~110. A code official might set the room's load at that number, not at your ticket cap. Ask the code consultant which one governs a sauna.

**The flow each round:** 40-48 enter through 3 doors (~15 each), and all of them leave at once when the round ends. The three doors lead into three different pockets, and without the inner lane those pockets don't connect. So people coming out of D1 and D2 can't reach the cove (the swim side) without crossing an amphitheater. **This is the strongest argument for Fix 1.**

## 8. Doors: heat vs flow (added 2026-09-30)

Davey: doors should be easy to open so little heat escapes, plus a larger second door so a whole round can move through quickly.

**How much heat an open door loses.** A doorway between a hot room and the outside acts like a chimney turned sideways: hot air pours out the top half, cold air pours in the bottom. Using the standard doorway exchange formula (discharge coefficient 0.6, door 2.03 m tall, room air ~60 K warmer than outside):

| Door | Air swapped | Heat lost while open |
|---|---|---|
| 0.9 m single | ~0.7 m3/s | ~43 kW (~146K BTU/hr) |
| 1.8 m pair | ~1.4 m3/s | ~85 kW (~290K BTU/hr), about 80% of the ~360K BTU/hr stove |

Loss scales with door height to the 1.5 power, so a 1.8 m door loses ~16% less than a 2.03 m one. Code likely needs 80 in, though.

**How fast people get through (est.).** Fire-engineering flow is ~1.3 people per second per metre of *effective* width (clear width minus 0.15 m each side). Barefoot, heat-flushed people stepping up out of a pit move slower, so I use half that:

| Door | Practical flow | 48 people out |
|---|---|---|
| 0.9 m | ~0.4 per second | ~2 min |
| 1.8 m | ~1.0 per second | ~50 s |

**Round change, today vs proposed (48 out, 48 in, est.):**

| | Today: 3 x 0.9 m, both directions | Proposed: 1.8 m out, 2 vestibule doors in |
|---|---|---|
| Turnover time | ~80 s (out then in through the same doors) | **~60 s** (out and in at the same time, no counterflow) |
| Heat lost | ~10.4 MJ (~9,800 BTU) | **~5.7 MJ (~5,400 BTU)** |
| Burner time to make it back | ~2 min | ~1 min |

During a round, ~20 single-door openings (latecomers, early leavers) at ~5 s each lose ~4.3 MJ. **Vestibules cut most of that**, because the outer door and inner door are never open together. The seated guests feel the vestibule far more than the round change: the round change happens when the room is empty anyway.

**The design:**
- **D1, D2: everyday doors.** 0.9 m, light, glazed so you see who's coming, no latch (roller catch plus soft closer; that also avoids panic-hardware rules), swing out. Each opens into a **vestibule tucked behind its screen petal (P6, P7)**, about 1.5-2 m deep, room for 3-4 people. The silhouette doesn't change.
- **The petal door: a round-change door.** ~1.8 m pair of leaves on the cove side, opened by the host at the end of each round, straight out to **cold steps** into the river. It's also the big egress door. A pair of petal-shaped leaves opening outward is a nice moment on its own: the flower opens and the round spills into the water.
- **The sunken pit helps.** Cold air that falls in through a door runs down to the pit, where the stove is, instead of pooling at people's feet. Put the doors so that cold stream falls toward the stove, not onto the lowest bench.
- **The real limit is on either side of the door.** The petal door needs a ~1.5 m stepped aisle inside and a wide deck to the water outside (the current 5 m2 pocket won't do). With the inner lane at 1.1 m, the lane itself would cap flow at ~0.5 per second.
- **Worth pricing:** a heat- and moisture-rated automatic operator on D1/D2 (touch-free, 2-3 s hold-open) so nobody props a door. Verify a rated unit exists before counting on it.

## 9. The land side (added 2026-09-30)

Arrival, check-in, lockers, changing, showers and toilets go **on land above the dock** (Davey). The program, the guest journey and the to-do list live in the Master Plan, section 9. What it changes here:
- The float doesn't need changing space. It needs a **robe drop**, a **towel station**, and ideally **1-2 toilets at the water**.
- The flow onto the Rose arrives in pulses (a session's worth at once), so the D2 pocket and gangway head need queueing room, or the land side meters people down in groups.

## 10. Decisions for Davey

1. **Inner lane (Fix 1):** 1.1 m flat ring between the hot room and the fire roses, costing ~5-8 amphitheater seats each. My pick: yes.
2. **D3 pile-up:** move the door to G5, or move the kids' slide to G5?
3. **Stem root at G2:** confirm, built together with the inner lane so the gangway can reach it.
4. **Stem to the fire pier:** keep the walkway ending at the last leaf (decision 55), or run it the full way as a gated emergency and fire-access route?
5. **Walkable leaf stalks:** yes (non-swimmers, boat step-off at L2) or keep the leaves swim-only?
6. **Crown service stair:** still open from the crown spec. Needed before the crown goes over 49.
7. **Round cap:** work to ~100 on the flower per round until the naval architect sets the real number?
8. **Model it:** draw 1-3 as the next version (v0.42; another session already published v0.41 for the jump ramp)?
9. **Doors:** vestibules in P6/P7 + a ~1.8 m petal door on the cove side for round changes (my pick), or a 1.8 m pair at every door?
10. **Big-door side:** D1 (west) straight into the cold petal (my pick since the cold side moved to P5, Master Plan section 10), or the cove side?
