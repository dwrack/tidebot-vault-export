Rose Senses

The first piece split off the Rose: how it looks lit, how it sounds, what you see from it and how it looks from the river, and what it feels like on skin and underfoot. One rule runs through all four: almost nothing electronic survives the hot room, so the gear lives in a cool ring under the benches or outside the wall, and only glass fiber, wood and a few 120 °C parts go inside.

Sense 1 · Light

Light: warm inside, a lantern outside

Inside, low and amber with no blue, lit from the side so the ribs throw shadows. Outside, the flower glows on the river and can change color for the night.

Hot room H1

  • Cariitti glass fiber, 2700 K. The fibers take 180 °C, and the projector sits outside the heat.
  • A star ceiling of 75-100 fiber tails for night löyly.
  • One point source at the foot of every rib, aimed up its side. A light in the center casts no rib shadows, so the light has to come from the sides.
  • Sundial chase: cross-fade the grazers around the ring once per 12-minute round, so the shadows sweep across the ceiling.
  • A warm uplight at the stove turns each pour into a lit column of steam.
  • A dim gobo projector outside the window throws river ripples into the room.

Crown C1-C4

  • Low and warm: step lights at the landing, a glow line under the bench ring, no overhead light.
  • A few small IP65 color fixtures in the parapet wash up into the DJ roof.
  • The leaded rose window over the booth (C3) glows at night. From the bridge it reads as the flower's heart.
  • One small hazer for beams on still nights. No lasers.

Petals and deck P1-P5

  • Five fire roses. Blackened steel holds a warm glow; polished stainless throws moving flecks across the steps.
  • Grazing spots at the base of each petal throw its rib shadows up the inside face.
  • Every rim and step edge lit low for safe footing after dark.

From outside whole flower

  • Outer petals in translucent GFRP, 4-6 mm, tinted pale. RGBW strips along every rib inside the skin, so the petal glows and the ribs read as veins.
  • Presets from the show desk: Rose for a normal night, Ember for Aufguss, Violet for a set, Sage in winter.
  • Uplights under the float edge, so the calyx floats on its own glow and the river doubles the flower.
  • A low green line along the stem to the last leaf.

Watch-outs

  • Navigation lights. No flashing red or green visible from the channel; those are navigation signals. A steady rose glow is probably fine. Confirm with Coast Guard Sector Columbia River in permitting.
  • Flicker. Slow drift on the stove zone only. Fast flicker reads as nightclub or Halloween.
  • DMX on Cariitti isn't confirmed. Plan 0-10 V dimmers driven by a DMX pack, or a DMX-native theatrical fiber illuminator outside the room.
  • Glare from stainless. Polished fire roses reflect best and also glare. Sample both finishes at night.

Decisions

  1. Exterior color: full RGBW color-change petals, or one fixed rose glow with color kept for events?
  2. Fire rose finish: blackened steel, polished stainless, or one of each for a side-by-side season?
  3. Osmosis test petal: build one two-layer petal with dyed water pumped through it (slow, alive, costly), or try thermochromic paint first?
  4. Night light from the bridge: should the hot room glow be visible from the bridge (a lantern) or kept dim behind the glass (privacy)? See V3.
Sense 2 · Sound

Sound: close, many, and felt

Many small speakers close to people, with the bass carried through the benches and the deck. Quiet in the air, strong in the body.

Hot room acoustics H1

  • Target reverb 0.5-0.7 s at any occupancy, so a host's voice carries without a mic.
  • Bare wood is about 1.3 s empty. About 35 m² of slatted thermo-wood over 50-100 mm stone wool brings it to about 0.7 s.
  • Round rooms focus sound. Facet the lobe walls in flat 1-1.5 m panels, never smooth arcs. That is the biggest single fix.
  • Ceiling ribs at irregular spacing as diffusers. The stone altar scatters the focal point.
  • Ceiling absorbers as removable cassettes, tuned after a sine-sweep test.

Hot room system H1

  • A ring of zones, one or two speakers per petal. Pan a sound petal to petal and it travels around the room.
  • Inside: EOS 945429, the only speaker rated for 120 °C, mounted low.
  • Or ordinary install speakers in the cool cavity under tier 1, firing through slotted risers with 10-15 mm gaps.
  • House level 60-65 dBA. A decibel meter on the host's iPad.

Crown C1 C2

  • About 95 dBA at the center of the floor during sets, 75-80 at sunset.
  • 6 weatherproof point sources in the parapet petals, aimed in and down 30-40°, with 3-4 m throws, so level drops fast past the rail.
  • 2 subs in the booth base as a cardioid pair, cancelling bass toward downtown and the condos.
  • 8-12 transducers under the dance floor, 6-8 under the bench ring.

The flower as an instrument idea

  • David's note: Tuntu, the sauna instrument. What on the Rose could be played?
  • Candidates: the pistil cages as tuned stamens, a sound bowl struck through the bench backs, the cascade under the slide petal.
  • Anything inside the hot room needs a heat rating, so start with the cages, which are steel anyway.

Watch-outs

  • The float is a drum. Bass that reaches the hull turns the building and the water into a subwoofer. Elastomer pads under every bench frame and transducer block.
  • The crown sits on the hot room. Float the crown deck on isolation pads, or footsteps and subs drum on the ceiling.
  • Sound carries over water. Downtown is 300 m across the river. I-5 masks the east side; complaints would come from the west. Amplified sets probably need a noise variance, so raise it at the pre-app.
  • Cavity heat. Every cool cavity gets intake air and a sensor that mutes the amps past about 55 °C.

Decisions

  1. In-room speakers: EOS 120 °C speakers on the wall, or drivers hidden in the cool cavities behind the risers (cheaper, invisible, slightly duller)?
  2. Crown sets in the hot room: can the show desk send the DJ set into the hot room at low level, or does the hot room always stay its own world?
  3. Instrument commission: brief a sound artist on the pistil cages as tuned stamens?
  4. Acoustic consultant: book the ray-trace ($3-10K) now, or once the hot room geometry is frozen?
Sense 3 · View

View: what you see, and who sees you

Two directions. Views out to the bridge, downtown and the sunset. And the Rose itself, seen from the bridge deck, the Esplanade and across the river.

First sightline check (from the brief's numbers, confirm in the model)

The benches face the stove, so the bridge window sits behind the people on its own petal. Everyone else sees it across the room. In the section above, a seated top-tier guest on the far side looks over the heads on the window petal's top tier and sees only the upper part of the glass: sky and the bridge's upper structure, not the water.

The guest directly across from the window loses it behind the stove. Seats to either side see around the stove, which is about 1.4 m wide. Tier 1 sits lowest, so it sees the least.

So "a seated view of the Hawthorne from every tier" doesn't hold yet. Three ways to fix it are in V1.

From the hot room H3

  • One window, 2.4 m wide, from 0.9 to 2.3 m, facing the Hawthorne 110 m downstream.
  • Heat-rated laminated tempered glass, tilted 5-8° so it doesn't flutter-echo with the opposite wall.
  • The north slide petal has a glass base, so the window keeps its view under it.

From the crown C1 C2

  • Seated behind a 1.5 m parapet you see sky and the bridge tops. The bench faces in, toward the floor.
  • Standing, you see the whole river: the Hawthorne lift towers, downtown, the Marquam and the Tilikum upstream.
  • From the booth, the DJ sees faces, then flame, then the Hawthorne towers.

From the petals P3 P4 P5

  • P3, the bridge petal, faces north to the Hawthorne.
  • P4, the west lounge, faces the river and downtown.
  • Sunset on the east bank: about 305° (northwest) at the June solstice, about 235° (southwest) in December. In summer the sun sets between the bridge and downtown, so P3 and P4 share it. In winter it moves south toward the Marquam.

The Rose, seen from outside

  • From the Hawthorne deck, 110 m off, looking down into the crown and the petals.
  • From the Eastbank Esplanade, close up on the same bank.
  • From the downtown waterfront, 300 m across the river. At dusk, this is the photo that sells it.
  • The Bud on the west bank glows back.

Watch-outs

  • Being seen. At night, a lit hot room behind clear glass is a lit stage for anyone on the bridge. That can be the lantern effect or a privacy problem.
  • Sightline from the water hasn't been checked. It's an open item in the Master Plan: one site visit with a phone camera at seated eye height.
  • Swimwear and the bridge. The crown and petals are visible from the bridge deck by day. That's fine for a public bathing culture, but say so up front in the pitch.

Decisions

  1. Fix the window view: (a) take the glass up to the ceiling, (b) make the window petal's top tier a standing alcove so nobody sits in front of the glass, or (c) accept that it's a sky-and-bridge view, not a water view.
  2. A second window? One facing downtown and the sunset (P4 side), so the far side of the room gets a view too?
  3. Privacy glass: clear, a fine rose-pattern frit band at standing eye height, or keep the interior dim enough after dark that the bridge sees glow, not people?
  4. Site visit: do the sightline walk this month (bridge deck, Esplanade, downtown bank, one point on the water)?
Sense 4 · Feel

Feel: heat, touch, air, and the music in the wood

Everything your skin and feet meet: the heat by tier, the wood you sit on, the rubber you walk on, the bass you feel through the bench, and a cool ring of scented air at the peak.

Heat H1 H2

  • Tier 1 about 70-75 °C, for first-timers. Tier 2 about 80-85 °C. Tier 3 about 90-100 °C.
  • Air at the ceiling runs 95-100 °C or more. Under the lowest bench it stays 35-50 °C, and that's where the gear goes.
  • Gas core from EOS (KUSATEK line), stones in steel pistil cages.
  • Fresh air comes in low by the stove and leaves low under the benches, about 9-12 L/s per person.

Touch materials

  • Benches: thermally modified aspen or alder, slats 40 mm minimum, screws from below. No cedar inside; resin and off-gassing at 90 °C.
  • Decks and steps outside: poured rubber (PIP) with an aliphatic binder. Soft and grippy wet, any color. Never over about 70 °C.
  • Slide: gel-coated GFRP, hard and smooth.
  • Door handles: hand-forged, rose-carved, a blacksmith commission.
  • Drapes: silicone-coated fiberglass, which wipes clean and holds no odor.

Music you feel H1 C1

  • Clark Synthesis AW339 transducers, the only kind we found sold for wet, humid installs.
  • 15 zones minimum (5 petals × 3 tiers), up to about 30 units at one per 4 × 4 ft of bench.
  • Bolted to blocking under each tier, never to the slats, with a drip shield above.
  • Body-feel range 30-120 Hz. A wave can roll petal to petal or climb tier to tier.
  • We found no large sauna anywhere doing per-seat tactile audio.

Air and scent H1

  • The signature moment: a vortex cannon outside the wall fires a cool scented ring through a port at the top tier. Cool air landing on skin at 90 °C.
  • Löyly is the main scent, dosed by EOS AromaTec II (1-3 essences) from the show controller.
  • Palette: Douglas fir, cedarleaf (sparing), rose geranium at the peak, petrichor as a cool ring, peppermint at the fade. In winter, a trace of birch tar.
  • Outside: wind from the NNW in summer, the cold Gorge wind from the ESE in winter.

Watch-outs

  • No glycol fog, no neat oil on stones. Oil on 300 °C stones breaks down into formaldehyde and acrolein, and eucalyptus flashes below the room's air temperature. Water-diluted only.
  • Wood soaks up scent. One house palette at a time, a 5-10 minute purge between sessions, and one fragrance-free session a day.
  • Dark rubber in July sun gets hot underfoot. Light colors on sun-facing decks.
  • Fire roses warm the view, not the top step. Reflector petals plus warm tread strips on the upper steps.

Decisions

  1. Bench wood: thermo-aspen or thermo-alder? Order one board of each and sit on both at 90 °C.
  2. Tactile density: 15 zones (one per petal per tier, softer coverage) or about 30 units at Clark's full density?
  3. Scent Cannon: talk to the Maine outfit about a custom install or a launch rental, after the $300 test cannon proves the idea?
  4. House scent: commission a signature Rose scent from a perfumer, the way AIRE did with orange blossom?
