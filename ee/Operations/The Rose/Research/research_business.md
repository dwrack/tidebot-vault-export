# The Rose: Business Research for the Design Brief

_Compiled 2026-09-26. Web research plus vault context (Holman Dock Pilot Plan, Siting Brief, Corporate & Buyout Draft, Event Partnership Framework, Revenue Forecast, Sauna Viral Research). Holman Dock is confidential. Nothing was contacted, sent, or submitted._

Where a number couldn't be verified it says so. My estimates are labeled **[est]**.

## 0. Flags to read first

1. **Build-in-public vs. Holman Dock confidentiality.** The Pilot Plan says the project is "off the record until the full team approves the strategy." A public build-in-public campaign that names the site would break that, and it would also get ahead of the City. Suggested order: (a) team approval, (b) LOI signed, (c) City comms aligned, and only then (d) a public reveal. Before that, the teaser can say "a rose is coming to the Willamette" without naming a site. The standing "no founder names publicly" rule (Dustin buyout) also applies to any founder-led build content.
2. **Geography check.** Holman Dock is on the east bank **just south of the Hawthorne Bridge** ([City ordinance 191700](https://www.portland.gov/council/documents/ordinance/passed/191700); [Portland Mercury](https://www.portlandmercury.com/news/2016/04/01/17807138/the-popular-dock-near-the-hawthorne-bridge-isnt-going-away-this-summer-after-all)). Its immediate neighbor is Live Nation's **Steelhead** venue at Water Ave between Salmon and Main (3,500 cap, opens **summer 2027**) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Nation_Entertainment_venue_in_Portland,_Oregon)). OMSI and Tilikum Crossing sit roughly half a mile to three-quarters of a mile downriver-south. The Hawthorne Bridge is the foreground, and Tilikum is a distant backdrop that shows up in shots looking south. Renders should reflect that, and the copy shouldn't say "next to OMSI."
3. **Contamination.** The ordinance notes possible pre-existing contamination at the dock "with cleanup costs potentially reaching millions of dollars." The Siting Brief also flags Eastbank Crescent, the stretch just south of there, as under an active DEQ Record of Decision. Anchors, spuds, or slide footings must not penetrate a cap. Everything should ride on the existing dock and piles.
4. **The City has a funding gap we can solve.** Prosper gave PP&R $270K of restricted maintenance money against an estimated **$74,255/yr** operating cost, which is about 3.6 years of runway. The dock's DSL submerged-land license **expires Sept 7, 2034** ([ordinance 191700](https://www.portland.gov/council/documents/ordinance/passed/191700)). A paying operator that takes on dock O&M is the City's cleanest answer. That's our lease leverage.

- **Our existing framework** (Event Brand Partnership Framework, Aug 2026) prices per-event tiers: Presenting $6K / Featured $3.5K / Supporting $2K, with a 100%-of-production-funded floor rule. The Rose can extend that to **season or structure** partners:
  - **Material partner** (the Hydro model): a Pacific NW timber, steel, glass, or lighting brand supplies petals, cladding, or LEDs in-kind in exchange for the case study and content. **[est]** $25K to $150K in-kind.
  - **Named element**, e.g. "The Stem, presented by X," a petal deck, or the sound or scent system. **[est]** $15K to $50K/yr cash.
  - **Season presenting partner** for the whole Rose program. **[est]** $50K to $100K/yr, consistent with the $20K "Season" tier scaled for a flagship.
  - Keep the **name of the structure itself** clean. "The Rose presented by [bank]" cheapens an art piece. Sell the elements, not the flower.

## 2. Build-in-public and pre-sale mechanics (small-team version)

### What's worked
- **The building is the ad.** Arctic Bath (Harström/Kauppi, Swedish Lapland) went "internet famous" off renders before it opened ([Dwell](https://www.dwell.com/article/one-night-in-swedens-internet-famous-arctic-bath-hotel-cold-plunge-pool-sauna-443e1ecf)). Trosten made **TIME's World's Greatest Places 2025** in its first year ([Hydro](https://www.hydro.com/en/global/media/news/2025/oslos-floating-aluminium-sauna-featured-in-times-annual-list-of-the-worlds-greatest-places/)). A rose-shaped floating sauna is a render people share by itself.
- **Origin myth.** OBF's first sauna was driftwood, built by two "anarchists," and **moved 14 times to avoid confiscation** before it was legalized ([Culture of Bathing](https://cultureofbathing.substack.com/p/water-for-the-people-the-no-and-low)). People want to back a story like that.
- **Our own data** (Sauna Viral Research, Sept 2026): build series hold steady long-form audiences on YouTube (WILD HOMESTEAD river-sauna episodes at 320K to 564K views each). A "build a sauna with me" TikTok hit 9.4M. Venue accounts rarely go viral; **creators carry it**. Ebb's IG winners are **local-plans hooks** ("looking for something different to do with friends in Portland?"), not wellness education. Portal's 400 founding members in month one is the conversion benchmark.
- **Presale best practice:** 20 to 30% off locked for life, hard-hat tours, pop-up test drives, biweekly updates ([LoyalSnap](https://www.loyalsnap.com/post/outreach-spotlight-selling-founding-memberships-the-secrets-of-a-successful-pre-sale)).

### Suggested ladder for The Rose **[est]**, anchored to current pricing
Current list: social $49 off-peak / $59 peak; private $449 / $539 (10 people). A 30 to 50 person Rose social session could list at **$55 to $75**.

| Tier | Cap | Price | What it gets | Gross if sold out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Waitlist ("Bud")** | unlimited | free, email + phone | First access, build updates, vote on petal names | $0, the list is the asset |
| **Seed deposit** | unlimited | $25 refundable, credited to any tier | Holds a place in line, proves demand for the City and lenders | n/a |
| **Petal** (founding annual) | 500 | $600 to $900/yr (vs. est. ~$1,100 list) | Year-one membership at a locked rate, name on the petal wall | $300K to $450K |
| **Thorn Circle** (Founding 100) | 100 | ~$2,500 one-time | Unlimited year one, 20% off for life, one private Thorn session a year, opening-night invite | ~$250K |
| **Rose Patron** | 12 (one per petal) | $10K | A petal deck named for them or their company, one annual full buyout, plaque | $120K |
| **Material / presenting partner** | 1 to 3 | $50K to $150K cash or in-kind | Element naming, case study, content rights | $50K to $150K |

A realistic sell-through of ~50% would still raise **$350K to $500K** of pre-opening cash. Portal's 400 in a month suggests 500 Petals is possible **once the site is public and dated**.

### Mechanics
- **Hard caps and a visible counter** ("317 of 500 petals claimed"). Put each price step on a dated deadline, not a vague "limited time."
- **Deposits first, charges later.** Take the $25 refundable seed now. Convert to paid tiers only after the LOI and key permits, and hold the money in a separate account with a written refund-if-not-open-by-[date] promise. Oregon prepaid / gift rules and the Plus Pool delay risk both argue for this.
- **Cadence for a small team:** 2 posts/week of build content (the Grant / Build Diary model already exists), a monthly "hard-hat" in-person event at the current Columbia sauna, and one email a month to the waitlist. Use creators for the viral lift, not the brand account.
- **Keep it distinct from Reg CF.** Memberships are prepaid services, not securities. If a revenue-share investor round runs later (the Sisu model), keep its messaging separate from the membership presale to stay clean on securities rules.

## 4. Rose City branding, trademark, naming

- **History:** "City of Roses" became the **official nickname in 2003**. The Rose City identity dates to the Madame Caroline Testout rose. Portland planted about 20 miles of Testout roses along its streets for the 1905 Lewis & Clark Exposition ([Wikipedia: Roses in Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roses_in_Portland,_Oregon); [Nicknames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicknames_of_Portland,_Oregon)). The **International Rose Test Garden** (Washington Park) is run by **PP&R, the same bureau that now owns Holman Dock**. A petal-variety tie-in (petal decks named for Portland-bred or Test Garden award roses) is a free, on-brand partnership.
- **Rose Festival** has been running since 1907 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Rose_Festival)). The mark "PORTLAND ROSE FESTIVAL" is registered (Reg. 1139303). Don't use it without a license.
- **Trademark knockout (web only, not a USPTO clearance).** The USPTO search API wasn't reachable from here:
  - No US sauna business found using **"Rose City Sauna,"** "The Rose Sauna," or "Rose Bathhouse."
  - Nearest conflicts: **Under The Rose Sauna House** (Vancouver **BC**, 20-person social sauna, events and parties, [site](https://www.undertherose.club/)). "Rose, a Luxury Spa" (Nashville). "Rose Sauna" rooms at Thermen Bussloo (NL) and a Visit Finland listing. The Ritz-Carlton Portland spa is "inspired by the unfolding petals of a Portland rose" ([Ritz](https://www.ritzcarlton.com/en/hotels/pdxrz-the-ritz-carlton-portland/overview/)).
  - **"The Rose" alone is crowded** in Class 41 (entertainment venues; e.g. the well-known music venue The Rose in Pasadena, CA) and weak because it's descriptive. Expect a narrow registration at best.
  - **Recommendation:** register a **composite mark** in Class 44 (saunas) plus Class 41 (events): "The Rose by Ebb & Ember," or a coined name. Keep "The Rose" as the everyday nickname. Commission a USPTO + state + common-law clearance from the trademark attorney before any public reveal.
- **Naming ideas:**
  - Structure: **The Rose** / **Testout** (after Portland's founding rose, ownable, has a story) / **Rosarium** / **The Bloom**
  - Small sauna: **The Thorn** (keep it)
  - Slide: **The Stem**
  - Decks: each petal named for a rose variety ("Caroline Testout," "Portland Gold," "Rose City")
  - Events: **"First Bloom"** (opening), **"Rose Court sauna,"** **"The Pruning"** (fall closing ritual)
  - Tagline: *"Portland finally has a rose you can sit inside."*

## 5. The Stem: waterslide into the Willamette

**Precedents**
- **Kalvebod Bølge, Copenhagen:** a public harbour-swim zone with a "huge outdoor slide" people also jump from, plus a floating 5.8 × 5.8 m dipping pool with an adjustable 0.6 to 1.4 m floor ([VisitCopenhagen](https://www.visitcopenhagen.com/copenhagen/planning/kalvebod-bolge-gdk723272), [Your Denmark Guide](https://yourdenmarkguide.com/harbour-baths-in-copenhagen-canals/)).
- **Islands Brygge harbour bath** (BIG/JDS, 2003): 600-person capacity with 3 m and 5 m springboards, so municipal harbour diving works ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Harbour_Baths)).
- **Badeschiff, Berlin** (2004): started as a **City Art Project** (artist Susanne Lorenz + AMP/Menis) and became a floating pool on the Spree ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badeschiff)). This is the "art piece that became infrastructure" precedent.
- Consumer and inflatable dock slides typically call for **5 ft minimum** and **10 to 12 ft ideal** depth ([DockG](https://dockg.com/the-best-way-to-make-your-lake-dock-stand-out-slides/), [EZ Dock](https://www.ez-dock.com/blog/how-deep-does-the-water-need-to-be-for-a-floating-dock/)).

**Engineering and safety numbers**
- **ASTM F2376** (commercial water slides): water depth **≥3 ft at the exit lip**, increasing to **4'6" at 4'6" out**, and **4'6" maintained 9 ft** along the centerline. The exit lip can sit **no more than 20 in** above the water ([UpCodes](https://up.codes/s/recreational-water-slides)). An open river needs a lot more. Design for **8 to 10 ft minimum at low water**, with a floating exit so the lip rides the ~16 ft seasonal stage swing (Siting Brief). Get bathymetry soundings at Holman before design.
- **Current:** City study bans beaches above **3 ft/s**. Near-shore summer runs **0 to 1 ft/s**, with spikes of 5 to 7 ft/s in floods and near bridge footings. **Holman sits next to the Hawthorne Bridge piers.** USGS at the Broadway Bridge read a mean **0.35 ft/s on Jul 21, 2026**, and flow can reverse on Columbia tides ([USGS 14211720](https://waterdata.usgs.gov/usa/nwis/uv?site_no=14211720)). Run the slide as a **July to September** feature. Close it on a posted velocity or stage trigger.
- **Water quality:** after the $1.4B Big Pipe, "nearly all" CSOs are eliminated. **2025 Check the Rec: 100% of samples under the DEQ E. coli standard.** 2026 testing runs May 22 to September, weekly at 5 sites. The nearest is RiverPlace Marina, across the river, so **Holman is not a test site**. Consider paying for our own weekly sampling ([BES 2026](https://www.portland.gov/bes/news/2026/5/20/willamette-river-recreation-season-back-so-check-rec)). Before Big Pipe, overflows averaged ~50/yr ([DEQ](https://www.oregon.gov/deq/wq/pages/swimming-on-the-willamette.aspx)). Rule: no contact **for 24 to 48 hrs after heavy rain**. DEQ also warns about cyanobacteria in warm, slow water.
- **Swim culture:** the Duckworth Dock (Eastbank, close by) is one of PP&R's **six designated safer swimming zones** (2022) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Duckworth_Memorial_Dock)). HAP's **Big Float ended in 2022**, so there's a gap for a July river-celebration anchor event ([Big Float](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Float)). The water was 74°F at a past July event.
- **Liability and insurance:** slides and diving are commonly **excluded** from standard GL. Budget for specialty aquatic/amusement coverage ([XINSURANCE](https://www.xinsurance.com/risk-class/water-park-insurance/)). Drowning is the catastrophic exposure, and "supervision is the only real control." That means a **certified lifeguard on duty whenever the slide is open**, a swim-ability waiver, and the rules: feet-first only, one at a time, no alcohol (already our stance), and a clear run-out zone kept free of boats and paddlers. Holman is a launch dock for rowing clubs, so a buoy-marked exclusion zone is needed. PP&R already requires $2M per occurrence with the City as additional insured (Pilot Plan). The slide may push the umbrella higher.
- **Permits:** the slide is part of the moored structure for **DSL, Corps Section 10, Greenway, and NMFS** (shading) review. Any footing needs cap clearance (DEQ).
- **ADA:** gangways must hit 1:12 **but need not exceed 80 ft**, and once gangways total ≥80 ft the slope rule is waived (2010 ADA §1003.2) ([Access Board](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-10-boating-facilities/)). Water slides generally aren't required to have an accessible route to the top, but confirm with an accessibility consultant. The **sauna and decks** should follow **Trosten**, which has a wheelchair-accessible interior with space for 2 wheelchairs and ventilation tuned for even heat at all heights. That's also a City-pleasing public-benefit point ([Designboom](https://www.designboom.com/architecture/estudio-herreros-trosten-oslo-floating-sauna-accessibility-05-23-2024/)).

**My read:** the Stem is the most viral element and the highest-risk one. **Phase it:** open the Rose and Thorn first, and add the Stem in summer 2 after a season of velocity, stage, and E. coli data at the actual dock. Render it from day one, because it sells the vision.

**Takeaways for the brief:**
- The ones that became icons (**SALT, Badeschiff, Frihamnen**) were **framed as art projects first**. That framing opened doors with cities and funders.
- Trosten shows that **accessibility plus a notable architect plus a material sponsor** gets you onto the TIME list.
- The Rose at 30 to 50 people is in Trosten/Othership territory and well below Löyly/SALT. A **~$1M to $3M** all-in build **[est]** is plausible for a sculptural floating structure. Löyly's €6M included a full restaurant on land.

## 7. Holman Dock / OMSI District context

- **Holman Dock:** floating dock + pilings + gangway on the east bank, **south of the Hawthorne Bridge**. It's used by rowing clubs, universities, paddlers, sunbathers, and swimmers. It's been called "crumbling." Prosper donated it to **PP&R** "as-is" on **May 8, 2024** with **$270K** of restricted O&M funds against ~$74K/yr of cost. The DSL submerged-land license runs to **Sept 7, 2034** ([ordinance 191700](https://www.portland.gov/council/documents/ordinance/passed/191700)). Topper Floats built it, and the City wants Topper or an engineer to sign off on tie-offs (vault).
- **Steelhead (Live Nation):** 3,500 capacity, Water Ave between Salmon and Main, Lever Architecture. Building permit Sept 2025, **opens summer 2027**. Local opposition didn't stop it in 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Nation_Entertainment_venue_in_Portland,_Oregon)). It uses the adjacent lots on show nights (vault). **Upside:** 3,500 people a night walking past a glowing rose. **Downside:** noise, parking, and bleed from the sound checks into an "immersive sound" program. Schedule around the show calendar.
- **OMSI District:** 24 to 34 acres, ~1,200 housing units (≥20% affordable), master plan approved by the Design Commission in 2023. City Council approved **$15.4M to $16.9M** of Prosper infrastructure (Sept 2024). **Tract C, just north of Tilikum,** gets a public plaza with esplanade and greenway improvements co-created with tribal governments. Construction was expected to start early 2026, with a 20 to 30 year buildout ([OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2024/09/04/portland-city-council-approves-15-million-advance-new-omsi-district/), [OMSI](https://omsi.edu/future-omsi-district/), [Prosper](https://prosperportland.us/our-work/omsi-district/)). That's our **Phase 3** hook: a permanent Rose as the district's waterfront landmark.
- **Eastbank Esplanade** trail passes the dock, and Greenway Title 33.440 requires public trail access on both banks (Siting Brief). The Rose's decks and "public benefit" hours (swim float, community days) help with Greenway review.
- **Tilikum Crossing** (2015, cable-stayed, transit/bike/ped only, lit by a river-condition-driven LED program) is the southern backdrop. The bridge's lighting already reacts to river data, which is a natural tie-in if the Rose's lights also "read" the river.
- **Waterfront festival friction:** WWeek (Sept 2, 2026) reports an unresolved City dispute over the cost of staging waterfront festivals. That's context for why the City likes paying, self-funded activations (the Pilot Plan's "pay anyway" advice).
