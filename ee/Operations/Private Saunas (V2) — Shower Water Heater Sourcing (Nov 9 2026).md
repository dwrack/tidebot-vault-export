## Answer to the question
No enclosure needed. Use an **outdoor-rated natural gas tankless**. It mounts on the center wall in the open, vents straight out of its own cabinet, and has built-in freeze protection (about 100 to 140 W, runs off outlet E11). The slatted cedar box on the drawings is fine as a screen as long as it is a screen, not a closet: open or louvered front, no lid over the exhaust, and the unit's own clearances kept inside it. An indoor unit would need a weather-tight closet plus a vent kit and intake air, which is more money and more to go wrong on a float.

## Sizing (why 199k BTU, not smaller)
- Portland river-season inlet water: about 42°F in winter. Shower at 105°F = roughly 63°F rise.
- Two showers at the same time with 1.75 to 2.0 GPM heads = about 4 GPM.
- A 199k non-condensing unit (0.82 UEF, about 163k BTU/hr delivered) gives about 5.2 GPM at that rise. Comfortable.
- A 160k unit gives about 4.2 GPM: works in summer, borderline in January. Skip it.
- Minimum activation flow on these is about 0.4 GPM, so a single low-flow head still fires it.

## Where it sits
- Center wall, river side, between the two showers. No roof over the center wall (Rev Q), which is what the exhaust wants.
- Keep the manufacturer's clearances inside the cedar screen. Typical for Rinnai outdoor units: about 12" above the exhaust, 6" at the sides, 24" open in front. Confirm against the RE199eN manual before Grant builds the box.
- Exhaust terminal must stay away from doors, windows and the stove intake louvers (4 ft is the usual rule for openings) and should not blow at face height on the shower side. Face the exhaust up and toward the river.
- Mount height: controls about 4 to 5 ft off the deck so the unit can be serviced without taking the screen apart. Make one slat panel removable.
