# Memo to counsel — intercompany loan, ET to ETE (sauna boat)

## What this is

We want to formally document a loan that already exists on the books. ET (the marina entity) advanced $250,940 to ETE (Ebb & Ember) during 2025 and Q1 2026 to fund the purchase and construction of the floating sauna vessel and lounge. It shows up as a $250,940 intercompany loan payable on ETE's March 31, 2026 balance sheet, and we cite it in the Dustin buyout valuation. There is no signed note papering it. The attached draft (`Intercompany Loan — ETE to ET (Sauna Boat) DRAFT.md`) is a secured promissory note that memorializes it.

Terms in the draft: $250,940 principal, effective 3/31/26, AFR-based interest (placeholder 4.50% mid-term, needs the actual figure), 60-month amortizing schedule ($4,678.28/mo), secured by the vessel.

## The issue I need you to drive

The same three parties own both entities (Davey, Jonah/Ohana, Dustin/Seaworthy), and Dustin is the adversarial party in an active buyout. Under ETE's Operating Agreement (5/6/25), formalizing this loan looks like a **Consent of a Supermajority of Units** matter on four independent grounds:

- **§4.5(k)** — incurring indebtedness over $10,000 outside the ordinary course.
- **§4.5(l)** — capital expenditure over $10,000.
- **§4.5(m)** — granting consensual liens on Company property (the vessel security interest).
- **§4.5(u)** — a transaction involving an actual or potential conflict of interest between a Member/Manager and the Company.

And **§4.9** requires affiliate dealings to be approved in advance by the Managers (or, for manager-conflicts, by a Majority of Disinterested Units).

"Supermajority of Units" is defined as Members holding **more than 75%** of Units. With three equal one-third Members, that threshold is only met by all three. So on its face this needs Dustin's signature.

Questions for you:

1. Does the original advance need separate authorization, or was it effectively authorized/ratified when it happened (the members knew ET was funding the build)? Does memorializing an already-consummated, already-booked advance change the §4.5 analysis versus authorizing a new borrowing?
2. The **vessel security interest** is the genuinely new grant (§4.5(m)). Can that be done without all three, or does it have to wait for Dustin's consent or a post-buyout membership change? We picked secured, but I want your read on whether to strip the lien to keep the note clean and signable now.
3. **§4.8(b)** removes indemnification for breach of the Agreement and for any transaction from which a Member/Manager derives an improper personal benefit. What's the personal exposure to Davey and Jonah if they sign a related-party secured note over Dustin's objection or without his consent?
4. Perfection method for the vessel lien: UCC-1, Oregon vessel lien, or preferred ship mortgage if it ends up USCG-documented? Coast Guard classification has been pending 3+ months, so the documentation status is unsettled.
5. Should the note's dispute-resolution clause point to the §26.11-style arbitration provisions in the operating agreements?

## What I need confirmed before anyone signs

- The actual **June 2026 mid-term AFR** (CPA) — the schedule recalculates if it differs from 4.50%.
- The **vessel identifying details** (name, HIN, registration/USCG number, dimensions, stove/equipment) for Exhibit B — from the build invoices and title.
- The **first payment date** and the **ETE authorized signatory**.
- Whether we proceed secured or unsecured, and how we get §4.5 authorization given Dustin.

## My instinct

Documenting reality is good and overdue. But signing a *secured* related-party note 2-of-3 without Dustin, mid-buyout, risks a later challenge that it was never validly authorized, which would undercut its value in the valuation and expose the signers under §4.8(b). I lean toward either (a) an unsecured confirmatory note now that just memorializes the booked debt, with the lien handled later, or (b) the full secured version only with proper §4.5 consent. Your call on which is defensible.
