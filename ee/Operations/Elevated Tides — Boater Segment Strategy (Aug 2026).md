# Elevated Tides — Boater Segment Strategy

**Status:** Davey's raw idea, worked into a first framework (2026-08-27). Internal only; none of this belongs on any shared page.

## The two customers on one waterfront

| | The Ritualists | The Fleet |
|---|---|---|
| Who | E&E's guest: wellness-driven, urban, books online | Boat owners at the marina: older, social, beverage-led, cash-rich |
| Peak hours | Sunrise, weekday evenings, sunset | Weekend middays and afternoons |
| Wants | Quiet, ritual, cold water, no phones | Dock life, company, ice, food, drinks, a scene |
| Buys | Sessions, memberships, gift cards | Moorage, fuel, provisions, food, upgrades |

Culturally these groups barely overlap, and that is the point.

## The paradox that works for us

They want opposite things at opposite times, so they never compete for the same hour or the same square foot of dock. That means:

1. **Their money funds the quiet.** Fleet revenue (moorage, provisions, food) is high-margin and low-labor, and it subsidizes the wellness side's capital projects (V2, dock glow-up) without touching the sauna experience.
2. **Our brand raises their property.** A beautiful, known wellness destination on the dock raises the perceived quality of every slip in the marina. We can charge a slip premium *because* the sauna exists. They pay for proximity to a thing they'll never book.
3. **Each side is the other's scenery.** From the sauna, an alive marina is atmosphere. From a boat, the glowing cedar sauna is the best-looking thing on the water.

## Revenue rails (ranked by margin vs. risk)

1. **Slip premium + waitlist.** The brand halo justifies above-market moorage. Zero incremental cost. The "Private Slip summer residency" on the prospectus is the productized version: slip + sunset session access + towels at the rail, priced as a bundle.
2. **Event perimeter moorage.** Raft-Up concert nights: paddle-in is free-spirited marketing; *boats* pay for perimeter anchor/moorage spots. They get the show and the scene; we get dockage revenue from people who'd never buy a session.
3. **The Dock Galley (provisions).** Ice, coffee, oysters to go, sunscreen, merch, "everything a boat forgets." High margin, no liability, cash flow all weekend when sessions are slow.
4. **Oyster Hour food revenue.** Food is safe; oysters + NA options we can sell directly.
5. **Gift cards / merch.** The Fleet buys gifts for wives, kids, guests.

## Risk limits (the "drinks a lot" problem)

- **We never serve alcohol ourselves.** Serving drinkers who then operate boats is BUI liability we don't want. If alcohol appears at an event, it's a licensed third-party caterer with their own OLCC license and insurance, or BYO on their own vessels (their boat, their liability).
- **Zone the property.** The sauna dock is a posted quiet zone, no outside alcohol, guests only. Marina row is their world. The distance between the two is a feature, price it, don't blur it.
- **Time-zone the calendar.** Wellness owns mornings and dusk; Fleet events live in the afternoon block. No overlap, no culture clash on the dock.
- **Paddle-in events need paperwork before the first show:** marine event insurance rider, PFD-required signage for the raft zone, no-wake enforcement, and a check on whether OSMB / Coast Guard marine event permitting applies. Ask the insurer first, not after.
- **Waivers** already cover sauna guests; extend a lightweight version to raft-zone entry.

## Event concepts feeding this (now on the prospectus)

- **The Raft-Up Sessions** — paddle-in music series; the marina is the amphitheater, your craft is your seat. Boaters pay perimeter moorage.
- **Sound Bath, On the Water** — bowls at dusk; calm water carries tone. Low cost, high Instagram.
- **The Dock Galley** — daily provisions counter; the Fleet's daily touchpoint.
