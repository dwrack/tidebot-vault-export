## Fix before framing (these get expensive later)

### 1. Stove clearance is drawn at the bare minimum, zero margin
Torch manual: the 80k needs **12" on three sides, 4" on the intake side**. Grant's West plan shows exactly 1' stove-to-wall and exactly 1' stove-to-upper-bench. Two problems:
- That 12" is to the *finished* combustible surface. After furring + 3/4" cladding the framed 12" becomes about 10.75". Frame it at 14-16".
- Torch also **requires a stove guard** (two rails 8" apart, top rail 28" off the floor) and the guard itself has to respect the clearances. No guard is drawn. With the bench end 12" from the stove there's no room for one.
- Answers Jonah's green-circle question: the stove zone is roughly 21" stove + 12" each side + guard, so plan on ~4' of that wall being stove territory before the first window can start.

### 2. Room grew 50%, stove is now at the top of its range
Torch sizing rule: L × W × H, plus window area (sq ft), = adjusted cubic feet. TS-80F covers 450-900.
- 8.5 × 12 × **8' ceiling** = 816, plus ~80 sq ft of glass on West = **~900. At the limit.**
- Same room with a **7' ceiling** = 714 + 80 = **~795. Comfortable.**
- East (60" round window + glass door, ~36 sq ft): 852 at 8', 750 at 7'.

So ceiling height is now the number that decides whether these rooms heat well. No ceiling height appears on any drawing. The handbook agrees from the other direction: flat dropped ceiling, top bench 39-47" below it. **Ask Grant for a flat insulated ceiling at 7' to 7'4", not the underside of the shed roof.**

### 3. Intake / gas valve location
Torch: the intake neck goes through the wall and the valve + intake "must not be installed outdoors or exposed to the elements." If outdoors, a protective cover that still lets air in is required. Both stoves sit on exterior walls on a floating platform in the Columbia wind. Needs a drawn, weatherproof intake box on each, plus the fresh air vent (min 7" × 5" for the 80k). Wall thickness over 5" needs Torch's recessed box or flashing. Not on the drawings, not on the materials list.

### 4. Door next to the stove (East, Sept 16 thread)
I wouldn't do it. Torch needs 12" + guard on that side, the door must swing outward and never lock, and people walk in wet and barefoot. If the door moves right to open up the mountain view, keep at least the guard plus a clear step between the door swing and the stove. Get Grant to draw it before anyone says yes.

### 5. Contract scope vs what's drawn
Grant's signed estimate (9/10, $46,000) is written for **8.5'×8.5' + 8'×8'**. The Sept 16 drawings are **two 8.5'×12'** rooms: 204 sq ft vs 136, 50% more floor, wall, ceiling, cladding. Roof estimate already "about double." Confirm in writing whether $46K still holds. Same for the float load: bigger rooms, more glass, and still no weight number given to TC Diving.

## Should be on the drawings and isn't

- Ceiling height and bench heights. Handbook numbers: top bench 39-47" below ceiling, lower bench 16-19" below the top bench, 2' of bench per person.
- Upper bench is drawn **4' deep × 8'**. Handbook calls 24" ideal for lying down. 4' is a platform. Fine if that's the intent (lounging, date night), but it eats floor and makes the back 2' hard to clean under. Confirm it's deliberate. Lower bench at 16" deep works as a foot bench.
- Vents and floor drains (Jonah asked 9/15, still missing). Handbook natural-vent layout matches what Grant's sheet says: intake low beside the heater, exhaust high on the opposite wall about 8" below the ceiling. Add a closable drying vent.
- Non-combustible wall behind/beside the stove. Handbook: fiber cement board, roughly 2' wide, floor to ceiling. Not drawn, not on the list.
- CO detector (Torch requires one). Control box location outside the hot room.
- 6" Class A chimney route through the roof. The roof is now much bigger with the extension, confirm the flue still clears.

## Windows

- Sheet says "unframed, double pane, insulated" from Lifetime Windows. Handbook: **tempered is a must**, ideally every layer. Ask Cindy to confirm tempered both panes AND that the insulated-unit edge seal is rated for sauna heat (standard IGU seals can fail around 180°F, the room runs to 194°F). This matters most for the big West panels and the 60" round.
- Handbook: wood frames only. No PVC (deforms at 176°F), avoid aluminum.
- One-way mirror glass for Sunset (Jonah's ask): reflective coatings only work when it's brighter outside than in. At night with lights on inside, it reverses and people outside see in. Worth knowing before paying for it.
- RO sizes have to be locked with the window vendor before framing (Grant said this 9/8). No window sizes are dimensioned on the Sept 16 drawings. This is the actual critical path.
