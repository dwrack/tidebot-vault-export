# Weather Hold Protocol — Ebb and Ember Floating Saunas

*Elevated Tides LLC | Davey Jones' Locker*

---

## The Decision Framework

Weather decisions for a floating sauna are different from kayak or barge operations. The sauna itself provides shelter from light rain, but you're still on the water, which means lightning and high winds are non-negotiable cancel conditions.

The twist: some weather that kills other tour types actually enhances the sauna experience. Rain on a hot sauna with a cold plunge is incredible. Cold weather drives demand up. The judgment call here is narrower than other operations, but the hard lines are harder.

---

## Hard Cancel Conditions

These are not judgment calls. These are automatic.

### Lightning / Thunderstorms
- If lightning is visible or thunder is audible, sessions stop immediately
- Get guests out of the water, off the dock, and into a safe structure
- Being on the water during lightning is the single most dangerous scenario in this business
- Do not resume until 30 minutes after the last visible lightning or audible thunder

### High Winds (>25mph sustained)
- Dock safety becomes compromised. Ladders shift, the floating structure moves unpredictably, and guests transitioning between sauna and water are at risk
- Cancel and reschedule

---

## Judgment Call Conditions

### Heavy Rain
This is a genuine judgment call because rain + sauna can be amazing.

**Run it if:**
- Rain is steady but not torrential
- No lightning in the forecast
- Winds are manageable (<15mph)
- Guests were informed and are excited about it
- Dock and entry/exit are still safe

**Cancel or offer reschedule if:**
- Downpour reduces visibility and makes dock surfaces dangerously slippery
- Guests seem uncomfortable or reluctant (don't force the "rain sauna" vibe)
- Rain is accompanied by temperature drops that make the transition between sauna and outside genuinely unpleasant rather than invigorating

**Communication:** "Hey, heads up, it looks like we'll have some rain during your session. A lot of our guests actually love the rain/sauna combo. That said, if you'd rather reschedule, we're happy to do that. Your call."

### Extreme Heat (Heat Index >105F)
- This is a sauna on top of extreme outdoor heat. The risk of heat-related illness goes up significantly.
- If running: shorten session time, increase hydration check-ins, consider lowering sauna temperature slightly
- If the ambient temperature is already dangerous, adding sauna heat on top of it is questionable. Use judgment.
- Cancel if you can't safely manage the total heat exposure

### Extreme Cold
- Cold weather actually increases sauna demand. The hot-cold contrast is the whole point.
- Watch for: ice on dock surfaces (slip hazard), frozen water for cold plunge complications, hypothermia risk during transitions
- De-ice the dock before guests arrive. If you can't make it safe, cancel.
- Guests may underestimate how cold the transition is in winter. Brief them specifically.

### Fog
- Minimal safety impact for a stationary floating sauna
- Only a concern if it affects guest ability to find the location or creates a dock visibility issue for entry/exit

## Communication Protocol

### When Holding
1. Decision made by host / management
2. Text or call booked guests immediately
3. Template: "Hey [Name], we're watching the weather for your [Time] session. There's [condition] in the forecast and we want to make sure you're safe and comfortable. We'll update you by [Decision Time] with a final call. If we need to reschedule, we'll get you the next available slot."

### When Cancelling
1. Contact all booked guests
2. Offer reschedule first, refund if they prefer
3. Template: "Hey [Name], we need to cancel your [Time] session today due to [weather]. Safety first. We'd love to get you rescheduled. What dates work for you?"
4. Process refunds within 24 hours if requested

## Seasonal Patterns

**Spring (March-May):** Afternoon thunderstorms common. Morning sessions are safer bets. Watch forecasts closely for pop-up storms.

**Summer (June-August):** Heat index is the primary concern. Early morning and evening sessions preferred. Hydration protocol is critical.

**Fall (September-November):** Best season for the sauna experience. Mild temperatures, lower storm risk, ideal hot-cold contrast.

**Winter (December-February):** Peak demand for the contrast experience. Ice and cold exposure management are the main concerns. Dock safety is priority one.
