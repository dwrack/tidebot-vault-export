# Safety Briefing Script — Ebb and Ember Floating Saunas

*Elevated Tides LLC | Davey Jones' Locker*

*This is not a script to read aloud. It's the content you internalize and deliver conversationally, in your own voice, before every session.*

---

## The Briefing

Deliver this after the health screening, before anyone enters the sauna. Tone: warm, confident, unhurried. You're not reading a liability statement. You're helping people have a safe, amazing experience.

---

### Hydration

"First things first: water. Drink before you go in, drink while you're in there, drink after. The sauna is going to make you sweat a lot more than you realize, and dehydration sneaks up on you. I'll have water accessible the whole time. Use it."

This is the single most important safety point. Dehydration is the gateway to every heat-related problem. Make it feel natural, not clinical.

---

### Listening to Your Body

"The sauna is not an endurance test. If you feel dizzy, lightheaded, nauseous, or just off, step out. That's not quitting. That's your body telling you it's had enough heat for now. Everyone's tolerance is different, and it changes day to day. I'd rather you step out early and feel great than push it and feel terrible."

Guests, especially in groups, will push themselves to keep up with each other. Normalize stepping out early. Make it the smart move, not the weak move.

---

### Time Limits

"I'll manage the timing, but as a general guide: 15-20 minutes in the sauna, then a cool-down break. We'll do a few rounds. The cool-down is actually where the good stuff happens. Your body responds to the contrast between hot and cold, and that's what leaves you feeling amazing afterward."

Setting expectations on timing prevents guests from camping in the sauna for 45 straight minutes and wondering why they feel terrible.

---

### Cooling Protocol

"For cooling, we have [cooling buckets / cold plunge / water access]. A few things to know: the cold is a shock to your system, especially coming straight from the sauna. Enter slowly. Breathe through it. The intense cold feeling lasts about 10 seconds, then your body adjusts and it actually feels incredible. Do not dive or jump in. Enter using the ladder, feet first, controlled."

Cold water shock is a real physiological response. Gasping, hyperventilation, panic. Coaching guests through it prevents problems and makes the experience better.

---

### Entry and Exit

"Use the ladder every time you get in or out of the water. It's there because wet surfaces are slippery, and going from hot sauna to cold water can make you a little unsteady. Take it slow. Hold the rails. No rushing."

The ladder isn't decorative. Wet feet on any surface after heat exposure is a slip waiting to happen.

---

### No Diving or Jumping

"No diving, no jumping from the dock or the sauna into the water. I know it's tempting, especially when you're hot, but the water depth varies and the landing isn't always predictable. Ladder in, ladder out."

Short and direct. Don't negotiate this one.

---

### If Something Goes Wrong

"If anyone feels seriously unwell, tell me or tell someone near you immediately. Heat-related illness can come on fast, and the sooner we catch it, the easier it is to handle. If someone near you seems confused, isn't responding normally, or passes out, call for me right away. I'll handle it from there."

You want guests to flag problems early, not wait until it's an emergency. "Tell me immediately" gives them permission.

---

### Medical Conditions Reminder

"Last thing: if anything changes during the session, if you start feeling something that doesn't seem right, come talk to me. There's no stupid question when it comes to how you're feeling in the heat."

This catches the person who didn't disclose something during the health screening but is now second-guessing whether they should have.

---

## Delivery Notes

- Keep it under 3 minutes. Guests are here to relax, not sit through a lecture.
- Eye contact. Speak to people, not at them.
- If it's a group, address the group but make it personal. "You" not "guests" or "participants."
- If someone looks nervous, address it privately after the group briefing. "Hey, have you done a sauna before? Here's what to expect."
- End with a genuine invitation: "Alright, let's do this. It's going to be great."

---

## Non-Negotiables

These are the lines that never move, regardless of guest preference:

1. Health screening happens every time, every guest
2. No one enters the sauna without being briefed
3. No diving or jumping
4. Hydration is not optional
5. If a guest shows signs of heat-related illness, they come out. Period. Their opinion on this does not matter.
6. If someone is unresponsive or confused, it's 911 immediately. Don't wait, don't "give them a minute," don't try to handle heat stroke yourself.
