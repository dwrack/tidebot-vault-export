# Safety & Compliance Overview — Ebb and Ember Floating Saunas

*Elevated Tides LLC | Davey Jones' Locker*

---

## How Compliance Works Here

Ebb and Ember doesn't fall neatly into a standard regulatory category. It's not a traditional vessel, and it's not a land-based spa. The compliance model is driven primarily by insurance requirements rather than a single regulatory body like USCG Subchapter T.

That said, "insurance-driven" doesn't mean "optional." Insurance requirements exist because they're the line between covered and catastrophically exposed. Every item in this document is non-negotiable.

---

## Insurance: The Core of Compliance

### Why Insurance Is Everything Here

A floating sauna involves heat, water, and people in a physically vulnerable state. The liability exposure is real: heat-related illness, slips on wet surfaces, drowning risk on the water, burns from heating equipment. Insurance is what stands between an incident and a business-ending lawsuit.

### What's Required

**For every contractor and programming partner:**
- Certificate of Liability Insurance with:
  - Commercial General Liability (CGL)
  - Professional Liability (Errors & Omissions)
- Both **Ebb & Ember Floating Saunas** and **Elevated Tides LLC** must be named as Additional Insureds
- A signed Independent Contractor Agreement (ICA) is what activates the Additional Insured status. The certificate alone is not enough.

**For subcontractors:**
- If a contractor brings subcontractors (assistants, co-instructors), each must either carry their own policy or have written confirmation from the lead contractor's insurer that they're covered
- Do not assume subcontractors are covered. Verify in writing before allowing them on site.
- "I'm pretty sure my policy covers them" is not verification

### When to Check

- Before any contractor's first session
- Before any programming activation launches
- At policy renewal time (ask for updated cert)
- If a contractor's scope of work changes (new activities may not be covered under existing policy)

## Sauna-Specific Safety

### Heat-Related Illness

This is your most likely emergency scenario. Know the difference:

**Heat Exhaustion**
- Heavy sweating, pale/clammy skin, fast/weak pulse, nausea, dizziness, headache, muscle cramps
- Treatment: Get them out of the sauna immediately. Move to shade/cool area. Cool cloths on neck, forehead, wrists. Sip water slowly. Rest. Monitor.
- Most people recover within 30 minutes with proper cooling and hydration
- If symptoms don't improve in 15-20 minutes, call 911

**Heat Stroke** (medical emergency)
- High body temperature (103F+), hot/red/dry skin (sweating may have stopped), fast/strong pulse, confusion, slurred speech, loss of consciousness
- Treatment: Call 911 immediately. Get them out of the heat. Cool them as aggressively as possible (cold water, ice packs on neck/armpits/groin). Do NOT give fluids to an unconscious person.
- Heat stroke can kill. Don't wait to see if it gets better. If someone is confused or unresponsive after heat exposure, that's 911.

### Contraindications

These conditions require either refusal or explicit medical clearance:

- **Heart conditions** (coronary artery disease, arrhythmias, recent heart attack) — heat increases cardiac workload significantly
- **Pregnancy** — elevated core temperature is dangerous to fetal development, especially in the first trimester
- **Recent surgery** (within 6 weeks) — heat dilates blood vessels and can complicate healing
- **Blood pressure medications** — some BP meds combined with heat cause dangerous drops in blood pressure
- **Epilepsy or seizure disorders** — heat can trigger seizures in some individuals
- **Acute illness or fever** — adding external heat to an already elevated body temperature is dangerous
- **Intoxication** — alcohol impairs thermoregulation and judgment. A drunk person in a hot sauna is a serious risk.

### The Health Screening Conversation

This isn't a form you rush through. It's a conversation. Ask clearly, listen carefully:

"Before we get started, I need to ask about a few health things. It's quick and it's for your safety. Do you have any heart conditions, blood pressure issues, or take medications for either? Are you pregnant or could you be? Any recent surgeries? Any seizure disorders?"

If someone discloses a condition, don't just refuse them. Discuss: "With that condition, I'd want you to check with your doctor before doing a sauna session. The heat is intense and it affects your cardiovascular system. I wouldn't feel right putting you in there without a green light from your doctor."

If someone refuses to answer, they don't enter the sauna. No exceptions.

## Hydration Protocol

- Guests should drink water before, during, and after the session
- Provide water at arrival and keep it accessible throughout
- Check in on hydration at least twice during the session
- Signs of dehydration overlap with early heat exhaustion: dizziness, dry mouth, dark urine, fatigue
- A guest who isn't drinking water is a guest you're watching closely

---

## Fire Safety

Sauna heating equipment involves combustion or high-wattage electrical elements. Fire risk is real.

- Fire extinguisher must be accessible and charged at all times
- Know where it is without thinking about it
- Heating equipment should be inspected per manufacturer's schedule
- Keep combustible materials away from the heater
- If something smells like burning and it shouldn't, shut it down and investigate before continuing
- Know the location of the nearest dock exit for evacuation

---

## Dock and Water Safety

- Entry/exit ladder must be inspected before every session: secure, no loose bolts, no slippery surfaces
- Dock surface should be clear of tripping hazards
- No diving or jumping from the dock or sauna
- Guests entering the water for cooling must be monitored at all times
- Cold water shock is real: when someone goes from a 180F sauna into cold water, their body can react with gasping, hyperventilation, or panic. Guide them: "Enter slowly. The cold is intense for about 10 seconds, then it gets better. Breathe through it."
- Never leave a guest unattended in or near the water

---

## Incident Reporting

Any incident involving injury, illness, or property damage:
1. Ensure guest safety first
2. Call 911 for medical emergencies
3. Contact management immediately
4. Document: what happened, when, who was involved, what actions were taken, witnesses
5. Photograph any relevant conditions
6. File written incident report same day
7. Preserve any relevant evidence (don't clean up the scene before documenting)
