## What This Job Actually Is

You're not running a sauna. You're holding space for people to slow down, feel something, and leave a little different than they arrived. Ebb and Ember is a wellness experience on the water. The sauna is the vehicle. The feeling is the product.

That means the job is part hospitality, part safety management, part energy curation. You set the tone before anyone touches the door. A calm, confident host makes guests feel safe enough to relax. A frantic, distracted host makes them wonder if they should have booked somewhere else.

## Requirements

### Minimum
- CPR and First Aid certification (current)
- Background check (clear)
- 2 professional references
- Certificate of Liability Insurance: Commercial General Liability + Professional Liability (Errors & Omissions)
  - Both **Ebb & Ember Floating Saunas** and **Elevated Tides LLC** must be named as Additional Insureds
  - Your signed ICA activates Additional Insured status, so the cert alone isn't enough. Both documents must be in place.
- Willingness to obtain any required local permits or certifications

### Why the Insurance Requirement Is So Strict

Every host and programming partner carries their own insurance naming both E&E and Elevated Tides. This isn't paranoia. Floating saunas involve heat, water, and physical vulnerability. If a guest has a medical event during your session, the liability chain needs to be clear and covered. If you bring subcontractors (an assistant, a co-instructor for a class), they either carry their own policy or you have written confirmation from your insurer that they're covered under yours. No assumptions. Verify in writing before they're on site.

## Training Progression

### Phase 1: Orientation
- Facility walkthrough: sauna, dock, entry/exit points, safety equipment, storage
- Sauna operation: heating system, temperature management, ventilation
- Guest health screening process (contraindications, disclosure questions)
- Emergency procedures: heat-related illness recognition and response
- Insurance and documentation review

### Phase 2: Shadow Sessions (2-3 minimum)
- Observe an experienced host running full sessions
- Watch how they manage temperature, timing, guest comfort
- Note how they handle the health screening conversation
- Pay attention to pacing. The sauna experience has a rhythm: arrival, warm-up, peak heat, cool-down, rest. A good host manages transitions between these phases.

### Phase 3: Assisted Sessions (1-2)
- You lead with experienced host present
- Run the health screening
- Manage the sauna temperature and timing
- Handle the safety briefing
- Debrief with the experienced host after

### Phase 4: Solo
- All insurance and documentation confirmed
- Management on call for first solo session
- Debrief after

## The Experience Arc

Good sauna sessions have a shape, not just a temperature setting.

**Arrival** — Guest arrives, likely a little unsure. Most people haven't done a floating sauna before. Your energy here is warm, unhurried, welcoming. Show them around, explain what will happen, run the health screening casually but thoroughly.

**Warm-Up** — First 10-15 minutes in the sauna. Lower temperature, let them acclimate. This is where you build comfort. Light conversation or comfortable silence, depending on the group.

**Peak Heat** — The real session. Temperature at operating level. Guests are settled, breathing, present. This is where the magic happens. Your job here is to be attentive but not intrusive. Read the room.

**Cool-Down** — Cooling buckets, cold plunge if available, fresh air. This is the contrast that makes the sauna experience transformative. Guide them through it. "Take your time. The cold is a shock but your body adjusts in about 10 seconds."

**Rest** — Post-sauna rest is as important as the heat itself. Water, quiet, space. This is where guests reflect and where the experience solidifies as a memory.

## Working with Programming Partners

Ebb and Ember hosts external programming: fitness series, meditation, workshops, pop-ups. When a programming partner is running a session, your role shifts to support:

- Ensure the space is set up per the partner's needs
- Handle guest check-in and health screening (they may not be equipped to)
- Monitor safety (heat exposure, hydration) even when someone else is leading the content
- You're the safety net, not the star

If you have concerns about a programming partner's safety practices, bring them to management immediately. Don't try to correct them in front of guests.

## Code of Conduct

### Required
- Professional, calm, present demeanor at all times
- Health screening for every guest, every session, no exceptions
- Hydration monitoring throughout the session
- Clean, maintained space before and after every session
- Timely communication with management about any issues

### Prohibited
- Operating the sauna while impaired by any substance
- Skipping or rushing the health screening
- Allowing guests to exceed recommended heat exposure times without checking in
- Ignoring signs of heat-related distress
- Operating without current insurance documentation on file
- Allowing uninsured subcontractors on site

### Violation Protocol
- **First violation:** Written discussion with management, re-read relevant SOPs, written acknowledgment
- **Second violation:** Probation, mandatory retraining
- **Third violation:** Contract termination
