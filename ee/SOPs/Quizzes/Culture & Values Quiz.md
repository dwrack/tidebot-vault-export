# Culture & Values Quiz — Ebb and Ember Floating Saunas

### Q1. A first-time guest arrives looking nervous and says "I've never done anything like this before." What's your move?

A) Hand them the waiver and point them to the sauna
B) Slow down. Walk them through exactly what will happen, step by step. Show them the space, the cooling area, the ladder. Let them know they can step out anytime. Your calm sets their calm.
C) Tell them "Don't worry, it's easy!"
D) Pair them with another guest who's done it before

**Correct: B**
Nervous guests need specifics, not reassurance. "Don't worry" doesn't help someone who doesn't know what to expect. Walking them through the process gives them a mental map of the experience, which is what actually reduces anxiety. Take the time.

---

### Q2. You're hosting a private session for a couple celebrating an anniversary. How do you manage the energy?

A) Keep the standard briefing and routine, treat it like any session
B) Read the room. An anniversary couple probably wants intimacy and quiet, not a chatty host. Deliver the briefing warmly, then give them space. Check in on hydration and comfort at natural intervals without hovering. The premium experience is feeling like the space is theirs.
C) Make conversation throughout to keep them engaged
D) Add extra touches like candles and music without asking

**Correct: B**
Premium doesn't mean more. It often means less, done intentionally. An anniversary couple doesn't want you hanging around. They want to feel like they have the space to themselves, with a safety net nearby. Check in at transitions (between rounds, during cooling) and otherwise be present but invisible.

### Q3. A regular guest asks if they can skip the health screening since "you already know me." What do you say?

A) Sure, save time for the regulars
B) "I hear you, and I appreciate you being a regular. But I do the screening every time because your health can change, and I need to know where you're at today. Takes 30 seconds."
C) Just ask "anything new since last time?"
D) Let them sign a blanket waiver covering all future sessions

**Correct: B**
Every guest, every session. Blood pressure changes, new medications start, pregnancies happen. "I know you" doesn't mean "I know your current health status." The screening takes 30 seconds and it protects both of you. Option C (anything new?) is close but misses conditions they might not think to mention because they've had them for a while.

### Q4. What does "wellness hospitality" mean at Ebb and Ember?

A) Offering health advice to guests
B) Creating an environment where people feel safe enough to slow down, combined with the practical safety knowledge to protect them while they're in a vulnerable state. It's the intersection of atmosphere and competence.
C) Running the sauna at the right temperature
D) Playing relaxing music and using nice towels

**Correct: B**
Nice towels and good music are table stakes. Wellness hospitality is deeper: it's the guest trusting you enough to let their guard down in a hot room on the water, and you being skilled enough to keep them safe while they do. Atmosphere without competence is dangerous. Competence without atmosphere is clinical. You need both.

### Q5. A programming partner (yoga instructor) is running a session and you notice a guest looking uncomfortable in a pose. The instructor hasn't noticed. What do you do?

A) Interrupt the class and tell the guest to stop
B) Quietly approach the guest and check in. "Hey, you doing okay? Want some water or need to step out for a minute?" You're the safety net, not the instructor, but guest safety is always your responsibility regardless of who's leading the session.
C) Wait for the instructor to notice, it's their class
D) Tell the instructor privately after the session

**Correct: B**
You don't override the instructor publicly, but you don't wait for them to notice either. A quiet check-in with the guest is your lane. If the guest is in genuine distress (not just stretching discomfort), you pull them out regardless of what the class is doing. After the session, talk to the instructor and management.

### Q6. How should the space look and feel when a guest walks in for their session?

A) Clean and functional
B) Intentional. Towels set, water visible, temperature building, dock clear, no clutter, no leftover signs from the previous session. The guest should feel like this space was prepared specifically for them, not like they're walking into a reset that barely happened.
C) Warm and cozy
D) It doesn't matter as long as the sauna works

**Correct: B**
First impressions set the tone for the entire experience. If a guest sees yesterday's towels, a half-filled cooling bucket, and a cluttered dock, the "premium wellness experience" pitch falls apart. Preparation is respect. It takes 15 minutes to reset properly. Don't shortcut it.

### Q7. A guest leaves a 3-star review saying "the sauna was fine but the host seemed distracted." How should this be handled?

A) Ignore it, you can't please everyone
B) Take it seriously. "Distracted" means the guest didn't feel attended to, which is a fundamental failure of the hosting role. Discuss with management, identify what happened (phone use? Rushing? Low energy day?), and adjust. Respond to the review acknowledging the feedback.
C) Respond defensively explaining what you were doing
D) Blame it on being short-staffed

**Correct: B**
Guest perception is reality when it comes to experience. If they felt you were distracted, you were distracted, even if you were technically doing your job. The fix is self-awareness: what was pulling your attention? Phone? Personal stress? Fatigue? Identify it, address it. One distracted host interaction can undo months of 5-star reviews.

### Q8. It's a slow week with only 2 sessions booked. What's your approach to those sessions?

A) Treat them as easy days, lower effort
B) These sessions get your full attention and energy. Low volume means each guest's experience carries more weight for reviews and word-of-mouth. Plus, this is when you can over-deliver: extra time, more personal attention, the kind of experience that creates evangelists.
C) Use the downtime to catch up on personal stuff
D) Suggest management cancel them to save costs

**Correct: B**
Slow weeks are where reputations are built. A guest who gets your undivided attention during a quiet week tells 10 people about it. A guest who gets half your attention during a busy week tells 10 people a different story. Volume doesn't determine quality. You do.

### Q9. David and Jonah are evaluating a new programming activation (sound bath). During the private walkthrough, you notice the instructor seems unprepared and the session feels disjointed. What do you do?

A) Stay quiet, it's David and Jonah's call
B) Share your honest observation after the session. The walkthrough exists specifically to evaluate quality, and your on-the-ground perspective matters. Frame it constructively: "I noticed the transitions felt unpolished and a few guests might find the pacing confusing. Worth another run?"
C) Tell the instructor they need to practice more
D) Suggest they just give it a try with real guests and see how it goes

**Correct: B**
The walkthrough is a Go/No-Go evaluation. Your input matters because you're the one who'll be supporting these sessions with real guests. If something feels off, say so. "Let's try it with real guests and see" is how you end up with a bad experience attached to your brand. Better to catch it now.

### Q10. What's the difference between a good host and a great host at Ebb and Ember?

A) A great host knows more about saunas
B) A good host runs a safe, clean, professional session. A great host does all of that and also reads the room: knows when to talk and when to be quiet, anticipates needs before guests voice them, and makes each person feel like the experience was designed just for them.
C) A great host upsells more services
D) A great host has more certifications

**Correct: B**
Operational competence is the floor. The ceiling is emotional intelligence. Can you tell when a group wants conversation vs. silence? Can you notice someone getting too hot before they notice it themselves? Can you make a group of strangers feel comfortable enough to be vulnerable in a sauna together? That's the difference.
