## 1. Other Words for "Ritual"

Some fit the brand better than others. "Ritual" is getting overused in wellness marketing, so it helps to have alternatives.

| Word | Feel | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| **Ceremony** | Formal, guided, has a leader | Hosted events (Aufguss, Banya, solstice) |
| **Rite / Rite of passage** | Marks a change or a threshold | Seasonal turns, birthdays, "first plunge" |
| **Practice** | Repeated, personal, humble | Memberships, morning series |
| **Rounds** | Sauna-native (Finnish and Banya people say it) | Session structure, "three rounds" |
| **Sitting** | Quiet, meditative (from the Zen *sit*) | Silent or stillness sessions |
| **Gathering / Circle** | Communal, equal footing | Community nights, closing circles |
| **Observance / Vigil** | Keeping watch over a moment in time | Solstice, full moon, longest night |
| **Crossing / Passage** | Movement from one state to another | Hot → cold transitions, the boat itself |
| **Offering** | Giving something (scent, song, water) | Aufguss infusions, the ladle |
| **Journey** | Story arc, beginning to end | Show-style Aufguss |
| **Custom / Tradition** | Rooted, inherited | Banya, Finnish löyly, Estonian smoke sauna |

**Sauna-native words worth knowing:**
- **Aufguss** (German) — "infusion" or "pouring over." The ritual and the pour itself.
- **Aufgussmeister / Saunameister** — the sauna master who leads it.
- **Wedeln** — the towel-waving.
- **Aufgussplan** — the posted daily schedule of infusions (a big part of the German ritual is that it *happens on the clock*).
- **Löyly** (Finnish) — the steam that rises off the stones. Older meanings tie it to "spirit" or "breath of life."
- **Gus / Gusmester** (Danish) — Denmark's name for guided infusion and its leader.
- **Parilka / Venik** (Russian Banya) — the steam room, and the birch or oak branch bundle.

**Brand idea:** we already own two ritual words in our name. **Ember** = heat, kindling, energy, intention. **Ebb** = release, cold, retreat, stillness. Every session is an Ember phase followed by an Ebb phase. Consider calling a round a **"tide"**: *"Tonight is three tides."*

## 2. What a Ritual Is Actually Made Of

A session turns into a ritual when it has a shape people can feel. Nearly every ritual tradition, from Aufguss to Banya to church, uses the same seven parts:

1. **Threshold.** A clear moment where ordinary time ends. Phones away, shoes off, a greeting, stepping onto the boat. The dock-to-boat crossing is our built-in threshold. Use it.
2. **Intention.** A short spoken framing: what this is, why we're here, what to notice. In Aufguss competitions this is capped at **90 seconds**, which is a good rule for us too.
3. **Repetition in rounds.** Usually **three**. Three is universal (Aufguss, Danish gus and Banya all use three rounds). It gives you a beginning, a build and a peak.
4. **Escalation.** Each round goes a little hotter, deeper or more intense than the last.
5. **Contrast.** Heat, then cold. Sound, then silence. Together, then alone. The contrast is where the feeling lands.
6. **Stillness / Integration.** Rest after each round, and a longer rest at the end. The body does its work here. Rest should be *at least* as long as the heat.
7. **Closing.** A shared act that marks the end: tea, a bell, a word, a moment looking at the river. Without a closing, it's just a class.

**The sensory layers a host can control:**
- **Heat:** temperature, humidity, the timing of each pour
- **Scent:** essential oils in water or ice on the stones
- **Sound:** music, silence, bells, bowls, the river itself
- **Light:** dusk, candlelight/lanterns (fire-safe), darkness, sunrise
- **Touch:** towel-waves of heat, venik, cold water, salt or honey scrubs
- **Story:** a theme or narrative that ties it together
- **Community:** eye contact, shared silence, a closing circle

## 3. Timing

### 3a. The classic Aufguss hour (German/Dutch spa model)

- Infusions run **on a posted schedule**, usually on the hour or half-hour. The clock is part of the ritual.
- Guests enter a few minutes early. **The door closes when it starts**, and no one enters after that. (Anyone can *leave* at any time. Always say this out loud.)
- **One infusion ≈ 10–15 minutes**, usually 3 pours with towel-waving after each, building in heat.
- Afterward: fresh air, cold plunge or shower, then **rest for as long as or longer than the heat**.
- Most guests do **2–3 infusions per visit**, with 20–30 min rest in between.

### 3b. The Danish saunagus format (60 min)

- **3 cycles** of about **12 min guided heat + 8 min cold/fresh-air break**.
- Adds breathwork, meditation, sound and sometimes salt/honey scrubs. It's closer to a guided journey than a show.
- This is the closest model to what we can realistically do on the boat.

### 3c. Competition format (Aufguss WM)

Per the [official 2026 rules](https://aufguss-wm.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/rules_aufgusswm_2026.pdf) (dated 3/2/2026):

- **12–15 minutes** per performance, welcome and farewell included. **14 minutes is "optimal."** The clock starts at the master's first action (opening the door, starting the light script) and stops when they leave the room.
- **Welcome is optional, max 90 sec**, and counts toward the time. English is advised.
- **Aromas are declared up front** and handed to the jury before the show.
- The host supplies buckets, ladles, water and ice. Costumes, décor, music and lighting are the performer's call; music and light cues get submitted online by a deadline.
- **Setup max 15 min with up to 2 assistants; cleanup max 10 min.** Nothing can block the entrance or exit or break fire rules.
- **Singles = one master alone. Teams = 2 or 3 masters.** No help from bystanders during the show.
- Breaking any rule = immediate disqualification. So does visible drunkenness, and a country can be DQ'd for its fans' misbehavior.
- *Not in the 2026 rules:* the "only water or ice with essential oils on the stones" and "hand fan max 2 minutes" limits we'd seen quoted elsewhere. Treat those as house or older rules, not current WM rules.

### 3d. Proposed E&E ritual session (fits our 90-min slot, max 8 guests)

| Time | Phase | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:08 | **Threshold** | Dock greeting, waivers done, phones in the box, crossing to the boat, a quiet word about the theme |
| 0:08–0:11 | **Intention** | ≤ 90 sec framing inside the sauna, safety line ("leave any time"), first scent introduced |
| 0:11–0:23 | **Tide 1: Ember** | Gentle pour + light towel work, ~12 min |
| 0:23–0:33 | **Tide 1: Ebb** | Plunge or river air, rest on deck |
| 0:33–0:45 | **Tide 2: Ember** | Hotter pour, fuller towel work, sound or story builds |
| 0:45–0:55 | **Tide 2: Ebb** | Plunge, rest |
| 0:55–1:07 | **Tide 3: Ember** | Peak round, or a deliberate silent round for stillness themes |
| 1:07–1:20 | **Tide 3: Ebb** | Longest rest. Blankets, tea, silence on the deck |
| 1:20–1:30 | **Closing** | Circle, one word each or a bell, leave the boat |

*For Banya Mondays (1h45) the same shape works, with venik in rounds 2–3 and a longer closing.*

### 3e. Timing to natural cycles

**Time of day (Portland):**
- **Sunrise sessions** match intention and "beginning" themes. Our 6:30 AM series already sits here. Winter sunrise is ~7:45–7:50 AM, so December mornings start in darkness, which works well with lanterns.
- **Sunset / blue hour** match release and "ending" themes. Sunset runs ~4:30 PM in late December and ~9:00 PM in late June.
- **Night** matches moon, stars and stillness.

**The river is tidal.** The Columbia and lower Willamette at Portland are tidally influenced all the way up to Bonneville Dam. The swing is small and gets swamped by high river flow in spring, but in low-flow late summer and fall you can see the water rise and fall twice a day. That gives us a true story: *the ocean reaches us here.* Check NOAA tide predictions for Portland/Vancouver when planning a "Slack Tide" or "Turning Tide" session. **Slack tide**, the still moment between ebb and flood, is a natural stillness ritual.

**Seasonal turns:**
- **Winter Solstice:** Mon Dec 21, 2026 (12:49 PM). Longest night. "Return of the Light."
- **Spring Equinox:** Sat Mar 20, 2027. Balance, equal day and night.
- **Summer Solstice:** Mon Jun 21, 2027. Longest light, late sunset session.
- **Fall Equinox:** Thu Sep 23, 2027.

**Other Portland/Columbia cycles:** spring freshet (snowmelt, high fast water), fall salmon runs, first frost, the first "real" rain in October, the first cold-plunge-under-45°F day.

> **Care note on salmon and river themes:** the Columbia's salmon are sacred to the river's tribal nations (Yakama, Umatilla, Warm Springs, Nez Perce, and others). We can honor the river and the season in our own words, but we should not borrow Indigenous ceremony. If we ever want to go deeper, partner with and pay Native-led organizations instead of staging it ourselves.

## 4. Aufguss Deep Dive

### What it is
A **guided infusion** led by a sauna master. Water or ice mixed with essential oils goes onto the hot stones, and the master uses a towel to push waves of scented heat to each guest. Moving air carries heat to the skin much better than still air, so waving makes the room feel far hotter at the same temperature. The tradition grew out of German sauna culture. It became a performance art mostly in Germany, the Netherlands, Poland, Czechia and Italy, and it's now spreading fast in the UK and US.

### Two styles
- **Classic / Modern Classic:** focused on the heat, scent and towel technique. Little or no costume or story. Judges look at professionalism, heat management, waving skill, fragrance choice and dosage, theme and atmosphere. Scored out of 100. *This is the style that fits our boat.*
- **Show Aufguss:** full theater with a story, costumes, props, lighting, choreographed music and multiple performers. 12–15 minutes, inside a sauna, in front of up to 200–300 people.

### Towel techniques (vocabulary)
- **Helicopter:** overhead rotation that spreads heat evenly around the room
- **Single-hand / figure-eight:** controlled waves aimed at one section
- **Two-towel technique:** a towel in each hand, symmetric and rhythmic
- **Throw & catch / release:** tossing the towel for a big push of air (showpiece)
- **Walking waves:** the master moves bench to bench, delivering heat personally
- **Fan (in competition, ≤ 2 min):** a large hand fan for a strong direct push

### How it's judged (Aufguss WM)
Juries score: **increase and distribution of heat**, **waving technique**, **fragrance choice and dosage**, **theme and its execution** (dramaturgy, music, emotion, audience connection), **safety and professionalism**, and **team spirit** (team events only). Max score is **750 points for singles** and **900 for teams**.

How the math works (2026 rules): each criterion is worth 8, 10, 12, 15 or 30 points, so one juror's max is 75 (singles) or 90 (teams). Six jurors score from inside the sauna, the highest and lowest are dropped, and the result is scaled to a 10-person jury, which is how you get 750/900. A show has to earn at least half the points to advance to the finals. The main jury is 12 people split into two panels for the finals.

*This rubric is a great internal training checklist for our hosts, even for a simple 12-min infusion.*

### The World Championship (Aufguss WM)
- Held every September. Recent hosts: **Satama Sauna Resort & Spa** (Germany; 2023 and again 2026, Sept 13–20), **Thermen Bussloo** (Netherlands; 2024; now the playoff venue), **Aquardens Terme Verona** (Italy; 2025).
- **78 starting places** in the finals (39 singles, 39 teams), plus one host wild card that always performs first. Competitors come from about 15 countries (including Japan and now the US).
- You qualify through your country's national qualifier. Countries without one get a single slot in the Playoffs (€150 entry, first come first served). You can compete for your home country or one you've worked in for at least a year.
- The US has its own qualifier: **Aufguss USA Nationals** (May 2026, Bathhouse in Brooklyn and Flatiron, NYC). The top two in show categories go straight to the WM Finals.
- Satama's competition sauna is **160 m², seats ~200, and runs on a 180 kW heater**. For scale, that's a room the size of a small house.

**What the winning shows were made of:**

- **"Behind the Door" (Niedermair, 2024).** An emotional portrait of a tortured artist who is afraid of the world. The scent carried the emotion: **rhododendron, styrax, frankincense and birch** were layered into "an agonized aromatic journey." The lesson: **scent can carry the story arc** from dark and resinous to fresh and green.
- **"False Fortune" (van Rijswijk, 2025).** Guests "enter" the shop of Charlotte, a fake fortune teller. The show opens with a customer arriving, then darkness and a crack of thunder. When the light returns, Charlotte is at her table. She reads the customer's future, and the customer draws the Devil card. The twist is that Charlotte starts to realize she may really have the gift. The lessons: **a blackout/sound cue as the threshold**, a single strong character, and a **turn** in the middle of the story.
- **"Reflections" (van Rijswijk & van Mierden, 2021).** A two-person show built on mirroring and symmetry. Mirrored towel work is one of the most striking things a team can do.
- **Rob Keijzer era.** Helped define the modern show: music, light and sound cues, freestyle plus classic technique, and "beautiful transitions." Other famous shows have acted out Shakespeare or *Top Gun*.

**Pattern across the winners:** one clear emotional arc, scent that changes with the story, a strong opening moment, heat that escalates round by round, and a quiet or resolving ending. They're built like a three-act play, which is the same seven-part ritual shape from section 2.

### Other big performances worth knowing
- **Opera in Sauna: *Carmen Aufguss* (Aquardens, Verona).** A world first, made with the Arena di Verona opera foundation. It opened Sept 14, 2025 in a new **circular 160 m² sauna-theater that seats ~300**, 150 years after Carmen premiered. It runs 12 intense minutes, and **large colorful fans replace towels** as part of the costume. It was followed in May 2026 by ***La Traviata Aufguss***. The lesson: partner with a local arts institution. (A Portland version could pair with a local musician, a choir or Portland Opera.)
- **Sauna-Theatre programming** (Aquardens, Satama, Thermen Bussloo). Daily show infusions on a schedule, with champions touring their shows to home spas afterward. Van Rijswijk's *False Fortune* went on tour at Thermen Resorts after her win.
- **Danish saunagus scene (Copenhagen).** Venues like Sauna House, Plugin Heat Club and Heat Studio. Gentler and more meditative, mixing breath, sound, scrubs and cold sea water. *Closest model to our river setting.*
- **US Aufguss** (Bathhouse NYC, Las Vegas resorts, Thermea in Canada). Aufguss is now the headline experience at big US bathhouses and is marketed as a "show."

### Adjacent traditions for depth
- **Russian Banya** (we already have it, Banya Mondays). Venik branches, herbal steam, a practitioner working on each guest.
- **Estonian smoke sauna (Võromaa)** is UNESCO-listed intangible heritage. Firewood and whisk branches are traditionally gathered **in a chosen season and moon phase**. Historically the sauna marked births, healings and deaths, and "ritual whisking ended one period of life and started a new one." That's a strong source for our moon and cycle themes.
- **Finnish löyly.** Steam as "spirit." The Finnish attitude is that you don't talk loudly in the sauna, and silence is part of the practice.

## 5. Theme Bank

Each theme lists **scent** (oils in water/ice), **sound**, **touch/props** and **the moment**, which is the one thing guests remember.

### Fire: *"Kindling"* / *"Ember"*
- **Scent:** birch tar (a tiny amount), cedarwood, frankincense, cinnamon/clove in winter
- **Sound:** crackling fire, low drums building to the peak round
- **Props:** a single lantern lit at the threshold and carried onto the boat
- **Moment:** each guest names one thing to "burn off" before round 3
- **Timing:** dusk in fall and winter, new moon

### Water / Tide: *"Slack Tide"* / *"Turning Tide"* / *"Three Tides"*
- **Scent:** sea salt, eucalyptus, mint, then cypress. Pour on **ice** for a slow, gentle release.
- **Sound:** the river itself. Open the door between rounds and let people hear it.
- **Touch:** a salt scrub between rounds (Danish-style), cold river air
- **Moment:** the host names the tide: *"Right now the ocean is pushing up this river, 100 miles from the coast."*
- **Timing:** match a slack tide in low-flow season (Aug–Oct)

### Moon: *"Full Moon Float"* / *"New Moon Sitting"*
- **Full moon (release, gathering):** jasmine, sandalwood, lavender. A moonrise sit on deck, no music in round 3, and the closing is quiet moon-watching. Book the night closest to full.
- **New moon (intention, darkness):** vetiver, black spruce, frankincense. Darkest sky, lanterns only, and guests write a short intention they keep.
- **Moment:** the host brings a simple note about that month's moon name or story in our own words.

### Cycles / Seasons
- **Winter Solstice: "Longest Night / Return of the Light."** Start in darkness, light one lantern per round, and finish with the full light.
- **Equinox: "Balance."** Equal-length heat and cold rounds, as a nod to equal day and night.
- **Spring Freshet: "High Water."** Energizing citrus and green scents, faster music.
- **First Frost / First Rain:** a coldest-plunge-of-the-year celebration.
- **Series structure:** a 4-session "one moon" series (new → first quarter → full → last quarter) as a membership perk.

### Community Connection: *"Circle"* / *"Gathering"*
- A shared bowl of tea or broth at the close
- A name round at the threshold (first names only, one word on how you arrived)
- **Banya-style paired whisking:** guests whisk each other with a practitioner guiding
- Monthly **members' night** on a fixed rhythm (for example, every full moon), so the calendar itself becomes the ritual
- Partner nights: run clubs finishing at the dock, choirs, local musicians (our version of Opera in Sauna)

### Stillness: *"The Quiet Round"* / *"Sitting"*
- **A silent Aufguss:** no music and no talking, just the pour, the hiss and the towel. A Finnish-style round.
- Breath cues only (4-count in, 6-count out) during the cold phase
- A longer final rest (15+ min) with blankets on deck, facing the water
- **Rule:** at least one round in every themed session should be quiet

### Yourself: *"Crossing"*
- An intention card at the threshold, carried through the session and kept at the end
- The cold plunge framed as a personal choice or "crossing," with no pressure
- A short journaling moment with a pencil and card on deck
- Milestone rites: birthday rounds, "first plunge" recognition, a 10th-visit ritual for members

## 6. Practical & Safety Notes for Doing Aufguss on the Boat

- **Never put undiluted essential oil on the stones.** It's flammable. Always use a few drops per ladle of water, or oils frozen into ice. Competition rules allow only water or ice with oils.
- **Confirm our heater/stove is rated for water and infusion pours** with the manufacturer before adding scented pours. This is especially important around propane.
- **Small room, strong effect.** With 8 guests in a small sauna, towel waves hit much harder than in a 200-seat theater. Start gentle, and let guests move to a lower bench.
- **Say it every time:** "Leave any time, no explanation needed." Watch for overheating (from [[How We Run Things — Ebb and Ember]], Safety Is the Job).
- **Light:** real flame only in lanterns and only on deck. Battery candles inside the sauna.
- **No jumping** stays true on every themed night, including the full moon ones.
- **Scent allergies/pregnancy:** tell guests the oils at the intro, and keep a no-scent option (plain water pours).
- **Host training:** use the WM judging categories (heat build, distribution, technique, scent dosage, theme, safety) as the checklist for training contractors. A host could eventually compete at Aufguss USA Nationals, which would be great PR.
