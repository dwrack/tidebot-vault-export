# Ebb & Ember — Event Brand Partnership Framework

*Version 2.0 — August 13, 2026. Owner: Davey. Applies to every produced event from here forward.*
*Numbers below are launch estimates, not history. Re-price after the first three sales.*

---

## The one-line version

We produce a small number of large, genuinely unrepeatable wellness events, we staff them with professional photographers and invited creators, and brands fund the whole thing in exchange for the room, the product placement, and the content that comes out of it.

**Default posture: every produced event is fully funded by brand partners before it goes on sale.**

---

## 1. What this actually is

Not a sauna session with a sponsor logo on it. A produced event:

- **40 to 60 guests** across a 4 to 5 hour window, rising to 60 to 80 once V2's two saunas double our throughput
- On the dock and the marina space next to it. Rolling sauna sessions as the anchor, with land programming (lounge, food, music, cold plunge, guided work) running continuously around them
- **Quarterly, four a year.** Seasonal anchors: solstice, equinox, summer kickoff, deep winter. Four events we actually produce well beats twelve we scramble through, and scarcity prices better
- **15 to 25 invited creators and press** in the room alongside members and ticket buyers
- A hired photographer and videographer working the whole event

That last two bullets are the product. Everything else is the set.

Say it this way when you're selling it:

> **"You're not sponsoring a party. You're commissioning a wellness event you didn't have to produce, professional content you own outright, and twenty creators posting from inside it."**

### Why the room has to be mixed

All creators and it doesn't convert, and everyone in the room knows they're at a media event. All public and there's no content engine. The ratio that works is roughly a third invited, two thirds paying guests and members. Brands should hear that ratio explicitly, because it's the reason the content looks real.

## 2. The economics

This is the spine of the whole framework. Everyone selling it needs these numbers in their head.

### What one event costs to produce

| Line | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Photographer (full event) | $800 – $1,500 |
| Videographer | $1,000 – $2,000 |
| Paid creators (2 to 3 mid-tier) | $1,500 – $3,000 |
| Food and beverage (50 to 60 people) | $1,000 – $2,500 |
| Staff and guides (4 to 6 people, 5 hours) | $800 – $1,200 |
| Rentals, lighting, heaters, tent | $500 – $1,500 |
| Music or DJ | $300 – $800 |
| Permits and insurance rider | $200 – $500 |
| **All-in** | **$5,000 – $8,000** |

Plan against **$7,000**. Anything under is upside.

### What we raise against it

| Source | Per event |
|---|---|
| Presenting Partner × 1 | $6,000 |
| Featured Partner × 1 | $3,500 |
| Supporting Partner × 2 | $4,000 |
| Product Partners × 2 | in-kind (covers gift bags and F&B) |
| Ticket revenue (~50 × $75) | $3,750 |
| **Total in** | **$17,250** |
| **Less production** | **($7,000)** |
| **Contribution** | **$10,250** |

That's the target shape. Even at half the partner sell-through it clears cost. This is why the rate card can't slide: a $3,000 top tier does not fund a $7,000 event, and a discounted Presenting deal quietly turns a profitable event into a break-even one.

**The floor rule: an event doesn't go on sale until confirmed partner cash covers 100% of the production budget.** Ticket revenue is upside, never the plan.

## 3. The two currencies

Brands pay in cash, in product, or both.

- **Product is valued at wholesale, not MSRP.** A brand offering "$3,000 of product" is offering what it cost them to make. Price the deal off that number.
- **In-kind alone caps a partner at Product Partner.** Every tier with a station, a name, or content rights requires cash. Product doesn't pay the photographer.
- **Every paid tier still includes a product component.** We want the thing in guests' hands, not just the logo on the invite. Write it into the agreement.
- **We only take product we'd hand a guest for free anyway.** If we wouldn't genuinely use it, no quantity of it is worth the placement.
- **Consumables and take-homes are the best in-kind there is.** Drinks, snacks, skincare, socks, towels, hats, robes.
- **Nothing that needs a health claim.** If the partnership only works when we make a promise about someone's body, it's a no.

## 4. The tiers

| Tier | Cash | Slots per event | Core of what they get |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Product Partner** | $0 (min $750 wholesale) | Unlimited, gift bag only | Product in the take-home and at the F&B station. Tagged in the recap post, one story mention. No exclusivity, no station, no content rights. |
| **Supporting** | $2,000 | 3 | Category exclusivity. A small station. Named in all promo. 10 edited photos with commercial rights. Story set. Recap report. |
| **Featured** | $3,500 | 2 | The above, plus a full station, a dedicated Reel, an email feature, inclusion in the creator brief, and the full photo and video package. |
| **Presenting** | $6,000 | 1 | Event carries their name. Everything above, plus custom content direction, opening remarks if they want them, website listing, and the opt-in guest list. |
| **Season Partner** | $20,000 / year | 1 | Presenting on two events, Featured on the other two, always-on product presence at the sauna year-round, first refusal on new formats. |

**Hard cap: six brands with on-site presence per event.** Product Partners in the gift bag don't count. Past six it reads as a trade show and the content gets ugly, which destroys the exact thing everyone paid for.

**Category exclusivity starts at Supporting.** It's the strongest upsell lever we have, and "another coffee brand is asking about this date" is true more often than you'd think.

**Season Partner is the goal.** One brand at $20,000 beats ten at $2,000: less pitching, less coordination, a deeper story, better content, and a partner who starts bringing us their own ideas. Run every single-event conversation as an audition for it.

## 5. What we're selling (asset inventory)

Use this to build any tier and to justify the price when someone pushes back.

| Asset | What it means | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Product in take-home | Every guest leaves with it | Product+ |
| Tag in recap post | Named in the post-event content | Product+ |
| Story set | 3 to 5 stories, day-of, with their handle | Product+ |
| Category exclusivity | No competing brand on site | Supporting+ |
| Activation station | A physical footprint they staff | Supporting+ |
| Named in promo | Their name in every pre-event post and email | Supporting+ |
| Photo package | Edited stills, cleared for commercial use | Supporting (10) / Featured (full) |
| Video package | Raw and cut vertical they can run as ads | Featured+ |
| Dedicated Reel | A Reel about them, not just the event | Featured+ |
| Email feature | Their block in our newsletter | Featured+ |
| Creator brief inclusion | Invited creators are briefed to feature them | Featured+ |
| Event naming | "Winter Solstice, presented by X" | Presenting |
| Custom content direction | They shape part of the shot list | Presenting |
| Opt-in guest list | Names and emails of guests who checked the box | Presenting |
| Website listing | Events page, logo and link | Presenting |
| Always-on presence | Product living at the sauna between events | Season |
| Recap report | What ran, what it reached, what guests said | Supporting+ |

**The content rights are the quietly expensive part.** A brand paying an agency for a half-day shoot in a location like ours spends $2,500 to $5,000 and gets worse footage with hired models. Add a creator campaign on top and they're at $10,000 to $15,000 for what a Presenting deal delivers at $6,000. That gap is the entire arbitrage. Say it out loud in the pitch.

Never hand over unlimited perpetual usage below Featured.

## 6. The creator and content engine

This is what makes the event worth more than the room, so it gets run deliberately, not casually.

### The mix

- **2 to 3 paid mid-tier creators** at $250 to $750 each, with a signed deliverable list. These are the ones we can hold to a date and a format.
- **12 to 20 comped micro creators and press.** Comped session, take-home product, no cash. Deliverables requested, not contractually required, and we invite based on who actually posted last time.
- **1 photographer and 1 videographer,** hired, working for us. Their output is what we license to partners, which means we control quality and rights.

### The brief

Every creator gets one short brief before the event. It names which partners to feature, gives the tags and handles, states the posting window, and says what we will not ask them to say. Never a script. If a creator's post reads like an ad, the whole event reads like an ad, and the brand loses the thing they paid for.

### The deliverable standard

Paid creators owe, in writing: one in-feed Reel or carousel, three stories with tags, and raw footage handed back to us. Posted within 7 days. Comped creators are asked for stories same-day and one post within 10 days.

### Rights

We hold the rights to everything the hired photographer and videographer shoot, and we license usage down to partners by tier. Creator content belongs to the creator, with permission to repost secured up front by both us and the partner. Get this in writing before the event, not after someone asks.

## 7. Ticketing

**Always ticket it.** $65 to $95, expect 60 to 80% sell-through without discounting.

Free events no-show at around 40%, which leaves gaps in a room that six brands paid for, and free framing tells everyone the experience isn't worth money. A paid ticket is also the cleanest filter for who's actually interested.

Members get first access and a member rate. Comped seats go to creators, press, and partner guests, and they're counted and capped before public tickets go on sale, not handed out as the date gets close.

## 8. Who we say yes to

**Natural fits:** non-alcoholic drinks, coffee and tea, local food, skincare and body care, technical apparel and outerwear, socks and base layers, swimwear, towels and robes, footwear, hydration, run clubs and outdoor clubs, Portland-made goods, hotels and lodging, breweries and cider with an NA line, cameras and audio gear, local artists and musicians.

**Three questions before you pitch anyone:**

1. Would a guest be glad this brand is here, or would they feel marketed at?
2. Does the product actually belong in a hot room, a cold river, or the hour after?
3. Would we recommend it if they weren't paying us?

Three yeses, pitch them. Any no, pass.

**Green flags:** they already post about Portland or the outdoors, their product survives steam and river water, they've paid for content before and know what it costs, a person replies to the email rather than an agency inbox, and they ask what the guests are like before they ask about reach.

**Auto-no:** MLMs, anything selling a wellness cure, supplements making claims, crypto, vape and nicotine, hard alcohol as the centerpiece (heat plus liquor is a safety problem, not a brand problem), any brand wanting approval over what guests or creators say, and anyone who leads with "we'll give you exposure."

## 9. Guardrails that don't move

The events get funded by brands. They don't get *authored* by brands.

- **No logo takeover.** The location is the hero. Stations are built from natural materials to our spec and approved by us. No step-and-repeat, no vinyl banners, no branded arch on a dock.
- **No scripted talking points,** for us or for creators. We describe what the product is and why we like it, in our voice. Language read verbatim is an ad and gets priced as one.
- **No health claims.** Not by us, not by a rep on the deck, not in a caption, not by a creator. This is the one that gets us in real trouble.
- **The plunge stays optional.** No activation ever makes going in the water the price of admission.
- **Guests aren't leads.** We only share emails from people who actively opted in, and the checkbox names exactly who receives it.
- **Six brands, maximum.** The cap protects the product every partner paid for. It is not negotiable for a bigger check.
- **Cedar voice holds.** Grounded, direct, no wellness guru energy. If you can't tell a partner post is ours without the logo, rewrite it.

## 10. How we sell it

### The five beats

1. **The event.** "Four times a year we produce a wellness event on the Columbia. Sixty people, five hours, saunas running the whole time."
2. **The room.** "A third of it is invited creators and press. The rest are members and people who bought a ticket. That mix is why the content looks real instead of staged."
3. **The output.** "We hire a photographer and a videographer. You get the files, cleared for commercial use, plus around twenty creators posting from inside it."
4. **The math.** "An agency shoot plus a creator campaign runs you $12,000 to $15,000 and you still have to produce the event. Presenting is $6,000."
5. **The proof.** "Recap in your inbox ten days after, with the content, the numbers, and what guests actually said about your product."

### Cold outreach email

> **Subject:** Floating sauna event, [Month] — thought of [Brand]
>
> Hey [Name],
>
> We run Ebb & Ember, the floating sauna on the Columbia in Portland. Four times a year we produce a full event on the water: about 60 guests over five hours, saunas running the whole time, plus a photographer, a videographer, and 20 or so invited creators.
>
> [One specific, real reason this brand fits. Something you actually noticed about them.]
>
> Partners get a station at the event, product in every guest's hands, and the full photo and video package cleared for their own use. Buying that separately as a shoot plus a creator campaign runs $12,000 and up, and you'd still have to produce the event.
>
> [Event name] is [date]. Presenting is $6,000, and there are supporting spots at $2,000. Happy to work part of it as product if that's easier on your side.
>
> Want the one-pager?
>
> [Name]
> Ebb & Ember

Short on purpose. The one-pager does the selling. The email only has to earn the reply.

### The one-pager

1. Photo of the sauna at golden hour, no text over it
2. Event name, date, format, guest count, creator count
3. Who's in the room, honestly described
4. What the partner gets, bulleted, for the one tier we're pitching
5. Reach numbers, refreshed within the last 30 days
6. Three photos from a past event
7. Price, and the date we need an answer by

Pitch **one tier**. Hold one upgrade in your pocket.

### The objections you'll always get

**"Sixty people is small."**
"For sixty people, yes. But you're not buying the sixty. You're buying a professional shoot in a place nobody can replicate, twenty creators posting from inside it, and your product in the hands of people in the best mood they've been in all month. The room is the set."

**"Can we just send product?"**
"Absolutely, and that gets you in the take-home bag with a tag. If you want a station, exclusivity in your category, and the content package with rights, that starts at $2,000, because it covers the photographer and the crew."

**"Can you do $6,000 for $3,500?"**
"I can move you to Featured at $3,500, which is a real spot. I can't discount Presenting, because the event budget is built on it and cutting it means cutting the photographer, which is the thing you're actually buying."

## 11. Running one

An event this size needs a 90-day runway. The dates that matter:

| When | What | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| T−90 | Concept locked, date set, production budget written, target brand list built (12 to 15 names) | Events |
| T−85 | Outreach begins. Presenting pitched first, always | Events |
| T−60 | **Funding gate: confirmed partner cash covers 100% of production budget.** Not there? Cut scope or move the date | Events + Davey |
| T−55 | Remaining tiers sold. Six-brand cap enforced | Events |
| T−50 | Photographer and videographer booked. Paid creators contracted | Social |
| T−45 | Tickets on sale. Members first, 48 hours ahead | Social |
| T−40 | Partner kickoff calls. What they want, what shots they need, what they'll post | Events + Social |
| T−30 | Content plan built. Every asset in every tier gets a date and an owner | Social |
| T−21 | Creator invites out with the brief. Partner cross-promo live | Social |
| T−14 | Ticket check. Soft? Comp members and creators before the room looks empty | Events |
| T−10 | Stations designed and approved. Product on site and counted | Events |
| T−7 | Shot list written. Permits, insurance rider, and safety plan confirmed | Events |
| T−2 | Run of show to everyone working it, partner reps included | Events |
| Day of | Shoot it. Stories live. Nobody pitches from the deck | Social |
| T+2 | Photos and clips delivered to partners. Speed here is most of the reason people renew | Social |
| T+7 | Creator posting window closes. Chase anyone who owes | Social |
| T+10 | Recap sent to every partner. Renewal ask in the same email | Events |

**The T−60 funding gate is the discipline that makes this whole framework real.** Once we run an unfunded event "just this once," we're back to eating $7,000 every quarter.

## 12. Who does what

- **Events lead** owns the pipeline, outreach, the pricing conversation, the production budget, the run of show, and the renewal ask. Owns the yes or no on brand fit.
- **Social lead** owns the content plan, the creator brief and roster, the shot list, everything posted, delivery to partners, and the numbers in the recap.
- **Davey** approves the production budget, the funding gate call at T−60, anything at Presenting or Season, and any exception to the guardrails.
- **Ops and guides** own the sessions themselves. The partnership never changes how a session is run.
- **Producer (hire later):** at $1,500 to $2,500 per event, once we're genuinely running four a year. Not before. Davey, Hannah, and Jess carry the first two.

Nobody sends pricing to a brand without the events lead. One voice, one rate card. That's the whole reason this document exists.

## 13. The recap is the product

The cheapest thing we do and the reason partners come back. Sent within 10 days, every time, to every tier including free Product Partners.

- Every asset we promised, with a link and a checkmark
- Reach, views, saves, and profile visits across our content and the creators'
- Creator posts, listed, with their individual reach
- Bookings or clicks driven, if we can trace them
- Two or three real quotes from guests about the product
- The photo and video files, delivered clean and usable
- One line proposing what's next, with a date and a tier

That last line is the entire renewal strategy. Never end a recap without an ask in it.

---

## 14. Tracking

One sheet, one row per brand. Columns: Brand, Category, Contact, Status, Tier discussed, Cash, In-kind value (wholesale), Event, Close date, Delivered, Recap sent, Renewal ask, Notes.

Status: `Target → Contacted → Replied → Pitched → Verbal → Confirmed → Delivered → Recapped → Renewed / Dead`

Review every two weeks. Three numbers matter:

1. **Funding coverage** — partner cash as a percentage of production budget, per event
2. **Pitch-to-close rate**
3. **Second-event rate** — how many partners came back. This is the one that decides whether the model works
