# Ebb & Ember — Event Brand Partnership Framework

*Version 1.0 — August 13, 2026. Owner: Davey. Applies to every event we run from here forward.*

---

## The one-line version

We don't sell brands a ticket count. We sell them a room full of exactly their customer, on the only floating sauna on the Columbia River, and the content that comes out of it.

**Default posture: every event has a brand partner, or we don't run it.**

---

## 1. Why we run events this way

The math forces it. A social session holds 8 guests. A private holds 10. No event on this boat will ever be an impressions play by headcount, so we stop pretending it is.

The guest count is the *set*, not the product. What a brand is actually buying:

- A curated room of 8 to 10 people who match their target almost exactly, for 1 hour 45 minutes
- Their product in someone's hands during a ritual, not in a swag bag in a parking lot
- A content package shot in a location nothing else in Portland can copy
- Distribution well past the room: our Instagram reached about 200,000 accounts in the last 28 days and adds 20 to 40 followers a day, our email list is around 2,300 people, plus the partner's own channels and whatever the guests post themselves

That last point is the whole pitch. A brand spends $1,500 on a photo shoot with hired models and gets sterile images nobody believes. They spend the same with us and get real people, real steam, real river, plus a live audience of their buyer and a partner who pushes the content out. Small room, big output.

Say it this way when you're selling it: **"You're not sponsoring a party. You're funding a content shoot that happens to have your customer sitting inside it."**

---

## 2. The two currencies

Brands pay in cash, in product, or both. Both are welcome. The rules that keep it from getting sloppy:

- **Product is valued at wholesale, not MSRP.** A brand that says they're giving us "$2,000 of product" is giving us what it cost them to make. Price the deal off that number.
- **In-kind alone caps a partner at Tier 2.** Featured, Presenting, and Season require real dollars. Product doesn't pay the guide, the photographer, or the boat.
- **We only take product we'd hand a guest for free anyway.** If it's not something we'd genuinely use, no amount of it is worth the placement.
- **Consumables and take-homes are the best in-kind of all.** Drinks, snacks, skincare, socks, towels, hats. Things that get used during the session or leave with the guest.
- **Nothing that requires a health claim.** We don't say cortisol, detox, immunity, recovery science, or anything a supplement brand wants us to say. If the partnership only works when we make a promise about someone's body, it's a no.

## 3. The tiers

Rates below are our **starting** rate card. They're a launch position, not scripture. Re-price after the first three sales based on what actually closed and what didn't.

| Tier | Cash | Or in-kind (wholesale) | Core of what they get |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Product Drop** | $0 | Under $500 | Product in-session, tagged in our recap post, one story mention. No naming, no exclusivity. |
| **1. Supporting** | $500 | $1,000+ | The above, plus a guest take-home, a named mention in event promo, and 5 photos they can use. |
| **2. Featured** | $1,500 | $2,500+ *(in-kind only allowed at this tier and below)* | The above, plus category exclusivity for that event, a dedicated Reel, an email feature, and the full content package. |
| **3. Presenting** | $3,000 | Cash required | Event carries their name. Everything above, plus custom content direction, on-deck presence, and the opt-in guest list. |
| **4. Season Partner** | $10,000 / season | Cash required | 4 events, always-on presence at the sauna between events, first refusal on new formats, quarterly recap. |

Two things to hold firm on:

**Category exclusivity starts at Tier 2.** Below that, a brand is sharing the room. Say so up front. It's also the single best upsell lever we have: "another coffee brand asked about this date" is true more often than you'd think, and it moves people up a tier.

**Season Partner is the goal, not the Product Drop.** One brand at $10,000 a season beats twenty at $500 in every way that matters: less pitching, less coordination, deeper story, better content. Every Tier 0 and Tier 1 conversation should be run as an audition for Tier 3 or 4.

## 4. What we're actually selling (asset inventory)

This is the menu. Use it to build any tier, and to justify the price when someone pushes back.

| Asset | What it means | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Product in-session | Their product used or served during the 1:45 | 0+ |
| Guest take-home | Every guest leaves with it | 1+ |
| Tag in recap post | Named in the post-event Instagram content | 0+ |
| Story set | 3 to 5 stories, day-of, with their handle | 0+ |
| Named in promo | Their name in every pre-event post and email | 1+ |
| Photo package | Edited stills, cleared for their commercial use | 1+ (5 photos), 2+ (15+) |
| Vertical clips | Raw and cut vertical video they can run as ads | 2+ |
| Dedicated Reel | A Reel about them, not just the event | 2+ |
| Email feature | Their block in our newsletter to ~2,300 | 2+ |
| Category exclusivity | No competing brand at that event | 2+ |
| Event naming | "Sunset Series, presented by X" | 3+ |
| On-deck presence | A rep on site, talking to guests, not pitching | 3+ |
| Opt-in guest list | Names and emails of guests who checked the box | 3+ |
| Website listing | On the events page with logo and link | 3+ |
| Always-on presence | Product living at the sauna between events | 4 |
| Recap report | What ran, what it reached, what guests said | 1+ |

The photo and video rights are the quietly expensive part. A brand paying an agency for a half-day shoot in a location like ours is spending $2,500 to $5,000 and getting worse footage. Price accordingly, and never give away unlimited perpetual usage at Tier 0 or 1.

## 5. Who we say yes to

**Natural fits:** non-alcoholic drinks, coffee and tea, local food, skincare and body care, technical apparel and outerwear, socks and base layers, swimwear, towels and robes, footwear, hydration, run clubs and outdoor clubs, Portland-made goods, hotels and lodging, breweries and cider with an NA line, cameras and audio gear, local artists and musicians.

**Ask three questions before pitching anyone:**

1. Would a guest be glad this brand is here, or would they feel marketed at?
2. Does the product actually belong in a hot room, a cold river, or the hour after?
3. Would we recommend it if they weren't paying us?

Three yeses, pitch them. Any no, pass.

**Auto-no list:** MLMs, anything selling a wellness cure, supplements making claims, crypto, vape and nicotine, hard alcohol as the centerpiece (heat plus liquor is a safety problem, not a brand problem), any brand that wants approval over what our guests say, and anyone who leads the conversation with "we'll give you exposure."

## 6. Guardrails that don't move

The events get funded by brands. They don't get *authored* by brands. Non-negotiable:

- **No logo takeover.** The boat is the hero. Signage is small, natural material, and approved by us. No step-and-repeat, no vinyl banners, no branded arch on a dock.
- **No scripted talking points.** We describe what the product is and why we like it, in our voice. If they need specific language read aloud, that's an ad, and it's priced as one.
- **No health claims. Ever.** Not by us, not by their rep on the deck, not in the caption. This is the one that gets us in real trouble.
- **The plunge stays optional.** No partner activation ever makes going in the water the price of admission.
- **Guests aren't leads.** We only share emails from people who actively opted in, and the checkbox says exactly who gets it.
- **Cedar voice holds.** Grounded, direct, no wellness guru energy. A partner post should still sound like us. If you can't tell it's ours without the logo, rewrite it.

## 7. How we sell it

### The five beats

Every pitch, in this order. It fits in an email or a two-minute conversation.

1. **The room.** "Eight people, hand-picked, for an hour and forty-five minutes. Not a booth they walk past."
2. **The place.** "Portland's only floating sauna on the Columbia. Your product has never been shot anywhere like it."
3. **The reach.** "Our Instagram reached about 200,000 accounts last month. Plus our list, plus yours, plus whatever the guests post."
4. **The ask.** One tier. Name a number. Don't present the full menu, present the tier you think fits and hold one upgrade in your pocket.
5. **The proof.** "We'll send you a recap in ten days with the content, the numbers, and what guests actually said."

### Cold outreach email

> **Subject:** Floating sauna event, [Month] — thought of [Brand]
>
> Hey [Name],
>
> We run Ebb & Ember, the floating sauna on the Columbia in Portland. We host small events on the boat, 8 to 10 people, and we build each one around one brand instead of cluttering it with five.
>
> [One specific, real reason this brand fits. Something you actually noticed about them.]
>
> The short version of what a partner gets: your product in guests' hands during the session, a photo and video package shot on the water you can use however you want, and promotion across our channels. Our Instagram reached about 200,000 accounts last month.
>
> [Event name] is [date]. Partnership runs $[X], or we can work it as product depending on what makes sense for you.
>
> Want me to send the one-pager?
>
> [Name]
> Ebb & Ember

Short on purpose. The one-pager does the selling, the email just earns the reply.

### The one-pager

One page. Build it once as a template, swap the event details each time:

1. Photo of the sauna at golden hour, no text over it
2. Event name, date, format, guest count
3. Who's in the room (the guest profile, honestly described)
4. What the partner gets, as a bulleted list, for the one tier we're pitching
5. Reach numbers, refreshed within the last 30 days
6. Three photos from a past event
7. Price and the date we need an answer by

### Handling the two objections you'll always get

**"Only eight people?"** — "Right, and that's why the content is good. Eight people who actually want to be there beats a hundred walking past a table. The room is the set. The audience is the 200,000 accounts that see what we make in it."

**"Can we just send product?"** — "Absolutely, and that gets you [Tier 0 or 1]. If you want your name on the event and the full content package with exclusivity, that's [Tier 2+] and it needs to include cash, because it covers the photographer and the crew."

## 8. Running one: the timeline

| When | What | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| T-45 days | Event concept locked, date on the calendar, target brand list built (5 to 8 names) | Events |
| T-40 | Outreach sent | Events |
| T-30 | **Partner confirmed and paid or shipped. Hard gate: no partner, we cancel or downgrade to a normal session.** | Events |
| T-28 | Kickoff: what they want out of it, what shots they need, what they'll post | Events + Social |
| T-25 | Content plan built. Every asset in the tier gets a date and an owner | Social |
| T-21 | Promo goes live. Booking link, email, partner cross-post | Social |
| T-14 | Guest list check. If it's soft, comp seats to members and creators before we let the room look empty | Events |
| T-7 | Product on site and counted. Photographer confirmed. Shot list written | Events |
| T-2 | Run of show sent to everyone working it, partner rep included | Events |
| Day of | Shoot it. Stories live. Nobody pitches from the deck | Social |
| T+2 | Photos and clips delivered to the partner. Speed here is most of the reason people renew | Social |
| T+10 | Recap sent. Renewal or next-tier conversation happens in the same email | Events |

The T-30 gate is the discipline that makes this framework real. If we run unfunded events "just this once," we're back to eating the cost every time.

## 9. Who does what

- **Events lead** owns the pipeline, the outreach, the pricing conversation, the run of show, and the renewal ask. Owns the yes or no on brand fit.
- **Social lead** owns the content plan, everything that gets posted, the shot list, delivery to the partner, and the numbers in the recap.
- **Davey** approves the fit filter, anything at Tier 3 or above, and any exception to the guardrails.
- **Ops and guides** own the session itself. The partnership never changes how the session is run.

Nobody sends pricing to a brand without the events lead. One voice, one rate card. That's the whole reason this document exists.

---

## 10. The recap is the product

The recap is why a partner comes back, and it's the cheapest thing we do. Sent within 10 days, every time, to every tier including free product drops.

What's in it:

- Every asset we promised, with a link and a checkmark
- Reach, views, saves, and profile visits on the event content
- Bookings or clicks driven, if we can trace them
- Two or three real quotes from guests about the product
- The photo and video files, delivered clean and usable
- One line proposing what's next, with a date and a tier

That last line is the entire renewal strategy. Never end a recap without an ask in it.

---

## 11. Tracking

One sheet, one row per brand. Columns: Brand, Category, Contact, Status, Tier discussed, Cash, In-kind value (wholesale), Event, Close date, Delivered, Recap sent, Renewal ask, Notes.

Status stages: `Target → Contacted → Replied → Pitched → Verbal → Confirmed → Delivered → Recapped → Renewed / Dead`

Review it every two weeks. Two numbers matter: how many pitches turned into a confirmed partner, and how many partners did a second event. The second number is the one that decides whether this works.
