# Profit Psych — Ebb & Ember Strategies

*Psychological triggers applied to Ebb and Ember, April 2026.*

---

## 1. The Hook — Standing Out in Portland's Saturated Wellness Market

**1.1 The "First Plunge" Challenge.** Run a standing campaign: your first cold plunge is $10. Frame it as a dare, not a discount. The language matters: "Most people talk about cold plunge. Few actually do it." This converts curious scrollers into paying first-timers at a price point that removes all friction.

**1.2 Fog and Fire Visuals.** Portland mornings are gray and moody, which makes steam rising off the sauna absolutely cinematic. Shoot content at dawn when the contrast between hot wood and cold air creates those billowing clouds. This is the single best organic content asset E&E has, and it should dominate the Instagram grid.

**1.3 Neighborhood Pop-Up Residencies.** Partner with Portland breweries, cideries, or restaurants (think Culmination, Wayfinder, or Ex Novo) for weekend sauna pop-ups in their parking lots. You inherit their foot traffic and social following, they get a unique draw. The hook is novelty: "sauna at a brewery" stops the scroll.

**1.4 The Contrast Therapy Elevator Pitch.** Most Portlanders have heard of cold plunge but don't understand contrast therapy. Create a 30-second Instagram Reel that explains the dopamine/norepinephrine spike from hot-to-cold cycling with actual numbers (up to 250% increase in dopamine, per Huberman-cited studies). Science sells to the biohacker crowd.

**1.5 "Date Night, But Make It Weird."** Position the couples experience as the anti-dinner-and-a-movie. Run targeted ads to 25-40 year olds in Portland with the hook: "Skip the overpriced cocktail bar. Sit in 180 degrees together, then jump in 39 degrees together. You'll actually talk." Lean into Portland's appetite for unconventional dates.

**1.6 Corporate Suffering as Team Building.** Reframe the cold plunge as a shared adversity experience for corporate groups. The pitch to HR: "Your team will bond more in 3 minutes of cold water than in 3 hours of trust falls." Cold exposure genuinely triggers social bonding hormones. Use that science in outreach to Portland tech companies and creative agencies.

**1.7 The "Why Are You Still Going to a Spa?" Positioning.** Draw a hard line between E&E and traditional Portland spas (Knot Springs, Löyly, Common Ground). Their thing is ambient relaxation. Your thing is active, intentional contrast therapy outdoors, in Portland air, with ritual and purpose. Don't compete with spas. Redefine the category.

**1.8 Hyperlocal SEO Content.** Write landing pages for every specific use case: "cold plunge Portland," "sauna date night Portland," "corporate wellness Portland," "bachelorette party Portland," "outdoor sauna Portland OR." Each page targets a different searcher with different intent. Most competitors aren't doing this.

**1.9 The Staff As Characters.** Jordan, Mack, Hanna, and Jess aren't "staff." They're sauna guides. Give each of them a recurring presence on Instagram with their own personality. Mack's cold plunge tips. Hanna's breathing walkthrough. People book experiences from people, not brands.

**1.10 Founder Story During Ownership Transition.** The ownership transition is actually a hook if framed right. "New chapter, same fire." People in Portland root for local businesses going through real transitions. Be transparent about it on social, not corporate about it.

**1.11 The Sauna Curious Quiz.** Build a 5-question quiz on the website ("What's Your Sauna Style?") that segments visitors into archetypes: The Biohacker, The Ritual Seeker, The Social Soaker, The Recovery Athlete. Each result recommends a specific experience and captures an email. Quizzes convert 30-40% of visitors who start them.

**1.12 Rain Season Marketing.** Portland gets 150+ days of rain. Flip the weather objection: "The rain hits the hot barrel. The steam rises. You're 185 degrees and the world is gray and wet and perfect." Make November through March the brand's peak content season, not a dead zone.

**1.13 "Bring Someone Who's Never Done It."** Referral hook with social pressure baked in. Offer $15 off when you bring a first-timer. The psychology is dual: the referrer gets to play guide, and the new person has a built-in companion to lower the intimidation factor.

**1.14 The Wim Hof / Huberman Funnel.** A huge percentage of people interested in cold plunge discovered it through Wim Hof or Andrew Huberman. Create content that explicitly says "You listened to Huberman. You watched the Wim Hof documentary. Now actually do it, with guides who know what they're doing, 10 minutes from downtown Portland."

**1.15 Local Media As Credibility.** Pitch Portland Monthly, Willamette Week, and The Oregonian for a "contrast therapy is the new happy hour" angle. One feature piece generates months of organic search traffic and screenshot-worthy social proof. Reach out before summer when editors are planning warm-weather wellness content.

**1.16 The Silent Sauna Session.** Offer a weekly "no talking" sauna experience. Phones off, eyes closed, just heat and breath. This hooks the meditation crowd who find regular group experiences too social. It also creates a distinct product that no competitor in Portland offers.

**1.17 Before/After Thermal Imaging.** If you can get a FLIR camera (they're $300-500), shoot thermal images of guests before and after sessions. The visual of a body lit up in red and orange after contrast therapy is arresting content that nobody else is producing. It also makes the physiological benefits tangible.

## 2. The Retention — Converting First-Timers Into Members and Regulars

**2.1 The 72-Hour Follow-Up.** Send a personalized text or email within 72 hours of someone's first session. Not a generic "thanks for visiting." Reference the actual experience: "How'd you sleep after that plunge on Saturday?" The specificity signals that you noticed them, which triggers reciprocity.

**2.2 Membership as Identity.** Don't sell memberships as discounts. Sell them as belonging. "You're not buying sessions. You're joining a sauna practice." Use language like "member" vs. "customer" in all communications. People who identify with a community churn at dramatically lower rates.

**2.3 The "Third Visit" Conversion Window.** Data across wellness businesses shows that if someone comes 3 times, they're 5-8x more likely to become a regular. Design a specific offer that triggers after visit 2: "You've done this twice. Your body is starting to adapt. Lock in your practice." Time it to hit during the adaptation narrative.

**2.4 Seasonal Membership Tiers.** Offer a winter-only membership at a lower price point for people who aren't ready to commit year-round. This captures the "I want to try being a regular" crowd without the annual commitment anxiety. Convert them to full membership in spring when they realize they don't want to stop.

**2.5 The Ritual Card.** Give every member a physical card (thick stock, branded, maybe wood-textured) that tracks their sessions with stamps or marks. It's deliberately analog in a digital world, which makes it feel like a ritual artifact rather than a loyalty punch card. After 10 sessions, they earn something meaningful.

**2.6 Member-Only Scheduling Windows.** Open booking for members 48 hours before the public on new time slots and special events. This isn't just a perk, it solves a real problem: popular slots fill fast, and members shouldn't have to compete with first-timers for access. The exclusivity also reinforces the value of membership.

**2.7 Monthly Member Rituals.** Host a monthly members-only session with an added element: a guided breathwork session, a tea ceremony after the plunge, or a local herbalist mixing custom post-sauna tonics. These become anchor events that members plan around and build social bonds during.

**2.8 Staff-Member Relationships.** Train Jordan, Mack, Hanna, and Jess to remember regulars' names, preferences (barrel vs. tent, how cold they like the plunge, whether they're talkers or quiet soakers). This is retention through human connection and it's almost impossible for competitors to replicate at scale.

**2.9 The "Bring Your Member Friend" Pass.** Give each member one free guest pass per month. This does two things: it makes members feel generous (reward psychology), and it creates a pipeline of warm leads who arrive pre-sold by someone they trust. Track which members bring the most converting guests.

**2.10 Progress Narratives.** Send members periodic updates on their practice: "You've done 14 sessions since January. Your average cold plunge time has gone from 1:30 to 3:15." People who see measurable progress are dramatically less likely to quit. If Periode doesn't track plunge duration, start logging it manually.

**2.11 Reactivation Before They Churn.** If a member hasn't booked in 3 weeks, trigger a personal text from their favorite guide: "Haven't seen you in a bit. Want me to hold a Tuesday evening slot for you?" The personal touch and the implied scarcity ("hold a slot") works better than any automated "we miss you" email.

**2.12 The Post-Session Glow Protocol.** Create a branded post-session routine that members associate exclusively with E&E: a specific tea blend, a cooling towel with eucalyptus, a 2-minute guided breathing cool-down. When the ritual becomes habitual, switching to a competitor means losing the ritual. That's a real switching cost.

**2.13 Seasonal Check-Ins.** At each season change, send members a short note about how to adjust their practice: "Summer sessions hit different. The cold plunge feels more refreshing, the sauna is about detox more than warmth. Here's what we recommend..." This positions E&E as a coach, not just a venue.

**2.14 Members-Only Merch Drops.** Release limited sauna towels, robes, or water bottles only to members. Not for sale to the public. The gear becomes a visible marker of belonging, and people who own branded gear have higher retention rates because the identity investment deepens.

**2.15 Couples Membership With Shared Perks.** Offer a couples tier where two people share a membership with combined perks (shared guest passes, joint session tracking). Couples who build a shared routine are stickier than individuals because canceling means disrupting a partner's habit too.

**2.16 Cancellation Friction Done Right.** When someone tries to cancel membership, don't guilt them. Offer a pause option (1-2 months, no charge). Most people who pause come back. Most people who cancel don't. The pause option should be the most prominent choice in the cancellation flow.

**2.17 The Birthday Session.** Automatically send members a free session (or upgrade) during their birthday month. Low cost, high emotional impact. "Happy birthday. This one's on us. Book whenever you want this month." Keep it short, no upsell attached.

## 3. The Suspense — Building Demand Through Scarcity and Anticipation

**3.1 The Waitlist as a Feature.** When sessions are full (and they should be, at least sometimes), don't just show "sold out." Show a waitlist with position number: "You're #4 on the waitlist for Saturday 10am." This creates perceived demand and makes the people who do get in feel like they won something.

**3.2 Seasonal Ritual Launches.** Announce seasonal experiences 3-4 weeks before they drop, with a countdown. "Winter Solstice Sauna. December 21. Details dropping November 30." The gap between announcement and details creates an information vacuum that people fill with anticipation and speculation.

**3.3 The Equinox and Solstice Sessions.** Build four anchor events per year around the astronomical calendar: spring equinox, summer solstice, fall equinox, winter solstice. Each gets a unique ritual (different aromatics, different guided elements, different food/drink pairings). These become the events people plan around months ahead.

**3.4 Limited-Run Collaborations.** Partner with a Portland maker for a 2-week-only experience: a local distiller provides a custom post-plunge spirit, or a ceramicist makes the tea cups for that run only. When it's gone, it's gone. The scarcity is real, which makes the FOMO authentic rather than manufactured.

**3.5 Weather-Triggered Sessions.** Announce surprise sessions based on weather: "First snow of the season. Opening 3 bonus evening slots tonight. Book in the next 2 hours." This creates Pavlovian excitement around weather events and positions E&E as spontaneous and alive, not just a booking calendar.

**3.6 The "Last Light" Summer Session.** During Portland's long summer evenings (sunset at 9pm+), offer a limited Thursday or Friday session that starts at golden hour and ends at dusk. Only available June through August. The temporal scarcity and the visual beauty make this inherently shareable and desirable.

**3.7 New Moon / Full Moon Sessions.** Monthly lunar sessions with adjusted lighting (candles only during new moon, open-air under moonlight during full moon). This taps into the growing astrology/lunar-curious crowd in Portland and creates a recurring anticipation cycle tied to a natural calendar.

**3.8 The Annual Cold Plunge Challenge.** Every January, run a 30-day cold plunge challenge. Participants commit to a certain number of plunges in 30 days. Track publicly on a leaderboard (with permission). The challenge creates urgency (limited spots), community (shared suffering), and content (daily updates).

**3.9 Secret Menu Experiences.** Offer unlisted experiences that only regulars and members know about. An extended 3-hour session, a sunrise session that starts at 6am, a "sauna and silence" evening. Don't put them on the website. Spread by word of mouth only. The secrecy IS the marketing.

**3.10 Pre-Season Hype Content.** Before each season, release a series of Instagram posts teasing what's coming: behind-the-scenes of new aromatics being tested, the team doing a trial run of a new ritual format, a close-up of a new barrel being seasoned. Build the narrative before the product drops.

**3.11 The "Sold Out Saturday" Effect.** Deliberately limit Saturday sessions to create consistent sell-outs. When a time slot sells out every week, it becomes a social signal. "Saturday morning is always sold out" is more powerful marketing than any ad you could run. Protect the scarcity even if you could add capacity.

**3.12 Seasonal Scent Drops.** Release a new signature aromatic blend each season (cedar and Douglas fir for winter, lavender and wild mint for summer). Announce it 2 weeks early. When the season ends, that scent is retired. People who loved the fall blend will book early next fall just to experience it again.

**3.13 The Collaboration Teaser Loop.** When planning a collab with a local brand, post a cryptic teaser: "Something's brewing with @localpartner. More soon." Then go silent for a week. Then reveal. The gap between tease and reveal is where word-of-mouth happens because people speculate and tag friends.

**3.14 Capacity Transparency.** Show real-time availability on the booking page: "3 of 6 spots remaining." This isn't manipulation if the numbers are real, and they should be. Seeing limited availability triggers loss aversion, which is one of the strongest decision-making biases humans have.

**3.15 The "Founding Members" Window.** During the ownership transition, offer a limited "founding member" rate for the first 50 memberships under new ownership. Frame it as a mutual commitment: "We're building this next chapter. These 50 memberships fund it. Once they're gone, standard pricing applies." Deadline plus scarcity plus narrative.

**3.16 Anniversary Events.** Treat E&E's business anniversary as a cultural event, not a sale. A special session format, a look-back video, an invitation to OG members to come back. The anniversary becomes a recurring hype cycle that deepens the brand's sense of permanence and history.

**3.17 Weather Countdowns.** In September, start a content series: "47 days until Portland's first freeze. Your sauna practice starts now." Tie the countdown to the coming cold season, when contrast therapy is most viscerally appealing. By the time November hits, you've been building intent for 8 weeks.

**3.18 The Invite-Only Beta Test.** When developing a new experience format, invite 10 members to test it before public launch. They get to shape the final product, and they become evangelists because they feel ownership over it. The "beta" framing makes even a rough experience feel exciting rather than unfinished.

## 4. The Reward — Reciprocity and Loyalty in the Wellness Community

**4.1 The Unexpected Upgrade.** Randomly upgrade a first-timer to the premium experience at no charge. Don't announce it in advance, don't make a policy out of it. Just have the guide say, "We've got some extra time, want to try the extended session?" Random rewards create stronger loyalty than predictable ones (variable ratio reinforcement).

**4.2 The Post-Plunge Surprise.** After someone completes their first cold plunge, hand them something small and physical: a branded sticker, a card with their plunge temperature, a mini sample of the sauna aromatic blend. The gift at the moment of accomplishment anchors the positive emotion to the E&E brand.

**4.3 Local Business Reciprocity Network.** Build a mutual referral network with 5-8 Portland businesses that share your customer: a juice bar, a yoga studio, a massage therapist, an acupuncturist, a running store. E&E members get perks at those businesses, and vice versa. The network effect makes membership more valuable without costing E&E anything.

**4.4 The "Thank You" Text From the Guide.** After a session, have the guide send a brief personal text: "Good session today. That 2-minute plunge was solid." Not automated, not templated. 15 seconds of effort that creates a real human connection. People don't ghost businesses where they have a personal relationship with the staff.

**4.5 Content Creator Micro-Rewards.** When someone tags E&E in a great Instagram post or story, DM them a free session or bring-a-friend pass. Don't create a formal "ambassador program" with applications and contracts. Just reward good content when you see it. The informality makes it feel like generosity, not a transaction.

**4.6 Milestone Celebrations.** Track member session counts and celebrate milestones: 10th session, 25th, 50th, 100th. At each milestone, the reward escalates: a sticker at 10, a branded towel at 25, a free month at 50, a custom robe at 100. The escalating rewards create a progress loop that's hard to abandon.

**4.7 The "Pay It Forward" Session.** Let members anonymously gift a session to a stranger. Put the gifted sessions on a board: "Someone left this session for you." The giver feels generous (reward), the receiver feels grateful (reciprocity), and everyone who sees the board feels warmth toward the brand.

**4.8 Community-Funded Scholarship.** Once a month, offer a free session to someone who can't afford it, funded by a small optional add-on at checkout ("Add $2 to send someone to the sauna"). Portland customers will opt in at high rates because it aligns with the city's values. Publish how many scholarship sessions have been funded.

**4.9 The Annual Member Gift.** At the end of each year, send every active member a physical gift. Not merch, not a discount code. Something thoughtful: a small bag of the year's signature aromatic blend, a handwritten note from the team, a custom playlist QR code. Cost per member: maybe $15. Retention value: enormous.

**4.10 Early Access to Everything.** Members hear about new experiences, schedule changes, and events before the public. Always. This isn't just a perk, it's a respect signal. "You're a member. You hear first." Consistency here matters more than the individual announcements.

**4.11 The Recovery Day Partnership.** Partner with Portland gyms, running clubs (Portland Running Company, November Project), or CrossFit boxes to offer post-workout recovery sessions at a group rate. The athletes get a genuine recovery tool, E&E gets a pipeline of high-frequency potential members who already understand physical discipline.

**4.12 Staff Empowerment to Reward.** Give each guide a small monthly budget ($50-100) to comp things at their discretion: an extra 15 minutes for a regular, a free session for someone having a rough day, a post-plunge drink on the house. When staff can be generous without asking permission, the generosity feels real.

**4.13 The Review Reciprocity Loop.** After a great session, ask for a Google review in person (not by email, not by text). Then, when reviews hit milestones (50, 100, 200), celebrate publicly and credit the community: "You got us to 100 five-star reviews. Free plunge upgrades for everyone this weekend." The celebration rewards past reviewers and motivates new ones.

**4.14 Seasonal Thank-You Events.** Once per quarter, host a free members-only social: food, drinks, no sauna required. Just hanging out. This signals that E&E values the relationship beyond the transaction. People who socialize at a business become emotionally invested in its survival and success.

**4.15 The "You Were Right" Follow-Up.** When a member refers someone who becomes a regular, tell them: "That friend you brought last month? They just signed up for a membership. You called it." People want to feel like their taste and judgment are validated. This reinforces the referral behavior by rewarding the ego, not just the wallet.

**4.16 Charitable Cold Plunge Events.** Run a quarterly fundraiser where participants get pledges per minute in the cold plunge, with proceeds going to a local Portland nonprofit (p:ear, Outside In, Friends of Trees). Participants get the reward of doing good, the challenge of the plunge, and the social proof of posting about it. E&E gets press coverage and community goodwill.

**4.17 The Loyalty Ladder Transparency.** Show members exactly where they are in the loyalty program and what's coming next: "You're at 18 sessions. At 25, you unlock the custom towel." Transparency about what's ahead creates goal-gradient motivation, where people accelerate effort as they approach a reward. Don't hide the ladder.

**4.18 Personalized Session Recommendations.** After 5+ sessions, have the guide or a follow-up message recommend a specific next experience based on what they've done: "You've been doing the classic contrast. Try the extended cold protocol next time, you're ready for it." The personalization signals attention, and the "you're ready" framing is a compliment that creates commitment.
