## What people SEE vs FEEL vs DO

| SEE (visual details they name) | FEEL (words they use) | DO (verbs) |
|---|---|---|
| the river, the Columbia, "open water" | relaxing, peaceful, calm, quiet | jump (off the roof, off the dock) |
| big windows, floor-to-ceiling windows | restorative, refreshing, invigorating | sweat, heat up, cool off, repeat |
| rain hitting the river | fun, "so fun", giggling | cold plunge, dip, shower off |
| boats, ducks, geese, beavers, planes | special, magical (a handful) | lounge on the roof deck, suntan |
| dock lights at night, sunset, sunrise | cozy, toasty | bring friends, celebrate a birthday |
| Mt. Hood in the distance (one review) | "reset", "grounded" (survey) | book a social session, buy out the sauna |
| cedar benches, wood, glowing sauna | proud (of braving the plunge) | come back, bring more friends |
| rooftop deck, wraparound deck, lily pad | safe/unsafe (friction, see below) | read a book on the top deck |
| tiled bathrooms, heated floors | | park, find the signs, self check-in |

Pattern: guests mostly describe things they can see and do. Feeling words are mild and plain ("relaxing", "refreshing"). The big claims ("detox", "boosts circulation", "immune system") come from one review and read like a brochure. Do not copy them.

## Words they never use (and we should drop or ration)

Near-zero or zero mentions across 123 reviews and 84 survey answers:

| Jargon | Guest usage |
|---|---|
| wellness | 4 reviews, never as the hook |
| detox, biohack, longevity, optimize, nervous system, ritual, journey, sanctuary, mindful, intentional wellness | none (one "detox" and one "ultimate wellness retreat" in a single-review outlier) |
| luxury / spa / retreat | 3 mentions combined |
| elevate / curated / immersive | none |
| "contrast therapy" | none. They say hot, cold, repeat |
| "Nordic" | none |

Mismatch with brand voice rules (Cedar voice, grounded, direct, say "guests", do not promise feelings):
- Guests are already on voice. They talk plain and physical: river, jump, hot, cold, windows, clean, easy.
- "Relaxing" is the biggest feeling word and it comes from them, not us. Safe to quote as theirs ("guests call it relaxing"), but do not write "you will feel relaxed" as our own promise.
- "Unique" and "amazing" are filler. They are real but weak. Prefer the concrete detail behind them (roof jump, rain on the river).
- Guests describe mostly what they did and saw. Lead captions with that, let feeling stay implied.
- Do not say wood-fired. A few guests say "wood sauna" or "cedar-lined", which is about the build. Cedar is fine to mention. No guest mentions fuel.

## Complaints and friction (brief)

Google:
- Crowded or loud social sessions: one guest on a rowdy group taking space, one on 8 people feeling tight.
- Safety and current: three reviews flag a strong, cold current, no live supervision, and one near-miss (a guest carried away, life ring used). Several guests praise the signs.
- Solo-guest comfort: one guest left after men lingered on the neighboring dock.
- No-show banya provider, no reply on the number (one 3-star).
- Limited outdoor seating, single changing area, price per hour (one 4-star).
- Lockers and chairs asked for early on (since built, per later reviews).

Survey:
- Price is the top barrier (7 mentions). Asks for punch cards, shorter or cheaper sessions, a better group-buyout deal than eight single spots.
- Sauna feels tight with 6 to 8 people; one asks for quiet hours or a person to set the tone.
- Drinking water and on-site signage (4 mentions), outdoor seating and roof chairs (5), privacy on the changing platform, bathroom availability once.
- Current and being swept under the sauna (recurring worry; guests appreciate the bumper and signs).
- A few say it is far, or they lack friends to go with.

Use in marketing: the same strengths guests praise (unstaffed, self-serve, river access) are the source of the complaints. Do not market it as supervised or quiet on every session. Do not promise a feeling. Do show the real details guests brag about.
