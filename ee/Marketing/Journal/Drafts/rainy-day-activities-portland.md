# Rainy Day in Portland, Oregon: What to Do When It's Pouring (Start With the Sauna)

The best rainy day activities in Portland, Oregon are indoor and slow: Powell's, the Portland Art Museum, OMSI, a long lunch. The most Portland one is a sauna on the Columbia River, where you sit in cedar heat and watch the rain hit the water. Below is a short list that works from October through May, with a plan for each kind of day.

## First, the rain

Portland rain is rarely a downpour. It's a steady gray drizzle that shows up in October and mostly stays until June. Locals don't carry umbrellas. They wear a shell, shrug, and keep going.

That's useful for planning. A drizzle day is an any-activity day with better layers. A real soaker, the kind where the gutters run, is a day for something indoors or something where the weather is the point.

## Indoor days: the reliable list

**Powell's City of Books.** A whole city block of books on West Burnside. You can lose three hours without noticing. The coffee shop on the ground floor is a decent place to wait out the worst of it.

**Portland Art Museum.** Downtown, in the South Park Blocks. Big enough for an afternoon, quiet enough to hear yourself think.

**OMSI.** The science museum on the east side of the river. Better with kids, but adults get through the submarine tour and the planetarium just fine.

**Lan Su Chinese Garden.** A walled garden in Old Town with covered walkways. Rain actually helps here. The tea house is open and the courtyards go glossy and green.

**Portland Japanese Garden.** Up in Washington Park. It's an outdoor garden, but rain suits it, and the moss looks its best wet. Bring a real jacket. Check current hours and ticketing before you go.

**Ground Kontrol.** A classic arcade and pinball bar on Burnside. Good for a group with nothing to do and a bag of quarters.

**Hollywood Theatre.** The nonprofit cinema on NE Sandy. Repertory screenings, old prints, a lobby that smells like popcorn.

## The outdoor-but-dry option

Walk the Eastbank Esplanade under the Burnside and Morrison Bridges. The bridges cover long stretches of the path, and the river is right there. Pair it with a coffee on the east side.

## Where the rain is the point: a sauna on the Columbia

Most rainy day lists leave this out, so here it is plainly. Ebb & Ember is a cedar sauna docked at Elevated Tides Marina, 173 NE Bridgeton Rd, in North Portland. It sits on the Columbia River, next to Hayden Island.

A session goes like this. You sit in the heat. You step out onto the deck. The rain is cold and the steam comes off your shoulders. If you want, you take the ladder down into the river, which is cold in a different way. You can skip that part. The cold plunge is optional, and plenty of guests just sit on the ladder or stay on the deck.

Rain doesn't cancel anything. It's most of what makes the heat feel like something. We never tell guests to push through bad weather, we just point out that it's part of the session. Sessions are self-guided, so you set your own pace, and the river has current, so go in with a buddy and use the life jackets on the dock.

Prices are $35 for one hour or $50 for two on weekdays, and $60 for a weekend 1 hour 45 minute session. Book on the [booking page](/book).

Other saunas in town are worth knowing too. Knot Springs, Bear Banya and Shiver & Sweat each do something different, and our [Portland sauna guide](/best-saunas-portland) compares the options without playing favorites.

## FAQ

### What are the best indoor things to do in Portland, Oregon when it rains?
Powell's City of Books, the Portland Art Museum, OMSI, the Hollywood Theatre and Lan Su Chinese Garden all work well in the rain. For something different, a sauna session on the Columbia River turns the rain into part of the experience.

### Does Ebb & Ember stay open when it rains?

No. The cold plunge is optional. You can sit in the sauna, step out onto the deck, or sit on the ladder. Sessions are self-guided, so you decide how far to go.
