# What to Wear to a Sauna (and What to Bring) at Ebb & Ember

**Short answer:** wear a swimsuit. Bring a towel, water, and something warm and dry for after. Skip jewelry, cotton layers, and anything you'd hate to get wet. That covers 90 percent of questions.

The rest of this post is the longer version, with a Portland spin. We're a cedar sauna docked on the Columbia River, so you also need to think about a dock, a ladder, and October weather.

## The swimsuit question

At Ebb & Ember you wear swimwear [confirm: exact clothing policy wording for the site]. Nobody is expected to be bare in a shared room, and people are often relieved to hear it. Any swimsuit that stays put works.

A few tips:

- **Choose something you're comfortable sitting in.** You'll sit still on a wood bench for a while. Skip the suit that rides up or digs in.
- **Go snug rather than loose.** If you plan to get in the river, you don't want a suit that floats away from you at the ladder.
- **Think about fabric.** Synthetic swimwear is the easy choice. Cotton shorts get heavy and stay wet.
- **Dark colors can show water and sweat less.** Light ones are fine too. It's your call.

Bring a second dry layer if you can. Putting dry underwear back on after a session is a small pleasure you'll thank yourself for.

## What to bring

Here's a packing list that fits in one bag:

- Towel [confirm: provided on site, or guests bring their own?]
- A second towel or a robe-style wrap, if you run cold after
- Water bottle, refilled before you leave
- Warm, dry clothes: sweats, wool socks, a hat
- Flip-flops or slip-on shoes for the dock
- A plastic bag for your wet suit
- Hair tie, if you have long hair
- A watch or phone-free way to track time, if that helps (see below)

On Portland days with rain, bring a warm top and a hat. Wet hair and a cold drive home is a miserable combination. A beanie is the cheapest fix we know.

## What to leave at home

- **Jewelry.** Metal gets hot against skin in a cedar room. Rings and chains can slip off at the river ladder too.
- **Phones, if you can manage it.** Heat and screens are a bad pair, and the quiet is half the point. If you need yours with you, keep it in your bag.
- **Alcohol.** None on the dock.
- **Fancy fabrics and wool you love.** Steam and river water get on everything.
- **Contact lenses, for some people.** Many wear glasses or take lenses out. Your call.

## Cold plunge gear

The river plunge is optional. If you're thinking about it, a few extras are useful:

- **Water shoes or grippy sandals.** The ladder and the deck can be slippery.
- **Neoprene gloves or booties.** Some people like them. Plenty go without.
- **A beanie for between rounds.** Warm head, easier cold.

Never go in alone, and stay close to your buddy. Check conditions on the day. If you'd rather start gentler, use the Downpour pull-cord cold shower instead. More on that order in [how to cold plunge](/journal/how-to-cold-plunge).

## After the session

Keep your after-session clothes simple and loose. You'll want to sit on the lounge deck for a bit and let your body settle. Drink water. Eat something if you're hungry. Don't plan a meeting in the 30 minutes after your session.

## A note on hair and skin

Tie long hair up so it stays off your neck. Skip heavy products before you come, since steam and sweat don't mix well with them. If you wear makeup, expect it to run, and that's fine. Most guests arrive plain-faced and leave the same way, with a towel over their shoulders.

## What people get wrong

1. **Too many clothes.** Don't wear a robe into the cedar room.
2. **Too few towels.** One towel for two people doesn't work.
3. **Wearing a big watch or beaded bracelet.** They get hot.
4. **Forgetting a bag for wet things.** Your car seat will thank you.

## FAQ

**Can I wear a one-piece or board shorts?**
Yes. Any swimwear you're comfortable in works. Snug is better if you plan to use the river ladder.

**Is it clothing optional?**
No. Wear a swimsuit. [Confirm: final wording of the clothing policy.]

**Do I need to bring a towel?**
Bring one unless you've been told otherwise. [Confirm: are towels provided?]

**Can I wear my glasses?**
Glasses can fog and get hot. Many guests leave them on a bench in their bag or wear them lightly. It's your preference.

**What should I wear if it's cold or rainy?**
A warm, dry layer for after. Weather is part of the experience, and the dock gets damp. Bring a hat and wool socks.
