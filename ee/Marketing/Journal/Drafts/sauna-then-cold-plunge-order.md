# Sauna, Then Cold Plunge, or the Other Way Around? How We Do a Round

**Short answer:** heat first, then cold, then rest. Sauna, cool off, sit down. That's one round. The cold comes after the heat, not before.

That's how most people do it, and it's how our sessions work at Ebb & Ember. The rest of this post explains the order and what happens at each step on our dock on the Columbia River. It's practical, not medical. If you have a condition or you're pregnant, talk to your doctor first.

## The basic order

1. **Heat.** Sit in the cedar room until you're properly warm.
2. **Cold.** Cool off with the Downpour, the river, or just air.
3. **Rest.** Sit on the lounge deck. Drink water.
4. **Repeat** if you want to.

Heat, cold, rest. That's a round.

## Why heat first

It comes down to comfort. If you start cold, you're cold. If you start in the cedar room, you walk to the water already warm, and the cold feels like a contrast rather than a chore. People also make better choices when they're warm. You're less likely to flinch at the ladder and more likely to ease in slowly.

Could you plunge first and sauna after? Sure, nobody's stopping you. Some folks like cold at the start. But most find the heat-first order natural, and it's the one we'd suggest for a first visit.

## What a round looks like on our dock

**Step 1: the cedar room.** Pick a bench. Sit until you feel warm and a little sweaty. Five to fifteen minutes is common for a first round, but there's no rule. Leave sooner if you feel dizzy or off. Read more in [how long to stay in a sauna](/journal/how-long-in-a-sauna).

**Step 2: step out.** Walk out onto the dock. Let the air hit you for a minute. In Portland, this might mean rain on your shoulders, which many people end up loving.

**Step 3: pick your cold.**

- **The Downpour.** A pull-cord cold shower on the deck. It's the gentlest way in, and a good choice if you've never done this.
- **The river.** A ladder runs off the dock into the Columbia. The water is cold and it moves. Hold the ladder, go in slowly, and never go alone. Bring a buddy.
- **Neither.** Sit on the lounge deck and let the air cool you. This is a fine option.

**Step 4: rest.** Sit on the lounge deck for a few minutes. Drink water. Don't rush back in. The pause is part of the round.

## How long in the cold?

Short. Seconds to a minute or two is typical for the river. There's no target and no score. Get out when you want to, not when you think you should. If your breathing gets fast or your hands stop working well, get out. We'd rather you leave early than push it.

For a step-by-step on the cold part itself, see [how to cold plunge](/journal/how-to-cold-plunge).

## How many rounds?

Two or three is a lot of people's sweet spot. Our weekday sessions run 1 or 2 hours, and weekend sessions run 1 hour 45 minutes. In that time you can do a few rounds with proper rests between. Rushing through six rounds in a 1-hour slot isn't the point.

## Between rounds

The gap between rounds is where first-timers go wrong. They finish the cold, feel pumped, and bolt back into the heat. Resist that. Sit down, drink water, and let your breathing settle. Wrap a towel around your shoulders if the air is chilly. When you feel ready again, not before, head back in. Your pace is the only one that counts.

## Safety, in plain words

- **Never plunge alone.** Go in with a buddy.
- **Ease in.** Use the ladder.
- **Hydrate.** Drink water before, between, and after.
- **Skip alcohol.** None on the dock, and it doesn't mix with heat or cold water.
- **Leave if you feel dizzy.** Step out, sit down, drink water.
- **Check conditions.** The river has current. When in doubt, don't go in.
- **Sessions are self-guided.** You and your group look after each other.

## What if I want to skip the cold?

You can. The plunge is optional. A lot of guests do heat, rest, repeat, and never get near the water. Opting out isn't missing out.

## FAQ

**Should I plunge before the sauna?**
Most people go sauna first, cold second. You can reverse it if you like, but we suggest heat-first for beginners.

**How long should I stay in the cold water?**
Short. Seconds to a couple of minutes is common. Get out when you want to. There's no target.

**Do I have to use the river?**
No. The Downpour cold shower or just cooling off on the deck both count. Cold plunge is optional.

**Can I go in the river alone?**
No. Never plunge alone. Go with a buddy and stay close.

**How many rounds should I do?**
Two or three is common. Take a real rest between rounds. Stop whenever you've had enough.
