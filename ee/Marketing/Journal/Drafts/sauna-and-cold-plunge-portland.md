# Sauna and Cold Plunge in Portland, Oregon: How a Round Works on the Columbia

At Ebb & Ember in Portland, Oregon, a round is simple: you sit in a cedar sauna, step out onto the dock, and, if you want to, go down a ladder into the Columbia River. Then you rest and do it again. The cold plunge is optional, sessions are self-guided, and the sauna is docked at Elevated Tides Marina in North Portland. This is how it works, start to finish.

## The setup

Ebb & Ember is a cedar sauna on a dock at 173 NE Bridgeton Rd, Portland, OR 97211, next to Hayden Island. It doesn't move. You walk down the dock from the marina and the sauna is right there.

The cold plunge isn't a tub. It's the river. A ladder goes from the deck straight into the water.

## What a round looks like

You can do as many or as few rounds as you like in a session. This is the usual pattern.

**1. Heat.** Sit in the sauna for as long as is comfortable. Everyone's different, and there's no target. If it's too much, you step out. Nobody counts.

**2. Cool.** Walk out onto the deck. The air, the wind and the rain all do work here. In winter, even standing outside feels cold.

**3. The river, if you want it.** Take the ladder down into the Columbia. Others sit on the ladder with their feet in. Some skip it.

**4. Rest.** Sit, drink water, talk. Then go back in when you're ready.

## The river part, plainly

The Columbia is a working river with real current, and it's deep at the dock. That's the point, and it's also why we're direct about it.

- **Go in with a buddy.** Don't plunge alone.
- **Life jackets are on the dock.** Use one if you want it.
- **Ladder in, ladder out.** Don't jump, and don't swim away from the dock.
- **If conditions look wrong, don't go in.** High wind, fast current, debris or boat wakes are all reasons to skip it. When in doubt, don't go out.
- **Drink water.** Before and after.

Sessions are self-guided. Nobody's watching the water for you, so the call is yours. If you want the full picture, our [cold plunge guide](/cold-plunge-portland) goes through it, and [how to cold plunge for the first time](/journal/how-to-cold-plunge) is a good primer for beginners.

- **Ebb & Ember** is the one on the Columbia, where the cold part is the river rather than a tub.

## What to bring

- A swimsuit
- Two towels (one for the dock, one for after)
- Shoes you can slip on and off
- A water bottle
- A warm hat or hood for winter
- A dry change of clothes

No alcohol on the dock.

## FAQ

### Where can I do a sauna and cold plunge in Portland, Oregon?
Several places in Portland offer sauna with cold plunge, including Ebb & Ember, where the cold plunge is the Columbia River, reached by a ladder off the dock. Our [Portland sauna guide](/best-saunas-portland) compares the options.

### Do I have to get in the river?
No. The cold plunge is optional. You can use the sauna, step onto the deck, or sit on the ladder.

### How long should I stay in the sauna before the cold plunge?
There's no set time. Stay as long as it's comfortable and step out when you're ready. Sessions are self-guided, so you set the pace.

### What should I know about the river before I get in?
Conditions change, and the river has current. We ask guests to use a buddy system, wear a life jacket if they want one, and skip the water if conditions look off. Check the Columbia Riverkeeper Swim Guide for current information.
