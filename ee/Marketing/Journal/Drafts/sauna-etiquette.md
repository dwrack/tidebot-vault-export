# Sauna Etiquette: The Unwritten Rules of a Social Sauna

**The short version:** be quiet, be quick with the door, share the bench, and look out for the people next to you. Everything else is detail.

Etiquette in a social sauna isn't strict. It's just a set of small courtesies that make a hot, tight room work for strangers. Here's how it plays out at Ebb & Ember, where up to 8 guests share a cedar room on a floating dock on the Columbia River.

## Coming in and out

**Be fast with the door.** Heat escapes in seconds. Step in, close it, then settle. The same on the way out. If you're the last one in, shut it gently.

**Say hi, then be quiet.** A nod or a "hey" is plenty. People come here to slow down. Long phone-style conversations aren't the vibe.

**Rinse or towel off first if you're dripping.** Water pooling on the bench is annoying for the next person. Wipe down after yourself if you get sweat on the cedar.

## Where to sit

Benches aren't claimed seats. Top benches run hotter and lower ones run milder. A few guidelines:

- **Don't spread out.** Pull your legs up or leave space. Eight people need room.
- **Slide over for newcomers.** If someone walks in and there's a gap, make it bigger.
- **Sit on your towel.** It's better for you and for the wood.
- **Lie down only if there's room.** In a full room, sit.

If the heat isn't working for you, move down a bench. You don't owe anyone a reason.

## Noise and talk

Conversation is welcome, just keep it low. Some rooms chat and some sit in silence. Take your cue from what's happening when you walk in. If everyone's quiet, be quiet. If people are talking, join in or don't.

Skip the phone calls, the speakerphone, and the loud stories. Yes, even the good ones.

## Phones and photos

Keep your phone in your bag. Heat is hard on devices, and nobody wants to end up in someone else's photo. Don't photograph other guests, ever. If you want a picture of the river or the deck, take it when it's empty or when your own group is in frame.

## Respecting the water and the dock

The river plunge is optional, and nobody should pressure anyone into it. A few rules for the ladder and the dock:

- **Never plunge alone.** Go in with a buddy and stay close.
- **Ease in.** Hold the ladder, take your time.
- **Wait your turn.** If someone's on the ladder, give them room.
- **Check conditions that day.** The Columbia moves. When in doubt, don't go in.
- **Walk, don't run.** Docks are slippery.

Sessions are self-guided, so you and your group are responsible for yourselves. Watch each other. If a friend looks pale, dizzy, or unsteady, help them out and sit them down with water.

## Looking after yourself

Leave the sauna if you feel dizzy, queasy, or unwell. Nobody will think less of you. Drink water between rounds, and skip alcohol before and during your session. If you have a medical condition or you're pregnant, check with your doctor first.

## Smells and products

Go easy on perfume, cologne, and heavy lotions. Heat amplifies everything. Showering before you come is a courtesy, not a rule. Strong scents get worse in a warm, closed room.

## Lounge deck manners

The lounge deck is for resting between rounds. A few small things:

- Keep voices low.
- Leave seats open for people who just came out of the heat.
- Take your trash with you.
- No alcohol on the dock.

## Groups and bookings

If you've booked with friends, remember the room is shared with others. Keep your group's energy in check, and don't take up half a bench. If you'd like the place to yourselves, a private booking is a better fit.

## If something goes wrong

If someone breaks a rule, a quiet word usually works. "Hey, could we keep it down?" is fine. If it doesn't, take a break outside and cool off. If you feel unsafe, get help right away.

## FAQ

**Is it rude to leave early?**
No. Leave whenever you need to. Step out quietly and close the door behind you.

**Can I bring my phone into the sauna?**
Better not. Heat is rough on devices, and photos of other guests are off limits.

**Can I talk in a social sauna?**
Yes, softly. Read the room. Some sessions are chatty, some are silent.

**Do I have to shower first?**
It's a courtesy. [Confirm: shower or rinse facilities on the dock.] Showering at home before you come is considerate.

**What if I don't want to talk to anyone?**
That's fine. A nod is enough. People come to be quiet as much as to be social.
