# Your First Time at a Social Sauna in Portland: What Happens, Start to Finish

**Short version:** you show up in a swimsuit, you sit in a cedar room until you're hot, you cool off however you like, and you repeat that at your own pace until your session ends. That's a first time at Ebb & Ember. Nobody quizzes you. Nobody hands you a script.

This guide walks through the whole thing in order, so the unknowns turn into a plan. It works anywhere, but we've grounded it in how it goes at our dock on the Columbia River, since that's the one we know.

## Before you book

Pick a session length. On weekdays you can book 1 or 2 hours. On weekends sessions run 1 hour 45 minutes. If you've never done this, the shorter weekday option is a perfectly good first try. If you like to settle in, take the longer one.

Pick a session type. A Social Sauna is shared with other guests, up to 8 people in the room. You'll be sitting near strangers, which sounds worse than it is. Most people nod, say hi, and then go quiet. If you'd rather have the place to yourselves, look at a private booking instead.

If you have a medical condition, or you're pregnant, check with your doctor before you book. We're not in a position to tell you what's right for your body, and heat plus cold water is a real thing to ask about.

## What to bring

Keep it simple. A swimsuit you can wear for a while, a towel [confirm: are towels provided or should guests bring their own?], water to drink, and something warm and dry for after. Shoes you can slip on and off help on a dock. We wrote a longer rundown in [what to wear to a sauna](/journal/what-to-wear-to-a-sauna).

Leave the alcohol at home. There's none on the dock, and heat and drinking don't mix well anyway.

## Arriving

We're docked at Elevated Tides Marina in North Portland. It stays tied up at the dock the whole time. Plan to arrive a little early [confirm: recommended arrival window] so you aren't rushing down the dock with your stuff.

Sessions are self-guided. That means there's no instructor leading you through a routine. You get the sauna, the river, the deck, and a few basic ground rules. You decide how it goes. If that sounds like a lot of freedom, it isn't. You'll find the rhythm in about ten minutes.

## The first round: heat

Walk into the cedar room and find a seat on a bench. Higher benches are hotter, lower ones are milder. Start low. There's no prize for sitting up top.

Sit until you feel properly warm and a little sweaty, then keep going a bit if it feels fine. For a first round, five to fifteen minutes is common, but your body sets the clock, not us. If you feel dizzy, lightheaded, or just plain wrong, leave. Step outside and sit down. Getting out early is always fine.

We go into this more in [how long to stay in a sauna](/journal/how-long-in-a-sauna).

## Cooling off: three ways to do it

You've got choices, and none of them is the wrong one.

- **The Downpour.** A pull-cord cold shower. You tug the cord, cold water comes down. It's the easiest step into cold, and a good place to start.
- **The river.** A ladder runs off the dock into the Columbia. The water is cold and it moves. Go in slowly, hold the ladder, and never go in alone. Bring a buddy and keep them in sight. We talk through the order of heat and cold in [sauna, then cold plunge](/journal/sauna-then-cold-plunge-order).
- **Nothing at all.** Sit on the lounge deck, breathe, and let the air do it. This is a completely respectable way to cool down. The plunge is optional, always.

If you're thinking about the river, check conditions that day and use your own judgment. The Columbia has current. When in doubt, don't go in.

## Repeat, or don't

Most people do a few rounds: heat, cool, rest, then heat again. Between rounds, drink water and sit on the lounge deck for a while. The rest part matters as much as the hot part. People who skip it tend to get restless and rush.

You can do two rounds or six. You can sit in the sauna the whole time and never touch the water. Pace yourself and stop when you've had enough.

## The social part

In a Social Sauna you're sharing a small room. A few things make it easy: say hi when you walk in, keep your voice low, and don't hog the door. We cover the rest in [sauna etiquette](/journal/sauna-etiquette). Some groups chat the whole time, others sit in near silence. Both are normal. Follow the room.

## When it's over

Dry off, get dressed, and drink more water before you leave. Give yourself a few minutes before driving. Plan your evening so you're not sprinting to the next thing. Many people say the first-time surprise is how quickly the time goes.

## First-time FAQ

**Do I have to get in the river?**
No. The cold plunge is optional. You can use the Downpour cold shower, sit on the lounge deck, or skip the cold entirely.

**Will I be with strangers?**
In a Social Sauna, yes, with up to 8 guests in the room. Most people keep to themselves and are friendly when spoken to.

**Can I go in the river by myself?**
We'd say no. Never plunge alone. Go with a buddy, ease in on the ladder, and stay within reach of each other.

**What if I feel dizzy or unwell?**
Leave the sauna, sit down, and drink water. Sessions are self-guided, so look after yourself and your group. If it doesn't pass, get help. Always check with your doctor ahead of time if you have a medical condition or are pregnant.

**Is it okay to bring a drink?**
Bring water. There's no alcohol on the dock.
