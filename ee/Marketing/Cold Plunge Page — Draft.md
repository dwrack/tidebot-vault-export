# Cold Plunge in Portland on the Columbia River

Most cold plunges in Portland happen in a tub. A box of water chilled to exactly 39 degrees, sitting indoors under the lights. Ours happens in the Columbia River: forty feet deep, moving, fed by Cascade snowmelt. You climb down a ladder off the dock, the cold takes your breath, and a minute later you're back in 190-degree cedar heat. That contrast, hot to cold and back again, is the whole practice.

## Two ways to get cold

You don't have to get in the river to get the cold. There are two ways to do it here, and both are optional. Always.

### The river plunge
Two ladders lead off the dock straight into the Columbia. The water is real and so is the current, so we keep it safe: life jackets on hand, throw rings on deck, and a buddy system any time someone's in the water. Some guests do three rounds. Some sit on the ladder to their knees. Some skip it and watch from the deck. We never treat opting out as missing out.

### The Portland Downpour
For the cold without the swim, there's the Portland Downpour, our bucket plunge tower. Pull the chain and a full dump of cold water comes down over your head in one shot. It's quick, it's bracing, and it's the most photogenic two seconds of the session. Show us your COOOLDFACE.

## How cold is the Columbia, really?

It changes with the season, because it's snowmelt, not a thermostat.

- **Winter:** around 42°F. Genuinely intense.
- **Spring:** low 50s as the snow runs off.
- **Summer:** 60 to 65°F. Cool, not shocking.
- **Fall:** dropping back down through the 50s.

That swing is the point. The river feels alive because it is. You can plunge year-round, and each season is a different experience.

## Is it safe to plunge in the Columbia?

Yes, with respect for the water. We're docked over 40-plus feet of river with real current, and we treat that as a safety reality, not a selling point. Two ladders, throw rings on the deck, life jackets available, and a buddy system whenever you're in. Our one rule is simple: when in doubt, don't go out. The Downpour and a cold shower are always there if the river isn't your day.

## Cold plunge and sauna, the full loop

The plunge isn't a standalone here. It's the cold half of a Nordic contrast cycle: heat in the [cedar sauna](/), cold in the river or the Downpour, then recovery on the deck with the water in front of you. Repeat as many times as your session allows. Sessions run 1 hour 45 minutes, seven days a week, because slowing down takes longer than people think.

## FAQ (visible block on page)

**How cold is the cold plunge at Ebb & Ember?**
It's the Columbia River, so it shifts with the season. Around 42°F in winter, low 50s in spring, 60 to 65°F in summer, and back down through fall. It's Cascade snowmelt, not a chilled tub, so the temperature is whatever the river decides that day.

**Do I have to get in the river?**
Never. The cold plunge is always optional. You can take the river off the ladder, use the Portland Downpour bucket tower, rinse in a cold shower, or skip the cold entirely and enjoy the sauna and the deck. We don't treat opting out as missing out.

**Is it safe to cold plunge in the Columbia River?**
Yes, with respect for the water. We're over 40 feet deep with real current, so we keep two ladders, throw rings, and life jackets on hand and run a buddy system any time someone's in the water. Our rule is simple: when in doubt, don't go out.

**What is the Portland Downpour?**
It's our bucket plunge tower. Pull the chain and a full dump of cold water comes down over your head at once. It's the fast way to get cold without getting in the river, and it's open every session.

**Can you cold plunge year-round in Portland?**
Yes. We run sessions seven days a week all year. Winter plunges are intense at around 42°F, and summer plunges are a cool 60 to 65°F. Each season feels different.

**Do I need to book the sauna to use the cold plunge?**
The cold plunge is part of the full sauna session, not a separate ticket. You book a social or private session, and the cedar sauna, the river plunge, the Portland Downpour, and the deck are all yours for the full 1 hour 45 minutes.
