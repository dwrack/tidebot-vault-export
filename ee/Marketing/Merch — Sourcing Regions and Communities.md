# Merch — Sourcing Regions and Communities

*Answering: should we source towels direct from Turkey, and what lesser-known regions and communities should the shelf support? Researched September 27, 2026 from web search only; open each link before ordering. Companion to the vendor tracker, product link list, and brand discovery notes in this folder.*

---

## Short Answer

1. **Turkey for towels: yes for the story, no for the savings.** Denizli, Buldan, and Babadağ make the best peshtemal and waffle towels in the world. But as of July 2026 a Turkish towel carries about 21% duty stacked, and the $800 duty-free parcel threshold is gone, so a 20-towel order pays duty and needs a customs entry like a container would. Indigo Traders in Portland already sources from family looms and has absorbed that overhead. Use them for the launch run. Do one small direct order from a named-weaver supplier (Qartaj in Tunisia at 20 foutas, or Peshmod or Hiera's Loom in Denizli) for the shelf-card story, shipped express.
2. **Regions that fit a sauna shelf better than most of the market:** Tunisia and Morocco for foutas and hammam goods (kessa glove, beldi soap, rhassoul clay, argan from women's co-ops); Portugal for Guimarães towels, Burel wool, cork, and the jersey behind our blanks; Japan for Imabari towels and binchotan through one $250 account at Morihata in Philadelphia; Nepal and Kyrgyzstan for felt hats through Ganesh Himal in Spokane; Peru for alpaca; Ghana and Togo for shea, with Alaffia in Olympia.
3. **At home, where a small order matters most:** Columbia River tribal fishers (Brigham Fish Market in Cascade Locks, CRITFC direct sales), Native-owned makers (Eighth Generation, Ginew in Portland), Wallowa County's 65-maker collective, Appalachian glass and soap, Alaska Native co-ops, and social enterprises like Thistle Farms.

### Import facts, September 2026

- Cotton towels are HTS 6302.60, base duty 9.1%.
- Turkey got a 12.5% Section 301 tariff on July 24, 2026, stacked on the base. Landed duty on a Turkish towel is roughly 21 to 22% before freight and brokerage.
- De minimis is gone. The $800 duty-free threshold ended for all countries August 29, 2025, and was suspended for all non-postal modes June 24, 2026. Every parcel, even 20 towels, needs a customs entry with duty paid.
- At 20 to 50 units, express courier (DHL, UPS, FedEx) is the practical route because they handle the entry. Freight only pays off at volume; towels are bulky relative to value.
- Price points seen: $10 a towel at 1 to 49, $5 at 50 to 499, ~$7 at 500+. Expect $8 to $14 landed before duty at our size.
- Certifications: ask for OEKO-TEX 100 and GOTS. SEDEX is a factory audit, not something a family loom will have. For a small weaver, ask for photos of the loom and weaver, a cooperative letter, or a curator's vetting. No third-party Fairtrade-certified peshtemal producer surfaced; "fair trade" claims from Qartaj and Fouta Tunisia are self-described.

### Verdict

Direct from Turkey is not a cost win at 20 to 50 towels once the 21% duty and the customs entry are counted. Indigo Traders in Portland has absorbed that overhead across volume and lets us see the weave in person. The case for going direct is story and traceability: a named cooperative or family loom on the shelf card. Middle path: one small direct order from a supplier with a published low MOQ and a named-weaver story (Qartaj at 20 foutas, Peshmod or Hiera's Loom for peshtemal), shipped express, and Indigo for restocks. Red flags: "handwoven" language from 100K-piece-a-month factories, Denizli showrooms that subcontract to unnamed factories, fair-trade claims with no named cooperative, and any supplier unwilling to show who wove it.
