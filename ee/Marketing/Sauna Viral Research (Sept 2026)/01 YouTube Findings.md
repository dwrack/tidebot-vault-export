# YouTube: what's going viral in sauna (Sept 2026)

Research only. Nothing was posted, commented, or sent. Data pulled 2026-09-25 with yt-dlp (no API). Raw data: `raw/youtube_raw.json`.

## Bottom line

- **Sauna on YouTube gets its biggest views from cold, not heat.** Ice baths, polar plunges and "last to leave" challenges take most of the top slots. Pure sauna content peaks lower unless it's a celebrity, a science experiment, or a build.
- **The one English long-form sauna hit of the last 12 months is a science experiment:** Bryan Johnson, "Is Sauna ACTUALLY Good For You? (90-Day Experiment)", 2.81M views on 2.23M subs (Nov 2025).
- **Shorts that pop are 9 to 18 seconds long**, show one visual moment (ice breaking, a plunge, a reaction), and carry a teaser line or a question. Every recent top-25 Short with a known length ran 47 seconds or less, and most ran under 20.
- **Floating/river sauna is wide open on YouTube.** The best "floating sauna" video uploaded in the last year has 264K views (a Japanese whale-sauna venue with a floating cold plunge). The #floatingsauna Shorts feed tops out around 67K views. Nobody owns the term.
- **Portland/PNW sauna video barely exists.** The best recent "Portland sauna" result has 1,265 views. The Columbia River floating sauna feature on KOIN 6 ("Kohr Explores", Jan 2026) has 1,013 views. The Willamette Sauna Festival news clip has 2,466.
- **Sauna businesses almost never go viral on their own channels.** The business channels that show big numbers (Sun Home, Mainely Tubs, Fire Cold Plunge, BUX) have like counts near zero, which points to paid-ad views (see Method notes). The businesses that win organically do it through creators (Othership via Shervin Shares) or a media series (Sauna Channel).

## Method (and limits)

- 34 search queries (all the ones in the brief plus lake sauna, river sauna, sauna boat, sauna etiquette, public sauna, sauna challenge, cold plunge shorts), each run twice: sorted by view count with the "this year" filter, and sorted by view count all-time, 60 results each. Plus 5 hashtag Shorts feeds (#sauna, #coldplunge, #saunaculture, #icebath, #floatingsauna). 3,021 unique videos, 2,192 of them on-topic by title keyword.
- Full metadata (subs, likes, comments, upload date, tags) fetched for 236 candidates. "Recent" means uploaded 2025-03-25 or later (last 18 months). 125 recent videos made it through, 106 after removing probable paid ads.
- **Outlier score = views / channel subscribers.** Above 1.0 means the video beat the channel's own audience.
- **Paid-ad flag:** over 100K views with likes under 0.1% of views, or a brand channel under 1,000 subs with a 75-second-or-shorter clip over 100K views. These are pulled out of the rankings and listed separately. This is an inference from the numbers. YouTube doesn't label ad views.
- **Shorts:** only 23 recent Shorts could be verified (vertical and 180 seconds or less, with a date). Hashtag feeds skew to older viral Shorts (2023 and 2024), and I stopped fetching to stay under the request budget. So the Shorts table has 23 rows, not 25.
- Search results are global. A big share of the top views are Hindi, Korean, Turkish, German and Russian. There's a separate English-only table below.
- Formats are assigned by title keywords, so a few are rough.

## 3. Title and hook patterns

Across 2,002 on-topic Latin-script titles (all dates), comparing 299 videos with 500K+ views against 556 with 10K to 100K views:

| Pattern | Share of 500K+ titles | Share of 10K-100K titles |
|:-|:-|:-|
| ALL-CAPS word (4+ letters) | 40% | 22% |
| "challenge" / "last to leave" | 22% | 2% |
| Money or prize ($, €, "wins") | 8% | 2% |
| Temperature / extreme ("-40°", "freezing", "extreme") | 6% | 3% |
| Duration ("24 hours", "90 days") | 4% | 2% |
| Any number | 32% | 30% |
| Question mark | 6% | 10% |
| Place name (Finland, Korea, Seattle...) | 9% | 12% |
| "How to" | 2% | 3% |
| "First time" | 1% | 3% |

What stands out:
- **Stakes beat information.** Challenge, money, extreme temperature and time-boxed words show up 2 to 11 times more often in the big videos. "How to", "first time" and place names show up more in the small ones.
- **Caps on one word** ("Is Sauna ACTUALLY Good For You?", "LAST TO LEAVE THE FROZEN ICE TUB WINS").
- **Shorts titles are teasers, not descriptions:** "Would you do this for $1 mil?", "Guess our age", "The ending", "Too much ice". Troy Lipis (743K subs) runs one template (couple in an ice bath, "Guess our age/height/the temperature") and it hit 1.4M to 47.6M views each time.
- **Parenthetical proof:** "(90-Day Experiment)", "(-71°C, -96°F)", "**SUPER COLD**".

Thumbnail text can't be pulled with yt-dlp. Judging from titles only, the likely thumbnail words are a single caps word, a temperature, a dollar amount, or a day count.

Where the openings are (my read of the numbers above):
1. **"Floating sauna" as a search term.** Low supply, and nobody owns it in English. The top English floating-sauna videos are years old (Cottage Life "How to build a FLOATING Sauna", 813K, 2015). A clear "What it's like to sauna on the Columbia River" video, or a "Floating sauna: how it works" explainer, has almost no competition.
2. **Contrast therapy, explained by doing it.** 0 videos with 100K+ this year, and 17 of 60 results are even on-topic (the rest are CT scans and baby-sensory videos). The science format wins (see Bryan Johnson), and "sauna then river plunge" is contrast therapy with a better backdrop than any studio.
3. **Social sauna / sauna club (the non-adult kind).** The search is swamped by gay-sauna and adult-club content. A "what a social sauna actually is" video fills a real gap. Sauna Channel's top episode ("Berlin's Secret Neighbourhood Sauna", 2.74M) shows the appetite for community sauna stories.
4. **Etiquette / first-timer.** Best recent video has 81,612 views. Most etiquette views sit on old German TV clips and 2018 to 2019 videos. "What to expect at your first floating sauna" is uncontested.
5. **Aufguss / ritual in English.** Aufguss content is almost all German competition footage. English aufguss with a story gets views: Sauna Channel's Kiezsauna aufguss has 1.36M.
6. **Portland / PNW.** Basically empty. Local news clips get 1K to 2.5K views, so the gap isn't demand-proven. It's a cheap one to own for local search and Maps-adjacent discovery, not a viral play.

## What this means for Ebb & Ember (for the channel plan)

Short version, grounded in the tables above:
- **Shorts:** one visual moment, 10 to 20 seconds, teaser caption. The river plunge after the sauna is Ebb & Ember's version of the ice-bath clip that dominates Shorts. Rain, steam off the river, the jump, the face. Test "guess the water temperature" style captions (the Troy Lipis template).
- **Long-form pillar 1, science by doing:** a "30 or 90 days of floating sauna + river plunge" experiment with real measurements. It borrows the one format that wins in English sauna content.
- **Long-form pillar 2, build diary:** the V2 build (already being filmed per the Build Diary plan) is exactly the episodic build format that holds 320K to 564K per episode for WILD HOMESTEAD. The difference is Ebb & Ember is building on water.
- **Search pillar:** own "floating sauna", "contrast therapy", "social sauna" and "sauna etiquette first time" with plain explainer videos. Supply is thin.
- **Skip:** trying to win with local news-style features or product walkthroughs. That's where business channels live, and the data says those stay under a few thousand views without ad spend.
