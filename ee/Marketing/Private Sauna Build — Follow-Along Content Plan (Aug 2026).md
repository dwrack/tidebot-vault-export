## 1. Why we're doing this

Five reasons, in the order they actually matter.

**1. It sells the thing before the thing exists.** Two private saunas is a new SKU at a new price point. A cold launch means starting from zero on day one. A build series means we open with an audience that already has a favorite (Sunrise or Sunset) and a waitlist we can email. Nobody books a room they've never seen; everybody books a room they watched get framed.

**2. Build content outperforms product content, and it isn't close.** Process has a built-in reason to come back. Product doesn't. Every post ends on an unresolved question (does the round window work, does the float hold the weight, does the glass wall pass), and the algorithm rewards the return visit. Our current grid is 30 videos of "what the ritual is." This is 30 videos of "what happens next," which is a different and better engine.

**3. It's proof, not marketing.** Our whole position is anti-wellness-industry: no cortisol claims, no guru energy. A build series is the most on-brand content we can make because it's just true. Here's the lumber. Here's the number the builder gave us. Here's the thing we got wrong and had to redo. That earns more trust than any testimonial we could shoot.

**4. It answers the objection we can't otherwise answer.** "Is a floating sauna safe / warm enough / actually nice?" People assume floating means flimsy. Watching a 690 lb rock cage get worked into a float load calculation kills that objection dead, and it's free to film.

**5. It builds the local-press asset.** Kyle's round window and a two-sauna floating platform on the Columbia are the pitch. Early Bird can't pitch a finished box, but they can pitch a build with a documented story and a photo library. Same footage feeds the pitch, the blog, and the /private-sauna-rental-portland pillar page that's still unbuilt.

**The honest counter-argument:** we're already framing and haven't shot anything, several design decisions are unresolved, and the SMM seat changes hands Sept 15. If we half-commit, we publish three posts and abandon it, which reads worse than never starting. So the plan below is deliberately low-effort-per-post.

## 2. The series

**Working title: "Two Saunas."** Alternatives: "Building Sunrise & Sunset," "Slip 32," "The Build." Not "Behind the Build" or anything with "journey" in it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cadence** | 2 posts/week — 1 Reel, 1 carousel or photo. Stories whenever anyone is on site. |
| **Home** | IG feed + Stories (highlight reel pinned "the build"), mirrored to YouTube Shorts |
| **Long form** | One blog post per phase on /journal, feeding the /private-sauna-rental-portland pillar |
| **Email** | Monthly build update to the list, plus a dedicated waitlist sequence |
| **Runtime** | From now through opening. Ends with a reveal, not a fade-out. |

**Voice rules.** Same quiet register as the 532 grid. Lowercase covers, no exclamation points, no "we're SO excited." No hype-build energy, no timelapse-with-trap-music. The tone is a builder's notebook: a number, a problem, a decision. Never call it wood-fired (it's gas). Never say "float" as a noun for the experience. It's the Columbia, not the ocean.

**Second audience: other sauna builders.** Davey's call, and it changes the edit. Builders are the ones who watch a floating-platform load calc all the way through, and they are the ones who share it. So keep the technical detail in — real numbers, real fixes, the header problem, the BTU math — instead of sanding it down for a general audience. Guests will still watch a clean 30-second Reel; builders will watch the six-minute YouTube cut. Make both from the same footage. That also means YouTube long-form is worth doing here even though it isn't worth doing for the ritual content.

**The recurring hook shape:** state a real constraint, then leave it open.
- "the heater lands on top of the glass floor. one of them has to move."
- "the rock cage adds 690 pounds. that number has to be in the float calc before we order the platform."
- "we asked for a floor-to-ceiling window. the builder said not on two adjacent walls, not without an engineer."

## 4. What to actually film

**The standing rule: one clip a day from the site, 20-30 seconds, horizontal-safe, no narration needed.** Volume beats polish. We can cut a good Reel from mediocre footage; we can't cut anything from no footage.

**Per visit, the shot list:**
- One wide, locked-off, no movement — the whole platform, 20 seconds. Same framing every time. This becomes the progress cut at the end, and it's the single most valuable thing on this list.
- Two or three hands-on close-ups — a saw, a level, a chalk line, glass being lifted, a bead of sealant. Tight, shallow, no faces needed.
- One ambient audio take with the phone still — nail gun, river, wind. Ambient sound is half of why the cinematic format works for us.
- One person doing one thing all the way through, uncut, 30 seconds. Not b-roll clips. A complete action.
- One vertical, framed for a Reel, of whatever changed since last time.

**Interview capture, when Kyle or Grant is on site and willing:** phone on a tripod, one question, let it run. "What's the hardest part of building this on water?" "Why does the window need a header?" "What would you have done differently?" Builders explain things well. Do not script them.

**Davey's on-camera pieces:** deadpan, own voice, no AI VO, same as the reel recipe. 15-30 seconds, one point per clip, shot leaning on the dock railing. Not a spokesperson read.

**What we are NOT doing:** drone every visit (once at framing, once on float day, once at reveal), music-video timelapses, or anything that needs an editor to be watchable.

**Gear:** a phone, a $30 tripod that lives in the truck, and a clip mic. That's it. Add a second cheap tripod at the dock for the locked-off wide so nobody has to re-frame it.

**The archive:** every clip lands in `Assets/Photos/<date>/` per the existing convention with a CATALOG.md line. Sloppy filing is how a build series dies — the footage exists but nobody can find the framing day six weeks later.

## 6. What the series is driving toward

Everything routes to one destination: a private-sauna waitlist.

1. Build `/private-sauna-rental-portland` early, even thin. It's already in the content queue as an unbuilt pillar. Make it the link in bio for the run of the series.
2. Waitlist form on that page. Every build post CTA is the same line, not a rotating cast of CTAs.
3. Monthly build email to the full list. Openers on build updates run high because they're not selling anything.
4. At reveal: waitlist gets first booking access before public. That's the whole payoff and the reason the series was worth doing.
5. Members get something ahead of the waitlist — first fire, or a walkthrough before opening. Cheap, and it's exactly the kind of thing the membership should feel like.

Success measures, so this isn't vibes: waitlist signups, saves and shares per build post vs. our normal grid, and how many of the first month's private-sauna bookings came from the waitlist.
