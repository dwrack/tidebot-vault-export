Researched 2026-10-29 with web search, page fetches and one small Apify profile pull (apify-ee, about $0.05, follower counts verified that day). Builds on `Sauna Content Research — What's Working (Oct 2026).md` (TikTok/IG view data). This note is about the marketing moves and what they got the business, not post-level views.

## Read this first

## Ten learnings

Ranked by how directly they would move bookings for a single floating sauna in Portland.

### 1. Sell a named night, not a sauna slot
- **Who:** Othership (themed classes, monthly social), Sauna Social Club (ambient listening, Sunday Aufguss).
- **What it gets them:** Pricing power and membership. A $64 class and about $400/month only works because the product is a structured evening. Sauna Social Club reports sell-out weeks and a capacity doubling.
- **Ebb & Ember, this winter:** Pick one weeknight and give it a plain name and a fixed format, say "Thursday Dark Hours": last session of the day, low light, one local musician or a playlist from a Portland record shop, tea instead of cocktails. Run it for six weeks before judging. Tie it to the themed weekday work already running.

### 2. Alcohol-free social time as the reason to come
- **Who:** Othership ("social self-care"), Sauna Social Club (non-alcoholic bar), Daybreaker-style crowds.
- **What it gets them:** Awareness and a different customer than the spa buyer. It also makes the sauna a Friday-night option, which is the slot most hard to fill.
- **Ebb & Ember:** Put "no alcohol, plenty of heat" into the Friday and Saturday evening copy and a 12 to 20 second dusk clip. Do not name wellness outcomes. Say what happens: eight people, a river, a cold plunge, phones in lockers.

### 3. Entertainment content beats calendar content
- **Who:** Othership's scripted skits (652K plays on one, per the prior pull) vs menus and event calendars from Löyly and Community Sauna Baths.
- **What it gets them:** Awareness only. Nobody reports booking lift from a skit, so treat it as reach.
- **Ebb & Ember:** One staged, funny, 20-second clip a month with staff or regulars (the group chat, the person who will not get in the plunge). Keep the venue calendar in email and the bio link.

### 4. Ticketed rituals and festivals
- **Who:** Therme's Culture of Bathe-ing (NYC, $60 to $95 sessions, 1,000 free tickets released), Toronto Sauna Festival (sold out, 1,500 reported), Great Northern Sauna Village (Aufguss on Tock).
- **What it gets them:** Awareness plus single-day ticket revenue, and a way to meet people who have never been in a sauna.
- **Ebb & Ember:** Co-host one ticketed evening in January or February with a guest sauna host who does infusion ceremonies, capped at the dock's real capacity. Pair with a partner who brings an audience (a Portland bathhouse-adjacent brand, a coffee roaster). Do not invent a festival we cannot staff.

### 5. Be the "first" and let press carry it
- **Who:** WYLD ("UK's first public floating sauna", Times and Vogue named in its bio, two awards in 2025), Löyly (400K year-one visitors, Time list), Allas (about 800K a year).
- **What it gets them:** Awareness, tourism demand and pricing power. WYLD went from launch to a permitted second structure in about a year.
- **Ebb & Ember:** We already hold "Portland's first floating sauna" (Axios, Jan 2026). Pitch a winter angle (steam on a cold river, a first-timer guide) to Travel Portland, Portland Monthly and the Oregonian's weekend section. Pitch the angle, not the brand.

### 6. Low off-peak price and a community lane
- **Who:** Community Sauna Baths (£10 off-peak, ~£23/month at 50% off, £230K community fundraiser), WYLD (£5 an hour at launch), Washington Baths ($35, walk-up waitlist).
- **What it gets them:** Loyalty, local press and in CSB's case community capital. It fills dead hours without discounting the prime ones.
- **Ebb & Ember:** Weekday 7 a.m. to 10 a.m. at a visibly lower price, set against the all-in price rule (the price shown is the price paid). Use it to anchor Service Industry Tuesday-style nights. Do not discount weekends.

### 7. Membership is a retention tool more than a revenue line
- **Who:** Remedy Place (about 200 members to break even, most revenue from non-members), Community Sauna Baths (monthly plan with half-price sessions), Othership (12 classes/month).
- **What it gets them:** Retention and predictable fill. At Remedy Place the cash comes from non-members, so memberships are a brand and cap-management device.
- **Ebb & Ember:** Keep Ember 1 and Ember 2. Report the member-to-guest-pass ratio monthly, and use the winter email to tell lapsed members what they missed (named nights) rather than a price offer.

### 8. Private charters and group hire carry the margin
- **Who:** Sisu Swim Sauna (C$995 for 2 hours, up to 11 people, then a second boat), WYLD (16-person private-hire floating sauna for corporate and retreats), Remedy Place (events revenue up 65%).
- **What it gets them:** Revenue per slot, and a booking that arrives 2 to 4 weeks ahead.
- **Ebb & Ember:** A winter "bring your crew" package for Portland teams (restaurants, studios, small offices) with one person to coordinate. Email 30 local owners directly in November for December and January bookings. Show the price on the page.

### 9. Partners who already own your customer
- **Who:** WYLD (gyms, hotels, tourist boards), Remedy Place (credit card cardmember benefits), Moki (rotating host sites).
- **What it gets them:** Awareness and low-cost bookings; for Remedy Place, a path to a rich buyer segment.
- **Ebb & Ember:** Continue the lodging outreach (staged, awaiting first send) with a hotel concierge card and a `ref=` link per hotel so we can see which ones book. Ask each partner for one thing, not a collaboration.

### 10. Real scarcity, shown honestly
- **Who:** Washington Baths (walk-up waitlist, 3-hour quote), Othership (3 weeks booked within 2 months), Remedy Place (capped at 200 in LA), Sauna Social Club (sell-out weeks).
- **What it gets them:** Pricing power and press interest, because a queue is its own story.
- **Ebb & Ember:** When a weekend is full, say "booked until Sunday 4 p.m., Monday has room" in the story. Never fake it. If a week truly sells out, screenshot it for the monthly email.
