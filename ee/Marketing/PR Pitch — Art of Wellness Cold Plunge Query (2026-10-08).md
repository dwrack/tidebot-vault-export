If there's still room, or a follow-up piece, here's the angle we think is missing from most coverage: the newest thing in cold water therapy is skipping the tub entirely and getting back into natural water.

Ebb & Ember is a floating sauna on the Columbia River in Portland, Oregon. Guests heat up, then step off the deck into the river itself. No chiller, no ice delivery. Quotes below from me, use any of them.

**What's new in cold water therapy**
"The big shift is people leaving the stainless steel tub behind and going back to rivers, lakes and the ocean. That's how the Nordic countries have always done it. A tub set to exactly 39 degrees is fine, but it's a controlled product. A river is alive. It moves, it changes every week, and you have to actually show up for it."

**Why natural water feels different**
"In a tub you're sitting in a box. In the river you've got current on your skin, open sky over you, and the water temperature is whatever the Columbia decides it is that day. People tell us the mental part is the real workout: standing on the edge, deciding to go."

**Jump vs. wade in**
"Jumping in and slowly walking in are two completely different experiences. Wading in, you negotiate with the cold inch by inch. Jumping, you commit, and the whole body resets at once. There's no halfway."

**Who it's for (and who should skip it)**
"Most healthy adults can handle a short dip. If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, or you're pregnant, talk to your doctor first. And nobody should treat cold water like a competition. A few breaths in and you get out."

**How to do it safely in open water**
"Never plunge alone. We run a buddy system, there's a ladder straight back onto the deck, a throw ring within reach, and life jackets for anyone who wants one. Know where you're getting out before you get in, keep it short, and get warm right after. That's the beauty of pairing it with a sauna: the heat is waiting for you on the deck. When in doubt, don't go out."

**The ritual**
"Hot, cold, rest, repeat. Sauna, river, sit on the deck and watch the water. That loop is older than any wellness trend."
