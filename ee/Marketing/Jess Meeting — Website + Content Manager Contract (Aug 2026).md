| Item | Spec said | Live today |
|---|---|---|
| `/about` | Ship it, 404 is embarrassing | **Still 404** |
| Blog at `/journal` | Home for 20 posts | **404. `/blog`, `/news`, `/stories` all 404 too. There is no blog.** |

So: the two 30-minute technical fixes shipped. Nothing structural did.

Meanwhile there are **seven finished blog posts sitting in the vault** — bachelorette parties, cold plunge FAQ, heart health, post-workout recovery, sauna near Vancouver WA, things to do near PDX airport, gift ideas — all written, all with schema and internal linking specced, and **nowhere to publish them** because the blog doesn't exist. That's the single cheapest unlock on the list.

### How to open it

Don't lead with the scoreboard. Lead with the question, then use the numbers if you need them.

> "Where did the site work stall out? I want to know if it's bandwidth, priority, unclear scope, or whether you were waiting on something from me."

Real possibilities worth naming so she doesn't have to: we never confirmed the Squarespace plan supports a blog, copy was owed from our side and never landed, the /wellness rename got flagged as a redirect risk, or she's simply been billed at 4-8 hours a week and it went elsewhere. Any of those is fixable. Don't guess out loud, ask.

### The must-ship list, in order

Everything is back-timed off a **mid-October opening**.

1. **Turn on the blog at `/journal`.** Squarespace native blog, auto BlogPosting schema, ~1 hour. Then publish the seven drafts on a schedule. Nothing else on this list unlocks as much for as little.
2. **`/private-sauna-rental-portland`, live before Aug 25.** Thin is fine: two renderings, a paragraph each on Sunrise and Sunset, "opening mid-October," and an email capture. This is the destination for the build series and the waitlist. Hard date, because the first build post goes out that week.
3. **`/about`.** It's been 404 since May. Team-only attribution, no names, per the buyout situation.
4. **`/wellness` → `/experiences`,** with the 301. Small SEO win, been pending since May, just close it.
5. **`/cold-plunge-portland`.** Real commercial upside, but it's fourth. If the eight weeks only fit three things, this is the one that waits.

### Ask her directly

- Is the current Squarespace plan capable of a blog, or does it need an upgrade?
- What's a realistic hours-per-week from you between now and mid-October?
- Which of those five do you want to own, and which should go to someone else?
- Anything on that list you'd push back on?

## Part 2 — The content manager contract

### The situation

Kimberlynn's last day is **Sept 15**. Lemonade PDX is the incoming social manager at a **$3K scope**. Jess and Hannah at Ours Here are already project-managing the sauna build. Three parties, overlapping surface area, and no written line between them.

### What has to get settled

**1. Who publishes to the site.** If Lemonade PDX writes a blog post, does it go through Jess to get live, or does she get CMS access? Routing every post through a second contractor is how a blog dies in week three. Recommend Lemonade PDX gets publishing access, Jess owns templates and structure.

**2. What's in the $3K and what isn't.** Named deliverables, not vibes:
- Feed and Stories cadence
- The build series, Sept 15 through the reveal, roughly six weeks
- Reveal push and waitlist email in October
- The monthly KPI report and action plan we told Kimberlynn we wanted

**3. Where the handoff artifact lives.** Kimberlynn owes a labeled `Assets/Photos/` folder, the Asana content calendar, and the 532 content grid. Someone has to receive it. If that's Hannah at Ours Here, say so now, because Sept 15 is three and a half weeks out.

**4. The gap.** Kimberlynn goes quiet Sept 15 in the middle of a build series that peaks Sept 22 at float launch. If Lemonade PDX starts cold on the 15th, we lose the highest-value week. Get her looped in during the first week of September, even informally.

**5. Overlap and double-pay.** Ours Here does site and design and project management. Lemonade PDX does social and content. Brand assets, photography direction, and blog production sit in between. Draw that line in the meeting rather than discovering it in an invoice.

### The question worth asking Jess plainly

> "Is Ours Here the right home for this coordination, or are we better off with the social manager reporting straight to me?"

More layers is not obviously better at our size. Her answer tells you a lot about how she sees the engagement.

## Part 3 — The build series as the forcing function

Short pitch, then a specific ask.

We're documenting the private sauna build as a follow-along, two posts a week, Aug 25 through the mid-October reveal, ending with booking opening the same hour a waitlist email fires. Grant's crew shoots a 30-second clip a day. Davey shoots site visits. Two paid half-days: float launch and reveal.

Why it's the right test of this whole setup: it needs the site (waitlist page), the content manager (the posting), and coordination (three vendors, one calendar). If we can run it, the rest of the plan works. If we can't, the plan was never real.

**Ask of Jess:** the pillar page by Aug 25, and her read on whether the series should be a named deliverable in Lemonade PDX's scope.

**Second audience worth mentioning:** other sauna builders. Davey's call, and it changes the edit toward keeping real technical detail in.
