# Community Framework: Fire Ladder + Stoke (DRAFT, Oct 2026)

Working idea bank, nothing approved or built. Two open conflicts with what already exists, see **Before we use any of this** at the bottom.

## The two reels this came from
- **Chain of whys:** keep asking "why" until you hit the real root of why the business exists. The community is built on that root, not on the product.
- **Identity reel:** people stay when leaving costs them part of who they are. Four levers: language, relationships, rituals, position. Content should signal what our people believe, not what we sell.

## Step 1: Chain of whys (needs Davey + Jonah's honest answer)
Sample chain to react to:
- We host sauna sessions. Why? So people feel better.
- Why does that matter? Most people have no reliable way to reset.
- Why? Modern life is warm, soft and always on.
- Why do it together? Doing hard things with others is how people used to bond, and there are few places left to do that.
- **Possible root:** "A place to do hard, good things together."

Everything below should serve whatever the real root turns out to be.

## Step 2: Four identity levers
- **Language.** Words outsiders don't use. Name the rounds ("first ember," "the ebb"), give regulars a name, name the cold plunge. Even a shared phrase said before the plunge works.
- **Relationships.** Standing slots like "Tuesday 6am crew" or "Sunday night reset." Same people, same time, so they notice when someone's missing. Quarterly members-only long-table dinner.
- **Rituals.** Same session arc every time (heat, plunge, silence, tea). A first-plunge moment where the room cheers. A solstice or first-snow plunge each year. Repeatable and a little weird.
- **Position.** Progress others can see: visit milestones, a founding member wall, a Keeper role (veterans guide first-timers through their first cold round). Status earned by showing up, not by paying more.

## Step 3: Content that signals identity
Post what our people believe. "Cold water doesn't care about your inbox." Member rituals. The 6am crew in the steam on the Columbia. The goal is someone scrolling and thinking "those are my people," not "that looks relaxing."

## The fire ladder (visit-based levels)
The twist: embers aren't the start of a fire, they're what it becomes after burning a long time. Hottest, longest-lasting part, and they relight everything else. So Ember sits at the top and ties to the name.

| Level | Rough visits | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Spark | 1st visit | Ignition. Everyone starts here. |
| Kindling | ~5 | Catching, still needs tending. |
| Flame | ~15 | Self-sustaining, a regular. |
| Blaze | ~40 | Visible, the energy others feed off. |
| Ember | ~100 | The steady core. Keeps the fire going for everyone else. |

Counts are placeholders. Tune so a weekly regular hits Flame in a few months and Ember takes about two years. Four levels (drop Blaze) is cleaner for a small team.

**Other fire words to keep in the pocket**
- **Hearth:** the gathering itself. "Hearth Night" for a monthly members' evening or dinner.
- **Keeper / Firekeeper:** a role, not a level. Embers who welcome first-timers.
- **Ash:** possible cold plunge ritual name (what's left after the fire).
- **Tinder:** skip, reads as the dating app.

## Stoke (referral program)
A verb people actually use: "Who stoked you?" "I stoked three people this winter." Referral becomes something members do, not a discount code.

- **Stoke Passes.** A few passes per season to bring someone to their first session, with the referrer there. The friend's first plunge happens next to someone they trust.
- **"Who stoked you?"** Ask at every signup. Say it out loud at their first session: "Welcome Sam, stoked by Jess." Costs nothing.
- **Fire lineage.** Wall, board or digital map of who stoked whom. Becomes the community family tree; big branches carry real status.
- **Stoke counts toward your level.** Each friend who becomes a member bumps you up, so status reflects contribution, not just spend.
- **Stoke Night.** One or two members-bring-a-friend nights a year. Easy growth, easy content.

**Rewards, lightest first:** recognition (name said aloud, lineage wall), then small perks (free session, "Stoked" patch or beanie), money last if ever.

## Risks
- Retention-by-identity gets manipulative if the belonging isn't real. If members sense engineered tactics it backfires, and Portland's sauna scene talks.
- Private sessions are core revenue. Lean too hard into crew culture and couples and solo bookers feel like outsiders. Keep a line between member programming and private-booking calm.
- Group programming can eat our highest-margin slots.
- Visit tiers can make it feel like a punch card. Let some steps come from stoking or a winter plunge challenge.
- Referral curdles when friends feel pitched. Cap passes so they stay a gift; no cash affiliate vibe. A bad first visit burns the referrer's credibility too.
