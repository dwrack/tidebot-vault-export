## 3. The survey (Van Westendorp, 4 price questions + 4 segment questions)

Send from the business, not a person. Subject: "Help us price the banya (2 minutes, we'll comp a plunge)". One screen, no login. Incentive: entered to win a private, or $10 off next social for everyone who finishes. Target 150+ responses so the curves can be split by segment.

Intro copy:

> We're building something new on the water: a traditional banya, 8 private sauna rooms with the full hot-cold-rest ritual, steam, and the river. Before we set a price we'd rather ask the people who already come. Four quick questions about price, four about you. No wrong answers.

Describe the product in one block first (duration, what's included, private room vs shared, plunge access, parking, towel), so everyone is pricing the same thing. Price per person for a 2-hour banya session, or per room if it sells by the room. Pick one before sending, don't ask both.

**Price questions (open number fields, per person per session):**

1. At what price would this be so expensive that you wouldn't even consider it?
2. At what price would this be so cheap that you'd doubt it's any good?
3. At what price would it be getting expensive, where you'd have to think about it but would probably still book?
4. At what price would it feel like a bargain, a really good deal?

**Segment questions (dropdowns):**

5. Who do you usually come with? Solo / partner / friends group / family / coworkers
6. How often do you sauna or plunge anywhere? First time / a few times a year / monthly / weekly or more
7. Have you been to a traditional banya or Russian/Ukrainian bathhouse before? Yes, regularly / once or twice / never
8. Zip code (optional) and household income band (optional, 5 bands). This is the slice Hormozi uses: the price curve for the top band is the one that sets the private-room price.

Optional 9: "If we offered a monthly banya membership, what's the most you'd pay per month?" Open number.

**Reading the result:** plot the four cumulative curves. The crossing of "too cheap" and "getting expensive" is the floor, "bargain" and "too expensive" is the ceiling. Price the social banya slot near the top of that range, the private room above the ceiling of the general curve but inside the top income band's range. Then split by Q5 and Q6: the weekly-or-more people set membership price, the friends-group people set the per-person price.

**Where to send:** the Période email list and members first (highest signal), then an IG Story link to the full follower base. Translate the intro and questions into Ukrainian, Russian and Romanian for the banya community outreach David noted, since that audience has a reference price from home and will anchor differently.

## 4. Fake-choice gate for the booking page (growth play #5)

Supplement brand raised prices 30% and sold more by asking one question before showing a price. Our version goes on the E&E booking page above the session picker, and in the OpenCX opener for pricing questions.

**On-page block:**

> **What matters most today?**
> ( ) The best time on the water and the full experience
> ( ) The lowest price

- Picks the first: show peak and private options first, sunset slots highlighted, "most booked" tag on private.
- Picks the second: show weekday off-peak socials, the 3-pack, and Ember 1. Headline: "Our lowest price is $49 off-peak."

Nobody is denied anything; both paths show everything below the fold. The point is that the guest tells themselves which kind of buyer they are before the number lands. Test for 30 days against the current page: conversion rate and average order value, from Période and GA4.

**OpenCX version** (for "how much is it?" messages): "Happy to help. Are you after the best slot for a group, or the lowest price to try it out?" Then the matching answer.
