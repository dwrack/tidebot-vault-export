## Why Tuesday, in one paragraph

Tuesday is our emptiest day (13% full in August). The 50%-off SERVICE code treats that as a pricing problem. It isn't. An empty Tuesday is an empty room, and a discount on an empty room is still an empty room. The Sept 15 Member Night proved the opposite move: a Tuesday, a reason to come, food from people we like, and the dock was full. This turns that into a weekly habit, and uses it to capture every new name that walks down the gangway.

## The frame: gifting, not giving away

The difference is who's doing the giving.

- **Giving away:** we cut the price, you take the deal. One direction. It trains people to wait for Tuesday and makes $59 feel like the ripoff.
- **Gifting:** everyone brings something, including us. We bring the heat and the river. You bring what you can afford and something for the table. Both directions. The price isn't lower, it's *yours to set*, and the dish you carried in is part of the ticket.

That's why the sliding scale reads as a gift and not a markdown: nobody is told what the "real" price was. There's no strikethrough. You pick your rung, you bring your thing, you're in.

Working lines (pick one, or none):

- **"Tuesday is a potluck on the water."**
- **"Bring a dish. Pay what fits. Stay as long as the heat holds."**
- **"Nobody's giving anything away on Tuesday. Everybody's bringing something."**

## How it runs

**When:** every Tuesday, 5 to 9pm. Doors stay open through the Moonlight slot so late service-industry folks can still land.

**Format:** rolling, not blocks. Today a Tuesday evening is two or three 1h45 social sessions with 8 seats each, and most of those seats sit empty. Hearth Night opens the whole evening as one event: you arrive when you can, you rotate through heat and river on your own clock, you eat on the deck, you leave when you're done. With the V2 private saunas opening mid-October, there are two rooms to rotate through, so the evening can hold 40 to 50 people across four hours without anyone feeling packed. (Confirm marina headcount and the waiver flow before saying 50 out loud, see Open calls.)

This is the "open up more availability" piece. We don't add sessions to the calendar. We take the calendar off Tuesday night entirely and replace it with one door.

**What you bring:** one thing for the table. A dish, a drink (no booze, see rules), a loaf, a jar of something, a playlist, or a skill: someone who wants to lead ten minutes of breathwork on the deck is bringing something. A sign at the gangway says exactly that, so the person who brought nothing feels it once and brings something next week.

**What we bring:** the heat, the river, tea, water, towels if they forgot, the focaccia if the county licensing question gets answered (it hasn't, keep it a guest-brought table until then).

**Rungs, not a discount.** Three tickets on Periode, same product, three prices, no "regular price" shown anywhere near them:

| Rung | Price | Copy |
|---|---|---|
| Ember | $25 | "Tight month. Come anyway." |
| Hearth | $39 | "What it costs us to keep the stove lit." |
| Keeper | $59 | "You're covering someone's Ember." |

The middle rung is the default selected. Nobody at the door asks which one you picked. Members come free, it's their night too.

If Periode can't do three prices on one event cleanly, the fallback is one $39 ticket plus a "pay it forward" add-on, which is the same math with less to explain.

**RSVP is the lead magnet.** Every ticket goes through the ActiveCampaign form (same one as Member Night, `/f/1`, or a copy of it) before Periode: first name, phone, email, "what are you bringing." That's the list. Texts go out Monday afternoon from the sauna Google Voice line, same as the Sept 11 blast, two waves to dodge the MMS limit.

## Why this beats the SERVICE code

| | SERVICE code (now) | Hearth Night |
|---|---|---|
| What it sounds like | 50% off | an event |
| Who it's for | restaurant staff | restaurant staff, plus anyone they bring |
| What we capture | a Periode booking | name, phone, email, and a reason to text them |
| Repeatability | a coupon you can run forever | a habit people plan their week around |
| Effect on $59 | makes it feel expensive | leaves it alone |
| Content | none | a table of food, people on the deck, every week |

Keep the SERVICE code alive but stop promoting it. Service-industry folks are the natural core of a Tuesday night anyway. Tell them about Hearth Night directly, they'll fill the Ember and Hearth rungs and bring the best food.

## The gifting layer, beyond the price

Three small things that make "gift" true instead of a slogan:

1. **The table gets a name card.** "Brought by Kennedy, Tues 10/21." Photographed every week. That's the recap post and it costs nothing.
2. **One guest leaves with the Keeper gift.** Once a night, whoever brought the thing everyone ate gets a free session for next Tuesday, handed over out loud. Cheap, public, and it tells the room what we value.
3. **The Ember rung is funded by the room, and we say so.** A chalk line on the board: "Keepers covered 6 Embers tonight." Nobody feels like a charity case and nobody feels like a sucker.

## Rules that have to be on the sign

- **No alcohol.** Heat and liquor is a safety line, not a vibe call. Cider, NA beer, shrubs, kombucha, tea all welcome. Partner brands from Member Night (Stray's NA line, Hetty Alice if they have one) are a natural fit later, not required at launch.
- **Food stays on the deck**, nothing in the sauna.
- **Buddy rule in the river**, same as always. Nobody plunges alone.
- **Waivers before heat.** Everyone, every time, the Member Night iPad flow.
- **Bring it labeled.** Allergens on a bit of tape. Guests bringing food for guests is a potluck, not food service, but the county question is still open for anything *we* serve.

## What to measure

Pull these every Wednesday for the first six weeks:

- Tuesday fill, as a share of the four-hour window's capacity (baseline is 13%)
- New names captured (never booked before)
- Rung split: Ember / Hearth / Keeper, and average paid
- Return rate: did a Hearth Night first-timer book any other day within 30 days
- Membership signups that trace back to a Hearth Night RSVP

Kill criteria: if after six weeks Tuesday is under 40% full or new names are under 10 a week, it isn't a lead magnet, it's a party for regulars. Still fine, but then stop calling it marketing.
