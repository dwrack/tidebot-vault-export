<ul>
<li><strong>Heat.</strong> Real sauna heat is 160 to 195 F with löyly (steam off the rocks). Infrared is a different thing: 120 to 140 F, you sweat, but it is not a sauna in the Finnish sense. Both are on this list, labeled.</li>
<li><strong>Cold.</strong> A sauna without a cold plunge is half the experience. We note what each place has: a plunge tub, a cold shower, or a river.</li>
<li><strong>Social or private.</strong> Some places are a community room you share with strangers. Some are a suite you rent. A few do both.</li>
<li><strong>Price.</strong> Listed where the business publishes it. Where we could only find a third-party number we say so and when it was published.</li>

<h2>The saunas, one by one</h2>

<h3>Ebb &amp; Ember (that's us)</h3>
<p>A cedar sauna built on a boat and docked on the Columbia at the north edge of the city. The windows face the river and the cold plunge is the river itself. If you are not ready for that, there is the Downpour: pull the cord and cold water pours over you from overhead for a full 20 seconds. Not a bucket splash, a steady downpour. Rooftop deck for cooling off between rounds. Sessions are 1 hour 45 minutes, daily 6am to 10pm. You can book a seat in a social session (up to 8 people) or take the whole boat for up to 10. Bias disclosure: we think the river plunge is the best cold in Portland. Honest downside: it is a boat in a working marina, so there is no lifeguard, parking is tight on peak nights, and there is one bathroom. <a href="/">Book a session</a>.</p>

<h3>Knot Springs</h3>
<p>The polished one. A members-first club on the east bank with soaking pools, a very hot cedar sauna, a steam room with custom scent blends and a single cold plunge that sits around 47 F. Skyline view, towels and robes handled, the whole thing feels like a hotel spa. The tradeoff is the crowd and the price. If you want a quiet, cheap sweat, this is not it. If you want a date night that feels like an occasion, it is.</p>

<h3>Löyly</h3>
<p>Two locations, NE MLK and SE 21st, and the closest thing Portland has to a classic Finnish public sauna. Electric cedar rooms, a steam room at the SE spot, timed reservations you can usually get same day, swimsuits required. No cold plunge, which is the one real gap: you cool off with a cold shower or a bucket. Massage and facial add-ons if you want them. The best value sauna in the city for a regular habit.</p>

<h3>Cascada Thermal Springs + Hotel</h3>
<p>The luxury end. Two dry saunas, an Italian marble steam room that runs 110 to 120 F, mineral soaking pools, a cold plunge and an ice fountain, all inside a hotel on Alberta. 21 and over. A day pass starts at $100 and you will want the full three hours to make it worth it. Go for an anniversary, not a Tuesday.</p>

<h3>Common Ground Wellness</h3>
<p>Old-school Portland. A dry cedar sauna, a courtyard soaking pool, clothing optional in the pool and sauna, prices from $18 for 30 minutes. No plunge, just an outdoor cold shower. It is not fancy and that is the point. If you have been going to saunas in this city since before it was a trend, you already know this place.</p>

<h3>SaunaGlo</h3>
<p>Technically Milwaukie, ten minutes south. A big Nordic sauna that holds around 18, a cedar barrel cold plunge, a dump bucket, and a family-friendly, walk-ins-welcome attitude that is rare here. Cheapest real sauna plus real plunge combination on this list by a wide margin. Worth the drive if you are south of Division.</p>

<h3>Everett House</h3>
<p>A converted Craftsman in NE with two personalities: a social side where people talk, and a silent side where they do not. Electric sauna, steam room, a circulating cold plunge and outdoor tubs, clothing optional. If silence is the thing you are after, this is the only place in town that guarantees it.</p>

<h3>Pure Sweat Sauna Studio</h3>
<p>Infrared, not Finnish. Six private suites on N Williams, some with a cold plunge for contrast therapy. You get the room to yourself, which some people love. The heat is gentler (infrared tops out well below a real sauna), so if you want löyly, keep scrolling. If you want privacy and a sweat on a lunch break, it works.</p>

<h3>Root Whole Body</h3>
<p>A wellness complex in Slabtown with a community cedar sauna in 30-minute slots, a private infrared booth and hydrotherapy soaks. No cold plunge. More of a spa menu item than a destination sauna, but a solid option on the west side where choices are thin.</p>

<h3>Guss</h3>
<p>A wood-fired barrel sauna on a trailer that parks at Sellwood Riverfront Park most weekends (and a few other spots monthly). You sweat, you walk into the Willamette, you repeat. About 12 people per hour-long session, advance sign-up required, with women-only sessions on the calendar. It is the only other river-plunge sauna in Portland and the most purely Finnish experience on this list. The catch is that it is weekends only and weather dependent.</p>

<h2>Best for...</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>First timers:</strong> Löyly. Cheap, easy to book, nothing intimidating.</li>
<li><strong>Cold plunge people:</strong> Ebb &amp; Ember or Guss if you want a river. Knot Springs or SaunaGlo if you want a tub. Ebb &amp; Ember if you want to stand under the Downpour.</li>
<li><strong>Date night:</strong> Knot Springs or Cascada. Or the whole boat at Ebb &amp; Ember if you want it to yourselves.</li>
<li><strong>Groups and birthdays:</strong> Ebb &amp; Ember private (up to 10), SaunaGlo private rental, Löyly private rental (up to 8).</li>
<li><strong>Silence:</strong> Everett House's silent side.</li>
<li><strong>Budget habit:</strong> Common Ground or SaunaGlo.</li>
<li><strong>Privacy:</strong> Pure Sweat.</li>
<li><strong>The view:</strong> Ebb &amp; Ember (river at water level) or Knot Springs (skyline).</li>
</ul>

<h2>Not on this list, and why</h2>

Connect Wellness in Vancouver, WA has wood-fired saunas and very cold plunges but we could not confirm current hours or prices. Sosta House is a sauna at a wine-country B&amp;B, not a Portland sauna.
