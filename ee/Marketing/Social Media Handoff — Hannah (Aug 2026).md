## 4. What has worked (and why)

**1. Reels, full stop.** Every post over 100 likes in four months was a Reel. Carousels and static graphics land between 17 and 67 likes with no exceptions. Statics are for announcements people already care about (member night, punch pass), not for reach.

**2. A direct question aimed at a Portland local.** Both breakout posts open the same way. "No plans tonight?" and "looking for something different to do with friends in Portland?" Both name the city or the river in the first line. Both are an invitation, not a claim. That is the pattern to clone.

**3. Naming a new thing.** The Downpour launch, Banya, sunset sessions, punch pass, dock glow-up. Product news outperforms product benefits by a wide margin. People will watch a thing being introduced. They will not watch a thing being explained.

**4. The Portland rain joke.** The whole approved hook bank is in `Marketing/Portland Downpour - Reel AB Test (for Kimberlynn).md`. Davey signed off on this angle in June ("these are way more fun"). Reframing Portland's most-complained-about weather as a downpour you book on purpose is a local inside joke, and locals are the audience.

**5. Asking a question in the caption.** The Banya post got 27 comments on 332 likes, the best comment-to-like ratio of anything that was not a giveaway. The comment-for-a-code post ("comment 'bonus'") pulled 12 comments on 32 likes. Comments are cheap to manufacture and the algorithm pays for them.

## What has NOT worked

- **Wellness education.** "Sauna supports heart health," "science is catching up," "the hotter it gets the more your body needs this." These are the lowest performers on the account, every time. This also conflicts with the brand position in `Marketing/532 Content Grid — 30 Videos (Aug 2026).md`, which explicitly bans cortisol/optimization talk. Stop making them.
- **Generic aspiration copy.** "summer by the river with your people. undefeated every time" got 22 likes. No place, no product, no joke, nothing to react to.
- **Cadence.** Roughly 40 posts in four months, about 2.4/week. The stated plan was 3–4 Reels/week plus daily Stories. The gap between plan and reality is the single most fixable thing on this account.

## 6. The operating rules from Davey

**Post cadence and platform.** Reels first, always. Instagram is the primary, TikTok and YouTube Shorts get the same cut with a platform-native caption. Do not spend design time on carousels.

**Don't repost as filler.** Reposting other accounts' content to the grid is not content strategy. The exceptions are (a) genuine guest UGC with written permission, credited and tagged, and (b) partner co-branded posts. Everything else on the feed should be footage we shot. The playbook for permissioned UGC is `Marketing/UGC Outreach & Repost Playbook.md`.

**Comment like a human, on purpose.** Commenting is now a tracked KPI, not a nice-to-have. Two blocks a day:
- Morning, 15 minutes: reply to every comment and DM from overnight.
- Evening, 15 minutes: 10 real comments on Portland accounts — run clubs, coffee shops, breweries, the food cart pods, PDX event accounts, other wellness operators, neighborhood accounts. Not emoji. An actual sentence.

That is **30 minutes a day, roughly 3.5 hours a week**, which is the honest answer to "how long should we be playing on Instagram." Instagram does reward session time, but it rewards *the account's* engagement behavior, not passive scrolling. Thirty focused minutes beats two hours of drifting. Cap it at 45 unless she is actively in a comment thread that is going somewhere.

**Train the feed.** The account's For You page should be sauna, cold plunge, Portland, and PNW outdoors — nothing else. Follow, save, and comment inside that lane deliberately for the first two weeks so the algorithm knows what this account is. Unfollow anything off-lane. This is a real setup task, not a metaphor.

**Go more PDX, less wellness.** The generic wellness lane is crowded and the account's own data says it does not work here. Portland-specific does. Name the river, name the bridge, name the weather, name the neighborhood. If a post could have been made by a sauna in Denver, rewrite it.

**Brand rules that are non-negotiable:**
- It is **Ebb & Ember** with an ampersand. Never spelled out.
- The sauna is **gas-fired**, never "wood-fired." Best practice is to just not mention the fuel.
- Never call it "a float" or "the float." It is a session, the sauna, or the floating sauna. "Float" reads like a sensory deprivation tank.
- We are on the **Columbia River in Portland, Oregon**.
- Do not name individual owners in public-facing content. Attribute to "the team" until further notice.
- Photos tagged **PRIVACY-HOLD** in a shoot's CATALOG.md need the member's OK before they post. Faces of guests need a verbal yes on the dock, every time.
- Never guess a fact about the business. Ask.

**The cold plunge face.** Nobody in this category films the face. Everyone films the water. The 532 grid already flags "everyone gasps, that's normal" as the most shareable line on the list. Build a recurring format out of it: tight on the face, ambient audio only, no music, no text until the last frame. Run it as a series.

- **Monday Banya with Danesh** — a whole ritual most Portlanders have never seen. Film the venik. That is unmatched footage.

**The "does this count as a downpour" seasonal flip.** Summer is "Portland gets one sunny day, we made it rain anyway." Winter is the real money angle — the Columbia hits ~40°F in January and 66°F in June is not impressive. Start banking winter footage in October.

**First-timer objection content.** "You don't have to go in the water" is both true, a safety rule, and the biggest booking objection removed. It is also the most useful thing this account can say. Related: what to bring, what the first ten seconds feel like, what happens if you hate it.

**The regulars series.** Bring a different person every week — a skeptic, an athlete, someone's mom, a first-timer, someone who has been 40 times. Multi-part arcs are the cheapest retention mechanic there is and the account has never run one.

**Faceless B-roll bank.** Steam, water, fog on the Columbia, last light at 4:30pm, the current moving. The lowest-effort content on the list and it is the only thing that lets her hit 3 Reels a week during a busy month. Every dock visit should end with ten new clips filed.

## 8. On-site cadence — the recommendation

She needs to be on the dock on a fixed schedule or the content bank runs dry, and dry banks are why the cadence slipped last time.

**Weekly, not bimonthly.** Two hours, same day every week. Recommended slot: **Friday 6:00–8:00pm.**

Why Friday evening:
- Golden hour on the river, which is the best light this location gets.
- Friday nights are busy, so there are real guests and real faces, not an empty boat.
- "Friday nights got a glow up" already tested well as an angle.
- The first hour before the session turns over gets clean empty-boat cinematic shots, the second hour gets people.

**Plus one monthly batch shoot: the first Saturday morning of each month, 4 hours.** That covers the 532 grid's three batch sessions — one full static-camera session, one cold fog morning, one round of dock talking heads. One long shoot per month produces most of the month's posts.

**Add for the seasonal content:** one early-morning fog shoot in October and one in January. The winter river is the strongest footage this business will ever get and it only exists for a few months.

Rotate in a Monday (Banya) and a Tuesday (service industry) visit once a quarter so those themed days actually have content.
