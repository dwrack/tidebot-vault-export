## CONTENT (Markdown body)

```markdown
Every year, somebody at your work does a white elephant gift exchange, and every year, at least one candle changes hands three times before someone finally keeps it. Nobody wants the candle. Nobody's ever wanted the candle.

An experience gift is different because it actually gets used. Someone goes somewhere, feels something real, and remembers who sent them. That's the whole pitch of this post, and also the whole reason gift cards are one of the most requested things in our inbox, right behind "how much does it cost" and "when are you open."

## What actually counts as an "experience gift"

An experience gift is anything you give that turns into a memory instead of an object. A cooking class. A concert ticket. A weekend trip. At Ebb & Ember, it's a [sauna and cold plunge gift card](/shop), redeemable for a real session on a [floating sauna](/) docked on the Columbia River in North Portland.

Here's the part that matters for gift-giving specifically: **you're not guessing at someone's taste.** You don't have to know if they like lavender or eucalyptus, whether they already own six candles, or if the scarf you picked matches anything they own. You're giving them time, heat, cold water, and a reason to put their phone down for 105 minutes. That's harder to get wrong.

We opened January 1, 2026, and gift cards showed up in our inbox almost immediately, more than we expected for a business that was, at the time, barely a month old. A guest wrote in not long after her visit just to say, "Beautiful spot, I'm giving these gift cards out!" That's not a review we paid for. That's someone doing our marketing for free because the gift landed.

## The gift card, straight talk on price

Two options, no fine print games:

- **Social Sauna Gift Card, $59.** Covers one seat in a shared session, up to 8 guests total on the float. This is the one you give a friend, a coworker, a sibling.
- **Private Sauna Gift Card, $539.** Covers a full private session for up to 10 guests. This is the one you give a couple for their anniversary, or the one a family splits the cost on for a parent's birthday.

Both run 1 hour 45 minutes: heat, [cold](/cold-plunge), recovery, repeat as many times as the guest wants. Both are delivered digitally so you're not mailing a physical card and hoping it doesn't get lost in someone's junk drawer. The recipient picks their own date through our booking system whenever it works for them, which takes the pressure off you to guess a good time. If they've never been, our [FAQ page](/faq) covers what to expect, what to bring, and how the cold plunge works before they book.

One honest note on how these get used in practice: if someone books a session and can't make it, our cancellation policy converts that booking into a gift card credit rather than a refund. So gift cards aren't just something you buy fresh, they're also what happens on the back end when plans fall through. It's a small detail, but it means the gift-card system at Ebb & Ember gets real, regular use, not just a once-a-year holiday spike.

## When this gift works (it's not just a holiday thing)

This is the part most gift guides get wrong: they write for one single occasion and the post is stale by February. A sauna gift card actually works year-round because the experience itself changes with the season, so it never feels like a rerun.

- **Holidays.** The obvious one, and it works, but it's far from the only one.
- **Birthdays.** No age-tag required. We've had guests in their 20s and guests in their 70s book the same session.
- **Valentine's Day or an anniversary.** A private session for two is a genuinely different date than dinner and a movie, and the cold plunge is optional, so nobody's forced into anything.
- **Mother's Day or Father's Day.** Especially for a parent who says they "don't need anything." They don't need it. They'll use it anyway.
- **Hostess or thank-you gifts.** A social gift card is a lighter-weight version of the same idea, useful for someone you owe a favor to but don't know well enough to buy them clothing.
- **New job, promotion, or "you survived a hard year" gifts.** We see more of these than you'd think.
- **Corporate and client gifts.** We've donated gift cards to school auctions (Bridlemile Elementary, for one) and to nonprofit fundraisers like Ukandu, and companies buy them directly for clients who already have everything.

The common thread: none of these require you to know someone's exact taste. You just need to know they have a body that gets tired sometimes. Most people do.

## How to give it without it feeling like a leftover idea

A gift card can feel impersonal if you hand it over with zero context. Four things fix that fast:

1. **Say why you picked it.** "You mentioned wanting to try a cold plunge" lands better than a blank envelope.
2. **Pair it with something small and physical.** A towel, a pair of flip-flops, a note. The gift card is the experience; the small object is what they unwrap.
3. **Offer to go with them.** For a social session especially, this turns a gift into a plan, and plans get scheduled while gift cards alone sometimes sit in an inbox for months.
4. **Tell them there's no deadline.** Under Oregon law, gift certificates generally can't carry an expiration date, so there's genuinely no rush. Say that out loud. It removes the guilt of "I still haven't used it."

## If you want to give something bigger: memberships

For someone who's already been, or someone you know is going to fall for this hard, a single session might undersell it. We run [three membership tiers](/memberships), from $99 to $299 a month, built for people who want a real practice, not a once-a-year splurge.

A membership is a bigger financial gift, so it's not for every occasion on this list. But for a partner, a parent, or a close friend who's already said some version of "I need to do this more often," it's the gift that keeps handing them a reason to actually follow through.

## What people actually say after

We don't promise transformation. We're not going to tell you a gift card will change someone's life, because that's not a claim we make about anything we sell. What we can tell you is what guests actually say when they leave: they mention the cold hitting harder than they expected, the quiet on the deck, the fact that they lost track of time in a way that doesn't happen often. One guest changed a booking to a gift card after getting sick and told us later she was glad she didn't just eat the loss. That's a small thing, but it's the kind of detail that tells you a business actually treats the gift-card system as a relationship, not a transaction.

If you're standing in a store aisle right now trying to decide between a candle and something that actually gets used, we'd argue the choice isn't close.

## FAQ

**Where can I buy an experience gift card in Portland?**
Directly at ebbandember.com/shop. A Social Sauna Gift Card is $59 for one seat in a shared session. A Private Sauna Gift Card is $539 for a private session up to 10 guests.

**Do Ebb & Ember gift cards expire?**
Oregon law (ORS 646A.276) generally bars expiration dates on gift certificates sold in-state, with narrow exceptions, and our cards follow that standard. No rush.

**What's a good gift for someone who's never been to a sauna before?**
The Social Sauna Gift Card. It's the lowest-commitment option, and the cold plunge is always optional, so a first-timer sets their own pace.

**Is a sauna gift card a good gift for a birthday or holiday?**
Yes. It's not tied to a fixed date, so it works for birthdays, holidays, Valentine's Day, Mother's and Father's Day, or just because.

**Can I gift a membership instead of a single session?**
Yes, for $99 to $299 a month. It's a bigger gift best suited for someone who already knows they love this.
```
